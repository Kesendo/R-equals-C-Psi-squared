## (b) Lemma A: on a path, M is non-derogatory

**Lemma A.** Let the graph be a path, vertices in their natural order, with every bond coupling `J_b ≠ 0`. Then `rank(M − λI) ≥ N − 1` for every λ ∈ ℂ, in either Δ book. Equivalently, the geometric multiplicity of every λ is at most one, hence exactly one at every eigenvalue.

This is the classical non-derogatory property of an unreduced tridiagonal matrix; it is stated and proved here because the repo does not hold it, and because the proof is two lines. *Proof.* On a path, M is tridiagonal, and its off-diagonal entries are `M[b, b+1] = M[b+1, b] = 2i·J_b` in both books, the Δ term being diagonal. Delete row 0 and column N−1 from `M − λI`. The remaining (N−1) × (N−1) matrix is triangular, with those off-diagonal entries on its diagonal, so its determinant is `∏_b (2i·J_b)`: free of λ, and nonzero whenever every coupling is nonzero. A matrix with a nonvanishing (N−1) × (N−1) minor has rank at least N−1. Rank is invariant under relabelling, so the natural order is a convenience and not a hypothesis. ∎

Gate **G2** carries this symbolically for both Δ and N = 2 to 6, comparing exactly, with no tolerance anywhere in it.

## (c) The corollary, and why no EP instrument appears here

**Corollary.** On a path edge block with all `J_b ≠ 0`, **any** repeated eigenvalue is defective, and an eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity m heads a single Jordan block of size m.

Geometric multiplicity one with algebraic multiplicity m ≥ 2 leaves room for nothing else. There is no diabolic alternative to exclude, which is why neither this proof nor its gate invokes `EpCharacter`, a Petermann factor, a phase rigidity, or any other defective-versus-diabolic discriminator: Lemma A closes by an exact rank argument what those instruments would have to decide numerically. The repo's history is why that matters, the real-axis Petermann magnitudes having been retracted as grid artifacts on 2026-06-21. Gate **G3** measures the rank statement where it can bite, at the EPs themselves, and prints the minimum eigenvalue gap over random draws to show why a random λ cannot test it.

## (d) N = 2 in closed form

With `M = [[−2γ₀, 2iJ], [2iJ, −2γ₁]]` the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial is

  **disc = 4·(γ₁ − γ₀)² − 16·J²,**

so the eigenvalues coalesce exactly on `|γ₁ − γ₀| = 2J`. On the branch `γ₁ = γ₀ + 2J` the doubled eigenvalue is `tr M / 2`, the nilpotent `K = M − (tr M/2)·I` equals `[[2J, 2iJ], [2iJ, −2J]]`, which is nonzero, and `K² = 0`. A genuine 2 × 2 Jordan block at real positive rates and a real coupling. The two Δ books differ at N = 2 by the scalar `−2iJ·I`, so the condition is the same in both. Gate **G4** carries all of it symbolically.

The repo already read this point as a Jordan point: `BlockSpectrumWitnessTests.cs:517-535` calls it *"an exact coalescence (a defective 2x2)"* and sqrt-scales its tolerance accordingly, and `OpenArcsRegistry.cs:3968-3969` says the same. Those are in the C# spin book, where `J_gate = 4·J_proof`. What §(d) adds is the symbolic certificate for a reading that was asserted and corroborated but never proved.

## (e) Existence, and the codimension that makes it cheap

**On the XY chain the discriminant is real.** A path is bipartite, so with `S = diag((−1)^k)` one has `S·conj(M)·S⁻¹ = M` **exactly** at Δ = 0, since `S·A_J·S⁻¹ = −A_J` while S leaves the rate diagonal alone. That identity is **not this document's**: it is `ChiralKClaim`'s `K·H·K = −H`, Tier 1, same K, same basis, any bond profile, combined with a rate diagonal that is real; `INHERITED_RULES_AND_THE_OWN.md:84-85` records the same pairing and shelves it in the Δ = 1 book, where the degree diagonal does break it. What follows from here is the new part. M is therefore similar to its own conjugate, its characteristic polynomial has real coefficients, and the discriminant is real. Gate **G5** asserts the similarity against literal zero, and asserts that the same similarity **fails** at Δ = 1, where the degree diagonal breaks it (`S·D_J·S⁻¹ = D_J`, not `−D_J`).

**So the real-λ EP set has codimension one.** `disc = 0` is one real equation in the rate space `ℝ^N` rather than two, and its solution set is a hypersurface of dimension N − 1 rather than the dimension N − 2 that two equations would leave. The scope is worth stating flatly, because the reality argument does not reach past it: a real characteristic polynomial makes the discriminant real, so its vanishing is a **coalescence on the real axis**, and every EP this route produces has Im λ = 0.

**What is gated about that reality, and what is not.** The gated quantity is the coalescing pair's **mean**, which is a trace over an invariant 2-space whose nearest neighbour sits 0.17 to 0.64 away, so it is stable and its computed imaginary part is a cancellation residual bounded by eps·‖M‖₂·n. **G6** asserts that bound and the isolation it rests on; **G6c** asserts it again at every witness across four decades of joint (γ, J) scaling, twenty-one readings whose worst is 0.46, with the alternatives printed beside them as a reading rather than asserted. Three assertions about those alternatives stood here across two earlier versions and all three were withdrawn: one was an arithmetic identity that passes on any input, and two turned on where the largest of twenty-one cancellation residuals happened to land, which a relabelling that changes no spectrum already moves. So the **shape** of the size factor is gated nowhere in this file, G1 included, and these witnesses span only N = 4, 5, 6, which could not separate it in any case. Two things this bound is **not**. It is not a bound on the *split*, which at a defective point is the unstable quantity and runs like √(eps·‖M‖₂); as a ratio of the two models that is 8.5·10⁶ to 2.3·10⁷ across the seven, and against the measured splits a wider 3.1·10⁶ to 3.5·10⁷, neither of them gated. And no raw magnitude is quoted, because a cancellation residual moves with the units: an earlier version of this sentence said 2.0e-16, which at a scale of 10⁴ becomes 1.4e-12 for the same physical EP. 

**Back to the codimension.** EPs between eigenvalues that are not each other's conjugates still occur as conjugate PAIRS of EPs and remain codimension two; the reality of the discriminant does not reduce those. What is codimension one is the real-λ stratum, and the witnesses below are all of that type.

**Existence, and what certifies it.** Existence then needs nothing more than a **sign change**: a real continuous function taking opposite signs at the ends of an interval has a zero between them. No topological degree, no radius, no point count, and no way for a near-coalescence to masquerade as a zero. Gate **G6** does this over the **rationals**: J, every rate and both bracket endpoints are exact decimals, so the discriminant is an exact rational, `Im(disc) == 0` is a literal identity and the two signs are exact.

**Why the certificate is exact, and two wrong reasons that were tried first.** An earlier version read the discriminant off a float eigensolver and asked only whether its imaginary part was small **relative to itself**, which cannot fail for an input whose imaginary part *is* a rounding residual, and that is every input here. Two attempts to reinforce that with magnitudes were both wrong and are recorded rather than dropped: the first claimed the witness and null values were comparable near 1e-24 (they are not, the null family sits at 1e-46 and 1e-40), the second offered the witnesses' spread of 3.65e-21 to 4.95e-10 as a noise floor, which confuses a spread *across* fixtures with noise *within* one; in fact all fourteen float endpoints reproduce their exact values to ten significant figures or better. So the exactness here is discipline rather than rescue, and the one place it genuinely bites is gate **G6b**: a bipartite star with **three** equal leaf rates, whose discriminant vanishes identically. Over the rationals it returns exactly 0 and the certificate refuses to fire; in floats it lands around 1e-46 at one gated rate and 1e-40 at the other, orders rather than values, since these are numbers whose signs and digits nothing determines. Three leaves and not two, because two span only one difference mode and leave the eigenvalue simple, with a discriminant that is not zero; and the semisimplicity is taken **exactly** there, algebraic multiplicity equal to geometric multiplicity (both 2 at the gated rates) over ℚ(i), rather than borrowed from §(h)'s different fixture or read off a float nullity, which would see only the geometric half.

## (f) What falls, and what survives

**The Edge lemma's conclusion is false under a rate profile.** §(e) contradicts it at N = 4, 5 and 6, on the operator it is about. Its premise fails first, `A = −2γ·I` being scalar only at uniform γ, but a failed premise merely removes a guarantee; §(e) removes the conclusion.

**The window-edge lemma survives, and it does the confining.** It needs only that the Hermitian part be Hermitian, which holds for any profile, and it forbids a defective eigenvalue only **at an edge** of the rate window `[−2γ_max, −2γ_min]`. Bendixson together with that lemma give, as a theorem and with no measurement:

> A defective eigenvalue on a path edge block **must** sit strictly inside `[−2γ_max, −2γ_min]`.

"Must", not "anywhere": the witnesses are seven points, and nothing here shows the interior is exhausted. Gate **G7** corroborates the containment; it does not establish it. At uniform γ the interval collapses to a point, there is no interior, and the old conclusion returns as the zero-width case. `PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY.md:115` says "the Edge lemma is the zero-width case" of a window too, and the two statements must not be merged: that window is in **n_diff** units, and on an edge block n_diff ≡ 1 at every γ profile, so its zero width is the constancy of n_diff and not the uniformity of γ. Read literally at :115 the sentence therefore claims a normal pencil on the edge block under any profile, which is one of the sites §(e) refutes rather than prior agreement with §(f). The bridge to use is the one `OpenArcsRegistry.cs:4134-4135` already wrote: "the gamma-profile bracket is that window with gamma in place of n_diff". The window statement is not new either: `hypotheses/INHERITED_RULES_AND_THE_OWN.md:88-89` states it for this block, Tier 3 labelled. One collision in that citation has to be on the page rather than left for the next reader: the same passage calls dropping the degree term "the same defect `site_resolved_vacuum_block` had already recorded". It is reading the **Heisenberg** chain, where the degree diagonal belongs and dropping it is indeed a defect; this document reads the **XY** chain, where there is no degree term to drop. The window content transfers either way, the degree diagonal being imaginary and the Hermitian part `−2·diag(γ)` in both books.

Of the two lemmas it is the weaker structural one that fails and the stronger derived one that holds. To be fair to the source: the Edge lemma carries its scope inside its own symbol (`A = −2γ·I`), and its proof runs at uniform γ = 1 throughout. What went wrong is that its **verbatim string was transported** into entries and claims scoped to arbitrary rates, and those are what §(i) lists.

## (g) Why uniform γ never showed this

Lemma A applies to the Hermitian Jacobi matrix `A_J` as well, so a path's hopping spectrum is **simple for any bond profile**. Hence at uniform γ the block's spectrum is simple: there is no repeated eigenvalue at all, and the Edge lemma's conclusion holds there for a reason that has nothing to do with normality. Gate **G9** measures 2700 random J profiles at uniform γ and never finds a degeneracy. It separately confirms, at uniform J, that M is normal at uniform γ, with a commutator that is exactly zero; that half holds for any real symmetric A_J and is not a reading of the 2700.

An earlier draft argued this from `2J(1 − cos(mπ/N))`, which is F2's closed form and misses the case twice over: it holds only at **uniform J**, and it is the **Laplacian** spectrum, Δ = 1, while §(g) is about the Δ = 0 adjacency block, whose spectrum is `4J·cos(mπ/(N+1))`. The Jacobi argument is both correct and free, being Lemma A again.

This gives the statement its final shape:

> On a path edge block, the per-site RATE profile is what can create a degeneracy at all, and Lemma A then leaves that degeneracy no form but a Jordan block.

A bond profile cannot do it. A rate profile can, and once it has, there is no choice left.

## (h) Scope, and three neighbours this is not

**Lemma A is about paths.** Gate **G10** is the control that isolates that hypothesis rather than a convenient one: on a **star with a rate profile**, M is genuinely non-normal, `‖[M, M†]‖` of order five to seven, and the repeated eigenvalue is nonetheless **semisimple**, nullity 2. So non-normality does not force defectiveness. What tridiagonality forces is the **conditional**, repeated ⟹ defective, and no more: §(g) says a path block at uniform γ has a simple spectrum and nothing defective on it at all.

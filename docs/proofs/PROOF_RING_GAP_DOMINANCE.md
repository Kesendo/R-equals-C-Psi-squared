## What this is about

A ring of spins, a loop closed on itself with no ends, stands in light. Light arrives from outside and wears its rhythms away. Different patterns of coherence fade at different speeds, and the one that lingers longest is the ring's memory, the last note still sounding after the others have gone quiet. What this document is about is the pitch of that note: how high it can be, and what decides it.

The answer turns out to be the same one a plain line gives, and the same one almost any network of the same kind gives: the highest surviving pitch is set by a single number read off the shape of the network, in units of the coupling, and how strongly the light arrives does not enter. It is tempting to credit the loop's evenness, since a ring can be turned around and nothing changes, and the smoothest pattern it can hold, a single wave spread evenly all the way around, is both the one that evenness would single out and the one that rings highest. But networks with no evenness at all obey the same rule, so the evenness is not what does it. On a loop it fixes the number at exactly two; that is all it buys.

There is a single exception, and it appears at one particular small loop. There a different pattern, built from two ripples rather than one, manages to fade just as slowly while ringing at a higher pitch than the even wave, and so it overtakes it. It only manages that while the arriving light stays weak against the coupling: strengthen the light far enough and the two-ripple note drops back under the even wave, and then past a second point it stops lingering at all. It is the same kind of two-ripple pattern that makes a few other small, highly symmetric networks behave strangely elsewhere in this project. The open chain, the ring's cousin with two loose ends, has the same quirk twice over. At three spins the odd extra note sounds just below the chain's highest, so the chain's answer still stands there. At two spins it sounds *above*, and the chain's answer is overtaken exactly as the ring's is. So the quirk points both ways on the chain alone, and which way depends on how short the chain is.

## Abstract

This is the cyclic sibling of chain gap-dominance, and running it beside the chain is what shows that the shared half is not either topology's. On the open chain we proved max|Im| over the exactly-(−2γ) modes is the single-particle band edge E1 = 2J·cos(π/(N+1)) = J·ρ_chain; on the ring it is 2J = J·ρ_ring (ρ_ring = 2, exactly the [TopologyBandEdge](../../compute/RCPsiSquared.Core/Symmetry/TopologyBandEdgeClaim.cs) "ring band edge"). Measured beside them, so is every other connected graph we tried: at N = 5 the star (√(N−1) = 2), the complete graph (N−1 = 4), the path and two graphs with no symmetry all read J·ρ to seven digits, and so do rings and paths with random bond weights against their own weighted radius. The **reach** half is not even topology-dependent in principle: |vac⟩⟨ψ_k| is an exact floor mode on any graph, so max|Im| ≥ J·ρ always. The content is the other half, that nothing exceeds it, and we have found two places where it fails: the 4-cycle here, and the two-site chain for Q > 2/√3 ([the chain sibling](PROOF_CHAIN_GAP_DOMINANCE.md) §4.2). Every sweep quoted above starts at N = 4, so the second one is outside all of them.

At N = 4 the half-filling (2,2) {0,2}-coherence forms a √-EP (a square-root exceptional point) that reaches 2√2·J, outranking the band top on the weak-dephasing side (Q > 1, the window below), because the Jordan-Wigner wrap bond splits the fermion problem into periodic / anti-periodic sectors and the anti-periodic two-fermion top (√2 + √2 = 2√2) lands on the −2γ floor only at N = 4. This is the same half-filling (2,2) sector that makes K_4 special (the [StructuralCeiling](../../compute/RCPsiSquared.Core/Symmetry/StructuralCeilingClaim.cs) outlier 2 − 2/√3), and it is the ring analogue of the chain's low-N exceptions. The chain has two, and they face opposite ways: at N = 3 its extra modes sit *below* E1 (so the chain max is unchanged), at N = 2 they sit *above* it for Q > 2/√3 (so the chain max is exceeded, as the ring's is here). The ring N = 4 case is the twin of the chain's N = 2 case, not of its N = 3 one.

**On the name.** "Dihedral lock" is this arc's identifier and the source of the same phrase in the [Heisenberg ring N=4 proof](PROOF_RING_N4_DIHEDRAL_LOCK.md), which is a different Hamiltonian and a different number. It names nothing in the argument: there is no reflection in it, and there is no cyclic structure in it either, since graphs with no symmetry at all obey the same law. The mechanism is Perron-Frobenius on a connected graph, and 2-regularity is what makes the ring's Perron vector uniform and its ρ equal to 2. The word is kept because the typed claims, the scripts and the arc name all carry it; read it as a label, not as a mechanism.

## Setup

**The Hamiltonian.** H = (J/2)·Σ_{⟨ij⟩} (X_i X_j + Y_i Y_j) over the cyclic bond set, under uniform local Z-dephasing at rate γ per site. The hopping amplitude is then J, the single-excitation block is H_se = J·A with A the ring adjacency matrix, and the band top is 2J. Every frequency in this document is in that convention, the one the verifiers run; the repo also carries a spin convention where the same chain is written J·S_i · S_j, and the numbers below do not transfer to it unchanged.

**Notation.** A Liouville-space coherence |i⟩⟨j| carries a sector label (a, b) = (the excitation number of the ket i, that of the bra j); n_XY is its Hamming weight, the number of sites at which i and j differ. A {0, 2} block is a 2×2 coupling a population (Hamming weight 0) to a two-site coherence (Hamming weight 2): their mean ⟨n_XY⟩ = 1 places the block on the −2γ floor.

**The floor.** By the [Absorption Theorem](PROOF_ABSORPTION_THEOREM.md), Re(λ) = −2γ·⟨n_XY⟩, so a mode sits at exactly −2γ iff its average ⟨n_XY⟩ = 1. That is weaker than n_XY ≡ 1, and the whole document lives in the difference. The pure n_XY = 1 subspace V_1 always qualifies: there the dephasing dissipator is the scalar −2γ, so L = L_H − 2γ (with L_H = −i[H, ·], the Hamiltonian part) and the frequencies are the L_H eigenvalues. A {0, 2} block qualifies as well whenever it closes on the floor, since its two diagonal cells carry 0 and −4γ and average to −2γ, and such a block lies *outside* V_1. Whether any {0, 2} block does close is exactly the question the completeness section answers: on the ring it happens at N = 4, at no other N the gate reaches, and there only above that block's own EP (see *The exception has a Q window* below).

## The band-top reach (general N): the (0,1) single-excitation sector

The cleanest exact-(−2γ) modes are the (0,1) sector: |vac⟩⟨ψ_k|, where ψ_k is an eigenvector of the single-excitation Hamiltonian H_se = J·A (A = the ring adjacency matrix), energy E_k = J·(adjacency eigenvalue). These are single-particle, so no Jordan-Wigner wrap subtlety arises.

- **n_XY = 1:** |vac⟩ and a single-excitation state differ at exactly one site, so the coherence has Hamming distance 1 ⟹ Re = −2γ.
- **Frequency E_k:** H|vac⟩ = 0 and H_se ψ_k = E_k ψ_k, so [H, |vac⟩⟨ψ_k|] = −E_k|vac⟩⟨ψ_k|, hence L_H[|vac⟩⟨ψ_k|] = +iE_k|vac⟩⟨ψ_k| and L[|vac⟩⟨ψ_k|] = (−2γ + iE_k)|vac⟩⟨ψ_k|, an exact eigenmode.
- **The band top:** the ring adjacency A is circulant, eigenvalues 2cos(2πm/N); the largest is 2 at m = 0, the **uniform** mode ψ_0 = (1/√N)Σ_i|e_i⟩, the Perron vector of a connected 2-regular graph. So max_k E_k = J·ρ = 2J is reached exactly, at every N. What the observable sees is |E_k|, and at **even** N that maximum is attained twice: m = 0 at +2J and m = N/2 (the staggered Néel mode) at −2J, since 2cos(π) = −2. Measured at N = 4, 6, 8; at odd N = 5, 7 the top is simple. So no symmetry argument singles out the uniform mode at the sizes where the exception lives; what is single is the **value**.

Verified for N = 3..6 (every (0,1) mode is an exact −2γ + iE_k Liouvillian eigenmode, band top = 2J): `ring_gap_dominance.py` STAGE 1.

## The N=4 exception: the half-filling (2,2) √-EP

The full Liouvillian (N = 3..6) gives max|Im| at the floor = 2J for N = 3, 5, 6, but at N = 4 it is √((2√2·J)² − (2γ)²) → 2√2·J (`ring_gap_dominance.py` STAGE 0, |diff| < 1e-5).

The wrap bond, under Jordan-Wigner, carries the fermion-parity factor (−1)^{N_tot}: odd parity → periodic boundary conditions (single-fermion energies 2J·cos(2πm/N), top 2J), even parity → anti-periodic (2J·cos(π(2m+1)/N)). At N = 4 the anti-periodic single-fermion energies are {√2, √2, −√2, −√2}·J, so the two-fermion top is √2 + √2 = 2√2·J. The half-filling (2,2) {0,2}-coherence built on this top forms a √-EP that lands on the −2γ floor: a γ-sweep confirms its frequency is √((2√2·J)² − (2γ)²) (STAGE 2, g = 0.05/0.10/0.20), exceeding the band top 2J. At N = 6 the (3,3) half-filling does **not** close on the floor at all: at the canonical γ = 0.05 every one of the 24 floor modes there is pure n_XY = 1, so max|Im| stays 2J. (Floor *counts* can move with γ, but not as a trend: at the isolated value γ = 0.5 the same N = 6 ring reads 28 floor modes, the four extra ones non-oscillating at Im = 0, while γ = 0.49, 0.495, 0.505 and 0.51 all read 24 again. That is a real eigenvalue pair parked near −1 that the sweeping −2γ line crosses transversally at γ = 1/2, not a {0,2} block coming onto the floor. max|Im| is the robust quantity, 2J at every γ tried.) N = 4 is the lone exception, the unique even half-filling where the {0,2} block lands exactly on −2γ. This is the same sector that makes K_4 and ring-4 special elsewhere in the project.

**The exception has a Q window.** Q = J/γ throughout, so large Q means the light is weak against the coupling. A {0, 2} block rides the floor only while its coupling beats the arriving light. Its two eigenvalues are −2γ ± √((2γ)² − B²), so they stay on the floor at frequency √(B² − (2γ)²) for B > 2γ, coalescing defectively at B = 2γ (its EP, a [coherence horizon](../../compute/RCPsiSquared.Core/Symmetry/CoherenceHorizonClaim.cs) in the sense of that claim; note that ring-4's own cell in the [`SecondClockRegimeClaim`](../../compute/RCPsiSquared.Core/Symmetry/SecondClockRegimeClaim.cs) regime map is GRADUAL, not the dispersive EP cell, so this block's EP and that map's cell are two different objects), and below that splits along the real axis and leaves the floor. Two thresholds follow at N = 4, and they are not the same number:

- **Q = 1** (γ = J), where the (2,2) frequency √((2√2·J)² − (2γ)²) has fallen back to the band top 2J. This is where the *exception* ends: for Q ≤ 1 the ring maximum is 2J at N = 4 like everywhere else, even though the block is still on the floor.
- **Q = 1/√2** (γ = √2·J), the (2,2) block's own EP, where the pair leaves the floor and the count drops to the pure V_1 dimension.

Measured (γ-sweep, `ring_gap_dominance.py` STAGE 3): the floor carries 26 modes while 2γ ≤ 2J, 18 once the four (1,1)/(3,3) blocks (B = 2J, eight modes, since each block contributes the conjugate pair) have passed their EP at γ = J, and 16 once the (2,2) block passes its own at γ = √2·J. max|Im| exceeds 2J exactly on γ < J. So the headline exception is a statement about the weak-dephasing side, Q > 1; the chain sibling draws the same line in its [§5 Consequence](PROOF_CHAIN_GAP_DOMINANCE.md), where the floor modes exist at every γ but whether the clock *reads* them is the separate regime question.

## Contrast with the chain, and with everything else measured beside them

| topology | adjacency radius ρ | floor max\|Im\| | lone exception | exception relative to band edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **open chain** | 2cos(π/(N+1)) | 2J·cos(π/(N+1)) = E1 | N = 3 | √(E1² − (2γ)²) **below** E1 (max unchanged) |
| **open chain** | 1 (N = 2) | J | N = 2 | 2√(J²−γ²) **above** E1 = J for Q > 2/√3 (max exceeded) |
| **cyclic ring** | 2 | 2J | N = 4 | 2√2·J **above** 2J (max exceeded) |
| **star** | √(N−1) | J·√(N−1) | none found | |
| **complete K_N** | N−1 | J·(N−1) | none found | |
| **asymmetric (bull, paw+tail at N=5)** | (no closed form) | J·ρ | none found | |

Every row reads floor max|Im| = J·ρ, the adjacency spectral radius (= the TopologyBandEdge band edge). The two closed forms are the chain's and the ring's; the law is not. Exhaustively at N = 4, all 38 connected labelled graphs obey it **except the three labellings of the 4-cycle**, and rings and paths with random bond weights obey it against their own weighted radius. So among those 38 graphs the ring's exception is the one genuine failure, and it is genuine (it exceeds the band top, for Q > 1) because the ring band top 2J sits low enough that the anti-periodic half-filling sum 2√2·J overtakes it. The chain's N = 3 extra modes sit below E1 and change nothing. Its N = 2 ones do not: they exceed E1 = J for Q > 2/√3, a second genuine failure that this sweep cannot see because it begins at N = 4.

**This is not the spectral max|Im|.** The quantity above is the maximum over the exactly-Re=−2γ modes, the slowest oscillating rung, and it is far from the largest imaginary part in the spectrum. [F148](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f148-the-imaginary-reach-is-the-hamiltonian-spread-on-every-graph-minted-2026-07-31) states a different law with the same notation, `max|Im λ_L| = ΔE_max(H)` over the whole spectrum, for isotropic Heisenberg with a fully polarised extreme. On the XY ring at N = 6, γ = 0.05, the two numbers are 2.0 and 7.996 against ΔE_max = 8.0; XY approaches ΔE_max as γ → 0 but never attains it, which is F148's own attainment failure for XY. Read `max|Im|` here as always carrying "on the floor".

## The second-clock floor frequency: chain N=3 and ring N=4 are one law

The chain's N=3 exception and the ring's N=4 exception look unrelated: one sits *below* the band edge (√(E1²−(2γ)²) < E1), the other *above* the band top (2√2·J > 2J). They are the same law (`simulations/second_clock_frequency.py`, gate-first, all stages green). The on-floor {0,2} √-EP is a 2×2 block whose diagonal cells are the population's 0 and the 2-Hamming coherence's −4γ, coupled by the bare amplitude B:

    [[0, B], [−B, −4γ]],     eigenvalues −2γ ± √((2γ)² − B²),

so the two eigenvalues ride the floor at the mean of the block's two cells, and for B > 2γ its floor frequency is

    |Im| = √(B² − (2γ)²),     B = the free-fermion band top of the {0,2} block's sector (the γ→0 coupling),

with the EP at B = 2γ (i.e. Q* = 2J/B), where the block is genuinely defective: M + 2γ has rank 1, one eigenvector, a Jordan block. (The equal-diagonal form [[−2γ, ω], [−ω, −2γ]] cannot be the block: its eigenvalues are −2γ ± iω at every γ, which would put the pair on the floor forever and leave no EP to speak of.) B is γ-independent (verified by a γ-sweep over γ = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20: spread 2.2e-15, 3.1e-15 and 7.1e-15 for the three instances the script sweeps, so < 1e-14; the chain N = 2 row is gated in [the chain sibling](PROOF_CHAIN_GAP_DOMINANCE.md) §4.2). The on-floor instances:

| instance | B | which free-fermion band top | floor freq vs band edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| chain N=3 (1,1), and its particle-hole partner (2,2) | √2·J | E1 = 2J cos(π/4), single-particle | √(E1²−(2γ)²) **below** E1 |
| chain N=2 (1,1) | 2J | E1 = 2J cos(π/3) = J, single-particle | √((2J)²−(2γ)²) **above** E1 for Q > 2/√3 |
| ring N=4 (1,1), and its particle-hole partner (3,3) | 2J | periodic single-particle top | **below** 2J (√-shift) |
| ring N=4 (2,2) | 2√2·J | anti-periodic TWO-fermion top | **above** 2J |

So "the second clock overtakes the first (band edge J·ρ)" reduces to **B > J·ρ**, and there are two exceed-cases, not one. The ring (2,2) has B = the anti-periodic two-fermion top 2√2·J, overtopping the periodic band edge 2J. The chain N = 2 (1,1) has B = 2J against ρ = 1, so B > J·ρ by a factor 2, and the √-shift only pulls it back below E1 once Q ≤ 2/√3. The chain N = 3 (1,1) instance has B = J·ρ exactly, and there the √-shift puts the floor frequency just below. One √-envelope; only B is sector/topology-specific. **Scope:** the {0,2} pins to the −2γ floor only at chain N=2, chain N=3 and ring N=4, and there only above its own EP (Q > Q\* = 2J/B; below it the pair splits off the floor along the real axis); at other N it drifts off-floor at every Q, so this is the unification of those three specials, not an all-N floor law. It characterizes the second-clock *floor frequency*; the off-floor seam (the ring 2-excitation `(2,2)/(N−2,N−2)` doublet V-Effect seam that kept `CoherenceHorizonClaim` at Tier 1 candidate) was closed separately by [the Ring Handover Slope proof](PROOF_RING_HANDOVER_SLOPE.md), not by this frequency result.

## The free-fermion completeness

Beyond the reach half, the all-N step is the **completeness**: that nothing exceeds 2J for N ≥ 5, i.e. that the n_XY = 1 free-fermion family *spans* the exact-(−2γ) subspace (the cyclic analogue of the chain proof's span argument). It is gate-verified by the same dimension-match the chain uses (`simulations/ring_gap_completeness.py`), worked in the n_XY = 1 **operator subspace** V_1 (dimension N·2^N, far smaller than the full Liouville space 4^N; N = 8 is 2048 vs 65536).

**The V_1 method.** An operator A **is** an exact-(−2γ) Liouvillian eigenmode if (i) it is n_XY = 1 (so L_D = −2γ is scalar) and (ii) it is an H-eigenoperator (so the n_XY = 1 → 3 leak cancels). Only that direction holds: ⟨n_XY⟩ = 1 is weaker than n_XY ≡ 1, and the N = 4 row below is exactly a floor mode that fails (i). So what this computes is the part of the exact-(−2γ) subspace lying inside V_1, the largest ad_H-**invariant** subspace of V_1, computed degeneracy-robustly as the leak null space ker(ad_H − P_1 ad_H P_1) iterated to its ad_H-invariant core. **Sanity (STAGE 0):** on the open chain this reproduces the full-Liouvillian counts exactly: dim_sub = 32 / 50 / 72 at N = 4 / 5 / 6 and max|Im| = E1, so V_1 sees the whole exact-(−2γ) subspace, not a part.

**The result.**

| N | V_1 dim_sub (n_XY = 1) | full-L dim_sub | spans? | full max\|Im\| | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 16 | 16 | **yes** | 2J | free-fermion complete |
| 4 | 16 | 26 | no | 2√2·J | the {0,2} exception |
| 5 | 20 | 20 | **yes** | 2J | free-fermion complete |
| 6 | 24 | 24 | **yes** | 2J | free-fermion complete |
| 7 | 28 | 28 | **yes** | 2J | free-fermion complete |
| 8 | 32 | 32 | **yes** | 2J | free-fermion complete |

The N = 8 row needs no separate V_1 run: the four outer sectors (0,1), (1,0), (7,8), (8,7) contribute 4N = 32 modes that are in V_1 and are exact floor modes by the Section-4 construction, so V_1 dim_sub ≥ 32; and V_1 dim_sub ≤ full-L dim_sub = 32 from the sector walk. The two bounds meet, so N = 8 spans as well.

The second column reads 4N from N = 4 on, but not below it: at N = 3 the invariant core is 16, not 12. The count splits as the usual twelve modes of the outer sectors (0,1), (1,0), (N−1,N), (N,N−1) plus four more in the *middle* sectors (1,2) and (2,1), which are n_XY = 1 as well (a one-excitation and a two-excitation state can differ at a single site) and happen to be ad_H-invariant at N = 3 and at no larger N the gate reaches. Why the middle sectors close there is not derived here; what matters for this proof is that they are inside V_1, so N = 3 still spans (16 = 16) and its maximum is still the band top. The 4N pattern is a reading of the table, not a law to extrapolate downward.

For N = 3, 5, 6 the n_XY = 1 free-fermion family **is** the entire exact-(−2γ) subspace (V_1 dim_sub equals the full-Liouvillian dim_sub, all three rows gated in `ring_gap_completeness.py` STAGE 2), so the dihedral-locked band top 2J is the maximum, nothing exceeds it. The V_1 method carries the n_XY = 1 dihedral lock (max|Im| = 2J) to N = 7 on its own. Beyond that the wall is not where it looks: L is block-diagonal in the sector label (a, b), because H conserves excitation number and the dephasing is diagonal, so the largest block at N = 8 is C(8,4)² = 4900 rather than 4^8 = 65536. Walking the blocks (`ring_gap_dominance.py --slow` STAGE 4, which first reproduces the N = 4, 5, 6 full-L answers) gives the **whole** floor at N = 7 and N = 8: 28 and 32 modes, max|Im| = 2.000000 at both, over every sector rather than inside V_1. At N = 7 that also closes the span question there (28 = 28); at N = 8 the V_1 dimension was not run separately, and the squeeze above closes the span. N = 4 is the lone exception precisely because the full-L subspace is *larger* than V_1 there (26 > 16): the ten extra modes are five {0,2} blocks that close on the floor at N = 4 and nowhere else, each contributing its conjugate pair: one half-filling (2,2) block whose pair reaches 2√2·J, and four (1,1) and (3,3) blocks whose eight modes sit at √((2J)² − (2γ)²), just below the band top. All ten are n_XY-mixed (support on 0 and 2), so all ten sit outside V_1; only the (2,2) pair exceeds. The **parity-split** is the wrap-bond Jordan-Wigner grading (periodic odd / anti-periodic even); it is what places the anti-periodic two-fermion top on the floor at N = 4 and nowhere else.

This closes the completeness to the same standing as the chain proof: a dimension match at the accessible N (chain 32/50/72; ring 16/20/24/28) plus the structural fact that only the smallest even half-filling (N = 4) puts a {0,2} block on the floor, which the sector walk now checks directly at the next even size: N = 8 reads 2J over every sector, half-filling included. The residual genuinely-all-N step is the same one the chain leaves open, a dimension count holding for every N, here split by the wrap-bond parity sectors.

## Scope: what the statement needs, and how thin the exception is

The Q window above fences γ. Three more fences belong to the sentence, and one of them is much tighter than γ. Every number in this section is produced by [`simulations/ring_band_edge_scope.py`](../../simulations/ring_band_edge_scope.py), which also runs the cross-topology sweep behind the Statement.

- **Uniform γ is load-bearing for the object, not just for the value.** With site-dependent rates there is in general no exactly-Re=−2γ̄ set at all to take a maximum over: at N = 4 and N = 5, a single site detuned to 2γ₀ leaves the set **empty**. An alternating profile at N = 6 keeps 24 modes but at max|Im| = 1.9998437, no longer the band top. The Absorption Theorem's Re λ = −2γ⟨n_XY⟩ becomes a site-weighted sum, and the degeneracy that defines the floor is gone.
- **The full single-site {Z_l} jump set is load-bearing.** Dropping Z₀ pulls the floor max from 2 down to 1.6180340 at N = 5 and to 1.0000000 at N = 6. Keeping only {Z₀, Z₁} inflates the floor to 126 modes at N = 4 and 696 at N = 6, and its max then simply equals the whole-spectrum max, so the dominance statement goes vacuous.
- **The XY form is load-bearing for the value.** At XXZ Δ = 0.01 the floor reads 24 modes at 2.0200000 (N = 4, the exception already gone), 20 at 1.9800000 (N = 5) and 24 at 2.0200000 (N = 6): the ZZ term shifts the frequency off J·ρ by ±2ΔJ, sign following the parity of N. Any Δ ≠ 0 moves it, so the closed form belongs to the free-fermion point.
- **J > 0 is not load-bearing.** J = −1 gives the identical 2 / 2.8266588 / 2 / 2 at N = 3..6, because ρ is a spectral radius. This is the opposite of [F148](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f148-the-imaginary-reach-is-the-hamiltonian-spread-on-every-graph-minted-2026-07-31), where the sign of J is what puts the polarised state at the extreme.

**The exception is fine-tuned in coupling space, far more than in γ.** Detuning the wrap bond alone to J(1 − δ) at γ = 0.05: at δ = 1e-6 the exception survives (26 floor modes, max 2.8266581); at δ = 1e-4 it is already gone (24 modes, max 1.9999500 = the detuned ρ); at δ = 1e-3, 16 modes. What is discontinuous is the *defined observable*, which reads only the exactly-Re=−2γ set; the underlying coherence is still there, it has simply left that set. So "the lone exception at N = 4" is a statement about the perfectly uniform 4-cycle, and any real ring with 1-part-in-10⁴ bond disorder does not have it. The continuous companion of this discontinuity is read in the time domain by [THE_CRACKED_BELL](../../experiments/THE_CRACKED_BELL.md): the same crack splits every m ↔ N−m pair by 4δJ/N and reverses a launched traveling wave at πN/(4δJ), the reversal carrying visibility exp(−πN/(2Qδ)), so the δ = 1e-4 crack the floor set sees carries a reversal amplitude of 10^(−1364) at N = 4, Q = 20, and even the most sensitive early-time read of the beat sits at 2.7e-7 there; the hypersensitivity belongs to the idealized set-membership read, not to anything a clock could measure.

**And the N = 4 floor is the one that is numerically thin.** The nearest off-floor eigenvalue sits 4.7e-5 from −2γ at N = 4, against 3.3e-2, 6.0e-2 and 3.3e-2 at N = 3, 5 and 6. The gates' tolerance of 1e-7 has about 2.5 decades of margin there rather than the 5 or 6 it has elsewhere; the counts are stable across 1e-12 to 1e-6, and max|Im| is stable at every tolerance, but this is the one place where "exactly −2γ" is a near thing rather than an obvious one.

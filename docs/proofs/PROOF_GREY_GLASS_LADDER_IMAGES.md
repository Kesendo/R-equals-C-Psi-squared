## What this means

A block of cells that the Hamiltonian keeps is coloured when the light prices its cells differently, and inside it the motion is bent as well as dimmed. The two ladders of the doubled picture commute, on the XY chain, with both parts of the generator, so each carries a grey room onto a grey room: under equal light the boundary blocks are grey as wholes, and their images are grey rooms inside every coloured block of the window, one for each Hamming distance. In a smallest coloured block of the uniform open chain, (1, 1), that is all there is. At the middle distance the chain's reflection builds a few more, and read on the half chain they are the maps between the quiet edge and the far edge of its cube. On the ring the wrap bond's sign follows the particle number, so whether an image survives is a question of the fluxes the two sides see: one flux on the even ring at even k, opposite fluxes at odd k, and on the odd ring never both at once, so there the argument joins the two ladders only at a corner; that the images it does not reach are lost is measured, not proved.

## 1. Setting

The XY chain or ring of N sites, H = Σ_b J_b (σ⁺_bσ⁻_{b+1} + h.c.) over its bonds with real couplings J_b, the ring's wrap bond J_w between the sites N and 1, under Z dephasing at one rate γ on every site, "equal light". A cell |a⟩⟨b| of the operator space, a and b bitstrings, lies in the joint-popcount block (p, q), p the excitations of a and q those of b, which every number-conserving Hamiltonian keeps; its Hamming distance k = |a ⊕ b| is the number of sites where a and b disagree, and under equal light the dissipator charges it −2γk and moves nothing. A block (p, q) holds the Hamming distances |p − q| ≤ k ≤ min(p + q, 2N − p − q) in steps of two, its window; it lies at one distance exactly when min(p, q) = 0 or max(p, q) = N, the 4N boundary blocks, called corners below where a ladder image lands on one. A grey room is a subspace V that L_H = −i[H, ·] keeps and on which the dissipator D acts as one real number μ. The letter cube of [THE_ONE_SQUARE](../THE_ONE_SQUARE.md) §7 places a Pauli string by its letter counts; under Z dephasing a string's height k_Z, its number of X and Y letters, is what the light charges, and a cell stands at the height of its Hamming distance since its strings carry X or Y exactly where ket and bra disagree ([PROOF_CHROMATICITY](PROOF_CHROMATICITY.md) Lemma 2). The quiet edge k_Z = 0 holds the strings of I and Z letters alone, the populations among them, and the far edge k_Z = N the strings with X or Y on every site; the cube's R is X^⊗N applied on one side, which carries the one edge onto the other.

The doubled picture of [PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4](PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4.md) §1 reads a cell as two species of fermions, the ket's excitations and the bra's, so that −2γk = −2γ(p + q) + 4γ·|a ∩ b|, a number per block plus a price on every doubly occupied site. Its §2 holds the two ladders, built from the chain's Jordan-Wigner fermions d_l: the η ladder Φ(ρ) = Σ_l d†_l ρ d_l, (p, q) → (p + 1, q + 1), and the spin ladder S⁺(ρ) = Σ_l (−1)^l d†_l ρ d†_l, (p, q) → (p + 1, q − 1), with S⁻ the adjoint of S⁺. Neither moves a disagreement set (its Lemma 2.1); Φ commutes with the Hamiltonian part for every single-particle hopping matrix h (its Lemma 2.2), and S⁺ for a real symmetric h exactly when ΣhΣ = −h, Σ = diag((−1)^l) (its Lemma 2.3), which the XY chain meets at any real couplings. On the ring the fermions' strings are counted from the site 1, and on p particles the spin ring is the fermion ring whose wrap entry carries the sign f = (−1)^(p−1) ([PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND](PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND.md) Lemma 1); h_f is that single-particle matrix.

## 2. The grey-room lemma

The sentence of [the page](../THE_GREY_GLASS.md) (its section "The sentence"), restated as Lemmas 2a and 2b, with 2c the transport step the page's section on coloured blocks uses.

**Lemma 2a.** On a grey room V at μ, e^{tL} = e^{μt}·e^{tL_H}, every eigenvalue of L on V has real part μ, and L_H on V is anti-Hermitian.

*Proof.* On V, L = L_H + μ·Id and the identity commutes with everything. L_H is anti-Hermitian in the Hilbert-Schmidt product, and a subspace an anti-Hermitian map keeps is kept by its adjoint, so L_H restricted to V is anti-Hermitian, with imaginary eigenvalues. ∎

**Lemma 2b (the converse).** e^{tL}ρ = e^{μt}e^{tL_H}ρ for every t exactly when D = μ on the span of the L_H^k ρ, the space ρ's motion reaches. For each μ there is a largest grey room, the largest L_H-invariant subspace inside the eigenspace of D at μ.

*Proof.* Differentiating both sides at every t gives D = μ on every e^{tL_H}ρ, and those span the Krylov space; the converse direction is Lemma 2a on that space. A sum of L_H-invariant subspaces inside one eigenspace of D is L_H-invariant and inside it, so the largest one is unique. ∎

**Lemma 2c.** A map that commutes with L_H and with D carries a grey room at μ onto a grey room at μ.

*Proof.* The image of an L_H-invariant subspace under a map commuting with L_H is L_H-invariant, and D acts on the image as it acts on the preimage. ∎

Under equal light both ladders commute with D (Lemma 2.1 of PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4, no disagreement set moves) and, on the XY chain, with L_H (§1), so by Lemma 2c each carries grey rooms onto grey rooms at the same μ. A grey room is spanned by joint eigenvectors of the two parts (Lemma 2a), so its eigenvectors of L do not move with γ.

## 3. Every coloured block holds the ladder images of the boundary blocks

**Every coloured block holds the ladder images of the boundary blocks.** A boundary block (k, 0) is annihilated by S⁺ and by Φ's adjoint, since both would empty the bra below zero, so it is a highest weight of the spin ladder, spin k/2, and a lowest weight of the η ladder, η-spin (N − k)/2. The two ladders commute (they are the two SU(2)s of the Hubbard model), so S⁻^s Φ^r, with S⁻ the adjoint of S⁺ and the order of the two immaterial, is one-to-one on (k, 0) for s ≤ k and r ≤ N − k. With s = (k − p + q)/2 steps down the spin ladder and r = q − s up the η ladder it reaches the block (p, q), p ≥ q, and the two bounds hold exactly when k lies in the block's Hamming window, |p − q| ≤ k ≤ min(p + q, 2N − p − q) in steps of two (the blocks with p < q follow by the transpose). So on the XY chain under equal light **every block (p, q) holds, for each Hamming distance k of its window, a grey room of dimension at least C(N, k) at −2γk, the ladder image of the boundary block (k, 0)** (G8 on the uniform chain, N = 3 to 6: every step intertwines the Hamiltonian part exactly, and the images sit at the one distance k with full rank). The row k = 1 is already certified: `FreeFermionFloorWitness` reads the family Wⁿ(P₀·c_j) in the blocks (n, n + 1), P₀ the vacuum's projector and c_j the fermion at the site j, so P₀·c_j the cell |0…0⟩⟨j|, with W the spectator map of [PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY](PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY.md), which is Φ, applied to the boundary block (0, 1), N modes at exactly −2γ per block, and `OneSidedSectorClosedForm` lists the ladder images of the one-sided sectors among the pieces of the two-sided sectors that close without the imaginary-interaction Bethe ansatz. What the window adds is every k and the spin ladder beside the η one. The fold by X^⊗N on one side, which carries (p, q) to (p, N − q) and every Hamming distance k to N − k ([PROOF_DEPHASING_FRONT_RENEWAL](PROOF_DEPHASING_FRONT_RENEWAL.md), the moves of the cube that carry blocks to blocks), commutes with L_H on the XY chain and sends D to −2γN − D, so it carries the grey room of (p, q) at distance k onto that of (p, N − q) at N − k.

## 4. The block (1, 1) of the uniform open chain

**The block (1, 1).** Its window is k ∈ {0, 2}. At 0 the ladder image is Φ of the vacuum cell, the identity on one excitation. At 2 it is S⁻ of the block (2, 0), whose states are antisymmetric in the two sites: the spin triplet in the doubled picture, whose two excitations never share a site, so the double occupancy stays zero along the motion. [PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR](PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR.md) §9.1 owns this half as the odd half of the involution U v_(a,b) = (−1)^(a+b)·v_(b,a), with v_(a,b) the cell of the sites a and b, the transpose composed with Σ: for a real symmetric h, U commutes with L_H exactly when h hops only between sites of opposite parity and has a constant diagonal. U fixes every population, so its odd half holds none; §9.2 shows that S⁺ is one-to-one on it and zero on the even half, and identifies it as the block (2, 0) recentred, and [PROOF_DEPHASING_FRONT_RENEWAL](PROOF_DEPHASING_FRONT_RENEWAL.md) reads it as living "on the envelope alone, as a block of chromaticity one does". On the cube, in words: the transpose turns the sign of every Y letter, Σ turns X and Y on every other site, and neither changes a height. The transpose reverses the order in a commutator and keeps the bond's letters XX + YY; Σ keeps the order and reverses the bond; so the commutator with the bond passes through U unchanged. On the quiet edge, the letters I and Z, both act as the identity, so an operator odd under U has no weight there, and in (1, 1) the only other height is 2.

**On the uniform open XY chain nothing more is grey in (1, 1).** The largest room at −4γ there is the set of operators whose whole motion under ad_h has no diagonal. ad_h is diagonalizable, so that room is the sum, over the frequencies ω, of the kernel of the diagonal map on the eigenspace E_ω. U commutes with ad_h and keeps every diagonal entry, so it keeps each E_ω, the diagonal map kills the odd part of E_ω, and what is left to show is that it is injective on the even part. With θ = π/(N + 1) the modes of h are ψ_a(l) ∝ sin(alθ), a = 1, …, N, at energies proportional to cos(aθ), and Σψ_a = ±ψ_ā with ā = N + 1 − a (`ChiralKClaim`'s spectrum inversion), so U sends |ψ_a⟩⟨ψ_b| to ±|ψ_b̄⟩⟨ψ_ā|, and the even part of E_ω has at most one basis vector per orbit {(a, b), (b̄, ā)} at the frequency ω. Put x = |a − b| and y the smaller of a + b and 2N + 2 − (a + b), so 0 ≤ x < y ≤ N + 1. The orbit's frequency is a multiple of s_x·s_y, s_m = sin(mθ/2), with the sign of a − b, and its diagonal is a multiple of c_x − c_y, c_m(l) = cos(mlθ), by the product formula for two sines. Draw each orbit of E_ω as an edge between x and y. No two orbits share an edge: x, y and the sign of a − b fix a − b and leave a + b to be y or 2N + 2 − y, and those two solutions are (a, b) and (b̄, ā), one orbit; the other sign gives the opposite frequency, and at x = 0 there is no sign. The graph has no cycle: at ω = 0 every edge ends at 0; at ω ≠ 0 none does, and s_m rises strictly on 0, …, N + 1, so two edges {u, v} and {v, w} of one frequency force u = w. The N + 2 vectors c_0, …, c_{N+1} on the sites 1, …, N have rank N, since the cosine transform of type I on N + 2 points is invertible, and their two relations carry the coefficients ε_m and (−1)^m·ε_m, with ε_m = 1/2 at m = 0 and m = N + 1 and 1 between. A relation among the edges' diagonals gives each vertex m the net weight v_m of its edges, and v must be a combination of the two relations. At ω ≠ 0 the vertex 0 is absent, which leaves a multiple of ε on the odd m; at ω = 0 the vertices are 0 and even, which leaves a multiple of ε on the even m. Either way v has one sign wherever it is not zero, while the net weights over each connected piece of the graph sum to zero, so v = 0, and on a forest zero net weights make every edge weight zero. The room at 0 is the identity alone: it holds only diagonal operators the hopping keeps (under light on every site a room at μ = 0 holds only diagonal operators, [PROOF_ASYMPTOTIC_SECTOR_PROJECTION](PROOF_ASYMPTOTIC_SECTOR_PROJECTION.md) Step 2(a)), which commute with h, and on the connected chain that leaves the multiples of the identity. ∎

So in (1, 1) the operators that stay on one height are the two ladder images and nothing else, C(N, 2) + 1 eigenmodes, 11 at N = 5 (G7 counts the room again, at N = 3 to 12).

The reading is PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4's doubled picture backwards, the two species of a cell laid end to end as the two halves of one chain, and the gauge is R90 §9.2's step run the other way: there ρ' = ρΣ turns the commutator on one excitation into an anticommutator on two fermions, here it turns the anticommutator with H_L into the commutator, on the whole space. From here on the operator read is ρ'; Σ_L is diagonal, so ρ and ρ' hold the same cells. On the half chain's cube the two kinds of configuration are its two edges. One excitation on every mirror pair is a cell whose ket and bra disagree at every site, the far edge k_Z = M; every pair empty or full is a population, the quiet edge k_Z = 0. The centre flip keeps both edges, and the commutator moves one side alone and so leaves either edge, so an eigenstate of the chain that lives wholly on one edge is a ρ' that commutes with H_L and is an eigenvector of 𝔣. On the quiet edge those are the diagonal operators commuting with H_L, constant on each connected piece of the configuration graph, which on a half chain without zero bonds are the popcount sectors: the projectors P_j. 𝔣 annihilates every diagonal operator, so they sit at energy 0; they are the staggered mirror pairs, (Λ†)ʲ|0⟩ ↔ j!·P_j, and on ρ' the mirror-pair creator Λ† acts as the η ladder Φ of PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4 times (−1)^(p+q) on the half chain's block (p, q) (G9). On the far edge every operator is X^⊗M times a diagonal one, so the commuting ones are the combinations of F22's XOR modes X^⊗M·P_j. 𝔣 pairs each cell of the far edge with the one whose ket holds one excitation more or fewer, at the same coefficient, and squares to the identity there; an eigenvector therefore carries one ratio λ = ±1 between the coefficients of neighbouring j, and exactly two remain, the plain and the alternating sum of the XOR modes, X^⊗M at (−1)^M·J_c and X^⊗M·Z^⊗M at the opposite energy: these are Ψ_e and Ψ_o, X^⊗M being Ψ_e at even M and Ψ_o at odd M (G9). That proves the uniqueness at every mirror-symmetric coupling without a zero, which G9 checks exactly at non-uniform couplings. Where the half chain has zero bonds, the central bond nonzero, the same argument counts 2·Π(|c| + 1) over its components c without the site M on the far edge and Π(|c| + 1) over all on the quiet one, the latter F4's count over components, met exactly (G9). So, read on the half chain in the frame ρ', the grey products |Ψ⟩⟨(Λ†)ʲ0| are the rank-one maps from the projector P_j onto the plain or the alternating sum of the XOR modes, their transposes the maps back, and their constant Hamming distance N/2 is the distance from the quiet edge to the far edge, one disagreement at every site of the half chain.

## 6. The even ring

**The even ring: two bonds across the axis.** On the even XY ring the chain's reflection, site l against N + 1 − l, has its axis through the midpoints of two opposite bonds, the central bond and the wrap bond J_w between the sites N and 1, and at couplings it keeps, the wrap bond joins the ket and the bra of the site 1 as the central bond does at the site M. With 𝔣_l(ρ) = σ⁺_l ρ σ⁺_l + σ⁻_l ρ σ⁻_l, the ring on ρ' is ad_{H_L} + (−1)^M J_c 𝔣_M − J_w 𝔣_1, the second flip taking its sign from Σ_L, which holds the site 1 (G10). On the cube a flip acts on the letters of its site: it keeps X, turns the sign of Y and annihilates I and Z, so it transposes that site and forgets its quiet letters. X^⊗M and X^⊗M·Z^⊗M, the latter a multiple of Y^⊗M, are therefore eigenvectors of both flips, at +1 and −1 respectively, and so eigenstates of the ring on the one-per-pair configurations, at ±((−1)^M J_c − J_w); the popcount projectors stay on the empty-or-full ones, at 0 (G10). In fermion language the filled mirror-even orbitals sit at J_c + (−1)^(M−1) J_w, the open chain's Tr(hR)/2 with the wrap bond's Jordan-Wigner sign at M particles, which makes that sector the ring threaded by a π flux at even M ([PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND](PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND.md) Lemma 1). So on the uniform ring at even M the flux's sign cancels the central bond's J_c, and the mirror products |Ψ⟩⟨(Λ†)ʲ0| and their transposes lie in the kernel of the Hamiltonian part, where they only decay; at odd M they oscillate at ±2J. The two flips together are no involution, so the open chain's one ratio does not carry over, but a case split on the bits at the sites 1 and M does. On a ring without a zero bond, the half chain's and the two across the axis, a subspace the ring keeps on the far edge lies in the span of the X^⊗M·P_j, and since 𝔣_1 and 𝔣_M are commuting involutions there, a 𝔣_M + b 𝔣_1 is real symmetric on it and the subspace is spanned by eigenvectors X^⊗M·Σ_j f(j)P_j, with a = (−1)^M J_c and b = −J_w. The cells holding exactly one of the sites 1 and M give a f(j−1) + b f(j+1) = λ f(j) = a f(j+1) + b f(j−1), so for a ≠ b the coefficients repeat with period two and λ = ±(a + b) leaves the two sums. For a = b the cells holding neither site give λ f(j) = 2a f(j+1) and those holding both λ f(j) = 2a f(j−1). At M ≥ 3 the first at j = 1 and the second at j = 2 give λ = ±2a and f(2) = ±f(1), the two chains carry that ratio to every j, and λ = 0 is excluded by the cells holding one of the two sites at j = 1 and j = M − 1: again the two sums. At M = 2, that is N = 4 with J_w = −J_c, they leave f = (1, 0, −1) at λ = 0, a third eigenstate, X^⊗2·(P_0 − P_2). G10 checks the two alone at ten rings and the third at that one. The ladders fare otherwise: on the even ring the ladder image of the boundary block (k, 0) is kept when k is even and, with the wrap bond nonzero, for odd k only where it fills its block, the latter proved at N = 4, 6 and 8 (the next paragraph). At N = 6, M = 3 is odd, the ladder images of (M, 0) are not kept in (M, 2), and the grey room at k = N/2 there is the mirror products alone: 2 at the non-uniform mirror-symmetric coupling tried, and on the uniform ring, where every axis through two opposite bond midpoints is a reflection with mirror pairs of its own, the 6 mirror products of its three axes (G10, the upper bound met exactly). The mirror products need no ladder; they are products of two eigenstates of the spin ring, whatever the parity of their sides. At N = 4 and 8, M even, the ladder images are kept, and fill the room with the mirror products at the non-uniform mirror-symmetric coupling tried at each N, C(N, M) + 2 (G10). The uniform ring at N = 4 holds two more than the ladder images and the mirror products of both its bond axes, 11 against 9, and at N = 8 two more again, 80 against 78, all exact (G10, G11); they come from the antipodal pairs (§8), which no bond axis has as its pairs.

**Which ladder images the even ring keeps.** On the even XY ring at any real couplings, the ladder image S⁻^s Φ^r of the boundary block (k, 0) in a block (p, q) of its window is kept by the Hamiltonian part when k is even, so under equal light it is a grey room at −2γk of dimension C(N, k). The proof runs in the Jordan-Wigner fermions d_l of the ladders, their strings counted from the site 1, so that the wrap bond joins the two ends of the string's order. On p particles the spin ring is the fermion ring whose wrap entry carries the sign (−1)^(p−1) ([PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND](PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND.md) Lemma 1), so on (p, q) the Hamiltonian part is the two-copy hopping with the single-particle matrix h_f on the ket, f = (−1)^(p−1), and h_f′ on the bra, f′ = (−1)^(q−1). In the window p − q has the parity of k, so for k even the two sides see one flux, f = f′. For either flux h_f hops only between neighbouring sites, and on an even ring the wrap bond joins two sites of opposite parity, so Σh_fΣ = −h_f (the step PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND §7 takes for its second ladder); by Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3 of [PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4](PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4.md) Φ and S⁺ then commute with the two-copy hopping of h_f, the same matrix on both sides, and so does S⁻, the adjoint of S⁺, that hopping being self-adjoint for a real symmetric h_f; and S⁻^s Φ^r carries the two-copy hopping of h_f on (k, 0) into the two-copy hopping of h_f on (p, q), which is the ring's Hamiltonian part there. The flux is the target's, and the start need not have it: the two-copy hopping of either flux conserves the particle numbers and so maps the whole block (k, 0) into itself, and the image of the whole block is kept. PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4's Lemma 2.1, which carries over to S⁻ since the adjoint of a map that keeps every disagreement set keeps them too, puts it at the one distance k, and the ladders are one-to-one on (k, 0) by the weights, as on the chain. ∎ At even p the flux is the one the spin ring has on (k, 0) itself, so the ladders intertwine the ring's own Hamiltonian parts; at odd p it is the other, and the image moves as (k, 0) would on the ring with the wrap bond's sign turned, J_w → −J_w: a grey room with the frequencies of that ring. It is the alternation [XY_FROZEN_BAND](../../experiments/XY_FROZEN_BAND.md) meets when Φ moves between the periodic and the antiperiodic fermion sectors of the spin ring. For the steps of the η ladder alone this follows from the spectator map's parity rule ([PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY](PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY.md) §5, the glossary's spectator map W), under which the η ladder carries a block of even height onto the spin ring with the wrap bond's sign turned: one step changes the flux, but a whole block is kept by the ring of either flux, so the start's flux need not match the target's. The steps of the spin ladder and the break at odd k are what the rule does not reach. The arc `sideways_spin_ladder` measured the spin ladder on rings of one fixed flux, where it holds at even N; on the spin ring the flux follows the particle number, and the condition p ≡ q (mod 2) is what that adds. For k odd the target's two sides see opposite fluxes unless one is empty or full, where the hopping has nothing to move. Off the start itself, the image is then the whole block at (0, k), (N, N − k) and (N − k, N), the boundary block (0, N − k) folded on the ket side and its transpose. At every other block of the window both sides are partly filled, and the Hamiltonian part differs from the two-copy hopping of the ket's flux on both sides, which keeps the image by the proof above, by J_w times a fixed map, the one that moves the bra alone across the wrap bond, with no coupling in it. So the image is kept exactly when that map keeps it, whatever the couplings once J_w ≠ 0, and at N = 4, 6 and 8 it is not: the image beside its image under that map has rank above C(N, k) (G12, modulo a prime, a lower bound on the rational rank, with the decomposition checked exactly on rings of both signs). Beyond N = 8 that is not proved, but for the block (5, 2) at N = 10, which the room's upper bound of 10, a rank modulo two primes on [the page](../THE_GREY_GLASS.md), settles on the uniform ring and so, the test holding no coupling, at every J_w ≠ 0. At k = 1 the pattern is the ring's Δn = 1 floor seen from the ladders, which on the uniform ring is the four outer blocks (0, 1), (1, 0), (N − 1, N) and (N, N − 1) alone at N = 4 to 8 ([PROOF_RING_GAP_DOMINANCE](PROOF_RING_GAP_DOMINANCE.md), `FreeFermionFloorWitness`). With the wrap bond zero the ring is the open chain, and there every odd-k image is kept (G12 at N = 6, by the exact identity). G12 checks the identity of the proof exactly on every block of the window at those rings, transposes included, for even k, and the decomposition for odd k; as the control, the identity for even k breaks with the staggering of S⁻ dropped on every block reached by a step of S⁻ but the single cell (0, N).

## 7. The odd ring

**Which ladder images the odd ring keeps.** On the odd ring the wrap bond joins the sites N and 1, both odd, so the staggering Σ turns the sign of every bond but the wrap, and that is the whole difference between the two fluxes: Σh_fΣ = −h_{−f}, with h_f the single-particle matrix of the fixed-flux ring of sign f ([PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND](PROOF_RING_FROZEN_BAND.md) §2 writes it U·h₊·U = −h₋, its U being Σ and not the involution of this document's §4, its sites counted from 0, and uses it to give the two fixed-flux rings the same frozen counts). The proofs of Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3 of [PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4](PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4.md) run unchanged with one single-particle matrix h_a on the ket and another, h_b, on the bra: Φ commutes with that two-copy hopping when h_a = h_b (the residue (h_ket − h_bra) that [PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY](PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY.md) §5 writes for the spectator map), and S⁺, and with it S⁻, when Σh_aΣ = −h_b. On the even ring both conditions ask for one flux on both sides, which the target has at even k, so there the ladders combine freely. On the odd ring Φ is served by one flux on both sides and the spin ladder by opposite ones, so, the wrap bond being nonzero, no choice of fluxes serves both, and the argument joins them only at a corner, a block with an empty or a full side, whose whole block is kept, the flux of the empty or full side having nothing to move. With s steps down the spin ladder and r up the η ladder, the ladder image of (k, 0) in a block (p, q) of its window is therefore kept, at any real couplings, when k is even and s is 0 or k, or when k is odd and r is 0 or N − k; the images that fill their block, at the corners, are among these. The ladders commute, so the image is the second ladder's image of the corner the first one fills: (k, 0) itself when the first ladder takes no step, (0, k) after S⁻^k and (N, N − k) after Φ^(N−k), each filled exactly, the ladders being one-to-one. At even k the target's two sides share a parity and so a flux, at odd k they differ, so in each case the target's own two fluxes are the ones the second ladder needs, and it carries the two-copy hopping of the corner at those fluxes, which keeps the whole corner, into the target's Hamiltonian part. ∎ Every other image is not kept, at N = 3, 5 and 7, uniform and with couplings of both signs, the wrap bond nonzero (with it zero the ring is the open chain, which keeps them all); that is measured, not proved. At k = 1 nothing is kept off the four outer blocks, and at N = 5 and 7 on the uniform ring that is where [PROOF_RING_GAP_DOMINANCE](PROOF_RING_GAP_DOMINANCE.md) finds the ring's whole Δn = 1 floor, a second route for that row; at N = 3 it finds four more floor modes in the middle blocks (1, 2) and (2, 1), which the ladder images of (1, 0), not kept there, do not account for. Of the ladder images at N = 3, 5 and 7, those kept without filling their block number 4, 16 and 36, 2(N − 1) of them at k = 0 and k = N, where the start is a single cell (G13, which checks every stage exactly, that the ladders commute, that the spin steps unstaggered break the identity at odd k, and the rest by the rank of the image beside its motion modulo a prime, above C(N, k)). On the even ring the parity of k alone decides; on the odd ring each ladder works on its own parity of k, and the other joins it only from a corner. In the block (1, 1) the image at distance 2 is S⁻ of (2, 0), one step at even k, and the odd ring does not keep it at the rings measured, consistent with its room there being only the frozen modes (the page's paragraph on other chains).

## 8. The antipodal map, and every division into pairs

**The antipodal map: opposite sites, no flip.** Number the bonds J_l between the sites l and l + 1, with J_N = J_w. On a ring with J_l = J_{l+M}, which includes J_c = J_w, the map l ↦ l + M sends each site to its opposite site and pairs the two as the reflection pairs a site with its mirror image; it is the rotation by half the ring, not a reflection, and on a ring with two perpendicular bond axes, which exist only when 4 divides N, it is their product. Read the site l + M as the bra of l: the bonds inside each half give H_L ρ + ρ H_L as before, and the two bonds between the halves, (M, M + 1) and (N, 1), each join one end of the half chain's ket to the other end of its bra, so the antipodal pairs form a ring of M whose last link is crossed. No bond lies inside an antipodal pair, so there is no flip, and every hop moves a particle into another pair: it moves out of both the configurations with one particle in every pair and those with one pair full and the rest empty, and an eigenstate on either sits at 0, whatever the couplings. On the first, write b_d = 1 when the pair of the site d holds its particle at d + M. At M ≥ 3 a configuration with the pair d empty and the pair d + 1 full is reached from exactly two of them, and one with d full and d + 1 empty gives the same equation, so the coefficient changes sign under 01 ↔ 10 on two neighbouring pairs and, across the crossed link, under 00 ↔ 11 on the pairs M and 1: v(b) = (−1)^inv(b) g(|b|), with inv(b) the number of d < e with b_d = 1 and b_e = 0 and g(k + 2) = −(−1)^M g(k), two states for couplings without a zero (three at N = 4, where all four bonds join the same two pairs and feed one equation). On the second, a sum Σ_d s_d |d, d + M⟩ of full pairs needs s_{d+1} = −s_d along the half and s_1 = −s_M across the crossed link, which, for couplings without a zero, closes exactly when M is even: at odd M the sign is frustrated around the ring of pairs, and no such state exists (G11). The sum of full pairs behaves the same way for any division of the sites into pairs that nonzero XY couplings keep: it needs opposite signs on two pairs joined by a bond, so on a connected ring it exists exactly when the pairs, joined by the bonds between them, form a bipartite graph, the condition ΣhΣ = −h that the spin ladder needs on the sites ([PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4](PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4.md) Lemma 2.3), read on the pairs and with any two-colouring of them allowed. A bond axis's pairs form a path, and its staggered sum is Λ†|0⟩, the half chain's P_1 on ρ', at 0 on every mirror-symmetric ring (G10, G11); the antipodal pairs form a ring of M. Each configuration of the first kind differs from each of the second at one site of every antipodal pair, so their products sit at the distance N/2, and, both factors being eigenstates at 0, each is grey, at frequency 0. These products exist at every even M; at the rings G11 measures they are the two more, and with the ladder images and the bond-axis products they fill the room at k = N/2 in (M, 2), 11 at N = 4 (transposes included, the two sides lying in one sector there), 80 on the uniform ring at N = 8, and at the rings tried 76 with two perpendicular bond axes and 72 where no bond axis is a symmetry (G11, the upper bound met exactly). At N = 6 there is no sum of full antipodal pairs at 0, and on the uniform ring the room stays the bond axes' 6. On a ring with J_l = J_{l+M} the two bonds on any bond axis are opposite and so equal, and at even M the bond-axis products sit at 0 too. At N = 4 a full antipodal pair is a Néel configuration and the one-per-pair states are the zero modes on the nearest-neighbour pairs; two of them are the bond axes' sums of full pairs, and the staggered Néel sum is a one-per-pair state at 0 of each bond axis, so two of the three products in each orientation are already the bond axes' and one is new.

**Every division into pairs.** The paragraph above finds the sum of full pairs for a division into pairs that the couplings keep exactly when its pairs form a bipartite graph; with positive couplings the converse holds too, and one relation, for couplings of either sign, says why. Write ι for the involution that exchanges the two sites of every pair, J(x, y) for the coupling of the sites x and y, 0 where they share no bond, and s_[x] for the coefficient of the full pair that holds x. A hop out of a full pair always leaves the configurations with one pair full and the rest empty, so a state on them that H keeps is annihilated by it. For a bond (a, b) between two pairs, the configuration holding ιa and b is reached only from the full pair of a, by the hop a → b, and from the full pair of b, by the hop ιb → ιa, and the configuration holding a and ιb likewise, so J(a, b) s_[a] + J(ιa, ιb) s_[b] = 0 and J(ιa, ιb) s_[a] + J(a, b) s_[b] = 0; a nonzero coefficient needs |J(a, b)| = |J(ιa, ιb)|, and a zero coefficient spreads to every pair bonded to it. Read with h_s, the matrix of H's hopping amplitudes in spin language (J_w on the wrap bond, where the Jordan-Wigner h of the ladders carries −J_w at an even number of particles), a state of two excitations is a symmetric matrix ψ with zero diagonal, on which H acts as h_sψ + ψh_s with the diagonal struck out. The sum of full pairs, its coefficients scaled to ±1, is ψ = G·P_ι, with P_ι the permutation matrix of ι and G the diagonal carrying on each site the sign of its pair, and the two equations say that h_sψ + ψh_s vanishes off the diagonal, that is P_ι h_s P_ι = −G h_s G on every bond between pairs: seen through ι the hopping between pairs is minus itself seen through a staggering of the pairs. On the chain at even N it is the relation behind Λ†, read on the bonds between pairs: ι is the reflection, G is Σ on one half of the chain and −Σ on the other, and ΣR, the pair matrix of Λ† in fermion language, anticommutes with h on every entry. So on a connected graph a sum of full pairs exists exactly when such a staggering exists, and it is then unique up to a factor; with positive couplings the relation asks that ι map every bond between pairs to a bond of the same coupling, which makes ι a symmetry, and that the pairs, joined by those bonds, form a bipartite graph, and the sum is the staggered one (R1 of [`simulations/grey_glass_ring_rooms.py`](../../simulations/grey_glass_ring_rooms.py) checks this over every division, (N − 1)!! of them, 10395 at N = 12, on the uniform rings at N = 6, 8, 10 and 12 and on four rings with other positive couplings, R1b the relation itself on random rings with couplings of both signs at N = 6 and 8). The symmetries of the uniform ring that exchange sites in pairs without fixing any are the N/2 reflections about the bond axes and the rotation by half the ring, and of their pair graphs only the rotation's, a ring of M pairs, can fail to be bipartite, at odd M.

## 9. What is not proved

- **Completeness.** That nothing beyond the ladder images and the mirror products is grey is measured, not proved: on the uniform chain at N = 3 to 6 and in the block (4, 2) at N = 8 (G8), with (3, 3) and (4, 4) at N = 8 holding only lower bounds; on the even rings to N = 12, where the rooms at k = N/2 in (N/2, 2) meet the count of the kept ladder images with the bond-axis products and, at even N/2, the antipodal products ([the page](../THE_GREY_GLASS.md), the counts that follow its paragraph on every division into pairs).
- **The even ring's losses at odd k beyond N = 8.** The image is kept exactly when the bra's hop across the wrap bond keeps it (§6); that it does not is computed at N = 4, 6 and 8 and, through the room's bound, for (5, 2) at N = 10.
- **The odd ring's losses.** That every image not reached from a corner by the ladder of its parity is lost is measured at N = 3, 5 and 7 (G13), not proved.
- **The four floor modes at N = 3.** [PROOF_RING_GAP_DOMINANCE](PROOF_RING_GAP_DOMINANCE.md) finds four floor modes in the middle blocks (1, 2) and (2, 1) of the ring of three that the ladder images of (1, 0) do not account for.

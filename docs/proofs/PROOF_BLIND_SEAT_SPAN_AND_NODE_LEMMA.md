## Abstract

Put the watching on one seat j of an N-site graph and restrict to the single-excitation sector. F157 counts what the seat cannot touch, `blind(j) = N − dim Krylov(e_j)`, and pairs that count with a second statement it could not prove: that the kernel of the watched sector's generator has dimension `1 + blind(j)`. (That kernel is a complex vector space; the manifold of stationary STATES inside it, positive and of unit trace, has one dimension fewer. Everything below computes the kernel.) The second statement is true on a zero-free chain and false on eight of twenty graphs on the Heisenberg book, nine of twenty off it, and the fence the repo could offer for it was the absence of a zero bond, which is not the discriminator (`[1,1,0,1,1]` breaks the identity, the zero-bond `[1,0,1]` holds).

The reason is that `1 + blind` is a shadow of an exact statement. The kernel is the commutant of the algebra A generated by H and the seat projector P; because e_j is cyclic in its own Krylov space K, that algebra is

    A  =  End(K)  ⊕  ℂ[H|_W] ,        W = K^⊥ the Krylov complement,

so its commutant is the scalars on K plus the commutant of H restricted to W:

    dim ker L_SE(j)  =  1 + dim commutant(H|_W)  =  1 + Σ_λ (m_λ − s_λ)² ,

with m_λ the multiplicity of λ in H and s_λ ∈ {0, 1} recording whether the seat sees λ. Since `blind(j)` is the SUM of those same numbers `m_λ − s_λ`, the identity `dim ker = 1 + blind` holds exactly when each of them is 0 or 1, that is, exactly when **H restricted to the Krylov complement W has a simple spectrum**. Simplicity of H is sufficient and not necessary, which is the polarity the registry records, and now with a reason: `[1,0,1]` is degenerate but its W is not, while `[1,1,0,1,1]` puts a repeated eigenvalue inside W because the cut leaves two copies of the same piece.

Lemma J carries the zero-free chain into that criterion. For an unreduced Jacobi matrix no eigenvector has two consecutive zero entries, hence none vanishes at an end; the spectrum is simple; and an eigenvector vanishes at seat j exactly when its eigenvalue is shared by the two principal submatrices the seat leaves behind. The first two make every multiplicity 1, so the criterion is met on either book; the third is the node lemma the two-halves criterion consumes, and squarefreeness of the two blocks' polynomials is what makes the shared-root count equal to the gcd DEGREE.

## (a) Statement

Let H be Hermitian on ℂ^N (the repo's single-excitation H_SE is real symmetric, site-indexed, in either book), let j be a site, e_j its unit vector and P = e_j e_j^† the seat projector. Let

    L_SE(j) : ρ ↦ −i[H, ρ] + γ ( P ρ P − ½{P, ρ} ) ,      γ > 0 .

Write K = span{e_j, H e_j, H² e_j, ...} for the seat's Krylov space, W = K^⊥, and `blind(j) = dim W = N − dim K`, F157's count. Write A for the unital ∗-algebra generated by H and P inside End(ℂ^N), and A′ for its commutant.

**Theorem A (the span is a commutant).** For every Hermitian H, every graph and every profile,

    dim ker L_SE(j)  =  dim A′  =  1 + dim commutant(H|_W)  =  1 + Σ_λ (m_λ − s_λ)² ,

the sum over the distinct eigenvalues λ of H, with m_λ the multiplicity of λ and s_λ = 1 if P_λ e_j ≠ 0 and 0 otherwise. Neither γ nor an overall scale of H appears. Gate **G6**, both sides over ℚ, 202 of 202 (graph, book, seat) triples on the twenty-graph list, zero bonds, negative couplings and disconnection included; **G6b** pins the committed GF(p) kernel route against the rational one, 202 of 202.

**Corollary C (the sharp criterion).** `blind(j) = Σ_λ (m_λ − s_λ)`, so

    dim ker L_SE(j) = 1 + blind(j)   ⟺   m_λ − s_λ ≤ 1 for every λ
                                     ⟺   H restricted to the Krylov complement W has a simple spectrum.

Gate **G7**: the identity holds on 129 of the 202 triples and fails on 73, and every one of the 73 is a triple where `1 + dim commutant(H|_W)` is the kernel and `1 + blind` is not.

**Lemma J (the Jacobi node lemma).** Let H be an unreduced Jacobi matrix: Hermitian, tridiagonal, with every off-diagonal entry nonzero. Let L and R be the principal submatrices on the sites before and after seat j, each possibly empty. Then

- **(J1)** no eigenvector of H has two consecutive zero entries; in particular no eigenvector vanishes at the first or last site;
- **(J2)** every eigenvalue of H is simple, and the same holds for L and R;
- **(J3)** an eigenvector v with Hv = λv satisfies v_j = 0 if and only if λ ∈ spec(L) ∩ spec(R);
- **(J4)** hence `blind(j) = #(spec(L) ∩ spec(R)) = deg gcd(χ_L, χ_R)`, the middle equality being where (J2) is spent.

Gates **G1** to **G5**, over **1674 zero-free profiles** run on both books, so 3348 (book, profile) pairs, N = 2..8: two uniform families per N (all-1 and all-2), every signed product over {1, −1, 2, −3} at N = 3..6, and 300 random irregular profiles. Each gate has its own index set and the totals differ accordingly: G1 6696 of 6696 over the END seats, two per (book, profile); G2 3348 of 3348, one per (book, profile); G3, G4 and G5 19006 of 19006 over (book, profile, seat). No failures anywhere.

**Corollary B (the zero-free chain, either book).** On an open chain with no zero bond, H_SE is an unreduced Jacobi matrix in either book, so by (J2) every m_λ = 1, Corollary C's condition is met and

    dim ker L_SE(j)  =  1 + blind(j) .

Gate **G8**: 1672 zero-free profiles at N = 2..7, run on both books, giving 18696 (book, profile, seat) triples, no failures.

## (b) Step 0: the kernel is a commutant, and that part is the repo's

`L_SE(j)` splits as a commutator plus an entry-wise mask. Writing L(ρ) = −i[H, ρ] + M ∘ ρ with M real, non-positive and zero exactly on the entries whose dephased-seat bit agrees, Hermiticity of ρ gives Tr(ρ · (−i)[H, ρ]) = 0, so

    0 = Tr(ρ · L(ρ)) = Σ_ij M_ij |ρ_ij|² ≤ 0 ,

forcing ρ_ij = 0 wherever M_ij < 0, and then [H, ρ] = 0 as well. L preserves Hermiticity, so its kernel is spanned by Hermitian elements and

    ker L_SE(j) = { ρ : [H, ρ] = 0 and ρ_ib = ρ_bi = 0 for every b ≠ j } = {H, P}′ = A′ .

The second equality is one line: [P, ρ]_{ab} = δ_{aj} ρ_{jb} − ρ_{aj} δ_{jb} vanishes for all a, b exactly when the seat's off-diagonal row and column are clear. Neither γ's magnitude nor a scale of H enters, because γ decides only which entries M kills and a commutant does not move under scaling.

This step is not new. It is the docstring argument of `exact_kernel_dim` in [`simulations/seat_cut_blindness.py`](../../simulations/seat_cut_blindness.py), and it is the opening move of the Wedderburn paragraph in [`docs/CAUGHT_ERRORS.md`](../CAUGHT_ERRORS.md) and of the two surfaces that state the same thing. It is the same FRAME as [the asymptotic sector projection proof](PROOF_ASYMPTOTIC_SECTOR_PROJECTION.md) Step 2, the fixed-point algebra as decoherence-free subalgebra ∩ commutant, but the one seat here is not a case of that step, whose hypothesis is γ_k > 0 at every site. What follows is the part that was open.

## (c) Step 1: the algebra is End(K) ⊕ ℂ[H|_W]

*Proof.* K is H-invariant by construction and contains e_j, so W = K^⊥ is H-invariant because H is Hermitian, and P W = 0 because W ⊥ e_j. Both K and W are therefore A-invariant, and every element of A is block diagonal for the decomposition ℂ^N = K ⊕ W.

Expand any element of A over words in H and P. A word with no P is a polynomial p(H). Consider first the words of the exact shape H^a P H^b:

    H^a P H^b  =  (H^a e_j)(H^b e_j)^† ,

using P = e_j e_j^† and H^† = H. As a and b run over the non-negative integers the vectors H^a e_j span K by definition, and rank-one operators x y^† with x and y running over spanning sets of K span all of End(K). Every such word annihilates W and has range inside K, so their span is exactly `End(K) ⊕ 0`, and

    A ⊇ End(K) ⊕ 0 .

That subspace is a two-sided IDEAL of the block algebra, since `p(H)·(X ⊕ 0) = p(H|_K)X ⊕ 0` and likewise on the right. So a longer word such as `H P H P H`, which is not of the displayed shape, still lands in it: any word containing P can be cut at its first and last P into `(word)·H^a P H^b·(word)`, and the ideal absorbs the factors on either side. Since the remaining generators contribute only polynomials in H,

    A = ( End(K) ⊕ 0 ) + ℂ[H] = { X ⊕ p(H|_W) : X ∈ End(K), p a polynomial } .

As SUBSPACES the two summands overlap, since any polynomial annihilating H|_W lies in both; what is meant by the direct sum is the block decomposition, and it holds because the two components may be chosen INDEPENDENTLY: given any X ∈ End(K) and any polynomial p, the element `(X − p(H|_K)) ⊕ 0` lies in the ideal, and adding `p(H)` to it gives exactly `X ⊕ p(H|_W)`. So A, as an algebra acting blockwise, is End(K) ⊕ ℂ[H|_W]. ∎

**Where the cyclic vector is spent, and it is the whole point.** The step `{H^a e_j} spans K` is the definition of K, so it is free; what is not free is that the SAME vector e_j supplies the projector. That is why one seat gives a full matrix block and several seats need not: with two projectors P and P′ the words available are H^a P H^b and H^a P′ H^b and their products, whose span is the algebra generated by two cyclic subspaces, not automatically all of End(K + K′). Theorem A is a one-seat theorem for that reason and no other.

## (d) Step 2: the commutant, and the multiplicity form

*Proof.* A contains the identity of End(K), which is the orthogonal projector P_K, so every Y ∈ A′ commutes with P_K and is block diagonal, Y = Y_K ⊕ Y_W. Then Y_K commutes with all of End(K) and is a scalar, and Y_W commutes with H|_W. Conversely every such pair commutes with A. Hence

    dim A′ = 1 + dim commutant(H|_W) .

H|_W is the restriction of a Hermitian operator to an invariant subspace, so it is Hermitian and diagonalisable, and the commutant of a diagonalisable operator has dimension Σ μ² over the multiplicities μ of its distinct eigenvalues.

It remains to identify those multiplicities. Let E_λ be the eigenspace of λ in H. Then K = ⊕_λ span{P_λ e_j}, since the Krylov space of a diagonalisable operator on a vector is the span of that vector's nonzero eigencomponents, so

    W = ⊕_λ ( E_λ ⊖ span{P_λ e_j} ) ,     dim (E_λ ∩ W) = m_λ − s_λ ,

with s_λ = 1 exactly when P_λ e_j ≠ 0, that is when the seat sees λ. So the multiplicities of H|_W are the numbers m_λ − s_λ and

    dim ker L_SE(j) = 1 + Σ_λ (m_λ − s_λ)² . ∎

**Corollary C follows by arithmetic.** `blind(j) = dim W = Σ_λ (m_λ − s_λ)`, and for non-negative integers Σ n² = Σ n holds exactly when every n is 0 or 1. So the identity `dim ker = 1 + blind` is equivalent to `m_λ − s_λ ≤ 1` for every λ, which is simplicity of H|_W and not of H. ∎

**What the failures look like once read this way.** In the run's table, the star at N = 5 with the HUB watched has blind 3 and kernel 10, so the commutant of H|_W has dimension 9; the only partition of 3 with squares summing to 9 is a single eigenvalue of multiplicity 3, which is the hub-blind spoke space. K₄ at any seat has blind 2 and kernel 5, so multiplicity 2. The zero-bond path `[1,1,0,1,1]` at seat 1 has blind 4 and kernel 7, so Σμ = 4 and Σμ² = 6, forcing the partition (2, 1, 1): one repeated eigenvalue and two simple ones, the repetition being the two identical pieces the cut leaves behind. Each of these is read off the two printed integers and needs no eigensolver.

## (e) Step 3: proof of Lemma J

Write the Jacobi matrix with diagonal a_0..a_{N−1} and off-diagonals b_0..b_{N−2}, every b_i ≠ 0, and extend the indexing at both ends by the convention v_{−1} = v_N = 0 and b_{−1} = b_{N−1} = 0, so that every row can be written in one shape. The eigenvalue equation is then the three-term recurrence

    b_{i−1} v_{i−1} + (a_i − λ) v_i + b_i v_{i+1} = 0 ,      i = 0..N−1 .

**(J1).** Suppose v_i = v_{i+1} = 0 for some −1 ≤ i ≤ N−1. For i ≤ N−3, row i+1 reads b_i v_i + (a_{i+1} − λ) v_{i+1} + b_{i+1} v_{i+2} = 0, so b_{i+1} v_{i+2} = 0 and, since b_{i+1} ≠ 0 in that range, v_{i+2} = 0; induction upward kills every entry above, and for i ≥ N−2 there is nothing above to kill. Symmetrically, for i ≥ 0 row i reads b_{i−1} v_{i−1} + (a_i − λ) v_i + b_i v_{i+1} = 0, so b_{i−1} v_{i−1} = 0, and for i ≥ 1 the coefficient b_{i−1} is nonzero and v_{i−1} = 0; induction downward kills every entry below. Hence v = 0, and no eigenvector has two consecutive zeros.

For the ends, note that the boundary convention is bookkeeping and is not what carries the conclusion: if v_0 = 0 then row 0 reads (a_0 − λ) v_0 + b_0 v_1 = 0 directly, so b_0 v_1 = 0 and v_1 = 0, and the induction above finishes. So v_0 ≠ 0, and symmetrically v_{N−1} ≠ 0, for every eigenvector of every unreduced Jacobi matrix with N ≥ 2. ∎

**(J2).** For N ≥ 2, given λ and the value v_0, the recurrence determines v_1 from row 0 (b_0 ≠ 0), then v_2 from row 1, and so on: the whole vector is determined by v_0, so the map v ↦ v_0 is injective on the eigenspace. Hence the eigenspace is at most one-dimensional, and by (J1) it is spanned by a vector with v_0 ≠ 0. Every eigenvalue is simple. (At N = 1 the statement is trivially true for the different reason that the matrix is 1 × 1.) L and R are themselves unreduced Jacobi matrices, being contiguous principal submatrices of one, so the same holds for them, and it is that instance and not the one for H that (J4) spends. ∎

**(J3).** (⇒) Let Hv = λv with v_j = 0. If j = 0 or j = N−1 this contradicts (J1), and correspondingly one of the two blocks is empty and has empty spectrum, so both sides of the equivalence are false and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise rows 0..j−1 involve only v_0..v_j, and with v_j = 0 they read exactly L u = λ u for u = (v_0, ..., v_{j−1}). By (J1), v_{j−1} ≠ 0, so u ≠ 0 and λ ∈ spec(L). Rows j+1..N−1 give the same for R.

(⇐) Let λ ∈ spec(L) ∩ spec(R), with eigenvectors u of L and w of R. By (J1) applied to L, its last entry u_{j−1} is nonzero; by (J1) applied to R, its first entry w_{j+1} is nonzero. Set

    v = ( u , 0 , c·w ) ,      c = − b_{j−1} u_{j−1} / ( b_j w_{j+1} ) ,

which is well defined because b_j ≠ 0 and w_{j+1} ≠ 0. Rows 0..j−1 are L's rows (row j−1 acquires the extra term b_{j−1} v_j = 0), rows j+1..N−1 are R's rows in the same way, and row j reads b_{j−1} u_{j−1} + (a_j − λ)·0 + b_j c w_{j+1} = 0 by the choice of c. So v is an eigenvector of H for λ with a node at j. ∎

**(J4).** By (J2) the spectrum of H is simple, so `blind(j) = N − #{eigenspaces e_j meets} = #{λ : P_λ e_j = 0} = #{λ : v_λ(j) = 0}`, which by (J3) is `#(spec(L) ∩ spec(R))`. By (J2) applied to L and R their characteristic polynomials are squarefree, so their gcd is squarefree too and its degree counts the shared roots without multiplicity. ∎

**The complex Hermitian case, and why the statement above says real symmetric.** For a Hermitian tridiagonal matrix row i is b̄_{i−1} v_{i−1} + a_i v_i + b_i v_{i+1}, and the conjugate is not cosmetic: the gluing constant of (J3) becomes c = − b̄_{j−1} u_{j−1} / (b_j w_{j+1}), and using the unconjugated one produces a vector that is not an eigenvector at all (at N = 3 with b_0 = 1+2i, b_1 = 3−i, a = (0, 5, 0) and the shared root λ = 0, the unconjugated construction leaves ‖Hv‖ = 4.0 instead of 0). The lemma itself survives, because a complex Hermitian unreduced Jacobi matrix is conjugate by a diagonal unitary to a real symmetric one with the same |b_i| and the same spectrum, and that conjugation multiplies each eigenvector entry by a phase, so it moves no node and changes no multiplicity. The sector supplies a real symmetric H, so this file proves the real case and records the reduction rather than carrying conjugates through every line.

**Why (J2) is not decoration here.** It is spent twice, on different things. Once to make the node count a count of EIGENVALUES rather than of eigenvectors, and once to make `deg gcd` equal to the number of shared roots. At a zero bond both spendings fail together, which is why the two-halves phrasing goes wrong there while the fence-free Cramer identity of [The Seat That Cuts](../../experiments/THE_SEAT_THAT_CUTS.md) §7 does not.

## (f) Corollary B, and the two books

On an open chain with no zero bond, H_SE is tridiagonal with off-diagonal entries 2J_b, all nonzero, in either book; the ZZ term changes only the diagonal, which Lemma J never constrains. So H_SE is an unreduced Jacobi matrix, (J2) gives m_λ = 1 for every λ, hence m_λ − s_λ ≤ 1, hence Corollary C's condition, hence `dim ker L_SE(j) = 1 + blind(j)`. Combining with (J4) and F157's uniform evaluations gives the two chain forms the registry carries:

    uniform Heisenberg:  dim ker L_SE(j) = 1 + (gcd(2j+1, N) − 1)/2
    uniform XY:          dim ker L_SE(j) = 1 + ( gcd(j+1, N+1) − 1 ) = gcd(j+1, N+1)

The XY line is written with its `1 +` kept visible on purpose: the two books' clauses have been stated in different shapes before, and the collapse to a bare gcd is arithmetic, not a different law.

**What this closes elsewhere.** [The Seat That Cuts](../../experiments/THE_SEAT_THAT_CUTS.md) §5 and F4's seat sub-bullets both ended on the same open question, the component mechanism being able to undercount the kernel by Σ mᵢ² − Σ mᵢ: the seat page asked it as "whether a zero-free open chain can do it; nothing here exhibits one either way", the registry bullet as "what stays open is whether a ZERO-FREE OPEN CHAIN can do it". Theorem A names the blocks, so the answer is no rather than not-yet-exhibited. On a zero-free chain, `End(K)` contributes one block with m = 1, and by Lemma J the simple `H|_W` contributes dim W blocks each with m = 1; every mᵢ = 1, the shortfall is zero, and the best eigenbasis reaches the kernel as its component count. The mechanism can only undercount where H is degenerate on the Krylov complement, which is exactly Corollary C's condition failing, and on a zero-free chain it never does.

## (g) Scope, and four things this does not say

- **One seat, and popcount 1.** §(c)'s cyclic-vector step is where both restrictions live. With several dephased seats the algebra need not contain a full matrix block, and above popcount 1 the Wedderburn blocks are not forced; the general dimension formula Σ mᵢ² stands there (`docs/CAUGHT_ERRORS.md`, 2026-08-24) but its blocks must be computed. F4's own question above popcount 1 stays open.
- **The theorem is about the Krylov complement W; the isolated-seat fence is about reading W as the blind subspace.** F157's Breaks-for records that at a seat whose ray is H-invariant the physical blind subspace carries one dimension the Krylov complement does not count. Theorem A does not touch that: it is a statement about W and holds at an isolated seat too, which the twenty-graph gate includes.
- **Simplicity reverses polarity between the two objects, and this file only sharpens one of them.** For the two-halves CRITERION on the XY book, simplicity is necessary and not sufficient. For the SPAN it was recorded as sufficient and not necessary; Corollary C explains that asymmetry by moving the hypothesis off H and onto H|_W, where it becomes necessary AND sufficient.
- **Five words are spent here in a sense the repo also spends elsewhere, and this is the fence at the door.** The **blind space** is NOT this file's criterion, and the difference is measurable rather than pedantic: the criterion is about the KRYLOV COMPLEMENT W. At the isolated seat of the twenty-graph table's `isolated seat N = 4`, watched at the isolated site, H is degenerate on the whole space (eigenvalues −4, 0, 2, 2 with the ZZ term and ±2√2, 0, 0 without) and the seat's own H-invariant ray is blind in the physical sense F157's Breaks-for records, yet H|_W is simple and the identity holds at 4 = 1 + 3. Reading the criterion on the blind subspace would predict a break there. A **block** in this file is either one of the two principal submatrices L and R the seat leaves behind, or a Wedderburn block of the algebra A; it is NOT MirrorWorld's `Block`, the joint-popcount block (p, q), which does not appear. The **span** is `dim ker L_SE(j)`, which F157 and the arc call the dimension of the stationary manifold; it is NOT [The Blind Site](../../experiments/THE_BLIND_SITE.md)'s span, which is a percentage change of a sweep window's mean and appears there about ten times. A **seat** is where the watching sits, not `Seed`'s unmirrorable reflection seat. **Striking** row and column j is never called cutting the chain, because at a nonzero bond nothing is cut: the dephasing clears the seat's row and column OFF the diagonal and the surviving (j, j) entry is what supplies Theorem A's `+1`.
- **This is not a statement about the steady STATE.** `dim ker L_SE(j)` is what is computed, which is the repo's "dimension of the stationary manifold" and one more than the dimension of the manifold of stationary STATES, as the abstract notes; which state a given initial condition relaxes to is [the asymptotic sector projection proof](PROOF_ASYMPTOTIC_SECTOR_PROJECTION.md)'s question, and where the kernel is one-dimensional the answer is I/N and says nothing about the seat, which is the negative result [The Seat That Cuts](../../experiments/THE_SEAT_THAT_CUTS.md) §1 opened with.

**Where the routes are one-sided, and where they are deliberately not.** A rank mod p can only drop, so a dimension read as a nullity mod p can only come out too large. Both sides of Theorem A are nullities, so ranking both mod p at the same prime would leave them free to inflate together; G6 therefore takes both over ℚ, and G6b pins the committed GF(p) route against it afterwards. G8 sweeps thousands of profiles rather than twenty graphs and keeps the GF(p) kernel route for cost; there the two sides genuinely come from independent routes, a kernel rank and a Krylov rank on different matrices.

**What the zero-bond reading costs, counted.** Part `commutant` prints it, because the claim that Corollary C replaces a fence deserves a number rather than a flourish: of the 73 failing triples, 12 carry a zero bond and 61 do not, and a further 20 triples carry a zero bond and the identity holds on them anyway. So the zero bond misses in both directions, and it is the degeneracy of H on the Krylov complement, not the bond, that decides.

**What the sweep deliberately does not exclude.** Negative and irregular couplings are in the zero-free sweep, because an argument that quietly assumed positive J would break there and nowhere else. Zero bonds, disconnection, an isolated seat and degenerate spectra are all in the twenty-graph list that gates Theorem A, which is the point: the theorem has no fence, and the gate is the place to show it.

# PROOF: the clock mirror, a qubit inside every even qudit

**Status:** Tier 1 derived, registered as [F172](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f172). The mechanism is Prosen's PT relation (2012), which [The Pairing Condition](../../experiments/THE_PAIRING_CONDITION.md) and [F158](PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md)'s sufficiency step carry out. The clock pair's one-sided element and the lone jump's first route are instances of the Lemma of Prosen's Brief Report of the same year, whose hypotheses, written for operators on a general Hilbert space, they meet as stated, though its examples are spin 1/2; the pair's transpose route and the lone jump's second are Sá, Ribeiro and Prosen's two steps, the transpose followed by a one-sided factor, the lone jump's in a rotated basis (§3). What this page adds: the instance read as the X of a qubit that every even qudit carries; the lone jump's two further routes, through the clock's half power and through complex conjugation (§3); the no-go at odd d, for every Hamiltonian, by the numerical range; and F158's two-end count carried from Hermitian involutions to unitary jumps whose dissipator is self-adjoint, with the lone jump as the case where it stops and its palindrome reduced to one spectral equality, kicks forward against kicks back. Gated exactly (§7): the transpose mirror on chains of d = 2, 4, 6, 8 levels, N = 1, 2, and N = 3 at d = 2, 4; the one-sided element and the lone jump's routes at d = 4, 6, N ≤ 2; the counts and the reduction at d = 4, 6, N = 2. Palindromic means here as a multiset, eigenvalues counted with multiplicity.
**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Authors:** Thomas Wicht, Claude (Anthropic)
**Statement:** Dephase every site of a chain of d-level systems by the clock Z = diag(1, ω, …, ω^{d−1}), ω = e^{2πi/d}, and its adjoint Z†, both at rate γ; X is the cyclic shift, and for even d, U = (X^{d/2})^⊗N.
- *The pair at even d.* If H commutes with U, then ρ ↦ Uρ carries L onto −L† − 4Nγ, and the spectrum is palindromic, λ ↦ −λ − 4Nγ; if H is moreover real symmetric, ρ ↦ ρᵀU satisfies Π L Π⁻¹ = −L − 4Nγ, the shape of F1's Π. The condition widens to a criterion: the spectrum is palindromic exactly when some invertible UΔ, Δ diagonal, commutes with H, equivalently when dim ker L = dim ker(L + 4Nγ), F158's two-end count, valid for unitary jumps whose dissipator is self-adjoint.
- *The qubit inside.* Writing a level as j = (d/2)·b + j', the clock is Z_b ⊗ diag(ω^{j'}) and U is X_b ⊗ 1 on every site: the element is the X of a qubit that every even qudit carries.
- *The lone jump at even d.* Z without Z† pairs as λ ↦ −λ − 2Nγ when H commutes with U and in addition commutes with the level reflection Q = P^⊗N, P|j⟩ = |−j⟩, or satisfies Q H̄ Q = H, H̄ being the complex conjugate in the level basis, or is reversed by the clock's half power, Y H Y⁻¹ = −H with Y = (Z^{d/2})^⊗N, or is purely imaginary, H̄ = −H. Here the count is necessary and not sufficient; where the counts agree, the spectrum is palindromic exactly when kicks forward and kicks back give one spectrum, spec L(H, Z) = spec L(H, Z†), the generator with the jump Z against the one with Z†.
- *Odd d.* The spectrum is palindromic for no Hermitian H, with the pair or with the lone jump, and the same bound rules it out for full-Cartan dephasing at one rate at every d ≥ 3.

## What this means

Under the dephasing the repository uses for qudits, which kills every coherence at one rate, of the d² operators on a site d stay immune and d² − d decay, and above d = 2 the spectrum is palindromic for no Hamiltonian at all. The clock is a different dephasing, a random phase kick by one step of the d-th roots of unity, forward or, with its adjoint, back; it decays a coherence by how far its phase winds, so its rates stand on a ring. A ring with an even number of places has an opposite place, and turning every coherence to its opposite pairs each rate with the rate the palindrome needs. That turn is the X of a qubit hidden in the qudit, which half of the levels a state sits in; the clock is that qubit's Z times a phase on the rest. So at even d, with kicks both ways, the palindrome survives for every Hamiltonian that respects the hidden qubit's flip, the SU(d) Heisenberg chain among them, or the flip followed by any phases on the levels; a clock field breaks the element; and there are no other palindromes there, since the two ends of the spectrum, counted, decide it. With kicks one way only it survives as well when the Hamiltonian is also suited to a reflection of the levels, or to one of two further symmetries that likewise make kicking forward and kicking back alike; here the two ends, counted, no longer decide alone, and where they agree what decides is whether kicking forward and kicking back give one spectrum. At odd d there is no opposite place, and the fastest decay is too slow to be the partner of the steady state: no Hamiltonian makes the spectrum palindromic.

## 1. Setting

N sites of d levels, |j⟩ with j ∈ ℤ_d, the shift X|j⟩ = |j + 1⟩ and the clock Z|j⟩ = ω^j|j⟩, ω = e^{2πi/d}, so that ZX = ωXZ. The clock here is the Weyl clock operator, the clock of the clock-and-shift pair, as in [PROOF_ANTILINEAR_TRIANGLE](PROOF_ANTILINEAR_TRIANGLE.md) §6; it is not MirrorWorld's `Clock`, F95's two hands. H is Hermitian, and H̄ is its complex conjugate in the level basis. The generator is

  L(ρ) = −i[H, ρ] + γ Σ_l (Z_l ρ Z_l† + Z_l† ρ Z_l − 2ρ),

each jump unitary, so its anticommutator term is −γρ, and σ = 2Nγ is the sum of the rates; L† is its adjoint in the Hilbert-Schmidt product. For an operator A, ℒ_A(ρ) = Aρ is left multiplication and Ad_A(ρ) = AρA⁻¹ conjugation. At d = 2 the pair is Pauli Z dephasing at the rate 2γ, which is why the centre is −2Nγ here and −Nγ in the qubit convention of one Z jump at rate γ. The Weyl operators X^a Z^b, (a, b) ∈ ℤ_d², are an orthogonal basis of one site's operators, the qudit's version of the four Pauli letters, and are called letters here; a is the letter's column. The columns are the wrapped parallels of [The One Square](../THE_ONE_SQUARE.md) §6, the cells whose bra index trails the ket index by a fixed step, read in the other basis: the cells |j + a⟩⟨j| span the column a.

## 2. One site on the letters

Z X^a Z^b Z† = ω^a X^a Z^b, so the clock pair multiplies a letter by γ(ω^a + ω^{−a} − 2):

  r(a) = 2γ(1 − cos(2πa/d)),

the rate depends on the column alone, each column holds d letters, and the mean rate over the columns is 2γ. That the letters diagonalize every Weyl-diagonal channel, ρ ↦ Σ_{a,b} p_{ab} X^aZ^b ρ (X^aZ^b)†, is standard, and the rates follow from it: [Chruściński, Macchiavello and Maniscalco](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02256) (Phys. Rev. Lett. 118, 080404, 2017, Example 6) write the Weyl generator's decay through 1 − Re ω^{…}, and [Chruściński and Siudzińska](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02616) (Phys. Rev. A 94, 022118, 2016) note in passing that above d = 2 the Weyl channel is not self-adjoint and has in general complex eigenvalues, the property of the lone jump in §3. What follows reads them as a ring with an opposite place. At d = 4:

| column a | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rate | 0 | 2γ | 4γ | 2γ |

four letters immune, eight at 2γ, four at 4γ: symmetric about the mean 2γ. Full-Cartan dephasing, the one rate for every coherence of §6, gives four immune against twelve decaying and is not.

A pairing about the mean, the only possible centre, needs a move a ↦ a' with r(a') = 4γ − r(a), that is cos(2πa'/d) = −cos(2πa/d), and a ↦ a + d/2 does it: a translation by the element of order two of ℤ_d, which exists exactly when d is even (gate G1, d = 2..8). At odd d the rate 0 sits in one column and the largest rate in two; on N sites the rate 0 then has d^N letters and the largest total rate (2d)^N, so the dissipator alone is not palindromic at any N.

Three maps act on the columns. Left multiplication by X^{d/2} sends X^a Z^b to X^{a+d/2} Z^b, the translation without a sign; right multiplication gives (−1)^b X^{a+d/2} Z^b, the same translation with a sign. The transpose sends X^a Z^b to ω^{−ab} X^{−a} Z^b ([F119](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md)'s θ on the Weyl operators), the reflection a ↦ −a, which keeps every rate because the cosine is even. The composite ρ ↦ ρᵀX^{d/2} is the reflection a ↦ d/2 − a. On a qubit the transpose fixes both columns and the composite a ↦ 1 − a is the translation a ↦ a + 1, which is why, on [the letter cube](../THE_ONE_SQUARE.md) of §7 there, the one-sided flip R (ρ ↦ ρ·X^⊗N) is a translation and the transpose D moves no letter. Above d = 2 the reflections and the translation are different maps. When 4 divides d the columns d/4 and 3d/4 sit at the mean 2γ and the composite a ↦ d/2 − a fixes them, a rung that is its own partner; at d ≡ 2 (mod 4) it fixes no column (at d = 6 the rates are 0, γ, 3γ, 4γ, 3γ, γ).

## 3. The element

**Lemma.** Let L(ρ) = −i[H, ρ] + Σ_l γ_l (A_l ρ A_l† − ρ) with unitary jumps, σ = Σ_l γ_l, U invertible and V unitary, with [U, H] = [V, H] = 0, U A_l U⁻¹ = −A_l for every l, and V carrying the dissipator onto its adjoint, Σ_l γ_l (V A_l V†) ρ (V A_l V†)† = Σ_l γ_l A_l† ρ A_l. Then M = ℒ_U Ad_V satisfies M L M⁻¹ = −L† − 2σ, and the spectrum of L is closed under λ ↦ −λ − 2σ.

*Proof.* Conjugation by V keeps the commutator and, V being unitary, turns each A_l ρ A_l† into (V A_l V†) ρ (V A_l V†)†, so Ad_V L Ad_V⁻¹ = −i[H, ·] + Σ_l γ_l (A_l† ρ A_l − ρ) =: L'. Left multiplication by U keeps the commutator, [U, H] = 0, and reverses each jump of L', since U A_l† U⁻¹ = (U A_l U⁻¹)⁻¹ = −A_l† for a unitary A_l: ℒ_U L' ℒ_U⁻¹(ρ) = −i[H, ρ] − Σ_l γ_l A_l† ρ A_l − σρ. And −L†(ρ) − 2σρ = −i[H, ρ] − Σ_l γ_l A_l† ρ A_l + σρ − 2σρ, the same. Similar maps have equal spectra, so the spectrum of L is {−λ̄ − 2σ : λ ∈ spec L}, and L preserves hermiticity, so its spectrum is closed under conjugation: it is {−λ − 2σ : λ ∈ spec L}. ∎

When M is a unitary involution, as in both uses below, this is the PT relation of Prosen (2012), L′P = −P L′† with P = M ([the literature review](../LITERATURE_REVIEW.md#pt-symmetric-liouvillians-prosen-2012)); the repository's computation of it is [The Pairing Condition](../../experiments/THE_PAIRING_CONDITION.md)'s, which [F158](PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) §(e) cites for Hermitian involutive jumps and which already runs past A² = 1. Both uses are instances of the Lemma of [Prosen's Brief Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.6588) of the same year (Phys. Rev. A 86, 044103), written for operators on a general Hilbert space; in letters free on this page it asks for ρ ↦ U₁ρU₂ with U₁ and U₂ unitary involutions commuting with H, a real orthogonal involution S on the jump indices with U₁ L_m = −Σ_{m'} S_{mm'} L_{m'}† U₁ and U₂ L_m = Σ_{m'} S_{mm'} L_{m'}† U₂, and {L_m, L_m†} ∝ 1 for every jump (his U, W and Z). The Lemma here asks U only to be invertible, which §5 needs, and V only to be unitary, carrying the dissipator rather than each jump; it asks the jumps to be unitary, where Prosen's jumps need, beside the mixing, only {L_m, L_m†} ∝ 1. §5 carries F158's two-end count, both directions, to unitary jumps whose dissipator is self-adjoint.

**The clock pair.** The jumps Z_l, Z_l† at one rate form a self-adjoint dissipator, V = 1. X^s Z X^{−s} = ω^{−s} Z, and at s = d/2, ω^{−d/2} = −1: U = (X^{d/2})^⊗N, a unitary involution, anticommutes with every jump. So at even d, for every Hermitian H commuting with U, ρ ↦ Uρ carries L onto −L† − 4Nγ and the spectrum pairs about −σ = −2Nγ (gate G9, with a field h·i(X − X†) that is Hermitian and not real): Prosen's Lemma with U₁ = U, U₂ = 1 and S exchanging Z_l and Z_l†. For real symmetric H the transpose reverses the commutator and sends each jump A to its complex conjugate, (A ρᵀ A†)ᵀ = Ā ρ Ā†, which exchanges Z and Z̄ = Z†; the pair is closed under that exchange, so its dissipator is kept, and ρ ↦ ρᵀU satisfies Π L Π⁻¹ = −L − 4Nγ (G2). These are Sá, Ribeiro and Prosen's two steps, the transpose followed by a one-sided factor (their §II E; their example in §III A, Eq. (76), has real symmetric jumps); since the transpose sends each jump to its complex conjugate, it keeps a dissipator exactly when the conjugate jumps give the same one, as the pair's do and the lone jump's does not. Their stated condition for the swap-modified chiral symmetry, a real Hamiltonian and symmetric jumps, is met by the lone jump at the real H of G15, whose spectrum is not palindromic there; the construction needs the conjugate jumps to give the same dissipator, which the real symmetric jumps of their example do. The map ρ ↦ ρᵀU is the shape of F1's Π_Z = R·D, ρ ↦ ρᵀ·X^⊗N ([the Π factorization](PROOF_PI_FACTORS_AS_R_TIMES_D.md)).

**The lone clock jump.** With Z_l alone at rate γ, σ = Nγ and the dissipator is not self-adjoint, and the transpose alone turns Z into Z†, so neither map above reaches it. The level reflection P|j⟩ = |−j⟩ has P Z P⁻¹ = Z† and commutes with X^{d/2}, and Q = P^⊗N gives two routes. If [Q, H] = 0, Q is the V of the Lemma, and M = ℒ_U Ad_Q, a unitary involution, gives M L M⁻¹ = −L† − 2Nγ (G10, the SWAP and clock-shift chains at d = 4, 6 and the shift-field chain at d = 4); in Prosen's form M(ρ) = (UQ)ρQ, with U₁ = UQ and U₂ = Q unitary involutions commuting with H and S = 1. If instead Q H̄ Q = H, the transpose followed by Ad_Q undoes the exchange of Z and Z† and sends the commutator to +i[Q H̄ Q, ·] = +i[H, ·], so M(ρ) = U·Qρᵀ Q⁻¹ gives M L M⁻¹ = −L − 2Nγ (G12). These are the same two steps in a basis T with TTᵀ = Q, which exists because Q is a real symmetric unitary: there Q H̄ Q = H makes H real and Q Z† Q = Z makes the jump real, though not symmetric, so the transpose of that basis keeps the dissipator and reverses the commutator, and followed by U it is M; the condition Q H̄ Q = H is that statement without choosing the basis. The two conditions on Q are different, neither implying the other: the field h·i(X − X†) meets the second and breaks the first; at d = 4 the one-site term i(|v₁⟩⟨v₂| − h.c.), v₁ = |0⟩ + |2⟩, v₂ = |1⟩ + |3⟩, commutes with U and Q and has Q H̄ Q = −H, the first without the second (by hand); and a bond i(Z_1 Z_2 − h.c.) commutes with U, breaks both, and breaks the identity (G12's control). Either way the lone jump pairs as λ ↦ −λ − 2Nγ. Any reflection P_c|j⟩ = |c − j⟩ does as P does, since P_c Z P_c⁻¹ = ω^c Z† and the phase drops out of the dissipator. Two further routes come from the other two kinds of symmetry of §5. With Y = (Z^{d/2})^⊗N, which keeps the clock and is not the Lemma's V (it reverses H and keeps the dissipator), a Hamiltonian commuting with U and reversed by Y, Y H Y⁻¹ = −H, gives M = ℒ_U Ad_Y with M L M⁻¹ = −L − 2Nγ, since Ad_Y turns L into the generator with −H and ℒ_U reflects that one back; and a purely imaginary one, H̄ = −H, commuting with U gives M(ρ) = Uρᵀ with M L M⁻¹ = −L† − 2Nγ, since the transpose then keeps the commutator −i[H, ·] and turns Z into Z† (G18, at Hamiltonians where the first two routes fail).

**The qubit inside.** Label j = (d/2)·b + j', b ∈ {0, 1}, 0 ≤ j' < d/2: b says which half of the levels j lies in, and for d a power of two it is the highest bit. Then ω^j = (−1)^b ω^{j'}, so Z = Z_b ⊗ diag(ω^{j'}), and the shift by d/2 flips b and keeps j', so X^{d/2} = X_b ⊗ 1 (G7). The labelling is a choice, and d = 6 below has a second one; what does not depend on it is the half-shift, the element of order two of ℤ_d, which conjugates the clock to −Z, −1 being the root of unity of order two. The element is the qubit's X on every site, anticommuting with the clock because the clock carries that qubit's Z as a factor. The factor on the rest is diag(1, i) at d = 4, the phase gate; at d = 8 the four-level diag(1, e^{iπ/4}, i, e^{3iπ/4}), the product S ⊗ T of the phase gate and the T gate on two further qubits; at d = 6 diag(1, e^{iπ/3}, e^{2iπ/3}). (In the labelling j ↦ (j mod 2, j mod 3) the clock of d = 6 is instead Z_b' ⊗ Z₃†, a qutrit clock beside a parity qubit b', with X³ = X_b' ⊗ 1 the same element: [F158](PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md)'s row of a qutrit tensored with a qubit, U = 1₃ ⊗ X, is that element.) At d = 4 the clock pair is exactly two-qubit Pauli-string dephasing, with jumps Z ⊗ 1 and Z ⊗ Z at the rate γ (G8), inside F158's class, and the two qubits are those of [the sodium nucleus](../../experiments/THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md): with the level j read as the spin projection m = 3/2 − j, b is the sign of m and Z ⊗ Z is ±1 on the outer and inner levels, the shape of the J₀ channel's T₂₀, so the clock pair on a spin 3/2 is that channel together with a dephasing of the sign of m, at one rate. At d = 8 it is not Z-string dephasing of three qubits in the binary labelling: the cells |2⟩⟨1| and |3⟩⟨0| disagree in the same bits, 011, and decay at r(1) and r(3), which differ. From d = 5 on no rewriting brings the pair into F158's class: every jump of the same dissipator is a linear combination Σ_l (a_l Z_l + b_l Z_l†) + c (the mixing of the jumps, with a multiple of the identity moved into H), a Hermitian one is diagonal with entries Σ_l 2|a_l| cos(2πk_l/d + φ_l) + c, a sum of functions of different sites takes at least as many values as any one of them, and a cosine at d ≥ 5 equally spaced angles takes at least three values, where an involution has two; so every a_l would vanish. At d = 3 (φ = 0) and d = 4 (φ = π/4) two values are possible, and the pair is inside the class there.

**The Hamiltonians.** [H, U] = 0 holds for the SU(d) Heisenberg chain H = J Σ SWAP_{l,l+1}, since SWAP commutes with every U ⊗ U (the class [PROOF_QUDIT_PARTIAL_PALINDROME](PROOF_QUDIT_PARTIAL_PALINDROME.md) §8 uses: at d = 3 its Σ_a λ_a ⊗ λ_a is 2·SWAP − ⅔·1); for the clock-shift chain J(X_l X_{l+1}† + h.c.) + Δ(Z_l Z_{l+1}† + h.c.), whose Z part picks up (−1)·(−1); and for shift fields h(X + X†) and h·i(X − X†). A clock field h(Z + Z†) breaks it, since U turns Z into −Z (G3, the identity).

## 4. Odd d

**No palindrome, for any H.** The self-adjoint part of L is the self-adjoint part of its dissipator, since −i[H, ·] is skew-adjoint, so every eigenvalue lies in the numerical range and Re λ ≥ λ_min(D_sa), D_sa the self-adjoint part of the dissipator: the strip of [F158](PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) §(b), here narrower. A palindrome λ ↦ −λ − c would fix its centre by the trace, c = −2 Tr L/dim, and would carry the eigenvalue 0, present because L preserves the trace, to −c. For the clock pair, D_sa is diagonal on the letters with the eigenvalues −Σ_l r(a_l), so λ_min = −N·r_max, and c = 2N·(mean rate) = 4Nγ. At odd d the largest rate is r_max = 2γ(1 + cos(π/d)) < 4γ, so −4Nγ lies below every eigenvalue and the partner of 0 does not exist. The lone jump's self-adjoint part is half the pair's dissipator, and its centre half the pair's centre, so the same inequality excludes it. At even d, r_max = 4γ and the bound meets the partner of 0 exactly (G13, exact, d = 2..9).

**The same bound for full-Cartan dephasing.** One rate r for every coherence of a d-level site, d ≥ 3, gives λ_min = −N·r and a centre c = 2N·r(d − 1)/d, the mean rate over the d² letters being r(d − 1)/d; since 2(d − 1)/d > 1 the partner of 0 lies below every eigenvalue again, so this dephasing is palindromic for no H at any d ≥ 3 (G13). This is stronger than [QUBIT_NECESSITY](../QUBIT_NECESSITY.md)'s exclusion of a complete local class swap, and needs the one rate: for unequal rates the bound excludes a palindrome whenever the largest total rate stays below twice the mean, and says nothing otherwise. The bound adds up over sites: a qubit under Z dephasing meets it with equality (largest rate 2γ, mean γ), a site of d ≥ 3 under one-rate full-Cartan dephasing or an odd-d clock site misses it strictly, so a chain mixing qubits with at least one such site is palindromic for no H either; the qubit-qutrit-qubit chain of QUBIT_NECESSITY §5 is one. At d = 3 the clock pair is this dephasing (§6), and §3a's ten broken qutrit Hamiltonians there are instances. Full-Cartan dephasing at one rate r is itself inside F158's class, a sum of the level reflections 1 − 2|k⟩⟨k| at the rate r/4 each. Their trace is d − 2, so F158 §(f5), which asks every jump to be traceless, forbids a pairing about F158's −σ at every d ≥ 3; but σ = Nd·r/4 there, and the centre the trace forces is half of 2N·r(d − 1)/d, the two agreeing only when (d − 2)² = 0. F158 forbids a centre the trace already rules out; the bound here forbids the one the trace forces, and with it every centre.

**No anticommuting operator.** The element of §3 is missing at odd d for a reason of its own: U Z U⁻¹ = −Z requires the spectrum of Z to be closed under negation, and the d-th roots of unity are closed under negation exactly when d is even. This is [F158](PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) §(f5)'s argument, U A U⁻¹ = −A making A similar to −A, with the spectrum's symmetry in place of the trace, which vanishes for the clock at every d. A second, independent route to the absence of a palindrome at the computed cases is the moment route of [F117](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md): at d = 3 with H = 0 (N = 1, 2) and the SWAP and clock-shift chains (N = 2), and at d = 5 with H = 0 (N = 1) and the SWAP chain (N = 2), each at one choice of couplings, an odd centred power sum Tr(L + 2Nγ)^k is nonzero modulo a prime (G4).

## 5. The count

[F158](PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) decides the palindrome by counting its two ends, the eigenvalues 0 and −2σ at the two edges of the numerical-range strip, for Hermitian jumps that square to the identity, and calls that the boundary of its class. Its proof spends less: unitarity in its three lemmas, and the self-adjointness of the dissipator, which Hermitian involutions have by themselves, in its last step. The centre is F158's, −σ; for traceless jumps, the clock's among them, it is the centre the trace forces.

**Theorem.** Let L(ρ) = −i[H, ρ] + Σ_l γ_l (A_l ρ A_l† − ρ) with H Hermitian, at least one jump, every A_l unitary, rates γ_l > 0, σ = Σ_l γ_l, and the dissipator self-adjoint, Σ_l γ_l A_l ρ A_l† = Σ_l γ_l A_l† ρ A_l for every ρ. Then the spectrum multiset of L is closed under λ ↦ −λ − 2σ if and only if dim ker L = dim ker(L + 2σ). Without the self-adjointness the "only if" survives and the "if" can fail.

*Proof.* F158's §(b) to §(e), with A_l ρ A_l† where F158 writes A_l ρ A_l; each step below names the hypothesis it spends.

- *Lemma 1,* ker L = 𝒩 = {K : [H, K] = 0, A_l K A_l† = K for every l} and ker(L + 2σ) = 𝒲 = {W : [H, W] = 0, A_l W A_l† = −W for every l}. The inclusions ⊇ are algebra. For ⊆, take the real part of ⟨W, LW⟩: the commutator contributes nothing, and Re⟨W, A_l W A_l†⟩ ≥ −‖W‖² by Cauchy-Schwarz, since ‖A_l W A_l†‖ = ‖W‖ for a unitary A_l; the rates are positive, so Re⟨W, LW⟩ = −2σ‖W‖² forces every term to its bound, A_l W A_l† = −W, and then [H, W] = 0. The near end is the same with the opposite bound.
- *Lemma 2,* both ends semisimple: the same bound makes L dissipative and L + 2σ accretive, and F158's two-line argument against a Jordan pair applies unchanged.
- *Lemma 3,* dim 𝒲 ≤ dim 𝒩, with an invertible element of 𝒲 exactly at equality. ℬ = 𝒩 ⊕ 𝒲 is a unital ℤ₂-graded *-subalgebra, since A_l(KK')A_l† = (A_l K A_l†)(A_l K' A_l†), unitarity again, and (A_l K A_l†)† = A_l K† A_l†, [H, K†] = −[H, K]† with H Hermitian, and 𝒩 ∩ 𝒲 = 0 because a jump is present; F158's block count then runs as written.
- *Necessity.* L is unital, A_l A_l† = 1, so 0 is an eigenvalue; a reflection sends it to −2σ with its algebraic multiplicity, and by Lemma 2 both multiplicities are the dimensions of the ends.
- *Sufficiency.* An invertible W ∈ 𝒲 satisfies A_l W = −W A_l, so W A_l W⁻¹ = −A_l, and the Lemma of §3 with V = 1 gives the palindrome; this is the one place the self-adjointness is spent.

At Hermitian involutions the dissipator is self-adjoint by itself, and the theorem is F158's. ∎

F158 §(f8)'s lift carries over as well: sufficiency gives the similarity ℒ_W L ℒ_W⁻¹ = −L† − 2σ, and L, which preserves hermiticity, is a real matrix in a Hermitian operator basis, so L† ~ L and L ~ −L − 2σ, the whole Jordan structure reflected.

**The clock pair.** Z_l K Z_l† multiplies the entry K_jk by ω^{j_l − k_l}. It is K for every l exactly when K is diagonal, so 𝒩 is the diagonal operators commuting with H; it is −K for every l exactly when j_l − k_l = d/2 at every site, so 𝒲 = {UΔ : Δ diagonal, [H, UΔ] = 0}. At even d the palindrome therefore holds exactly when some invertible UΔ commutes with H: the element of §3 widened from U to U followed by any invertible diagonal (G14, an H commuting with UΔ and not with U, reflected exactly by left multiplication with UΔ), and exactly when the counts agree. At odd d no difference of levels gives −1, 𝒲 = 0, and the count says §4's no from its own side; at even d a generic H has the far count 0 and an odd centred power sum nonzero modulo a prime, the two noes exact and agreeing (G16).

**Where the count stops.** The lone jump's dissipator is not self-adjoint. Necessity holds for it; sufficiency does not. [H, U] = 0 makes the counts agree, U being an invertible element of the far end, and the palindrome can still fail: for the gate's H, which commutes with U and meets none of the four routes of §3, both counts are 1 exactly, the identity and U bounding them from below and the rank modulo a prime from above, while an odd centred power sum is nonzero (G15, d = 4, 6).

**What the self-adjointness supplies.** It can be said exactly, for unitary jumps in general under the theorem's other hypotheses. Write L(H, {A_l}) for the generator with its Hamiltonian and jumps named, and let the counts agree, so that an invertible W ∈ 𝒲 commutes with H and anticommutes with every jump. The left-multiplication step of §3's Lemma, applied to L itself with W in place of U, spends only [W, H] = 0, the anticommutation and A_l†A_l = 1, and gives

  ℒ_W L(H, {A_l}) ℒ_W⁻¹ = −L(−H, {A_l}) − 2σ,

and L(−H, {A_l})† = L(H, {A_l†}); L(−H, {A_l}) preserves hermiticity, so its spectrum, the complex conjugate of its adjoint's, is the spectrum of L(H, {A_l†}). So wherever the counts agree, the spectrum of L(H, {A_l}) is the reflection about −σ of the spectrum of L(H, {A_l†}), and the palindrome holds exactly when the two are equal: for unitary jumps, with nothing asked of the dissipator, the spectrum is palindromic about −σ if and only if dim ker L = dim ker(L + 2σ) and replacing every jump by its adjoint leaves the spectrum unchanged. A self-adjoint dissipator makes the second condition automatic, which is the theorem above. In the form ℒ_W L ℒ_W⁻¹ = −L(H, {A_l†})† − 2σ, which for unitary jumps is the one above, the identity is the one [The Pairing Condition](../../experiments/THE_PAIRING_CONDITION.md) writes for jumps with A†A + AA† ∝ 1, landing on the Liouvillian with every A replaced by A†; the count is what supplies W. For the lone clock jump with [H, U] = 0 the spectrum under kicks forward is therefore the exact reflection of the spectrum under kicks back, and the palindrome says that the direction of the kick does not matter. Four kinds of symmetry make the two spectra equal, since spec L(−H, Z) = spec L(H, Z†): a unitary that keeps H and carries Z to Z†, and one that reverses H and keeps Z, carry L(H, Z) onto L(H, Z†) and onto L(−H, Z) respectively; an antiunitary that keeps H and keeps Z, and one that reverses H and carries Z to Z†, do the same antilinearly. The four routes of §3 are one of each: Q, Y = (Z^{d/2})^⊗N, Q after complex conjugation, and complex conjugation alone. The complementary kinds buy nothing; an antiunitary that keeps H and carries Z to Z†, for one, relates L(H, Z) to L(H, Z)†, whose spectra agree for every H. G15's H is real, so complex conjugation is such a map there, and spec L(H, Z) and spec L(H, Z†) still differ: the odd centred power sums of the two generators are negatives of each other and nonzero (G17).

## 6. The dephasers, one at d = 2

At d = 2 the clock is the Pauli Z, the full-Cartan construction is Z dephasing, all powers of the clock are Z, and continuous phase noise through n = diag(0, 1) = (1 − Z)/2 is Z dephasing at a quarter of the rate of the jump Z. Above d = 2 they part, though not all at once, and the palindrome sorts them:

- **full-Cartan, and all powers Z^k at one rate:** a binary split, d immune against d² − d decaying, palindromic for no H at any d ≥ 3 (§4; the dissipator's own count is [QUBIT_NECESSITY](../QUBIT_NECESSITY.md)'s). At d = 3 the clock pair is this class too: Z² = Z†, both nonzero columns decay at 3γ, and the pair is full-Cartan dephasing at another rate. The clock and the full-Cartan class part from d = 4.
- **the single clock generator Z,** the Absorption proof's first dephaser, whose rates have real part γ(1 − cos(2πa/d)): palindromic at every even d for H commuting with U and meeting one of the four routes of §3, and for no H at odd d.
- **the clock pair {Z, Z†},** whose dissipator is the self-adjoint part of the single generator's, at twice the rate: palindromic at every even d for H commuting with U, and for no H at odd d.
- **continuous phase noise,** the jump n, a fluctuating field along the level number rather than a discrete kick, not among the Absorption proof's three: a cell |i⟩⟨j| decays at γ(i − j)²/2, whose multiset is symmetric about a centre only at d = 2 (G6, d = 2..8), so it parts from the others already at d = 3; the dissipator alone, not the Hamiltonian's question, is decided here.

The Absorption proof leaves "which qudit channel is the right analogue of single-qubit Z-dephasing" a modeling choice for the rung spacing it asks about. The palindrome does not fix that spacing; it says that among its three only the clock keeps the mirror above d = 2, alone or with its adjoint, at even d, under the conditions on H of §3, and that at odd d none of them does.

## 7. The gate

The identities live in ℤ[ζ_m], m = lcm(d, 4), and are reduced modulo two primes p ≡ 1 (mod m), where ζ_m is an integer of order m, so each reduction is a degree-one prime; complex conjugation is the Galois map ζ ↦ ζ⁻¹ and is written into the construction, never applied to residues, and G11 checks that the builders act on every matrix unit |j⟩⟨k| by the rate the clock gives it, so a wrong conjugation in the lone-jump builder fails (the pair's builder is symmetric in Z and Z† and cannot carry one). Every mirror here permutes the matrix units, ℒ_{UΔ} of G14 up to the roots of unity its Δ is made of and ℒ_U Ad_Y of G18 up to signs, so a residual entry is an element of ℤ[ζ_m] whose Galois conjugates are all at most B = 2·max|L| + c in modulus, max|L| taken on a majorant with every root of unity replaced by its modulus. Zero modulo degree-one primes above p₁ and p₂ makes p₁p₂ divide its norm, which is at most B^φ(m); the gate checks B^φ(m) < p₁p₂ beside every identity of G2, G9, G10 and G12, so zero modulo the two primes is zero. The identities of G7, G8 and G11 compare entries whose Galois conjugates have modulus at most 16 (at most 8 at d = 5, where φ(20) = 8) and are checked at both primes, where that bound to the power φ(m) stays below p₁p₂. The vanishing odd power sums (G5 and the moment lines of G9, G10) have heights far above p₁p₂; their vanishing is consistency, not exactness, and the identities imply it. The obstruction G4 runs the other way and is exact as it stands: nonzero modulo a prime is nonzero. G13 checks the inequalities of §4 in sympy. G14 is an identity under the same norm bound; G15 and G16 read nullities modulo two primes, which can only exceed the nullity over ℚ(ζ_m), hence over ℂ, so a nullity 0 is exact and a nullity 1 is exact beside a known vector in the kernel. G17's identity at G15's H stands under the same norm bound, and on the rows of the two routes it is checked modulo p only, without the bound; G18's identities stand under the bound. G17's power sums are read as G15's: two that differ are exact, and their being negatives of each other is a consistency, which the identity implies together with the reality of the exact traces.

## What this is about

Three qubits sit on a triangle of bonds. An excitation hops along the bonds with complex amplitudes, so the product of the three amplitudes round the triangle carries a flux, and each bond may carry an Ising coupling ZZ. Every qubit stands in a bath that takes its excitation away at a rate γ↓ and gives one at a rate γ↑, so each site is a point (γ↓, γ↑) in a plane of rates. The question is F137's: when is the spectrum of the Lindblad generator paired, its eigenvalues in pairs λ and −c − λ, with c the sum of all the rates? For these bonds that is the same as the spectrum not changing when the flux is reversed. The odd centred traces Tr((L + c/2)^m) test it: each must vanish for a pairing, and one that does not vanish rules it out.

The fifth trace is a constant times the imaginary part of the loop (on equal |t|, |t|³ times the sine of the flux) times the area of the triangle that the three rate points span ([F137](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f137)): at a flux other than 0 and π a pairing needs the three rate points on one line. The seventh trace has a part of second degree in the rates, closed in [the transport formula's proof](PROOF_TRANSPORT_FORMULA_LOSS_AND_GAIN.md), and a part of fourth degree, which is closed here.

The part of fourth degree uses the three hops round the triangle and nothing else of the Hamiltonian, so it is the imaginary part of the loop times one polynomial in the rates, the same for every triangle. On the letters of the Pauli strings it is a sum over closed walks: the arrow I → Z of the net cooling once, the three hops round the loop, and in between the heights of the visited strings, the diagonal of the rate part on each (the words with three arrows cancel, which an exact fit shows). Written with the areas that the rate points of two sites span with the origin, each pair of sites reads the area of its own two rate points, weighted by the squared heights of the three sites, the opposite corner twice. On any graph the part of fourth degree is a sum over its triangles, each read by the same walks, the sites off a triangle entering only through the product of lower traces; so with the part of second degree the seventh trace is closed on every graph of these bonds. Taken apart into that product and what is connected, the connected part carries the heights of a triangle's sites with the weights the transport formula gives the bonds that touch it: a squared height counts like minus the squared amplitude of a bond at that site, once at the two sites of a pair and half at the opposite corner (the pair's own bond, a ZZ on an edge of the triangle and, on larger graphs, the borrowed triangles and the rings of four and five excepted); this is a reading of two closed forms side by side, not derived.

On a line of rate points the two parts combine into one cubic. On the triangle with equal |t| and one Δ it leaves three cases: one ratio of the rates, two sites with the same rate point, or the same total rate at every site. The first two are carriers F137 names, known reasons for a pairing. The third is broken by the ninth trace, except where it meets the second. So on that triangle the spectrum pairs at a flux other than 0 and π exactly where a known carrier applies. On every other triangle the seventh trace leaves a cylinder over a surface on the lines γ↑ = e + m·γ↓ and over a curve on the lines γ↓ = e, and whether a pairing lives on it is not decided.

## Abstract

Setting: three sites, bonds t_ab = J + iD_z (the hop a → b) with ZZ couplings Δ_ab, loss γ↓_l and gain γ↑_l per site, no fields, no dephasing; s_l = (γ↓_l + γ↑_l)/2 the height of site l and δ_l = (γ↓_l − γ↑_l)/2 the coefficient of its arrow; ω_ab = γ↓_a·γ↑_b − γ↓_b·γ↑_a; Im = Im(t₀₁·t₁₂·t₂₀), the imaginary part of the loop; V(x) = (x₀ − x₁)(x₁ − x₂)(x₂ − x₀). The rates are not negative.

**Theorem 1.** The part of Tr((L + c/2)⁷) of fourth degree in the rates is

  13440·Im·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1}·(s_l² + s_{l+1}² + 2·s_{l+2}²),

Im times a polynomial the same for every triangle; with the same arrow δ on every site it is 26880·Im·δ·V(s). That the polynomial is universal is an argument; it is evaluated by an exact fit over all 126 monomials, and as a sum over closed walks on the Pauli letters it extends to any graph (§2). On any graph of these bonds the part of fourth degree is the sum over its triangles of

  4^N·Im·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1}·(630·|s|² − 420·(s_l² + s_{l+1}²) − 210·s_{l+2}²),

with Im and the rates of the triangle and |s|² over every site; at N = 3 this is the form above (§2). With the transport formula's Theorem B the seventh trace is then closed on every graph of these bonds.

The connected part of Theorem 1's form, its part in 4^N·κ₇ with κ₇ the seventh cumulant (§3), is −26880·4^(N−3)·Im·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1}·(s_l² + s_{l+1}² + ½s_{l+2}²); read beside Theorem B, the heights carry the weights of the bonds that touch the triangle (§3, a reading).

**Theorem 2.** On the triangle, on a line of rate points γ↑ = e + m·γ↓, Tr((L + c/2)⁷) = −e·[3360·Im·(1 + m)²·V(γ↓) + 10752·⟨γ↓, b⟩], with β the antisymmetric matrix of Theorem B's part of second degree, −168·4³·Σ_{l<k} β_lk·ω_lk, and b_l = Σ_k β_lk its flow out of the site l; on a line γ↓ = e it is e·[3360·Im·V(γ↑) + 10752·⟨γ↑, b⟩] (§4). The shear δ_l → δ_l + ν·s_l keeps the fifth and seventh traces on every graph and not the ninth.

**Theorem 3.** On the triangle with equal |t| = r and one Δ, at a flux other than 0 and π, the spectrum is paired exactly when the rates have one ratio γ↑/γ↓ on every damped site (cooling or heating alone included) or two sites have the same rate point (§5). On equal heights s_l = S the ninth trace is 36864·Im·(r² + 7Δ²)·S·V(δ).

The 430080 that F137 records for a temperature gradient is Theorem 1 with the same arrow on every site: 13440·γ⁴·V(n̄) on the unit triangle (t = 1, 1, i, no ZZ, flux π/2, Im = 1), at γ = 2 and n̄ = (1, 2, 3).

## 1. Setting

As in [the transport formula's proof](PROOF_TRANSPORT_FORMULA_LOSS_AND_GAIN.md) §1: H = Σ_bonds J(X_aX_b + Y_aY_b) + D_z(X_aY_b − Y_aX_b) + Δ_ab·Z_aZ_b, t_ab = J + iD_z the amplitude of the hop a → b, t_ba its conjugate, H̄ the complex conjugate of H in the computational basis (every hop conjugated, every flux reversed), loss σ⁻_l at γ↓_l ≥ 0 and gain σ⁺_l at γ↑_l ≥ 0, c = Σ_l(γ↓_l + γ↑_l), and

  L + c/2 = −i·ad_H + Σ_l (s_l·𝓗_l + δ_l·𝓐_l),  𝓗(ρ) = σ⁺ρσ⁻ + σ⁻ρσ⁺,  𝓐(ρ) = σ⁻ρσ⁺ − σ⁺ρσ⁻ + ½{Z, ρ}.

On one site's Pauli letters (I, X, Y, Z), with Z = −1 on the excited state, 𝓗 is diag(1, 0, 0, −1) and 𝓐 is the arrow 2·|Z⟩⟨I|: 𝓗(I) = I, 𝓗(Z) = −Z, 𝓗(X) = 𝓗(Y) = 0, 𝓐(I) = 2Z and 𝓐 kills the other three (F137's step (1)). So on Pauli strings the rate part is diagonal, with the height of the string P

  η(P) = Σ_l s_l·ε(P_l),  ε(I) = 1, ε(X) = ε(Y) = 0, ε(Z) = −1,

plus the arrows. This is the letter cube's reading of [THE_ONE_SQUARE](../THE_ONE_SQUARE.md) §9: a height charges each string and moves nothing, and the net cooling is an arrow I → Z. On one letter ε = 1 − (k_X + k_Y) in the cube's coordinates, so the height of loss and gain is one minus the sum of the heights that X and Y dephasing would give. η(P) is the rate part's diagonal entry on P: the rate at which P would decay if neither H nor the arrows moved it, counted from the mean and with the sign turned.

A *carrier*, in F137's sense, is one of its listed reasons for the pairing: an explicit map (a similarity, a transposition or a conjugation, composed with turns where needed) that gives the generator at the reversed flux the spectrum of L, or, at ratio 0 or ∞ on a whole component (cooling or heating alone), the one-ratio carrier's limit through the characteristic polynomial, where the pairing survives and no map does.

ω_ab is twice the signed area of the triangle (origin, rate point a, rate point b), and ω_ab = −2·(s_a·δ_b − s_b·δ_a); Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1} is twice the signed area of the three rate points, the fifth trace's factor. The shear δ_l → δ_l + ν·s_l, with one ν at every site, keeps every ω_ab and every s_l.

**Pairing is flux reversal.** By F137's paragraph on loss and gain exchanged, for an H that commutes with Z⊗N the spectrum pairs exactly when exchanging loss and gain on every site, δ → −δ, leaves it unchanged, and every odd centred trace changes sign under that exchange. The spin flip X⊗N exchanges σ⁺ and σ⁻ and carries these bonds' H to H̄, so conjugating by it carries the generator at (H; s, δ) to the generator at (H̄; s, −δ): the exchange is a flux reversal (F137's heading). So the spectrum pairs exactly when it does not change as the flux is reversed, and Tr((L + c/2)^m) at H̄ is minus its value at H for every odd m, at every rate, and so is each of its parts by degree in the rates.

**The degrees of the seventh trace.** F137's three maps send the centred generator (by conjugation, the first composed with a transposition) to its value at (H̄; −s, δ), (H̄; s, −δ) and (−H̄; s, δ), so every trace of a power is even in the rates, and the degree of a part in H plus its degree in the arrows is even. In Tr((L + c/2)⁷) the part of degree zero is Tr((−i·ad_H)⁷) = 0, the spectrum of ad_H being symmetric about zero, and the part of degree six has one factor of H and is zero by F137's argument at first order. So the seventh trace has parts of degree two and four only, as the transport formula's proof records; and each part is odd under δ → −δ, so the words with an even number of arrows add nothing to it.

## 2. Theorem 1

**Only the loop.** A term of fourth degree in the rates of the seventh trace has three factors of H. A turn about Z on one site commutes with loss and gain and multiplies the hops at that site by a phase, so the trace is a polynomial in what such turns keep; at degree three in H on a triangle that is the loop t₀₁t₁₂t₂₀, its conjugate, |t|²·Δ and Δ³. The last two, and the real part of the loop, do not change under flux reversal, so by §1 they drop out. What is left is Im·R₄ with R₄ a polynomial of degree four in the six rates alone, the same for every triangle.

**The polynomial.** One triangle determines R₄. The gate fits the fourth-degree part of the unit triangle (t = 1, 1, i) over all 126 monomials of degree four in the six rates γ↓_l + γ↑_l and γ↓_l − γ↑_l (twice the heights and the arrows) at 140 integer points, exactly, the parts of each degree separated by scaling every rate by 1, 2, 3 and 4, the odd degrees being zero by §1; the system has full rank 126, the surplus rows are consistent, and the 18 nonzero coefficients are those of the form in Theorem 1 in these variables. On three more triangles (unequal |t| with ZZ, equal |t| at another flux with ZZ, a pure DM bond) the fourth-degree part is the same form at random rates. Written with the arrows, R₄ = 26880·Σ_l δ_l·(s_{l+1} − s_{l+2})·(s₀² + s₁² + s₂² − s_{l+1}s_{l+2}). It is linear in the arrows; exchanging the rates of two sites changes its sign, as the exchange of the two sites reverses the loop; and it is unchanged under the shear of §1, since Σ_l s_l·(s_{l+1} − s_{l+2})·(|s|² − s_{l+1}s_{l+2}) = 0. With the same arrow on every site, Σ_l (s_{l+1} − s_{l+2})(|s|² − s_{l+1}s_{l+2}) = V(s).

**A second road: the walks.** By §1 only the words of Tr((L + c/2)⁷) with three letters −i·ad_H and four rate letters that have one or three arrows contribute. One with a single arrow, at a site k, is a closed walk of Pauli strings: P (with the letter I at k) → P′ (with the letter Z at k) → Q₁ → Q₂ → P, three hops returning the Z to I. The three heights sit in the four gaps between the hops and the arrow, and summed over their places they give the complete homogeneous polynomial h₃(η(P′), η(Q₁), η(Q₂), η(P)). Every such word is one of the seven rotations of a word with the arrow first, so the words with one arrow give

  7·Σ_k Σ_walks (product of the three hop amplitudes)·2δ_k·h₃(η(P′), η(Q₁), η(Q₂), η(P)),

the part of Im·R₄ linear in the arrows. The gate evaluates this sum as a polynomial on the unit triangle and finds Theorem 1, and at random rates on the three other triangles, the flux-even walks cancelling. On the unit triangle there are 144 walks, 48 per arrow site, each with amplitude ±8, the product of three entries of −i·ad_H; for the arrow at site 0 their h₃-sums, the amplitude 8 taken out, add to 240·(s₁ − s₂)·(s₀² + s₁² + s₂² − s₁s₂), and 26880 = 7·2·8·240. The words with three arrows and one height have fourth degree too; they are exactly the part of Im·R₄ cubic in the arrows, which is zero by the fit, on every triangle since R₄ is universal; why they cancel is open (§8). The walks do not evaluate R₄ without the fit; they carry it to every graph.

**Every graph.** On any graph of these bonds a word of fourth degree in the rates of the seventh trace still has three factors of H, and by the turns about Z and §1 only those that close a flux survive: three hops once round a triangle of the graph. So the part of fourth degree is a sum over the triangles T, each the walk sum of T with every other site a spectator. A spectator's letter is moved by no hop of the word, and an arrow there would leave a Z that no hop returns, so the spectators add one constant w = Σ_q s_q·ε(P_q) to all four heights. For four variables h₃(x + w) = h₃(x) + 6w·h₂(x) + 15w²·h₁(x) + 20w³, and summed over the spectators' letters, where Σ_P ε(P) = Σ_P ε(P)³ = 0 and Σ_P ε(P)² = 2 over the four letters of one site, what is left beside 4^(N−3) times T's own sum is 7·15·2·4^(N−4)·Σ_q s_q² times the walk sum with the one height h₁. That walk sum, with its five rotations and the 4^(N−3) letters of the spectators, is T's part of the fifth trace, so each spectator q adds 21·(s_q²/2) times T's part of Tr((L + c/2)⁵), its height in μ₂ times μ₅ (§3), and nothing connected: the linked-cluster statement of [WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING](../../experiments/WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING.md) (cumulants add over connected clusters) at this order, read off the walks. The words with three arrows add nothing here either: their one height carries w linearly, which sums to zero over the spectators' letters, so what is left is 4^(N−3) times the triangle's own sum of such words, which is zero. With μ₅ = 60·Im·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1} (F137) this is the form of Theorem 1 on any graph.

## 3. The product, the cumulant and the touch rule

Tr(L + c/2) and Tr((L + c/2)³) vanish here (the second by F137's third moment for every traceless H under cooling and heating, whose terms in the transverse fields and in Tr(Z_l·H²) are zero for these bonds), so with μ_m = Tr((L + c/2)^m)/4^N the seventh cumulant is κ₇ = μ₇ − 21·μ₅·μ₂, as in [the transport formula's proof](PROOF_TRANSPORT_FORMULA_LOSS_AND_GAIN.md) §1. The rate part of μ₂ is |s|²/2 (the heights are diagonal with Σ_letters ε = 0 and Σ_letters ε² = 2, the arrows square to zero and Tr(𝓗𝓐) = 0), and μ₅ = 60·Im·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1} (F137). The product puts 21·60·½·4³ = 40320 = 3·13440 times Im·|s|²·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1} into Theorem 1's form, and what is left is

  −26880·Im·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1}·(s_l² + s_{l+1}² + ½·s_{l+2}²).

The transport formula's Theorem B has the same shape for the bonds. Its connected part counts a hopping bond that touches the triangle −10·|t|² at l or k and −5·|t|² at the third corner j, in the units of β, and a ZZ bond off the triangle's edges −10 and −5 per Δ². Write a² for a bond's squared amplitude in −i·ad_H on Pauli strings: (2J)² + (2D_z)² = |2t|² for a hop, whose entries are ±2J and ±2D_z, and (2Δ)² for a ZZ; in the part of second degree, −168·4^N·Σ_{l<k} β_lk·ω_lk, these weights are 26880·4^(N−3) and 13440·4^(N−3) per a². Call the two sites of ω_{l,l+1}, Theorem B's l and k, the pair, and l + 2, Theorem B's j, its third corner (the opposite corner). Then, up to the exceptions below, the connected parts of second and fourth degree read together

  −26880·4^(N−3)·Im·Σ_cyc ω_{l,l+1}·(Ξ_l + Ξ_{l+1} + ½·Ξ_{l+2}),  Ξ_m = s_m² − Σ_{bonds at m} a²,

**the touch rule**: at each site of the triangle the squared height less the squared amplitudes of the bonds at that site, with weight 1 at the pair's two sites and ½ at the third corner, and no product of two different sites or bonds. The edges through the third corner fit the rule (−15 = −10 − 5). Two kinds of bond Theorem B counts otherwise: the pair's own edge (−22 against the −20 of the rule) and a ZZ on an edge of the triangle (its quadratic form). On a graph beyond the triangle Theorem B's connected part also holds the borrowed triangles and the rings of four and five, which have no partner among the heights and stand outside the rule. The minus sign reads as (−i)², the hop entering −i·ad_H twice and the height entering real. Why the heights carry the bonds' weights is not derived here; the rule is the two closed forms read side by side.

## 4. Theorem 2: a line of rate points

On the line γ↑ = e + m·γ↓, ω_lk = e·(γ↓_l − γ↓_k), so the part of second degree, −168·4³·Σ_{l<k} β_lk·ω_lk ([the transport formula's proof](PROOF_TRANSPORT_FORMULA_LOSS_AND_GAIN.md), Theorem B), is −10752·e·⟨γ↓, b⟩. For the part of fourth degree, with m ≠ −1, the shear δ_l → δ_l + ν·s_l with ν = −(1 − m)/(1 + m) carries the arrows δ_l = ((1 − m)γ↓_l − e)/2 to the same arrow −e/(1 + m) at every site, leaving the heights s_l = ((1 + m)γ↓_l + e)/2; Theorem 1 is unchanged by the shear and gives 26880·Im·(−e/(1 + m))·((1 + m)/2)³·V(γ↓) = −3360·e·Im·(1 + m)²·V(γ↓), at m = −1 too, both sides being polynomials in m. On a line γ↓ = e the shear ν = 1 gives the same arrow e at every site and heights s = (e + γ↑)/2, so 3360·e·Im·V(γ↑), and ω_lk = e·(γ↑_k − γ↑_l) gives +10752·e·⟨γ↑, b⟩. ∎

So for the seventh trace a line of rate points is a temperature gradient: the shear takes it to the same arrow on every site, the form of one bath strength at different temperatures (as polynomials; the sheared rates need not be rates of a bath). The shear keeps ω and s (§1), and with them the fifth trace, Theorem B's part and, by §2, the part of fourth degree, so it keeps the fifth and seventh traces on every graph of these bonds; it does not keep the ninth trace (gate (G)), and so not the spectrum. The temperature gradient F137 records, γ↓ = γ·(n̄ + 1) and γ↑ = γ·n̄ on the unit triangle at flux π/2, has the same arrow γ/2 on every site and heights γ(2n̄ + 1)/2, so Theorem 1 gives 26880·(γ/2)·γ³·V(n̄) = 13440·γ⁴·V(n̄), at γ = 2 and n̄ = (1, 2, 3) the recorded 430080; the part of second degree is zero there, the three weights being equal. The 322560 that the transport formula's proof records on the triangle t = 1, 1 + i, 2i (Im = 2) at γ↓ = (3, 5, 2) and γ↑ = γ↓ − 2, where ⟨γ↓, b⟩ = 0, is Theorem 2 at m = 1 and e = −2: 2·3360·2·2²·V(3, 5, 2) with V = 6.

**The cylinder the seventh trace leaves.** Off one ratio (e ≠ 0) the seventh trace vanishes on 3360·Im·(1 + m)²·V(γ↓) + 10752·⟨γ↓, b⟩ = 0. V and ⟨γ↓, b⟩ are unchanged by γ↓ → γ↓ + v·(1, 1, 1), since Σ_l b_l = 0 for an antisymmetric β, and neither contains e, so the zero set is a cylinder along e and along (1, 1, 1) over a surface in m and the differences x = γ↓₀ − γ↓₁, y = γ↓₁ − γ↓₂; at each m it is the cubic curve −3360·Im·(1 + m)²·x·y·(x + y) + 10752·(b₀·x − b₂·y) = 0, for m ≠ −1 the curve of m = 0 scaled by 1/|1 + m|, and at m = −1 the line b₀·x − b₂·y = 0. On a line γ↓ = e the zero set, 3360·Im·V(γ↑) + 10752·⟨γ↑, b⟩ = 0, is a cylinder along e and (1, 1, 1) over a curve in the differences of the γ↑. For t₀₁ = t₁₂ = 2 + i and t₂₀ = 3 + 4i (Im = 24) with ZZ 2 on (0, 1) and (1, 2) and 3 on (0, 2), the weights of the three pairs are 1661⁄3, 1661⁄3 and 396 in units of Im and b = Im·(473⁄3, 0, −473⁄3); with b₁ = 0 the curve splits into the line x + y = 0, which is γ↓₀ = γ↓₂, two equal rate points that the reflection fixing site 1 exchanges (t₀₁ = t₁₂ and Δ₀₁ = Δ₁₂, the carrier of §5 (ii)), and the hyperbola (1 + m)²·x·y = 7568⁄15, so the seventh trace on the line is a multiple of e·(γ↓₀ − γ↓₂)·[(1 + m)²·(γ↓₀ − γ↓₁)(γ↓₁ − γ↓₂) − 7568⁄15]. The gate places a point on the hyperbola and finds the seventh trace zero there, still zero when every γ↓ moves by the same amount, and not zero when one site moves.

## 5. Theorem 3: the equal triangle

Let the three bonds have one |t| = r and one Δ. Then the three pairs have the same weight in Theorem B and b = 0. Any permutation of the three sites keeps every |t| and Δ: an even one carries H to H up to a phase per site, a turn about Z that commutes with loss and gain, and an odd one carries H to H̄ up to such phases, reversing the flux.

*The fifth trace.* At a flux other than 0 and π it vanishes only on collinear rate points (F137), so a pairing needs a line γ↑ = e + m·γ↓ or a line γ↓ = e.

*The seventh trace.* By Theorem 2 with b = 0 it is −3360·e·Im·(1 + m)²·V(γ↓) on the first kind of line and 3360·e·Im·V(γ↑) on the second. It vanishes in three cases. (i) e = 0: one ratio γ↑/γ↓ = m on every site, cooling alone at m = 0, or γ↓ = 0 on every site, heating alone. One ratio is one temperature on every site, F137's first carrier (the thermal transpose of [PROOF_ANTILINEAR_TRIANGLE](PROOF_ANTILINEAR_TRIANGLE.md) §7, detailed balance); heating alone is cooling alone seen through the spin flip X⊗N, which exchanges σ⁺ and σ⁻ and carries H to H̄, and cooling alone pairs at every H of these bonds (F137). (ii) V = 0: two sites with the same γ↓ on the first kind of line, or the same γ↑ on the second, hence the same rate point; the reflection that exchanges them keeps the rates and carries H to H̄ up to a phase per site, which is F137's carrier by the reflection fixing one site of the triangle, with rates symmetric under it. (iii) m = −1: the same height s_l = e/2 on every site, the same total rate, the arrows free. The criterion is F137's, the spectrum unchanged when every arrow flips (its paragraph on which rate); flipping every arrow keeps the heights, so on equal heights it is a question about the arrows alone, and the ninth trace decides it.

*The ninth trace on equal heights.* Write s_l = S and δ_l the arrows. By §1 every part of the ninth trace by degree in the rates is odd under flux reversal, and an odd permutation of the sites reverses the flux, so on equal heights each part is alternating in the arrows and divisible by V(δ), as on [WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING](../../experiments/WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING.md)'s triangle. By F137's three maps (§1) every trace is even in the rates and the degree of a part in H plus its degree in the arrows is even. The part of degree zero in the rates is Tr((−i·ad_H)⁹) = 0, the spectrum of ad_H being symmetric about zero; the part of second degree is zero, V(δ) having degree three; the part of eighth degree has one factor of H and is zero by F137's argument at first order; the part of sixth degree has three factors of H, so as in §2 it is Im times a polynomial in the rates alone, the same for every triangle, and the gate fits it on equal heights of the unit triangle and finds it zero. The part of fourth degree has odd degree in the arrows, which excludes (δ₀ + δ₁ + δ₂)·V(δ), so it is α·S·V(δ), with α a polynomial of degree five in the couplings, odd under flux reversal: Im times a squared |t| or a product of two Δ, on the equal triangle Im·(A·r² + B·Δ²). The gate's fit on the unit triangle gives A = 36864, the unit triangle at Δ = 1 gives B = 7A, and the unit triangle at Δ = 3 and the triangle with r² = 5 and Δ = 2 are checks. So

  Tr((L + c/2)⁹) = 36864·Im·(r² + 7Δ²)·S·V(δ)   on equal heights,

which, with r > 0, is zero only when S = 0, no rates at all, or two arrows are equal, which on equal heights is two equal rate points, case (ii). ∎

The same shape, a Vandermonde whose zeros are the reflections, is the ninth trace of the triangle under cooling alone for the two bonds that do not conserve the excitation number computed in [WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING](../../experiments/WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING.md), for which the triangle pairs exactly when two rates agree; the classes differ and the two are not joined here. In the repo's words for dark states ([THE_SEAT_THAT_CUTS](../../experiments/THE_SEAT_THAT_CUTS.md)), every point where the equal triangle pairs is forced, a carrier (one ratio or a symmetry of the sites) guaranteeing it; §6 asks whether a met one, an agreement nothing forces, exists elsewhere.

## 6. Every other triangle

Off the equal triangle the seventh trace leaves, off one ratio, the cylinders of §4, of dimension four in the five dimensions of the rate points on lines γ↑ = e + m·γ↓, and of dimension three in the four of the lines γ↓ = e. Whether the ninth and higher traces leave a point of it that no carrier explains is not decided here. Equal weights alone do not settle it: with t₀₁ = t₁₂ = 11 + 3i, t₂₀ = 5 and Δ₀₂ = 21 the three weights are equal and b = 0, so the cylinder contains all of equal heights, but the triangle is not symmetric under every permutation and §5's argument does not apply; at S = 4 and arrows (3, −1, 2) the fifth and seventh traces are zero and the ninth is −53828247306240 (gate row (S)). On equal heights the whole ninth trace there is

  −6082560·S·(δ₀ − δ₂)·(2436·S² + 4851·δ₀² + 5846·δ₀δ₁ + 2890·δ₀δ₂ − 260·δ₁² + 5846·δ₁δ₂ + 4851·δ₂² + 2122512)

(gate row (S)). The factor δ₀ − δ₂ is the reflection fixing site 1, which this triangle does have. The quadratic is indefinite and vanishes at rates a bath can have: at S ≈ 62.87 and arrows ≈ (−29.86, 62.87, −28.29), γ↓ ≈ (33.0, 125.7, 34.6) and γ↑ ≈ (92.7, 0, 91.2), the odd traces through the ninth are zero to double precision and the eleventh is not, and the spectrum misses its mirror by 0.30 (double precision, read with the search script's Liouvillian). So on this triangle the ninth trace leaves a surface of equal heights, and the eleventh breaks the point read on it.

Evidence, not proof: [`simulations/triangle_pairing_search.py`](../../simulations/triangle_pairing_search.py) minimises f/g², f the sum over the eigenvalues of the squared distance to the nearest mirror image about −c/2, divided by c² (a zero of f is necessary for a pairing, not sufficient, since several eigenvalues may share one mirror image), and g² the spread of the log ratios ln(γ↑/γ↓) between the sites, over log rates in [−2, 2], 24 starts each. Near one ratio f and g² vanish together and f/g² stays finite; a pairing off one ratio is a zero. On an equal triangle (flux 3·arctan 0.4, ZZ 0.3) the six best minima are at 2·10⁻³⁰ to 5·10⁻²⁸, each a pair of equal rate points. On a triangle with |t| = 1, 1.30 and 0.82, flux about 1.72 and ZZ 0.3, −0.5 and 0.2 the best is 4.9·10⁻⁷ (f = 2·10⁻¹⁰ with g² = 4·10⁻⁴, near one ratio, both small rather than a zero), against a median of 6.2·10⁻³ at 200 random points of the box, and all 24 minima sit at the edge of the box, none inside, the six best near one ratio or with one site at the floor of the box and the other two near one ratio; g² does not vanish on the carrier with one site undamped and the others at one ratio, which the floor of the box approaches, and an interior zero close to a face would not be told apart.

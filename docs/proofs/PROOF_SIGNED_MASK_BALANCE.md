# PROOF: the signed balance of the flip masks (F173)

**Status:** Tier 1 derived. Under Z dephasing on every site (any strictly positive rates), for a Hamiltonian that is a real combination of Pauli strings in which every flip set (the X/Y mask of a string) carries exactly one string and the odd-weight diagonal part vanishes, the palindrome holds exactly when the signed Cayley graph of the masks is balanced. Three closed forms follow: two templates on the open chain, one template on the ring, and any number of templates on the ring, which reads N only through whether 2^(v₂(N)) exceeds the least valuation of the masks. Where a flip set carries several strings and its ratio is a constant times a character of x, the palindrome is a symmetric reading of the masks plus the relations read against the read sets and a holonomy on them (Theorem E), with closed forms for one flip shape on the ring and for two on the open chain. With one string per flip set on the torus the verdict is read on the 2-part of the translations, where the signs must be a linear function of the folded masks modulo the differences of their translates (Theorem F); the ring's one number per mask is the case of a cyclic 2-part. Typed as `SignedMaskBalanceClaim`, with live batteries (`inspect --root signedbalance`, `inspect --root torusbalance`) against Theorem 2 walked on the bitstrings.

## The setting

N sites, each dephased along Z at a strictly positive rate. H is a real combination of Pauli strings. A string P has an X/Y mask (the sites carrying X or Y) and a sign

  s(P) = (#Y + #Z) mod 2,

so that X^⊗N·P·X^⊗N = (−1)^s(P)·P; s is the repo's bit_b, the Π²_Z parity. The **flip sets** of H are the masks of its strings; a string with mask 0 is diagonal.

**Assumption (one string per flip set).** No two strings of H have the same nonzero mask, and the odd-weight part of the diagonal of H vanishes (every diagonal string of odd weight has coefficient zero once equal strings are collected).

A **template** is a string of w letters, placed at every window: on the open chain at the starts 0, …, N − w, on the ring at every start, wrapped. Its mask is read as a polynomial m(x) over GF(2), bit j ↦ x^j, with the identity letters at its ends kept inside the window. The placed masks are x^p·m on the chain and x^p·m mod (x^N + 1) on the ring. Two templates whose masks are not translates of each other never place the same mask on the chain, nor on the ring of N ≥ 2·max(w₁, w₂) − 1 sites (a mask in an arc of at most (N + 1)/2 sites has one largest gap, which fixes how it sits on the ring); one template places N distinct masks on the ring of N ≥ 2w − 1 sites (a mask invariant under a rotation by k is a union of cosets of the subgroup generated by gcd(k, N), each spanning at least N − gcd(k, N) + 1 ≥ N/2 + 1 consecutive sites, which a window of w ≤ N/2 + 1/2 sites cannot hold).

## Theorem A (the balance)

Under the assumption, the palindrome holds exactly when **no GF(2) relation among the placed masks uses an odd number of negative strings**: for every subset S of the strings with ⊕_{P∈S} mask(P) = 0, Σ_{P∈S} s(P) is even. Equivalently, the Cayley graph of the masks, each edge signed by its string, is balanced in Harary's sense. Where it fails, no component is good and the far end is zero.

**Proof.** Theorem 2 of the complement connection: the palindrome holds exactly when every component of Γ is good. Take an edge x – x ⊕ m, m the mask of the string P = c·X^m Z^z (up to a phase). Its entries are H_{x,x⊕m} = c·u·(−1)^((x⊕m)·z) and H_{x̄,x̄⊕m} = c·u·(−1)^((x̄⊕m)·z), u the phase of P, so both are nonzero, of modulus |c| (condition (i)), and their ratio is (−1)^|z| = (−1)^s(P), the same at every x (|z| = #Y + #Z). No other string contributes to that edge, by the assumption. So Γ_H is the Cayley graph of the masks on each coset of their span, every edge present at every vertex. Condition (ii) is the vanishing of the odd-weight diagonal part, since X^⊗N·Z^v·X^⊗N = (−1)^|v|·Z^v.

A closed walk from x uses the strings P with multiplicities n_P and closes exactly when ⊕ (n_P mod 2)·mask(P) = 0, that is when ε_P = n_P mod 2 is a relation. Its holonomy is Π (−1)^(n_P·s(P)) = (−1)^(Σ ε_P·s(P)). Conversely every relation ε is walked from every vertex (the strings commute as flips, and each step stays inside Γ_H). So if every relation has even negative count, every cycle has trivial holonomy and every component is good; if one relation has odd negative count, every component carries a cycle of holonomy −1, none is good, and the far end is zero while the near end, the number of components, is not. ∎

**Remark (the same proof, wider).** The proof uses only that the ratio on each flip set is one sign at every x and that its entry never vanishes. A flip set may carry several strings of one sign whose entries never cancel; F115 statement 3 reads its y-parity-mismatched pairs that way. Where strings of different signs share a flip set, the ratio reads bits of x (the bits where their Z letters differ), and even cycles, squares among them, can fail. Theorem E below reads the case where the ratio is a constant times a character; the witness of [SOFTNESS_IS_N_DEPENDENT](../../experiments/SOFTNESS_IS_N_DEPENDENT.md), whose ratios are not, stays outside.

**The two pure cases.**

- **Every sign negative** (every string of odd #Y + #Z): balanced means bipartite, no relation of odd size. This is [F103 §7.4](PROOF_F103_F87_Z2_CUBED_REFINEMENT.md) and F115 statement 3, the diagonal cell's hardness.
- **Every sign positive** (every string Π²_Z-even): every relation is balanced, and X^⊗N commutes with H, a colouring: [F108 Part 1](PROOF_F108_PART1_PI2_EVEN_ALWAYS_PALINDROMIC.md). F110 Aspect A's Klein-homogeneous cells fall on both sides: the cells of even #Y + #Z are all positive, and its (1, 1) cell, odd popcount and odd #Y + #Z, is all negative with odd masks, where every relation uses an even number of strings (an odd number of odd-popcount masks cannot XOR to zero), coloured by Y^⊗N.

A balanced signing in general is a linear φ over GF(2) with φ(mask(P)) = s(P) for every string, and X^⊗N·Z^φ is then a lit string commuting with H. [The colouring page](../../experiments/THE_PALINDROME_AS_A_COLOURING.md) reads the palindrome under uniform Z dephasing as some diagonal phase gauge making H commute with X^⊗N; under the assumption Theorem A says that gauge can be taken to be a ±1 character, the lit string X^⊗N·Z^φ (F103 §7.5's FK in the all-negative case).

## Theorem B (two templates on the open chain)

Let H place two templates t₁, t₂ of widths w₁, w₂, with nonzero masks m₁, m₂ that are not translates of each other and signs s₁, s₂, plus any diagonal part with vanishing odd-weight part, every coefficient nonzero. Let g = gcd(m₁, m₂), q_i = m_i/g and

  c = s₁·q₂(1) + s₂·q₁(1)  (mod 2).

If c = 0, the chain pairs at every N. If c = 1, it pairs exactly for N < N* and breaks for every N ≥ N*, where

  N* = max(w₁ + deg q₂, w₂ + deg q₁).

**Proof.** A relation among the placed masks is a pair of polynomials u₁, u₂ (u_i's coefficient at x^p says whether the placement of t_i at p is used) with u₁·m₁ = u₂·m₂, deg u_i ≤ N − w_i. Dividing by g, u₁·q₁ = u₂·q₂ with gcd(q₁, q₂) = 1, so u₁ = r·q₂ and u₂ = r·q₁ for some polynomial r. The number of negative strings it uses is s₁·|u₁| + s₂·|u₂| ≡ s₁·u₁(1) + s₂·u₂(1) = r(1)·c (mod 2), the weight of a polynomial being its value at 1 modulo 2. If c = 0, every relation is balanced at every N, and Theorem A gives the palindrome. If c = 1, an unbalanced relation needs r(1) = 1. The choice r = 1 fits exactly when deg q₂ ≤ N − w₁ and deg q₁ ≤ N − w₂, that is N ≥ N*. Any r ≠ 0 has deg(r·q_j) = deg r + deg q_j ≥ deg q_j, so it needs N ≥ N* + deg r ≥ N*. Below N* every relation is balanced, from N* on r = 1 is unbalanced. ∎

**With both signs negative** (F115's diagonal cell), c = q₁(1) + q₂(1). The two quotients are coprime, so at most one is divisible by 1 + x; c = 1 exactly when one is and the other is not, that is when v_{1+x}(m₁) ≠ v_{1+x}(m₂): F115 statement 1, the valuation criterion. N* is its window floor, exactly; N ≥ 2k − 2, the floor F115 statement 3 and PROOF_F111 use, bounds it for every pair of width k (both masks are divisible by 1 + x, so deg q_i ≤ k − 2). At k = 4 six of the fourteen valuation-different mask pairs have N* = 5 and eight have N* = 6, the classifier's "six of the fourteen valuation-different mask pairs are already hard at N=5". For IXZX + XIZX, m₁ = x + x³, m₂ = 1 + x³, g = 1 + x, q₁ = x + x², q₂ = 1 + x + x², both of degree 2, both widths 4: N* = 6, soft below and hard from N = 6, as [THE_PALINDROME_CLASSIFIER](../../experiments/THE_PALINDROME_CLASSIFIER.md) records.

**With mixed signs**, outside F115:

- +XIY + XXX: m₁ = 1 + x² (s₁ = 1), m₂ = 1 + x + x² (s₂ = 0), g = 1, c = q₂(1) = 1, N* = 5. The relation r = 1 uses XIY at 0, 1, 2 and XXX at 0, 2: five strings, three of them negative, the only unbalanced relation at N = 5 (no square and no triangle carries it).
- +XIY + XIZ: m₂ = 1 (s₂ = 1), g = 1, c = 1·1 + 1·0 = 1, N* = 5: a triangle, {0, 2} = {0} ⊕ {2}.
- +XIY + XXZ: m₂ = 1 + x, g = 1 + x, q₁ = 1 + x, q₂ = 1, c = 1, N* = 4.
- +XIX + XXX: both signs positive, c = 0, every chain pairs.

On the window family of [the sieve](../../compute/MirrorWorld/Sieve.cs) (three-site templates with a letter at each end, two of them, signs ±) 864 cells are two-template cells with nonzero masks of different shapes: 432 have c = 0 and pair at every N, 432 have c = 1 and are, among the family's 528 mixed chain cells, the ones whose flip sets carry one string each, each pairing below its N* (4 or 5) and broken from it.

## Theorem C (one template on the ring)

Let H place one template t of width w with nonzero mask m and sign s on the ring of N ≥ 2w − 1 sites, plus any diagonal part with vanishing odd-weight part, every coefficient nonzero. Write v = v_{1+x} for the (1 + x)-adic valuation and v₂ for the 2-adic valuation of N. The ring breaks exactly when

  s = 1 and v(m) ≥ 2^(v₂(N)).

**Proof.** A relation is a polynomial u of degree below N with u·m ≡ 0 modulo x^N + 1. With h = gcd(m, x^N + 1), u is a multiple of (x^N + 1)/h, u = r·(x^N + 1)/h with deg r < deg h. Its negative count is s·u(1) = s·r(1)·((x^N + 1)/h)(1) (mod 2). Write N = 2^a·N' with N' odd: x^N + 1 = (x^N' + 1)^(2^a), and x^N' + 1 has 1 as a simple root, so v(x^N + 1) = 2^a. Then ((x^N + 1)/h)(1) = 1 exactly when v(h) = 2^a, and v(h) = min(v(m), 2^a). So an unbalanced relation exists exactly when s = 1 and v(m) ≥ 2^a, and then h ≠ 1 and r = 1 is one (deg h ≥ 1). ∎

The examples, each read by Theorem 2 on the bitstrings at N = 2w − 1 (from 3) to 10:

- XY (and XY + ZZ): m = 1 + x, v = 1, s = 1: breaks at every odd N, pairs at every even N. The even side is the alternating map X⊗Y⊗X… of V_EFFECT_FINE_STRUCTURE.
- XIY: m = (1 + x)², s = 1: breaks unless 4 | N. XIIIY: m = (1 + x)⁴: breaks unless 8 | N (its ring needs N ≥ 9; read at 9 and 10).
- XIIY: m = 1 + x³ = (1 + x)(1 + x + x²), v = 1: breaks at odd N.
- XYX, XXIY: v = 0, never breaks. YY, XIX, YIIIY: s = 0, never breaks.

## Theorem D (any number of templates on the ring)

Let H place templates t₁, …, t_k of widths w_i, with nonzero masks m_i no two of which are translates and signs s_i, on the ring of N ≥ 2·max w_i − 1 sites, plus any diagonal part with vanishing odd-weight part, every coefficient nonzero. Write T = 2^(v₂(N)), v_i = v(m_i) and v₀ = min v_i. The ring breaks exactly when

  T ≤ v₀ and some s_i = 1,  or  T > v₀ and two templates of valuation v₀ differ in sign or a template of greater valuation has s = 1.

In one line: the ring pairs exactly when some σ ∈ GF(2) has s_i = σ·[v_i = v₀ < T] for every i (where T ≤ v₀ every bracket is 0 and every sign must be even; where T > v₀ the templates of least valuation share one sign σ and the others are even). The second condition does not involve N, so the ring reads N only through whether 2^(v₂(N)) exceeds v₀, and each mask only through its valuation. With one template it is Theorem C.

**Proof.** A relation is a vector (u₁, …, u_k) of polynomials modulo x^N + 1 (u_i's coefficient at x^p says whether the placement of t_i at p is used; the placements are distinct masks, by the setting) with Σ u_i·m_i ≡ 0 modulo x^N + 1, and its negative count is Σ s_i·u_i(1) mod 2. As in Theorem C, x^N + 1 = y^T·F with y = 1 + x and F(1) = 1. A relation reduced modulo y^T is a relation in L = GF(2)[x]/(y^T) with the same negative count, since u(1) is read modulo y. Conversely, if ū is a relation in L, then u_i = F·ū_i is one modulo x^N + 1 (Σ F·ū_i·m_i is F times a multiple of y^T), and u_i(1) = ū_i(1). So an unbalanced relation exists on the ring exactly when one exists in L.

Write m_i = y^(v_i)·e_i with e_i(1) = 1; in L, e_i is a unit, and m_i = 0 when v_i ≥ T. If v₀ ≥ T, every m_i is 0 in L, every vector is a relation, and the unit vector at i has negative count s_i. If v₀ < T, choose i₀ with v_{i₀} = v₀; for each j ≠ i₀ the vector ρ_j with u_j = 1, u_{i₀} = y^(v_j − v₀)·e_j·e_{i₀}^(−1) and the other entries 0 is a relation (u_{i₀}·m_{i₀} = y^(v_j)·e_j, which is m_j, also where v_j ≥ T and both are 0), and its negative count is s_j + s_{i₀}·[v_j = v₀], since y^(v_j − v₀) is 1 at x = 1 exactly when v_j = v₀ and a unit is 1 there. For any relation u, u − Σ_{j≠i₀} u_j·ρ_j is a relation whose only entry a sits at i₀, with a·y^(v₀)·e_{i₀} = 0 in L, so y^(T − v₀) divides a and a(1) = 0, T − v₀ being at least 1. Evaluation at 1 is a ring map, so the negative count of u is Σ_{j≠i₀} u_j(1)·(the negative count of ρ_j), and an unbalanced relation exists exactly when some ρ_j is unbalanced: s_j + s_{i₀} odd for a j of valuation v₀, which is two templates of valuation v₀ differing in sign (one of the two then differs from s_{i₀}), or s_j = 1 for a j of greater valuation. ∎

**At odd N** (T = 1) a mask has v ≥ 1 exactly when its weight is even, and the law reads: the ring breaks exactly when an even-weight mask carries an odd sign or two odd-weight masks carry different signs. **Where T > v₀**, the verdict for two templates is the long chain's of Theorem B: q_i(1) = 1 exactly when v_i is the least of the two, so c = s₂ when v₁ < v₂ and c = s₁ + s₂ when v₁ = v₂, which is what the law gives. Where T ≤ v₀, every mask is divisible by (1 + x)^T and the ring breaks on any odd sign.

The examples:

- +XXZ ± XZY, +XXZ ± YZX and their relatives, the 16 cells of the sieve's 24 law-in-N ring cells that carry one string per flip set: XXZ has v = 1 and s = 1, XZY v = 2 and s = 0. At odd N, T = 1 ≤ v₀ = 1 and XXZ's sign is odd, so the ring breaks; at even N, T ≥ 2 > v₀, XXZ alone has the least valuation and XZY, of greater valuation, has s = 0, so it pairs. Exactly at even N, at every N.
- XX + XIY: v = 1, 2 and s = 0, 1, broken on every ring: at T = 1 ≤ v₀ XIY's sign is odd, and from T = 2 on XIY has the greater valuation and s = 1.
- XX + XIIY (v = 1, 1, s = 0, 1) breaks on every ring; XY + XIIY (v = 1, 1, both signs odd) breaks at odd N and pairs at every even N.

**What the ring does not see.** The negative count is read at x = 1, so of the factors of x^N + 1 only 1 + x enters the verdict; the other cyclotomic factors drop out. Where one flip set carries several strings they do not: the window family's X·(X ± Y)·X, whose flip set 1 + x + x² carries two strings, pairs on the ring wherever gcd(1 + x + x², x^N + 1) = 1, read for every such N from N = 8 on by [the stitch's](../../compute/MirrorWorld/Stitch.cs) free chain, and breaks exactly at 3 | N (from N = 6), where 1 + x + x² divides x^N + 1 and does not divide its read set: Corollary E2 below.

## Theorem E (the character connection)

Drop the assumption of one string per flip set. Under Z dephasing on every site let H be a real combination of Pauli strings whose odd-weight diagonal part vanishes, and for each flip set m let g_m(x) = H_{x,x⊕m}, the sum over the strings of mask m. Suppose every g_m is nonzero at every x and its **ratio** is a constant times a character:

  r_m(x) = g_m(x̄)/g_m(x) = ω_m·(−1)^⟨R_m, x⟩,  ⟨R, x⟩ = |R ∧ x| mod 2.

R_m, a set of sites, is the flip set's **read set**. For a relation u among the flip sets (a set of them whose masks XOR to zero), listed in some order m₁, …, m_k (under condition (1) below the order does not matter), write

  hol(u) = Π_t ω_{m_t}·(−1)^⟨R_{m_t}, m₁ ⊕ … ⊕ m_{t−1}⟩.

The palindrome holds exactly when

1. ⟨R_a, b⟩ = ⟨R_b, a⟩ for every two flip sets a, b,
2. ⊕_{m∈u} R_m = 0 for every relation u, and
3. hol(u) = 1 for every u of a basis of the relations.

Where (1) fails no component is good; where (1) holds and (2) fails for some u, the components split into those where u's cycle has trivial holonomy and those where it carries −1. Theorem A is the case R_m = ∅ and ω_m = (−1)^(s_m), where (1) and (2) are empty and hol(u) is the parity of u's negative strings.

**Proof.** Every g_m is nonzero everywhere, so Γ_H has the edge x – x ⊕ m at every x for every flip set, it is its own complement image, and its components are the cosets of the span W of the flip sets. By definition r_m(x)·r_m(x̄) = 1, so ω_m²·(−1)^|R_m| = 1: |r_m| = 1, which is condition (i), and ω_m = ±i exactly when |R_m| is odd. Condition (ii) is the vanishing odd-weight diagonal part. The complement connection's ratio on the edge x → x ⊕ m is 1/r_m(x), so a cycle has trivial holonomy exactly when the product of the r's along it is 1. A closed walk from x is a list of flip sets m₁, …, m_L with ⊕ m_t = 0, and its holonomy is Π_t r_{m_t}(x ⊕ m₁ ⊕ … ⊕ m_{t−1}).

Swapping two adjacent steps a, b taken at y multiplies the holonomy by r_a(y)·r_b(y ⊕ a)/(r_b(y)·r_a(y ⊕ b)) = (−1)^(⟨R_b, a⟩ + ⟨R_a, b⟩), the same at every y. Two consecutive steps along one flip set cancel: H is Hermitian, so g_m(y ⊕ m) is the conjugate of g_m(y), and r_m(y ⊕ m) = 1/r_m(y). If (1) fails for a and b, the square a, b, a, b has holonomy −1 from every vertex, and no component is good. If (1) holds, every closed walk from x has the holonomy h_u(x) of its relation u (the flip sets it uses an odd number of times) walked once in a fixed order, and since walking u and then v is walking u ⊕ v, u ↦ h_u(x) is a homomorphism.

Moving the start by δ multiplies each factor by (−1)^⟨R_{m_t}, δ⟩, so h_u(x ⊕ δ) = h_u(x)·(−1)^⟨S_u, δ⟩ with S_u = ⊕_{m∈u} R_m. By (1), ⟨S_u, m'⟩ = Σ_{m∈u} ⟨R_{m'}, m⟩ = ⟨R_{m'}, ⊕_{m∈u} m⟩ = 0 for every flip set m', so h_u is constant on each component. If S_u ≠ 0, some δ has ⟨S_u, δ⟩ = 1, and the components of x and of x ⊕ δ carry h_u of opposite signs, so one of them is not good. If S_u = 0 for every relation, h_u(x) = h_u(0) = hol(u) at every x, and by the homomorphism it is 1 on every relation exactly when it is 1 on a basis. ∎

Where the palindrome holds, the flat section obeys d(x ⊕ m) = d(x)·ω̄_m·(−1)^⟨R_m, x⟩, so the carrier X^⊗N·D has D the phase of a quadratic form over GF(2), with values in {±1, ±i} where some ω_m is ±i and fixed up to one phase per component: the stitch's ansatz (`compute/MirrorWorld/Stitch.cs`) reads such a D from local factors, and Theorem E reads every cycle, the ones round the ring included.

**Which flip sets have a character ratio.** At x̄ a string's entry gains (−1)^|z|, z the sites where it carries Y or Z, and |z| ≡ s; so one string has ω = (−1)^s and R = ∅, and several strings of one sign have the same ratio, as long as their entries never cancel. Two strings c₁P₁ and c₂P₂ of different signs on one flip set have entries c₁α₁χ₁(x) + c₂α₂χ₂(x), up to a common phase, with χ_k(x) = (−1)^⟨z_k, x⟩, phases α_k, and real c_k; their mirrored entries are ±(c₁α₁χ₁ − c₂α₂χ₂). If α₂/α₁ = ±1, the two moduli differ wherever neither vanishes, and condition (i) fails. If α₂/α₁ = ±i, that is if the numbers of Y differ by an odd number, both moduli are (c₁² + c₂²)^(1/2) at every x, and the ratio takes one of two values according to χ₁χ₂(x); the two are negatives of each other, as a character needs, exactly when c₁² = c₂². Then R = z₁ ⊕ z₂, the sites where the two Z parts differ. X·(X ± Y)·X is such a pair, R its middle site, and so is the flip set {l} of XZ ± ZY, with strings X_l Z_(l+1) and Z_(l−1) Y_l and R = {l − 1, l, l + 1}. These are not all: four strings of mixed signs can combine into a character as well. XII + XIZ + XZI − XZZ is X ⊗ 2·CZ, its entries 2·(−1)^(x₁x₂), with ω = −1 and R = {1, 2}, a real ω beside a nonempty even read set, which two strings never give; and (aX + bY)⊗(cX + dY), four strings on one flip set, has ω = ±1 and R = f.

**Corollary E1 (one flip shape, no relation).** If every flip set is a translate x^p·f of one mask, carrying the translated strings, the masks are independent on the open chain and there is no relation: the palindrome holds exactly when (1) holds, a condition between overlapping windows, the same at every N past them. The free chain of the stitch (`Stitch.FreeEveryN`) checks the same squares at any ratio, exactly in Gaussian integers; E1 is its case of a character ratio. On the ring of N ≥ 2w − 1 sites the same holds wherever gcd(f, x^N + 1) = 1, with (1) read on the ring, across the wrap.

**Corollary E2 (one flip shape on the ring).** On the ring of N ≥ 2w − 1 sites, w the widest template, let every flip set be x^p·f modulo x^N + 1, carrying the rotated strings, with ratio ω·(−1)^⟨x^p·R, x⟩ (R the read set of the flip set at p = 0, read as a polynomial modulo x^N + 1), and let (1) hold. With h = gcd(f, x^N + 1) and g = (x^N + 1)/h, the palindrome holds exactly when

  h = 1, or h divides R and either 1 + x does not divide h or hol(g) = 1.

**Proof.** As in Theorem C, the relations are the multiples r·g, r taken modulo h, so there is none exactly when h = 1. For u = r·g, S_u = r·g·R modulo x^N + 1, zero for every r exactly when g·R ≡ 0 modulo x^N + 1, that is when h divides R. If so, hol is a homomorphism on the relations with hol(x·u) = hol(u), since the rotation by one site carries H to itself and the walk of u from 0 to the walk of x·u from 0. So hol((1 + x)·u) = 1, and hol factors through the relations modulo (1 + x) times them, which r ↦ r·g identifies with GF(2)[x]/(h, 1 + x): zero unless 1 + x divides h, and then hol(r·g) = hol(g)^(r(1)). ∎

The examples, each read by Theorem 2 on the bitstrings:

- X·(X ± Y)·X: f = 1 + x + x², R = x. Its flip sets read each other's middle sites both ways, so (1) holds, and on the chain it pairs at every N (E1). On the ring h = 1 unless 3 | N, where h = f does not divide x: it breaks exactly at 3 | N (from N = 6) and pairs at every other N.
- XZ ± ZY: f = 1, h = 1, no relation, and R_l = {l − 1, l, l + 1} is read symmetrically: it pairs on every ring, the sieve's first residue. On the open chain the flip set at an end carries one string, its read set is empty, and (1) fails against its neighbour: it breaks at every N ≥ 3.
- XXX + YYY: R = f, so h divides R, and 1 + x does not divide f: it pairs on every ring, at 3 | N although relations exist.
- (X − Y)⊗(X + Y) = XX + XY − YX − YY: four strings on the flip set {l, l + 1}, ω = 1 and R = f = 1 + x. Here h = 1 + x at every N, it divides R, and hol(g) is the ordering term alone, (−1)^(N − 2): the ring pairs exactly at even N.
- XIY + XZX: both signs odd, R = ∅ and ω = −1, a flip set with two strings of one sign. h = (1 + x)^min(2, T), and hol(g) = (−1)^|g| is −1 exactly when T ≤ 2: it pairs exactly at 4 | N, Theorem C's (1 + x)² read through E2.

**Corollary E3 (two flip shapes on the open chain).** Let H place two templates t₁, t₂ of widths w₁, w₂, each a real combination of strings of its width on one nonzero mask m_i, the two masks not translates of each other, plus any diagonal part with vanishing odd-weight part, no entry vanishing and every flip set's ratio a constant times a character: ω_i·(−1)^⟨x^p·R_i, x⟩ at the placement p, R_i the read set at p = 0. With g, q_i and N* as in Theorem B, write

  D = q₂·R₁ + q₁·R₂,

and let u₀ be the relation r = 1, which uses t₁ at the placements q₂ and t₂ at the placements q₁. At every N where (1) holds, the chain pairs for N < N*, and for N ≥ N* exactly when D = 0 and hol(u₀) = 1. Where (1) fails, it breaks.

**Proof.** As in Theorem B the relations are r·(q₂, q₁), the first of them fitting at N*. The read sum of r·(q₂, q₁) is r·q₂·R₁ + r·q₁·R₂ = r·D, so (2) holds on every relation exactly when D = 0, GF(2)[x] having no zero divisors. By (1), hol is a homomorphism on the relations (Theorem E), and hol(x·u) = hol(u) wherever both fit, since the shift by one site carries the strings of each placement to the next and the walk of u from 0 to the walk of x·u from 0. The relations x^k·u₀ that fit form a basis, so where D = 0 the chain pairs exactly when hol(u₀) = 1, and u₀ fits at every N ≥ N*. With the read sums zero, h_u(x̄) = h_u(x), while r_m(x̄) = 1/r_m(x) gives h_u(x̄) = 1/h_u(x): hol(u₀) = ±1, and hol(r·u₀) = hol(u₀)^(r(1)). ∎

**On the chain the read sum is a boundary effect.** Condition (1) is read between placed flip sets, so it holds at N exactly when it holds for every pair of placements that fit in N sites, and once it fails it fails at every larger N. Where it holds at every offset (at N ≥ 2·max w − 1), D = 0. The reason in words: by (1) the read sum of a relation is orthogonal to every placed flip set. On the infinite line a polynomial orthogonal to every integer translate of a nonzero mask is zero; the finite chain lacks the translates that would stick out past its ends, and that is where a nonzero read sum survives, as in the second example below. On the ring it can sit in the factors of x^N + 1 that the mask shares, which is what E2's test h | R reads. In Laurent polynomials, with A*(x) = A(1/x), (1) at every offset contains the identities R₁·m₂* = R₂*·m₁ and R₁·m₁* = R₁*·m₁ (beside R₂·m₂* = R₂*·m₂), so R₂·m₁* = R₁*·m₂ and

  g·m₁*·D = m₂·(R₁·m₁*) + m₁·(R₂·m₁*) = m₁·m₂·R₁* + m₁·m₂·R₁* = 0,

and since g·m₁* ≠ 0 and the Laurent polynomials over GF(2) have no zero divisors, D = 0. The same identities show that a template without a read set beside one with a read set fails (1) at some offset (R₂ = 0 gives R₁·m₂* = 0, so R₁ = 0): where (1) holds at every offset, either both templates carry read sets or neither does, and where neither does the verdict is Theorem B's. So D ≠ 0 only brings the break forward to N*, at an N where some pair of placements does not fit yet, and a square breaks the chain at a larger N anyway. Where (1) holds at every offset the verdict is Theorem B's with c read off hol(u₀): the chain pairs at every N if hol(u₀) = 1, and exactly below N* if hol(u₀) = −1. Theorem B is the case R_i = ∅ and ω_i = (−1)^(s_i), where hol(u₀) = (−1)^(s₁·q₂(1) + s₂·q₁(1)) = (−1)^c.

- XXX ± YYY beside the field X + Y on every site: the field's flip set {l} has ω = i and R = {l}, and XXX + YYY has ω = −i and R = f = 1 + x + x² (XXX − YYY: ω = i). Every read set is its own flip set, so (1) holds at every offset. Here g = 1, q₁ = 1, q₂ = f, N* = 3 and D = f·1 + 1·f = 0; u₀ is the field at the three sites of a window and the window's string once, and the string, walked last, reads all three flips before it: hol(u₀) = ω₁³·ω₂·(−1)^⟨f, f⟩ = i³·(∓i)·(−1). With XXX + YYY that is 1, and the chain pairs at every N; with XXX − YYY it is −1, and the chain breaks at every N ≥ 3.
- X·(X + Y)·X beside XIIX: m₂ = 1 + x³ = (1 + x)·f, so g = f, q₁ = 1, q₂ = 1 + x, N* = 4 and D = (1 + x)·x ≠ 0. The chain pairs at N = 3, where XIIX is not placed yet, and breaks at N = 4, where the relation f ⊕ x·f = 1 + x³ first fits, by the read sum (D = {1, 2} is orthogonal to the three flip sets placed there); from N = 5 a square fails as well: the window at 2 reads site 3, which lies in the flip set of XIIX at 0, and XIIX reads nothing back.

## Theorem F (the torus, read on the 2-part of its translations)

Let H place templates t₁, …, t_k at every translate of the L × M torus, sites (p, q) ∈ ℤ_L × ℤ_M, wrapped, each template's letters on distinct sites, with masks m_i (the site (p, q) read as x^p·y^q, a mask as a polynomial over GF(2)) and signs s_i, on a torus where no two placements coincide (so no two masks are translates of each other, and the torus is large enough for each template), every placement its own flip set, plus any diagonal part with vanishing odd-weight part, every coefficient nonzero. Write T₁ = 2^(v₂(L)) and T₂ = 2^(v₂(M)), and **fold** each mask onto the small torus ℤ_T₁ × ℤ_T₂, whose sites are called cells: the cell (c, d) lies in the folded mask m̄_i when an odd number of the sites (p, q) of m_i have p ≡ c mod T₁ and q ≡ d mod T₂. The torus pairs exactly when

  some set φ̄ of cells of the small torus meets every translate of every folded mask m̄_i in a number of cells of the parity s_i,

that is, when a colouring of period (T₁, T₂) exists. Equivalently, no choice a ∈ GF(2)^k with Σ aᵢsᵢ odd has Σ aᵢm̄ᵢ equal to a sum of differences g·m̄_j + m̄_j of translates of the folded masks. In A = GF(2)[x, y]/(x^T₁ + 1, y^T₂ + 1), the group algebra of the small torus, those differences span 𝔪·I, with I the ideal of the folded masks and 𝔪 = (1 + x, 1 + y): the torus pairs exactly when the signs are a linear function of the folded masks in I/𝔪I.

**Proof.** By Theorem A the torus pairs exactly when a balanced signing exists, a set φ of sites with |φ ∩ m| ≡ s for every placement m of sign s (the lit string X^⊗N·Z^φ). The placements are carried onto themselves by every translation, so every translate of such a φ is one as well. Let K be the translations of odd order, the subgroup T₁ℤ_L × T₂ℤ_M of (L/T₁)·(M/T₂) elements. The sum modulo 2 of the |K| translates k + φ meets each placement in |K|·s ≡ s sites modulo 2, so it is again such a set, and it is invariant under K: it is the preimage of a set φ̄ of cells of (ℤ_L × ℤ_M)/K = ℤ_T₁ × ℤ_T₂. For any set S of sites the preimage of φ̄ meets S in as many sites, modulo 2, as φ̄ meets the fold of S, and the fold of a translate of m_i is a translate of m̄_i. So a set φ exists on the torus exactly when φ̄ exists on the small torus, the preimage of a φ̄ being a φ. The second form is linear algebra over GF(2): the system |φ̄ ∩ g·m̄_i| ≡ s_i is solvable exactly when every relation Σ ε_{i,g}·g·m̄_i = 0 among the folded translates has Σ ε_{i,g}·s_i even. A relation gives aᵢ = Σ_g ε_{i,g} with Σ aᵢm̄ᵢ = Σ ε_{i,g}·(g·m̄_i + m̄_i), a sum of differences with the same sign count, and a sum of differences equal to Σ aᵢm̄ᵢ rearranges into a relation with the sign count Σ aᵢsᵢ. In A, (g + 1)·h·m̄ = (gh + 1)·m̄ + (h + 1)·m̄, and 𝔪 is spanned by the g + 1, so the differences span 𝔪·I; the m̄ᵢ span I modulo 𝔪·I, and the second form says that some linear function on I/𝔪I takes the value sᵢ at m̄ᵢ. ∎

**The reading on the cube.** The far element W = X^⊗N·Z^φ carries Y on φ and X everywhere else. It sends the near edge k_Z = 0 to the far edge k_Z = N, so it lies on the far edge itself, and its only freedom is the letter, X or Y, at each site. At one site a letter X of a term clashes with a Y of W, a Y with an X, and a Z with both (Z sits on the quiet edge, W on the far one); a term commutes with W when its clashes are even, |φ ∩ m| + #Y + #Z even, which is the balance, the sign being the parity of the term's k_X. A two-site term reads it plainly: XX and YY ask for the same letter at both ends, XY and YX for different ones. The fold is the sum over the odd translations, an odd number of colourings adding up to a colouring. What the cube does not say is which sign patterns the demands allow together; that is read in I/𝔪I, whose dimension is the number of signs that can be chosen freely.

**The ring, the odd side, and why one number per mask is the ring's.**

- The ring of N sites is the N × 1 torus (T₂ = 1). There, with T = T₁, v_i = v_(1+x)(m_i) and v₀ the least of them as in Theorem D, A = GF(2)[x]/((1 + x)^T), folding is reduction modulo x^T + 1 = (1 + x)^T, and m̄_i = (1 + x)^(v_i)·e_i with e_i(1) = 1, zero where v_i ≥ T. Where v₀ < T, I = (1 + x)^(v₀)·A and 𝔪I = (1 + x)^(v₀+1)·A, so Σ aᵢm̄ᵢ lies in 𝔪I exactly when Σ_{vᵢ = v₀} aᵢ = 0, and the signs must be σ·[vᵢ = v₀]; where v₀ ≥ T, I = 0 and every sign must be even. That is Theorem D's one-liner. The ideals of this A are the powers of 𝔪, a chain, so I/𝔪I has dimension at most one and each mask enters through one number.
- At odd L, T₁ = 1 and the fold forgets x: the torus reads as Theorem D's one-liner on the masks summed along x, m_i(1, y), with their (1 + y)-valuations (infinite where m_i(1, y) = 0) and T = T₂.
- One template whose mask is (1 + x)^a·(1 + y)^b times a unit of A (an element with an odd number of cells), with an odd sign, pairs exactly when T₁ > a and T₂ > b: its folded mask is zero exactly when a ≥ T₁ or b ≥ T₂, and a single nonzero folded mask carries either sign. Each direction asks for its own power of 2.
- Where T₁, T₂ ≥ 2 the small torus is not cyclic and A is no chain: 𝔪 needs two generators, 1 + x and 1 + y, which stay independent modulo 𝔪², so two masks independent modulo 𝔪², such as 1 + x and 1 + y, can carry different signs. Write the 𝔪-adic order of a folded mask for the least a + b over its terms (1 + x)^a·(1 + y)^b; on the ring it is the valuation. The one-liner's form, one sign shared by the masks of least order and even signs for the rest, has no counterpart there: at even L and M the first example below has 1 + x and 1 + y with different signs, and the torus pairs.

The examples, each read by Theorem 2 on the bitstrings on 3 × 3, 3 × 4, 4 × 3 and 4 × 4, and by the law and the balance on every torus with L, M ≥ 3 and at most 64 sites:

- XX across (s = 0) beside XY down (s = 1): m̄₁ = 1 + x, m̄₂ = 1 + y. At odd M, m̄₂ = 0 carries an odd sign, and the torus breaks; at even M the two are independent modulo 𝔪I (or, at odd L, m̄₁ = 0 with an even sign), and it pairs. So it pairs exactly at even M, whatever L: on 3 × 4 it pairs, on 4 × 3 it breaks. The colouring on 4 × 4, rows constant because XX asks for one letter, alternating down because XY asks for two; a walk once round the torus downwards changes the letter M times, which closes only at even M:

  ```
  X X X X
  Y Y Y Y
  X X X X
  Y Y Y Y
  ```

  On the ring two templates of valuation one and different signs break at every N (XX + XIIY, Theorem D); on the torus the two directions keep them apart.
- The plaquette XX/XY (X at (0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1), Y at (1, 1)), mask (1 + x)(1 + y), s = 1: its folded mask is zero exactly when T₁ = 1 or T₂ = 1, so it pairs exactly at even L and M, with Y on the cells of two even coordinates, one in every plaquette:

  ```
  Y X Y X
  X X X X
  Y X Y X
  X X X X
  ```

  The ring's XIY, mask (1 + x)², pairs only at 4 | N: two steps in one direction ask for a period of 4 in it, one step in each direction for a period of 2 in each.
- XY across beside XY down, every sign odd: m̄₁ = 1 + x and m̄₂ = 1 + y, each zero at odd L or odd M with an odd sign, so it pairs exactly at even L and M, every bond asking for different letters, the checkerboard. It is the all-negative case, F103 §7.4's bipartite test on the square torus, and its W, X on one colour and Y on the other, is the far element behind the site-alternating maps of [NON_HEISENBERG_PALINDROME](../../experiments/NON_HEISENBERG_PALINDROME.md) (Result 3, XY and YX terms) and [MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF](MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF.md) (DM bonds), which every bipartite graph carries.
- XX across, XY down and XY on the anti-diagonal (X at (1, 0), Y at (0, 1), mask x + y): the three masks close a triangle, (1 + x) + (1 + y) + (x + y) = 0, on every lattice, so the signs must obey s₃ = s₁ + s₂. With s = 0, 1, 1 it pairs exactly at even M, as without the third template; with XX on the anti-diagonal (s₃ = 0) it breaks on every torus. Three masks of 𝔪-adic order one, and the condition a linear relation among their signs.
- XY/XI and XX/XI, mask 1 + x + y, three sites, a unit of A, as an odd-weight mask is on the ring: XY/XI (odd sign) alone pairs on every torus, and XX/XI beside XY across (1 + x, odd sign, in 𝔪 = 𝔪I) breaks on every torus, as a template of valuation 0 beside one of greater valuation and odd sign breaks every ring.

## Open

- **Which flip sets carry a character ratio.** Strings of one sign share their ratio wherever their entries never cancel, and two strings of different signs are classified (above); for three or more strings of mixed signs only examples are known, X ⊗ 2·CZ and the products (aX + bY)⊗(cX + dY) among them, and a classification is not worked out.
- **Ratios that are no character.** SOFTNESS_IS_N_DEPENDENT's flip set and F116's golden router carry the ratios 1, ρ^(±1) and −1, −ρ^(±1), with SOFTNESS's ρ = (1 − 2i)/(1 + 2i). One flip shape on the open chain is read at any ratio by the stitch's free chain (`Stitch.FreeEveryN`, its squares exact); where relations exist, on the ring or with several shapes, no law of Theorem E's kind reads such ratios.
- **The torus beyond one string per flip set, and open patches.** Theorem F reads the torus with one string per flip set. Theorem E decides each torus with flip sets of a character ratio, and Theorem A each open patch in two dimensions, at one size; closed forms in L and M for them (Theorem E's conditions read through the fold, the analogue of Theorem B with its relations cut by the edges) are not worked out.
- **Absent edges** are read where every flip set is a hop or a one-site field, by Theorem 8 of [the complement connection](PROOF_PALINDROME_COMPLEMENT_CONNECTION.md) (hopping with DM parts and planar fields: every cycle of H's block on at most one excitation a real product); other flip sets with entries that vanish somewhere are not: a pure pairing bond (XX − YY or XY + YX alone, whose entries vanish where the two bits differ) and a hop with Z letters between its sites (XZX + YZY) among them. A pairing term on the same bond as a hop, J(XX + YY) + Δ(XX − YY), has no vanishing entry and falls under Theorems A and E.

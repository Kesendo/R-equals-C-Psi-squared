**Status:** Tier 1 derived. Theorem 5 lets one site go undephased under Heisenberg bonds of nonzero weights on a connected graph with letter fields: the palindrome still holds exactly when a colouring exists, F138's converse a third time, and with two undephased sites, or at other bonds, it fails. ## Setup

N sites, H a real combination of Pauli strings (so H is Hermitian), and on every site l exactly one jump, a Pauli letter A_l, at any strictly positive rate. F158's other fences hold by themselves here: every jump squares to 1, and the dimension 2^N is even. A proper rotation of the letters on each site turns A_l into Z_l (Z fixed; for X the Hadamard, X ↔ Z and Y → −Y; for Y the cyclic X → Y → Z → X); it is conjugation by a one-site unitary, it maps H to another real combination of Pauli strings, and it changes no kernel dimension. So take A_l = Z_l on every site. Write x ∈ {0,1}^N for the computational basis, x̄ for the bitwise complement, and H_xy = ⟨x|H|y⟩.

By F158's corollary, the near kernel 𝒩 is the kernel of W ↦ [H, W] on the strings commuting with every jump, and the far kernel 𝒲 the same on the strings anticommuting with every jump. With every jump a Z, the first span is the diagonal matrices and the second is X^⊗N times the diagonal matrices. So

  𝒩 = {D diagonal : [H, D] = 0},  𝒲 = {X^⊗N·D : D diagonal, H·X^⊗N·D = X^⊗N·D·H}.

Since (X^⊗N H X^⊗N)_xy = H_x̄ȳ, the far condition reads, entry by entry,

  H_x̄ȳ · d_y = d_x · H_xy  for all x, y.  (★)

## Theorem 2 (the complement connection)

Let Γ_H be the hopping graph of H: vertices the bitstrings, an edge x–y (x ≠ y) where H_xy ≠ 0. Let Γ be Γ_H joined with its image under x ↦ x̄. Call a component C of Γ **good** when

- (i) |H_xy| = |H_x̄ȳ| on every edge of C,
- (ii) H_xx = H_x̄x̄ on every vertex of C,
- (iii) the complement connection d_y / d_x = H_xy / H_x̄ȳ has trivial holonomy around every cycle of C (the product of the ratios around the cycle is 1; the word is used in its plain sense, not for the seed's EP holonomy of `SeedHolonomyClaim`).

Then dim 𝒩 is the number of components of Γ_H, dim 𝒲 is the number of good components of Γ, and **the palindrome holds exactly when every component of Γ is good.** Where it holds, the far element X^⊗N·D with D the flat section, normalised to modulus one, is a unitary carrier of the palindrome. The frame theorem's diagonal frame V (VHV† commuting with X^⊗N) gives such a D as D = X^⊗N·V†·X^⊗N·V, that is d_x = v̄_x̄·v_x, which forces d_x·d_x̄ = 1; not every unitary flat section has that form, but a phase per component brings it there (below).

**Proof.** [H, D]_xy = H_xy (d_y − d_x), so D ∈ 𝒩 exactly when d is constant along every edge of Γ_H: dim 𝒩 is the number of its components.

For 𝒲, take an edge x–y of Γ and write a = H_xy, b = H_x̄ȳ, not both zero. (★) at (x, y) and at (y, x), with H Hermitian, gives b·d_y = a·d_x and b̄·d_x = ā·d_y. Multiplying the first by ā and using the second, |a|²·d_x = |b|²·d_x. If |a| ≠ |b|, then d_x = 0, and from whichever of the two equations has a nonzero coefficient on d_y, d_y = 0. If |a| = |b| (so both are nonzero), the two equations are one, d_y = (a/b)·d_x, a unimodular ratio. On the diagonal, (★) at (x, x) is (H_x̄x̄ − H_xx)·d_x = 0. So on a component that is not good, d vanishes at some vertex and, the nonzero ratios carrying zero along, everywhere on it; on a good component d is fixed by its value at one vertex, the flat section, nonzero everywhere and of constant modulus. The solutions are the flat sections, one dimension per good component.

On a good component every edge has a ≠ 0, so it is an edge of Γ_H: a good component of Γ is a single component of Γ_H. Hence #good ≤ #components(Γ) ≤ #components(Γ_H), and the two ends are equal exactly when every component of Γ is good. F158 turns that equality into the palindrome. Where every component is good, the flat sections normalised to modulus one assemble a unitary diagonal D, and X^⊗N·D ∈ 𝒲 is unitary. ∎

**The frame, from a flat section.** For a flat section of modulus one, the product p_x = d_x·d_x̄ is constant on each component C of Γ, since the complement edge x̄–ȳ carries the inverse ratio of x–y, so d_ȳ/d_x̄ = (d_y/d_x)⁻¹ and d_y·d_ȳ = d_x·d_x̄; call the value p_C, and C̄ the complement component. Multiplying the section on C by a phase t_C with t_C·t_C̄ = p_C⁻¹ (a square root of p_C⁻¹ when C = C̄; otherwise one of the two phases is free) keeps it flat and makes d_x·d_x̄ = 1 everywhere, and then v_x = d_x, v_x̄ = 1 on one representative x of each complement pair is a frame. Without the phase the formula can fail: at N = 2 with a Heisenberg bond and Z on both sites, D = diag(1, i, i, 1) on 00, 01, 10, 11 is flat (constant on the popcount shells), X^⊗2·D is a unitary element of the far end, and d_x·d_x̄ = −1 on {01, 10}.

**Colourings are characters.** A lit string is X^⊗N·Z^s up to a phase, so the palindrome is carried by a colouring exactly when some flat section on all of Γ at once is a character, d_x ∝ (−1)^(s·x). Other flat sections are other carriers. On P₃ with XX + YY bonds and jumps Y, Z, X, the far element XXZ + ZYY of the colouring page squares to 2·1, so in the jumps' eigenbasis it is X^⊗N times a diagonal of constant modulus √2 that is a sum of two characters: a flat section that is no character. The canonical case (Heisenberg or XX + YY bonds, no fields, uniform Z) has the popcount shells as components, every one good with the constant section, so both ends count N + 1 per connected graph, F158 §(f2) and F4.

## Theorem 1 (hopping bonds under uniform Z dephasing)

Let H = Σ_{(i,j)∈E} J_ij·(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j) + B + Σ_l h_l·P_l on a connected graph (V, E) with N ≥ 2, every J_ij ≠ 0 and real, B a real diagonal operator with B_x = B_x̄ for every bitstring (any ZZ terms Δ_ij·Z_iZ_j of any weights, zero included, are of this kind; so Heisenberg, XXZ and XY bonds are all covered), each P_l ∈ {X, Y, Z} and each h_l real (a site with h_l = 0 carries no field), and every site dephased along Z. **The palindrome holds exactly when no field lies along Z and the fields do not use both X and Y.** With no field its carriers are the N + 1 of the canonical case; with fields along X alone the carrier is the colouring X^⊗N, with fields along Y alone the colouring Y^⊗N. No condition on the magnitudes or signs of the fields or of the bonds enters. At Heisenberg and XX + YY bonds, the bond sets of F138 that contain hopping, this is the converse of F138 holding exactly in this class: F138's clause 2 (one axis, orthogonal to Z) gives the palindrome, and here nothing else does, at every magnitude, including the coincident magnitudes that break the converse where a site is left undephased.

**Proof.** The components of Γ read the edges of (★). A bond's XX + YY moves one excitation along the bond, x ↦ y with x and y differing in the bits i, j, H_xy = 2J_ij, and the complements differ in the same two bits, H_x̄ȳ = 2J_ij: ratio 1. The diagonal part B is unchanged by x ↦ x̄ by assumption, so it adds nothing to condition (ii); a ZZ term Δ_ij·(−1)^(x_i + x_j) is such a part. An X field at l joins x to x ⊕ e_l with H_xy = H_x̄ȳ = h_l: ratio 1. A Y field at l joins them with H_xy = i·h_l·(−1)^(y_l) and H_x̄ȳ = i·h_l·(−1)^(ȳ_l) = −H_xy: ratio −1. A Z field at l adds h_l·(−1)^(x_l) to the diagonal, so condition (ii) needs Σ_{l: P_l = Z} h_l·(−1)^(x_l) = 0 on every vertex of a component.

The transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate every permutation of the sites. A transposition of two sites whose bits differ is a bond edge of Γ_H, and one whose bits agree fixes x; so writing any permutation that carries x to y (both of one popcount) as a product of edge transpositions and skipping the steps that fix the string gives a path of bond edges from x to y. Each popcount shell is connected, by edges of ratio 1: a flat section is a function f of the popcount alone. An X field then forces f(k + 1) = f(k) and a Y field f(k + 1) = −f(k), for k = 0 … N − 1. With both, f = 0 and 𝒲 = 0, while the fields join the shells into one component of Γ_H, dim 𝒩 = 1: broken. With X fields alone, f is constant, D = 1, the carrier X^⊗N; with Y fields alone, f(k) = (−1)^k f(0), D ∝ Z^⊗N, the carrier X^⊗N Z^⊗N ∝ Y^⊗N; with no transverse field, the shells stay separate components with the constant section on each. A Z field: with a transverse field Γ has one component, containing every x, and Σ_{Z sites} h_l·(−1)^(x_l) vanishing at every x forces every such h_l = 0; with no transverse field the components are the shells, and vanishing on the shell of popcount 0 and on each shell of popcount 1 (x = e_m gives Σ h_l − 2·h_m for Z sites m, the rest unchanged) forces h_m = 0 for every Z site as well. So any Z field breaks the palindrome, F158 §(f5)'s Tr(H·Z_l) ≠ 0 seen as a vertex condition. ∎

## Theorem 3 (Heisenberg bonds under mixed dephasing axes)

Let H = Σ_{(i,j)∈E} J_ij·(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j + Z_iZ_j) + Σ_l (h^X_l·X_l + h^Y_l·Y_l + h^Z_l·Z_l) on a connected graph (V, E) with N ≥ 2, every J_ij ≠ 0 and real, the fields real and of any direction, magnitude and sign, and every site l dephased along one letter a_l (at any rate), with at least two different letters among the a_l. **Then the near end is one-dimensional, and the palindrome holds exactly when exactly two letters occur among the axes and every field lies along the third letter c; the carrier is then the colouring c^⊗N and no other element, and on every broken row the far end is zero, not merely short of the near end.** This is F138's converse holding exactly in a second class: its clause 1 (at most two axes) and clause 2 (one field axis orthogonal to every dephasing axis, which next to two different axes can only be c) give the palindrome, and here nothing else does, at any magnitudes, directions and signs.

**Proof.** Turn every site to Z with one proper rotation per letter, the same rotation on every site dephased along that letter. A term b_i·b_j of a bond flips bit i exactly when b ≠ a_i, since the rotation sends a_i to Z and the other two letters to X and Y up to sign. So H falls into three kinds of pieces:

- a bond whose ends share an axis (a *same-axis bond*) stays a Heisenberg bond, proper rotations keeping XX + YY + ZZ: it swaps the bits i, j where they differ, with H_xy = H_x̄ȳ = 2J_ij (ratio 1, as in Theorem 1), and puts a complement-invariant ZZ on the diagonal;
- a bond whose ends have different axes (a *mixed bond*), c_ij the third letter: c_i·c_j flips both bits, (a_i)_i·(a_i)_j flips j alone, (a_j)_i·(a_j)_j flips i alone, and nothing is diagonal;
- a field's component along a_l is diagonal (Z_l after the turn), its other components flip bit l.

The entries with x ⊕ y = e_i ⊕ e_j for a mixed bond come from c_i·c_j alone (every other bond joins another pair, a field one site), so they are nonzero at every x: a *pair flip*, on every vertex. For a single string P, X^⊗N·P·X^⊗N = ±P, so its ratio H_xy / H_x̄ȳ is one sign σ_ij at every x. The entries with x ⊕ y = e_i have the weight

  w_i(x) = Σ_{k∈K(i)} J_ik·τ_k(x_i)·(−1)^(x_k) + φ_i(x_i),

K(i) the sites joined to i by mixed bonds, τ_k(x_i) ∈ {±1, ±i} the entry of the turned letter at i, and φ_i the entry of the turned transverse field at i. The weight depends on x only through x_i and the bits of K(i).

**Lemma A: the hopping graph Γ_H is connected, so dim 𝒩 = 1.** Pair flips change the popcount by 0 or ±2 and swaps not at all, so both keep its parity. (a) *Each parity class is connected by swaps and pair flips.* Call a *cluster* a component of the same-axis bonds. Inside a cluster the swaps connect any two arrangements of one popcount (Theorem 1's argument), so a vertex is fixed, up to paths, by the vector n of cluster popcounts, 0 ≤ n_A ≤ |A|. The graph is connected and uses two axes, so every cluster meets a mixed bond and the clusters, joined where a mixed bond runs, form a connected graph. A mixed bond between i ∈ A and j ∈ B changes n_A and n_B by one each, and the sign is free where there is room: rearranging A puts a 0 on i whenever n_A < |A| and a 1 whenever n_A > 0, independently of B. Take a spanning tree of the cluster graph and a target m of the same total parity. At a leaf L with neighbour P, step n_L toward m_L, letting n_P move whichever way it has room (every cluster has at least one site, so one way always does), until n_L = m_L; then drop L and repeat. At the last cluster R the parity forces n_R − m_R to be even, and two steps with a neighbour Q, one moving n_Q up and one down (in the order Q's room allows), move n_R by 2 and leave n_Q where it was. (b) *A single flip exists.* Take a mixed bond (i, k). Flipping x_k with every other bit held changes w_i by 2·J_ik·τ_k(x_i)·(−1)^(x_k) ≠ 0, so w_i is nonzero at one of the two vertices, and that edge joins the two parity classes. ∎

**The far end.** Suppose 𝒲 ≠ 0. By Theorem 2 and Lemma A, Γ is one good component, and its flat section d is nonzero at every vertex, of constant modulus, and unique up to a scalar.

(c) *d is a character up to one parity constant.* A pair flip gives d(x ⊕ e_i ⊕ e_j) = σ_ij·d(x) at every x. A swap gives d(x ⊕ e_i ⊕ e_j) = d(x) where x_i ≠ x_j, and also where x_i = x_j: there the step moves n_A by 2, and two pair flips on a mixed bond (k, l) out of the cluster A, with k rearranged to carry the needed bit before each (there is room, since the target's n_A ± 2 lies in [0, |A|]), do the same at the price σ_kl² = 1, x_l flipped twice. So for every bond the ratio d(x ⊕ e_i ⊕ e_j)/d(x) is a sign independent of x. The vectors e_i ⊕ e_j over the bonds of a connected graph span the even-weight vectors, so these signs extend to a homomorphism χ from the even-weight vectors to ±1 with d(x ⊕ v) = χ(v)·d(x), and χ extends to a character (−1)^(s·x) of all of {0,1}^N. Then d(x)·(−1)^(s·x) is unchanged by every even-weight step, a function of the parity of x alone: d(x) = c_π(x)·(−1)^(s·x) with two constants c_0, c_1 of one modulus.

(d) *The parity constant is a sign, c_1 = ±c_0.* Suppose some site i has K(i) nonempty and some site m lies outside {i} ∪ K(i). By (b), w_i(x) ≠ 0 at some x, and w_i does not see x_m, so the single-flip edges at x and at x ⊕ e_m carry the same ratio H_xy / H_x̄ȳ. On the side of d the two ratios are (c_1/c_0)·(−1)^(s_i) and (c_0/c_1)·(−1)^(s_i), one from each parity. Hence (c_1/c_0)² = 1. If no such pair (i, m) exists, then a site on a mixed bond is joined by mixed bonds to every other site, those sites are too, every pair of sites is a mixed bond, and the axes are pairwise different: N = 2, or N = 3 on the triangle with three axes. At N = 2 the frame paragraph's constant does it, applied to d normalised to modulus one: d_x·d_x̄ is the same on the whole component, x and x̄ have one parity at even N, and d_x·d_x̄ = c_π(x)²·(−1)^|s|, so c_0² = c_1². On the triangle with three axes the pair flips close a cycle x → x ⊕ e_1 ⊕ e_2 → x ⊕ e_1 ⊕ e_3 → x whose holonomy is σ_12·σ_23·σ_13, the sign that X^⊗3 gives the product of the three turned pair-flip strings (a3·a3·I)(I·a1·a1)(a2·I·a2), the letters numbered by their sites' axes. That product is (a3·a2) ⊗ (a3·a1) ⊗ (a1·a2), a phase times a1 ⊗ a2 ⊗ a3 = A_1·A_2·A_3, which the turn makes Z⊗Z⊗Z, and X^⊗3 reverses it: holonomy −1, condition (iii) fails, and 𝒲 = 0 against the assumption.

(e) *A character is a colouring.* With c_1 = ±c_0, d is a multiple of one character, D of one string of Z's, and the far element X^⊗N·D is one lit string, which the turn back keeps a single string: a lit string commuting with H. Distinct terms of H are distinct strings, and a string commuting with H must commute with each term separately (the commutator with a string is a sum of distinct strings), so with X_iX_j, Y_iY_j and Z_iZ_j on every bond, which forces the same letter at i and j; the graph being connected, the string is p^⊗N. Lit on every site means p anticommutes with every axis, so p is no axis: exactly two axes occur and p = c. A field component along a letter other than c anticommutes with c^⊗N, so every field lies along c. Conversely c^⊗N is then lit and commutes with every term.

So 𝒲 ≠ 0 forces the rule, the rule gives the colouring c^⊗N, and with dim 𝒩 = 1 the palindrome, dim 𝒲 = dim 𝒩, holds exactly when 𝒲 ≠ 0. ∎

## Theorem 4 (undephased sites that keep a letter)

Let every site of a nonempty set S carry exactly one single-site Pauli jump, and let every site u of the rest F (the undephased sites) keep a letter P_u: every term of H, a real combination of Pauli strings, has I or P_u at u (a site no term touches keeps any letter; take Z). Turn each jump and each kept letter to Z by the proper rotations of the setup. Every term then acts on the bits of F by I or Z, so H is block diagonal in them,

  H = Σ_σ H_σ ⊗ |σ⟩⟨σ|,  σ ∈ {0,1}^F,

each H_σ a Hermitian matrix on the bitstrings x of S; write H̄_σ for its complement image on S alone, (H̄_σ)_xy = (H_σ)_x̄ȳ. For two Hermitian matrices A, B on the bitstrings of S let hom(A, B) be the dimension of {g : B_xy·g_y = g_x·A_xy for all x, y}. **Then**

  **dim ker L = Σ_{σ,τ} hom(H_τ, H_σ),  dim ker(L + 2σ_γ) = Σ_{σ,τ} hom(H_τ, H̄_σ),**

(σ_γ the sum of the rates, to keep it apart from the sector labels), **and each hom(A, B) is the number of good components of the graph whose edges are the nonzero off-diagonal entries of A or of B**: |A_xy| = |B_xy| on every edge, A_xx = B_xx on every vertex, and trivial holonomy of g_y / g_x = A_xy / B_xy. The palindrome holds exactly when the two sums agree. With F empty there is one sector and this is Theorem 2 (hom(H, H) the components of Γ_H, hom(H, H̄) the good components of Γ). The terms with σ = τ are one sector read against itself; the terms with σ ≠ τ, the *cross-sector* terms, pair two sectors, and they are where an undephased site adds elements that no single sector holds.

**Proof.** The strings commuting with every jump are those with I or Z on every site of S and any letter on F, so they span the matrices M = Σ_x |x⟩⟨x| ⊗ m_x with m_x any matrix on the bits of F. The entry of [H, M] at ((x, σ), (y, τ)) is (H_σ)_xy·m_y[σ, τ] − m_x[σ, τ]·(H_τ)_xy, so M lies in the near end exactly when, for every pair (σ, τ), the function g = m[σ, τ] satisfies (H_σ)_xy·g_y = g_x·(H_τ)_xy, and the pairs do not interact: the near end is the direct sum of the spaces hom(H_τ, H_σ). The strings anticommuting with every jump are those with X or Y on every site of S and any letter on F, which span X^⊗S·M with M as before. X^⊗S·M commutes with H exactly when H̄·M = M·H with H̄ = X^⊗S·H·X^⊗S, whose blocks are the H̄_σ, and the same entry-by-entry reading gives the direct sum of the spaces hom(H_τ, H̄_σ). By F158's corollary these are the two ends.

For hom(A, B), take an edge x–y and write a = A_xy, b = B_xy. The equation at (x, y) is b·g_y = a·g_x; at (y, x), with A and B Hermitian, it is b̄·g_x = ā·g_y. This is Theorem 2's pair of equations with (a, b) in place of (H_xy, H_x̄ȳ), and the same three lines give the rest: if |a| ≠ |b| both g_x and g_y vanish, if |a| = |b| the two equations are one, g_y = (a/b)·g_x, and on the diagonal (B_xx − A_xx)·g_x = 0. So g vanishes on every component that is not good and is fixed by one value on each good one. ∎

**What it reaches, and what it does not.** An undephased site keeps a letter when the bonds touching it use one letter there and its field, if any, lies along that letter: the Ising bond with a field along its own letter, or none. A Heisenberg or XX + YY bond touching the site uses two letters or more at it, and then no letter is kept; the blocks of H over the bits of S become matrices of size 2^|F| (2 × 2 for one undephased site) that do not commute, and the reduction above no longer splits into scalar problems; Theorems 5 to 7 read much of that case, and what remains is in the Open list. The proof uses only that the kept direction can be turned to Z by a one-site unitary, so it covers, by the same argument, an undephased site whose terms group into (n·σ)_u times strings on the other sites for a direction n that is no letter; that extension is proven and not gated, since `SectorConnection` reads letters and declines such rows. Gated in MirrorWorld (`EndCount.SectorConnection`, `EndCountTests`): 700 draws of random Pauli Hamiltonians at N = 2 to 5 (a draw of identities only is skipped) with one or two undephased sites, each keeping a random letter, the two sums equal to the end count's two counts on every row where its modular ranks and its lifted, exactly checked vectors meet (more than 450 rows, both verdicts, more than 30 rows with a nonzero cross-sector term, which dropping the cross-sector terms from the sums fails); with no undephased site it returns Theorem 2's reading on 200 random rows; and on the census's own family (ZZ bonds of weight 100, fields 30, 22, 41, the path at N = 3, every pattern of dephasing and fields with at least one site undephased and at least one dephased) it applies to exactly the 1008 rows in which every undephased site's field is absent or Z (864 with one undephased site, 144 with two), equal on each to the end count's upper counts, and to its verdict wherever that is exact, 340 of them palindromic (measured).

## Theorem 5 (Heisenberg bonds with one undephased site)

Let H = Σ_{(i,j)∈E} J_ij·(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j + Z_iZ_j) + Σ_l h_l·P_l on a connected graph with N ≥ 2, every J_ij ≠ 0 and real, letter fields P_l of any real magnitudes (h_l = 0 allowed); let exactly one site u carry no jump and every other site l one single-site letter jump a_l (the axes equal or mixed). **Then the palindrome holds exactly when some letter p is the axis of no dephased site and every nonzero field, the one at u included, lies along p;** its carrier is then the colouring p^⊗N. With two undephased sites this fails: F138's 78 coincident-magnitude rows (the three-site path with equal field magnitudes on its two ends and only the middle site dephased; the triangle has 234 such rows) pair without a colouring, carried by SWAP₀₂·(n·σ)^⊗3. So one free site is the boundary. The bonds must be Heisenberg: at XX + YY or at ZZ bonds a row with one undephased site can pair without a colouring (Scope).

**Proof.** The colouring is lit and commutes with every term, so it carries the palindrome. For the converse, let the palindrome hold. By F158's Lemma 3 the far end holds an invertible W; W† lies there too, so W†W lies in the near end, a commutant and so closed under the functional calculus, and W·|W|⁻¹ is a unitary far element: take W unitary. Write S for the dephased sites, K for the neighbours of u, J_k = J_ku, and for k ∈ S let K(k) be its S-neighbours j with a_j ≠ a_k. Turn every jump to Z by the setup's proper rotations and leave u alone; then W = X^⊗S·D with D = ⊕_x d_x, each d_x a unitary on u, and (★) holds with 2 × 2 blocks: H_x̄ȳ·d_y = d_x·H_xy. In the turned frame a Heisenberg bond between i and j is Σ_c c_i ⊗ O(c)_j with O a proper rotation, its ZZ coefficient â_i·â_j; a letter vector â, ν, f is read in u's frame (ν the field at u, f_k the field at k ∈ S, both as vectors), and ε_j(x) = (−1)^(x_j).

*The blocks.* The diagonal block at x is E(x)·1 + K_x with K_x = (μ(x) + ν)·σ and μ(x) = Σ_{k∈K} J_k·ε_k(x)·â_k: the bond (k, u) contributes its a_k term, diagonal on S, and u's field sits there. The block for a flip of one site k ∈ K (x_k = 0 to 1) is M = ω(x)·1 + 2J_k·τ⁻ and its complement block M' = ω̃·1 + 2J_k·τ⁺, with τ± the raising and lowering operators in the eigenbasis of a_k at u and ω, ω̃ the scalar single-flip entries at k (its transverse field, its mixed S-bonds). Every other off-diagonal block is a scalar: swaps and pair flips inside S (Theorem 3's moves, their entries never zero) and single flips at sites not adjacent to u. On a scalar edge (★) gives d_y ∝ d_x.

*N1, N2 (the diagonal).* (★) at (x, x) says d_x conjugates E(x) + K_x into E(x̄) + K_x̄, with K_x̄ = (−μ(x) + ν)·σ. Traces give E(x) = E(x̄), which forbids a field along a dephased site's own axis (F158 §(f5)). Bloch norms give |μ + ν| = |−μ + ν| at every x, so μ(x)·ν = 0; the ε_k are independent signs, so ν ⊥ â_k for every k ∈ K.

*N3 (the square of a flip).* The two edge equations of a k-flip combine to M'M'†·d_x = d_x·MM†: W commutes with the square of H's k-flip part. Its traceless part is 2J_k·(t_k(x) − J_k·ε_k(x)·â_k)·σ with t_k = f_k^⊥ + b_k(x) and b_k(x) = Σ_{j∈K(k)} J_jk·ε_j(x)·â_j (f_k^⊥ the part of f_k transverse to a_k); at x̄ the same with ε flipped. The rotation of d_x maps both vector pairs, so 2f_k·μ(x) + 2b_k(x)·ν = 0 at every x. Independence of the signs gives f_k ⊥ â_j for every j ∈ K and ν ⊥ â_j for every j ∈ K(k) (the case j ∈ K being N2).

*Lemma C (the hand-off).* If every d_x anticommutes with one letter c at u, then W anticommutes with c_u as well, so W lies in the far end of the row R_c in which u is also dephased along c. W is invertible, so R_c pairs (F158, Lemma 3), and R_c has every site dephased on the same connected graph with the same Heisenberg bonds and letter fields: Theorem 1 (after a global rotation, when R_c has one common axis) or Theorem 3 (mixed axes) gives its rule, and that rule is the rule of the row itself, u's axis c included, since a colouring of R_c is one of R. So it suffices to find such a letter.

*Lemma T (transfer across a flip at k).* Write P_a(x) for "d_x anticommutes with a". On a k-flip, unitarity of both sides forces |ω| = |ω̃|, and the (1,1) and (2,2) entries of the two edge equations show that P_{a_k}(x) holds exactly when P_{a_k}(x ⊕ e_k) does; where ω = ω̃ = 0 the edge equation d_x·τ⁻ = τ⁺·d_y forces the diagonal of d_x in a_k's basis to vanish, P_{a_k} at both ends outright. Across a scalar edge d_y ∝ d_x, so every property that ignores a scalar factor transfers (each one used here does). The transfer of P_{a_k} is local: across flips at k itself and scalar edges, not across flips at another neighbour of u.

**Case ν ≠ 0.** By N2, ν ⊥ â_k, and P_{a_k}(x) forces d_x to be ∝ ν̂·σ (the diagonal equation with d_x a π rotation about an axis n ⊥ â_k gives 2(n·(μ + ν))·n = 2ν). Call that Q(x). Q transfers across every edge, flips at every k ∈ K included, and the graph of K-flips and S-bond moves on the bitstrings of S is connected (every component of G − u meets K; inside a component swaps and pair flips reach every arrangement of one popcount, or of one parity where the component has a mixed bond, and the flips at its sites in K change that), so one vertex with Q suffices. Then every d_x is ∝ ν̂·σ, which commutes with ν's own letter and anticommutes with every letter orthogonal to it; N2 gives ν ⊥ â_k for k ∈ K, so Lemma C applies with c = a_k.
(S1) Some k ∈ K carries no field and no mixed S-bond: ω = ω̃ = 0 on its flips, P_{a_k} holds, Q holds.
(S3) Some scalar edge x–x' has μ(x) ≠ μ(x'): d_x is proportional to d_{x'}, so its rotation sends δ = μ(x) − μ(x') to −δ and μ(x) + ν to −μ(x) + ν; a rotation reversing δ is a π rotation about some n ⊥ δ, and the second condition forces n = ±ν̂: Q(x). Such an edge exists when S ≠ K (the graph being connected, some S-bond joins a site of K to a site outside K, and its swap, where the two bits differ, or its pair flip changes μ), when a mixed S-bond has an end in K (its pair flip changes μ), or when a same-axis S-bond joins i, j ∈ K with J_ui ≠ J_uj (its swap changes μ).
Otherwise u is joined to every site of S, every S-bond is same-axis with equal couplings to u, and every site of S carries a field (not S1). With two axes among S, N3 puts every field of S along the third letter c and N2 puts ν along c: the rule holds. With three axes N2 leaves no direction for ν ≠ 0. With one common axis (Z after the turn) d_x conjugates K_x to K_x̄ and M·K_y·M⁻¹ to M'·K_ȳ·M'⁻¹ along a k-flip, so tr(K_x·M·K_y·M⁻¹) = tr(K_x̄·M'·K_ȳ·M'⁻¹); for ν = h·X and f_k = h_k·Y the difference is −16i·J_k²·h/h_k (symbolic), nonzero, and for f_k ∥ X it vanishes; a rotation about Z carries the other letter orientations to this one. So every field of S lies along ν: the rule holds.

**Case ν = 0.** The diagonal equation reads d_x·(μ·σ) = −(μ·σ)·d_x, so where μ(x) ≠ 0, d_x = e^(iφ)·n_x·σ with n_x ⊥ μ(x), and N3 becomes (n·(t_k − J_k·ε_k·â_k))·n = f_k^⊥ with f_k·b_k = 0.
(ii) Some k ∈ K carries a transverse field: n_x ∥ f_k^⊥ ⊥ â_k wherever μ(x) ≠ 0, so P_{a_k}(x); a vertex with μ(x) = 0 has μ(x ⊕ e_k) = ±2J_k·â_k ≠ 0 and receives P_{a_k} by Lemma T across its k-flip. Lemma C with c = a_k.
(iii) Some k ∈ K carries no field and no mixed S-bond: (S1), P_{a_k} everywhere.
(iv) Otherwise every k ∈ K has a mixed S-bond and no field, so every component C of G − u (each meets K) is connected, fully dephased and has a mixed bond: Theorem 3's Lemma A (a) applies to it, and its swaps and pair flips connect each of its parity classes. A *scalar component* Γ of the bitstrings of S, one parity class per component of G − u, is joined by scalar edges, so n is one vector n_Γ on Γ, orthogonal to every difference of μ inside Γ. If some component has three sites or more, or pairs k with a partner outside K, those differences contain â_k (for any two further sites j and l of the component, bonded to k or not, the flips of the pairs {k, j}, {k, l} and {j, l} stay inside one parity class, which Lemma A's moves connect, and with Δ_ab = −2(J_a·ε_a·â_a + J_b·ε_b·â_b) the μ-difference of the pair flip {a, b} (J_a = 0 for a site outside K) they combine as Δ_kj + Δ_kl − Δ_jl = −4J_k·ε_k·â_k; a partner outside K gives 2J_k·â_k directly), in every scalar component alike, so n_Γ ⊥ â_k on every Γ, and P_{a_k} holds wherever μ ≠ 0 and, by Lemma T, where μ = 0: Lemma C with the one letter c = a_k.
What remains is the *windmill*: G − u a perfect matching of mixed pairs {k, j}, both joined to u, no field anywhere. In one triangle (u, k, j), within a scalar component, n_Γ is orthogonal to three vectors of span{â_k, â_j}: J_k·ε_k·â_k + J_j·ε_j·â_j (the component's μ-difference across the pair), and J_jk·ε_j·â_j − J_k·ε_k·â_k and J_jk·ε_k·â_k − J_j·ε_j·â_j (N3 at k and at j, with no field). Unless all three are parallel, n_Γ is orthogonal to the whole plane, so to â_k and â_j; and all three are parallel exactly when J_k = J_j = −J_jk (their pairwise determinants vanish there and only there). Off that coincidence, in some triangle, n_Γ ⊥ â_k on every Γ, and Lemma C applies with c = a_k. If every triangle sits at its coincidence, each contributes n_Γ ⊥ ε_k·â_k + ε_j·â_j, one vector per triangle within one scalar component, the signs chosen by the component's parity classes. With two letters among the axes the rule holds directly (no fields, and the third letter is free). With three letters and three triangles or more, some scalar component's vectors span ℝ³ (two triangles on one letter pair give â + b̂ and â − b̂ within one scalar component, their parity classes being chosen independently, and a third triangle carries the third letter; three distinct pairs give det[â + b̂, b̂ + ĉ, â + ĉ] = 2), so n_Γ = 0 on it, d_x = 0 there, against unitarity. With exactly two triangles on the letter pairs {a, b} and {b, c} (the bowtie, N = 5), every flip and pair-flip equation is proportional, on both sides, to its own triangle's coupling, so the coupling cancels from it. At a vertex the condition is that d_x anticommutes with μ(x)·σ, where μ(x) = J_1·v_1(x) + J_2·v_2(x) mixes the two triangles; but d is one matrix up to a scalar on each scalar component Γ, and within Γ the signs of v_1 and of v_2 vary independently (each triangle's bits range over its own parity class), so anticommuting with every μ(x)·σ on Γ is anticommuting with v_1·σ and with v_2·σ separately, a condition with no coupling in it; this holds for every element of the far end, unitary or not. So at every nonzero coupling the far end is the solution space of one coupling-free system. The end count's modular rank (an upper bound on the far count, over a prime ≡ 1 mod 4 so that i is an element) finds it zero for all 24 three-letter assignments at every coupling gated (below), and a verifier's independent recomputation over p = 10⁹ + 9 and p = 998244353 agrees, the 12 two-letter assignments giving one far element, as the rule says. So the palindrome fails there, as the rule says: three letters, no free one. ∎

## Theorem 6 (an invertible spanning tree: the connection is a local system)

Let every site of a nonempty set S carry exactly one single-site Pauli jump and the sites of a nonempty set F none, with H a real combination of Pauli strings and no condition on what H does at F. Turn the jumps to Z by the proper rotations of the setup, on S only. With V = (ℂ²)^⊗F write H = Σ_{x,y} |x⟩⟨y| ⊗ H_xy over the bitstrings x, y of S, each H_xy an operator on V and H_yx = H_xy^†, and H̄_xy = H_x̄ȳ, x̄ the complement on S. Theorem 4's first lines use nothing about F, so

  𝒩 = {Σ_x |x⟩⟨x| ⊗ m_x : H_xy·m_y = m_x·H_xy for all x, y},  𝒲 = X^⊗S·{Σ_x |x⟩⟨x| ⊗ d_x : H̄_xy·d_y = d_x·H_xy for all x, y}.

Let Γ be the graph on the bitstrings of S with an edge x–y (x ≠ y) where H_xy ≠ 0, and Γ̄ the same for H̄. Let C be a component of Γ ∪ Γ̄. **Then:**

- (a) if Γ and Γ̄ differ on C, the far end has no invertible element on C;
- (b) otherwise, if C has a spanning tree whose blocks of H and of H̄ are all invertible (the other blocks may be singular), choose a base point x₀ in C and such a tree, reaching each vertex a from a vertex b already reached, and set T_a = H_ab·T_b and T̄_a = H̄_ab·T̄_b with T_{x₀} = T̄_{x₀} = 1; with the generators g_ab = T_a⁻¹·H_ab·T_b over every ordered pair (a, b) in C with a = b or H_ab ≠ 0, and ḡ_ab the same with bars, the near end on C is {m ∈ End V : g·m = m·g for every g} and the far end on C is {d ∈ End V : ḡ·d = d·g for every g};
- (c) **where every component has such a tree, the palindrome holds exactly when on every component the supports agree and the tuples (g_ab) and (ḡ_ab) are simultaneously similar, which holds exactly when tr w(g) = tr w(ḡ) for every word w in the generators;**
- (d) with one undephased site (V = ℂ²), words of length at most 3 suffice, and length 2 does not.

Read with F empty, outside the theorem's hypothesis and where the gate does not run it, the same construction recovers Theorem 2's criterion on the components where the supports agree (every nonzero number being invertible): V = ℂ, a tree edge gives the generator 1, the same edge walked back gives |H_ab|², so the walk-backs agreeing is condition (i) on the tree; a chord gives H_ab·T_b/T_a, which, once |T̄| = |T| along the tree, agreeing is condition (i) on the chord together with the trivial holonomy (iii); a vertex gives H_xx, condition (ii); and that both blocks are nonzero on every edge is (a).

**Proof.** (a) The far equations on one pair are H̄_xy·d_y = d_x·H_xy and H̄_yx·d_x = d_y·H_yx. On an edge of Γ that is not one of Γ̄ the first gives d_x·H_xy = 0 with H_xy ≠ 0, which no invertible d_x allows; an edge of Γ̄ that is not one of Γ does the same at d_y. The far end need not vanish there: a mismatched pair is never a tree edge, and as a singular chord it leaves room for a nonzero d (two dephased sites under Z and one undephased, H = ½(XII + XIZ + XZI + XZZ) + IXI + XXI, one component with a tree of identity blocks and a mismatched pair whose block 2|0⟩⟨0| is singular, reads near 2 and far 1).

(b) Only the tree edges are inverted. On the tree edge from b to a the near equation at (a, b) reads H_ab·m_b = m_a·H_ab, so m_a = H_ab·m_b·H_ab⁻¹ and, along the tree, m_x = T_x·m·T_x⁻¹ with m = m_{x₀}. Substituted, the equation at a pair (a, b) is H_ab·T_b·m·T_b⁻¹ = T_a·m·T_a⁻¹·H_ab, that is g_ab·m = m·g_ab, defined whether H_ab is invertible or not; a pair with H_ab = 0 and a ≠ b gives nothing. The far end is the same with d_x = T̄_x·d·T_x⁻¹: H̄_ab·T̄_b·d·T_b⁻¹ = T̄_a·d·T_a⁻¹·H_ab is ḡ_ab·d = d·g_ab. Different components share no equation.

(c) By F158's Lemma 3 the palindrome holds exactly when 𝒲 holds an invertible element, that is a far solution with every d_x invertible. By (a) that needs the supports to agree on every component, and by (b), the transports being invertible, it is one invertible d with ḡ_ab = d·g_ab·d⁻¹ for every generator on each component: simultaneous similarity. For the traces, let A be the algebra on V generated by the g_ab. Every g_ab is a combination of closed walks at x₀ in H's blocks: T_a⁻¹ = T_a^†·(T_a·T_a^†)⁻¹, and (T_a·T_a^†)⁻¹ is a polynomial in the closed walk T_a·T_a^† at a. Conversely a closed walk at x₀ is a word in the generators, since a step from b to a is T_a·g_ab·T_b⁻¹ and the transports cancel along the walk. The same holds for ḡ. Two finite-dimensional semisimple modules over an algebra over ℂ are isomorphic exactly when their characters agree, and on the free algebra the characters are the traces of words.

(d) The invariants of tuples of 2 × 2 matrices under simultaneous conjugation are generated by the traces of words of length at most 3 (Sibirskii; Procesi), and over ℂ two tuples whose modules are semisimple, so whose orbits are closed, are simultaneously similar exactly when every invariant agrees. ## Theorem 7 (a conserved charge splits the ends into sectors)

Take the setting of Theorem 6: the sites of a nonempty S under single-site Pauli jumps turned to Z, the sites of a nonempty F undephased, H a real combination of Pauli strings, H = Σ_{x,y} |x⟩⟨y| ⊗ H_xy with blocks on V = (ℂ²)^⊗F. Let Q = Σ_l w_l·Z_l with real weights, not all zero, read in that frame (the undephased sites in their own Z basis, after any one-site rotation that turns the letter a charge needs there to Z), and suppose [H, Q] = 0. Write Q(x, f) for its value on the basis state with bitstring x on S and f on F. For a value q let V_{x,q} ⊆ V be spanned by the f with Q(x, f) = q, and W_{x,q} = V_{x̄,q}. **Then** H_xy maps V_{y,q} into V_{x,q} and H̄_xy maps W_{y,q} into W_{x,q}, so H's representation of the hopping graph is a direct sum of charge sectors ρ_q (spaces V_{x,q}) and its complement image of sectors ρ̄_q (spaces W_{x,q}), and

  **dim ker L = Σ_{q,q'} dim Hom(ρ_q', ρ_q),  dim ker(L + 2σ_γ) = Σ_{q,q'} dim Hom(ρ_q', ρ̄_q),**

Hom(α, β) the families f_x : α_x → β_x with β_xy·f_y = f_x·α_xy on every pair. **Where every V_{x,q} has dimension at most one, each Hom is a count:** in the basis vectors, Hom(α, β) is the number of good components of the graph on the bitstrings x where α_x or β_x is nonzero, with an edge where α_xy or β_xy is nonzero, a component being good when both spaces are nonzero at each of its vertices and Theorem 4's three conditions hold (equal moduli on every edge, α_xx = β_xx, trivial holonomy of f_y/f_x = α_xy/β_xy). Theorem 4 is the case of a charge on F alone whose weights separate the bitstrings of F, each kept letter turned to Z: with such weights [H, Q] = 0 says exactly that every undephased site keeps Z.

**Proof.** An entry ⟨x, f|H|y, f'⟩ is nonzero only where Q(x, f) = Q(y, f'), since H commutes with the diagonal Q; so H_xy maps the span of the f' with Q(y, f') = q into the span of the f with Q(x, f) = q, and H̄_xy = H_x̄ȳ maps V_{ȳ,q} = W_{y,q} into V_{x̄,q} = W_{x,q}. The representations are therefore direct sums over q, and both ends, which are End(ρ) and Hom(ρ, ρ̄) by the first lines of Theorems 4 and 6, split as the sums over pairs of sectors, Hom being additive in each argument. In the scalar case f_x is a number at each vertex where both spaces are nonzero and absent where one is. Theorem 2's three lines on each edge give the good-component count where every vertex has both spaces. A vertex with one space only carries no unknown, and the zero it stands for crosses every edge: at an edge x–y with f_x = 0, the equation at (x, y) gives β_xy·f_y = 0 and the one at (y, x) gives f_y·α_yx = 0, and one of β_xy, α_yx = conj(α_xy) is nonzero. So such a component contributes nothing. ∎

**What it reaches.** XXZ bonds J·(XX + YY) + Δ·ZZ of any weights on any graph, Z jumps on the dephased sites and fields along Z conserve the total magnetization (every weight 1); the sector spaces are then spanned by the bitstrings of F of one popcount, of dimension C(|F|, k). With one undephased site every sector space is at most one-dimensional and the count is complete, singular blocks included: the flip 2J·τ that leaves Theorem 6 without a tree becomes a nonzero number between two sectors' basis vectors. With two or more the theorem splits the linear system into smaller ones whose spaces may change dimension from vertex to vertex: a hop between a dephased and an undephased site moves the popcount of F by one, so its block joins spaces of dimensions C(|F|, k) and C(|F|, k ± 1), never invertible when |F| is even, while at odd |F| = 2k + 1 the two middle dimensions agree and the block can be invertible (a star with its hub dephased and three undephased leaves under XX + YY of weights J₁, J₂, J₃ has a hub-flip block of determinant −16·J₁·J₂·J₃ in the middle sector), so the condition Theorem 6 asks of a spanning tree can hold on some sector pairs and fail on others (Theorem 6 is stated for whole components, so reading it per sector pair is an extension not proven here). ## A common carrier for the triangle's whole field line

Let sites (d, u, v) = (0, 1, 2) form a Heisenberg triangle with real nonzero couplings s = J_du, t = J_dv and r = J_uv. Only d is dephased, along Z at a positive rate. Write

  H(h) = H_b + hF,
  H_b = s(XXI + YYI + ZZI) + t(XIX + YIY + ZIZ) + r(IXX + IYY + IZZ),
  F = IXI + IIY,   e₂ = st + sr + tr.

The question here is one **common carrier**, independent of h: [H_b, W] = [F, W] = 0 and {ZII, W} = 0. F158's Lemma 3 turns an invertible such W into a palindrome at every h. The triangle family and the SWAP construction already supply sufficient carriers; the statement below exhausts this common space. The sweep for that exhaustion reached F138 and F158 in the formula registry, their proofs and the colouring page, the typed claims and `f138_converse_failures`, the glossary and CAUGHT_ERRORS, and the Python/C# Confirmations and IBM experiment records. They supplied the two carriers and the invertibility and quantifier fences, but no classification of this common space or hardware test of it.

**Proposition.** For s,t,r ∈ ℝ \ {0}, the common space is exactly ℂ·W(s,t) when (s − t)e₂ = 0, and {0} otherwise, where

  W(s,t) = X_d ⊗ (st²·II + s³·XY + st²·YX − st²·ZZ)
         + Y_d ⊗ (s²t·II + t³·XY + s²t·YX − s²t·ZZ).

Moreover W(s,t)² = (s² + t²)³·I, so every nonzero element of the common space is invertible. Its dimension is one even at the intersection s = t, r = −s/2. This classifies a single W valid for every h, not all carriers at a fixed h.

**Proof.** The jump condition gives W = X_d ⊗ A + Y_d ⊗ B. On (u,v), the commutant of XI + IY has the basis

  (E₀, …, E₅) = (II, XI, IY, XY, YX − ZZ, YZ + ZX).

Each listed element commutes with XI + IY, they are independent, and they exhaust the commutant: XI + IY has eigenvalues 2, 0, −2 with multiplicities 1, 2, 1, giving dimension 1² + 2² + 1² = 6. Write A = Σ a_j E_j and B = Σ b_j E_j. The XYZ, IIZ, IYY, IZI, YZX and IXX coefficients of [H_b,W]/(2i), respectively, give

  r a₁ = 0,   t a₂ = 0,   −t a₅ = 0,   −s b₁ = 0,   −r b₂ = 0,   s b₅ = 0.

All three couplings are nonzero, so only

  W = X_d ⊗ [a·II + b·XY + c·(YX − ZZ)]
    + Y_d ⊗ [d·II + e·XY + f·(YX − ZZ)]

remains. The IXZ, IYZ, IZY, ZIX, ZIY and ZYI coefficients give

  tb − sf = 0,   sc − tf = 0,   tc − se = 0,
  −sc + td = 0,   −ta + se = 0,   −sa + tf = 0.

Thus c = a, d = f = sa/t, e = ta/s and b = s²a/t². Every candidate is (a/st²)W(s,t); only s and t were divided by, so no exceptional nonzero-coupling case has been discarded. Direct multiplication now gives

  [H_b,W(s,t)] = 2i(s − t)e₂·[s(XIZ − XZI) − t(YIZ − YZI)],
  [F,W(s,t)] = 0,   {ZII,W(s,t)} = 0,
  W(s,t)² = (s² + t²)³·I.

The four strings in the bracket are independent, with nonzero s and t. Hence a nonzero common solution exists exactly when (s − t)e₂ = 0; its square proves invertibility for real s,t. ∎

At s = t this is the SWAP carrier: W(s,s)/(2√2 s³) = SWAP_uv·[(X + Y)/√2]^⊗3. Off that branch e₂ = 0 is the reciprocal-coupling family below. The proposition does not imply that a fixed H(h) off the two branches fails to pair: at h = 0, for example, XXX is a carrier at every coupling.

**Exact gate:** [`triangle_common_carrier_gate.py`](../../simulations/triangle_common_carrier_gate.py), run with `python simulations/triangle_common_carrier_gate.py`. It reconstructs the full 32-coordinate field constraint by dense matrices and the 12 remaining bond commutators by sparse Pauli multiplication, comparing every column to the dense route. An 11 × 11 minor is exactly −r²s³t⁶, nonzero throughout the stated domain; the remaining column equations give the displayed factorization. The square and commutators are symbolic identities. Eight full dense common-kernel controls include both branches, their intersection, s = −t and off-locus points; changing a coupling, unequal fields and removing a carrier term break the same equations. The h = 0 control guards the distinction between common and fixed-field carriers.

## The triangle's palindromic locus at N = 3

The proposition above fixes the field line and asks for one carrier along it. This section fixes nothing: the same Heisenberg triangle with the jump Z on d alone, a single-letter field of free real magnitude on every site, f_l = h_l·σ_{w_l} for a word w ∈ {X, Y, Z}³ (a site without a field is h_l = 0 inside any word), and real couplings J_du, J_dv, J_uv of which at most one is zero, so the graph is connected (the path among them, with d at an end or in the middle). Read f_l as a vector in ℝ³.

The sweep for it went to F138 and F158 with their proofs, the colouring page and the rest of `experiments/`, the proposition above, `f138_converse_failures` in the OpenArcs registry, `PalindromeComplementConnectionClaim` and the `twoendstrings` witness, the Confirmations registry, the glossary and CAUGHT_ERRORS. Checked for adjacency: the word ZZZ conserves the total magnetization, so its rows are Theorem 7's with that charge, and the floor in step 5 of the proof below is that theorem's near end holding the functions of the charge; F138 (b)'s 78 rows on the path, with only the middle site dephased, are class B below at J_uv = 0 once their jump is turned to Z, their element SWAP·(n·σ)^⊗3 being B's; the common-carrier proposition above is the sufficiency of class C below; and the CAUGHT_ERRORS entry on a measured closure read past its one bond weight is the failure this exhaustion closes, since C needs mixed coupling signs.

**Proposition (exact, computer-assisted).** A row of this family is palindromic exactly when it lies in one of

- **A**, a colouring: every nonzero field lies along one letter p ∈ {X, Y};
- **B**, the u ↔ v site symmetry: J_du = J_dv, and a π rotation R_n about an axis n ⊥ z has R_n f_d = f_d and R_n f_u = f_v;
- **C**, the reciprocal locus: e₂ = J_duJ_dv + J_duJ_uv + J_dvJ_uv = 0, f_d = 0, f_u and f_v ⊥ z, |f_u| = |f_v|.

The three overlap (a row of C at h = 0, or with f_u ∥ f_v, is coloured) and are not of one kind: A and B are named by their carriers, C by a locus in the parameters, so the statement covers the palindromic locus rather than sorting carriers. It speaks of rows at a fixed field, the companion of the proposition above, which speaks of one carrier for every h.

**Sufficiency.** By F158's Lemma 3 and its theorem an invertible W with [H, W] = 0 and {Z_d, W} = 0 makes the row pair. In A it is p^⊗3 (F138). In B it is SWAP_uv·(n·σ)^⊗3: the rotation fixes every Heisenberg bond and turns Z_d into −Z_d because n ⊥ z, the swap exchanges J_du and J_dv, which are equal, and the two together carry the fields to themselves. In C all three couplings are nonzero, since with one of them zero e₂ is the product of the other two. Fields with f_u ∥ f_v are coloured; otherwise a global rotation (the π rotation about x, the quarter turn about z), which fixes the bonds and sends Z_d to ±Z_d, brings the field to h(X_u + Y_v), where W(s, t) of the proposition above carries it.

**Necessity.** Write a *region* as {E = 0, every q ∈ 𝒰 nonzero}, for a list E of polynomial equations and a list 𝒰 of factors recorded nonzero, in the six parameters.

1. *The ends.* By Lemma 1's second corollary in F158, near and far are the kernels of W ↦ [H, W] on the Pauli strings whose d-letter is in {I, Z} and in {X, Y}. Each end is closed under the adjoint and so spanned by Hermitian elements, real combinations of strings, and [H, W] = 0 becomes a real linear system each of whose entries is, up to the common factor 2i, ± one of J_du, J_dv, J_uv, h_d, h_u, h_v. A row pairs exactly when near = far ≥ 1.
2. *Ten words.* The swap u ↔ v (with J_du ↔ J_dv) and the quarter turn about z preserve the family, the jump and the three classes, and leave the 27 words in ten orbits: XXX, XXY, XXZ, XYY, XYZ, XZZ, ZXX, ZXY, ZXZ, ZZZ.
3. *Two charts.* Every entry is linear and homogeneous in the six parameters, so neither count changes under a common nonzero scaling, and neither does membership in A, B or C, whose conditions are homogeneous too. A connected row has J_du ≠ 0, or J_du = 0 and J_dv ≠ 0, so the charts J_du = 1 and (J_du = 0, J_dv = 1) cover every connected row up to scale; for XXY, where the case splits in that chart grow far larger, the mirrored pair J_dv = 1 and (J_dv = 0, J_du = 1) does the same.
4. *Elimination.* Gaussian elimination of the far system with case splits. A pivot is a nonzero constant or a polynomial all of whose factors are in 𝒰; otherwise the vanishing of each factor and the case where none vanishes are separate branches. Rows are divided only by factors in 𝒰, and a branch is dropped only when 1 lies in its ideal or a factor in 𝒰 does. The finished branches are the *far leaves*: regions that together contain every connected row, each carrying its exact far count k.
5. *Decisions.* For a word, each class becomes finitely many ideals: A one per letter p, B one per axis among x, y, x + y and x − y (whenever some axis n ⊥ z satisfies B, one of these does: a nonzero field off z pins n to one of them, zero fields and z fields on u and v with h_v = −h_u admit every n, and a nonzero z field on d admits none), C one. A region lies inside a class when every generator g of one of its ideals vanishes on it, checked with a new variable ξ as 1 ∈ ⟨E, 1 − ξ·∏𝒰·g⟩. A far leaf with k ≥ 1 is decided on its own region, either as lying inside a class (the first case) or as holding no connected palindromic real row (the other three), when
   - it lies inside A, B or C;
   - two couplings vanish on it;
   - it has no real point, shown by an equation that is a sum of monomials with every exponent even and every coefficient of one sign, so that it vanishes only where each monomial does, one of them built of variables in 𝒰, or by a sum of squares Σ c_i·p_i² (c_i > 0) recorded for the region, which vanishes on it and has a term p_k each of whose factors is in 𝒰 or cannot vanish there (1 ∈ ⟨E, factor⟩), both checked on use;
   - or the word is ZZZ and k < 4: there I, M, M² and M³ with M = Z_d + Z_u + Z_v (eigenvalues ±1, ±3) commute with H and with Z_d at every parameter point, so near ≥ 4 > k.

   A leaf decided by none of these has its near system eliminated on its region, and every subregion with near = k is palindromic and is decided the same way; one decided by none would be reported open.

Over the ten words the far elimination gives 1,305 leaves, 938 with k ≥ 1. A leaf can be empty over ℂ, since a branch is dropped only when 1 or a factor in 𝒰 lies in its ideal, not when its zero set merely lies inside the zeros of 𝒰, so these count branches, which may also overlap, and an empty leaf lies inside every class vacuously. Of the 938, 575 lie inside A, 19 inside B, 10 inside C, 60 are disconnected, 9 have no real point (4 of them by a recorded sum of squares), 157 fall to ZZZ's floor, and on the remaining 108 the near count never equals k. No region is open, and no palindromic row needed the near step: every one lies in a far leaf decided as a whole. ∎

The four recorded sums of squares sit on regions whose own equations hide the square. On one of them (XXY in its J_dv = 1 chart) the equations force h_v² = 4J_du²(J_du + 1)(J_du² + 1)/(J_du − 1)³ and h_u² = −4(J_du + 1)(J_du² + 1)/(J_du − 1)³, so h_v² = −J_du²·h_u², and with J_du, h_u and h_v recorded nonzero one of the two squares would be negative.

## Blind dephased seats, and palindromes beyond colourings and site symmetries

The sweep for this section went to F157 (the blind seat) with [its proof](PROOF_BLIND_SEAT_SPAN_AND_NODE_LEMMA.md) and [THE_BLIND_SITE](../../experiments/THE_BLIND_SITE.md), the live witnesses `inspect --root blind`, `blindlocus` and `nodepair` and MirrorWorld's `BlindSeat`, [F152](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f152) (the vacuum-block generator is the graph Laplacian), [EXCHANGE_FROM_V_EFFECT](../../experiments/EXCHANGE_FROM_V_EFFECT.md) and the rest of `experiments/`, [PROOF_UNIFORM_LAW](PROOF_UNIFORM_LAW.md), the OpenArcs registry, the Confirmations registry, the Claim graph, the glossary and CAUGHT_ERRORS. F157 and F152 name the objects below and the Confirmations hold no hardware row on them. Checked for adjacency: F157's span identity is F158's near end in the one-watched-seat setting restricted to one excitation (the tie this document's first section already records), but no earlier document ties blindness to the far end or to the pairing; EXCHANGE_FROM_V_EFFECT has the selection rule of the family's bridge term below (both pairs must flip between singlet and triplet at once to be reached), in another setting; and the N = 7 construction below is a change of basis in a triangle's spin multiplicity space, the decomposition of [PROOF_UNIFORM_LAW, Layer A](PROOF_UNIFORM_LAW.md#layer-a-any-su2-invariant-h), there used for one Hamiltonian and here to relate two.

**The identity.** A Heisenberg bond J·(XX + YY + ZZ) acts on the one-excitation sector as J·(1 − 2·𝓛_edge), so there the bond part is a constant minus 2·𝓛_J, 𝓛_J the J-weighted graph Laplacian (F152's derivation holds for signed weights as well). F157 calls a seat j blind when the Krylov space of e_j under H on the one-excitation sector, equivalently under 𝓛_J, is smaller than N, which happens exactly when some eigenvector of 𝓛_J vanishes at j. On the triangle with s = J_du, t = J_dv, r = J_uv the Krylov matrix of the dephased seat d has

  det [e_d, 𝓛_J·e_d, 𝓛_J²·e_d] = −(s − t)·e₂,

exactly the factor (s − t)·e₂ of the common-carrier proposition. The two factors are two different ways for d to be blind. At s = t the swap of u and v fixes d, and its antisymmetric eigenvector (0, 1, −1) vanishes at d: this blindness belongs to d and comes from the symmetry, and it is B's coupling condition. At e₂ = 0, e₂ being the Laplacian's spanning-tree sum (the matrix-tree theorem), 𝓛_J drops to rank one: the null space is two-dimensional, so it holds a vector vanishing at any chosen seat and every seat is blind, d among them; this is the doublet that meets the quartet, C's coupling condition. So at N = 3 every palindrome that is not a colouring sits on a blind dephased seat, by a site symmetry fixing it or by a rank drop of the signed Laplacian; N = 7 below has a palindrome reached by neither, its dephased seat blind all the same.

**Proposition (N = 4, exact, computer-assisted).** With the jump Z on site 0 alone, the rows

  H = e·σ₁·σ₂ + a·(σ₀ − σ₃)·(σ₁ − σ₂) − h·X₁ + h·X₂ + g·Y₃

(J₀₁ = a, J₀₂ = −a, J₁₃ = −a, J₂₃ = a, J₁₂ = e, J₀₃ = 0, σ_l the vector of Pauli matrices on site l) pair for every real a ≠ 0, g ≠ 0 and every real e and h, with near = far = 1. For h ≠ 0 none of them is a colouring (X and Y fields both present) or carries a site symmetry fixing site 0 (J₀₁, J₀₂, J₀₃ are a, −a, 0, all distinct); at h = 0 the only field is g·Y₃ and Y^⊗4 colours the row. *Proof:* the exact elimination of the earlier section, reimplemented on the family in the parameters (a, e, h, g), leaves three leaves with a and g nonzero: the generic one (a, g, h and 4a² + h² recorded nonzero), of far count 1 and near count 1 on every sub-leaf; h = 0, of far and near count 1; and 4a² + h² = 0, which has no real point. ∎

**Why it pairs: the carrier in closed form.** In the basis of the pair (1, 2) given by |s⟩ = (|01⟩ − |10⟩)/√2 and |α⟩ = σ₁^α|s⟩ (α = x, y, z), with operators on the external spins (0, 3) as entries and K_α = a·(σ₀^α − σ₃^α) − h·δ_αx, the Hamiltonian is a 4 × 4 block matrix: −3e + g·Y₃ on the singlet, e + g·Y₃ on each triplet state, and 2K_α joining the singlet to |α⟩, the difference σ₁ − σ₂ having no part within the triplet. With d = 4a² + h²,

  S = h·X₀ + a·Y₀Y₃,  A = [h³·X₀ + a(h² − 4a²)·Y₀Y₃]/d,  B = [2ah²·X₀Y₃ + 4a²h·Y₀]/d,
  C = i[−4a²h·X₀Y₃ + 2ah²·Y₀]/d,  E = i[2ah²·X₀ + 4a²h·Y₀Y₃]/d,

the block matrix W with rows (S, 0, 0, 0), (0, A, B, C), (0, B, −A, E), (0, −C, −E, A) has [H, W] = 0, {Z₀, W} = 0, W† = W and W² = (a² + h²)·1, so W/√(a² + h²) is a unitary involution, invertible at every h for a ≠ 0 (at h = 0 it is −a·Y^⊗4). It does not depend on e or g. It is found by asking for a map on the two external spins that extends through the dimer's three singlet-to-triplet channels: the singlet block must commute with Σ_α K_α² = (6a² + h²) − 2a²·σ₀·σ₃ − 2ah(X₀ − X₃), which inside the span of X₀, X₀Y₃, Y₀, Y₀Y₃ fixes S up to a factor, and the triplet block then intertwines both ways, Σ_i K_i·T_ij = S·K_j and Σ_i T_ji·K_i = K_j·S. A level crossing alone supplies no such equations. The carrier depends on h: on the line g = h at a = −1, e = 2 no single element carries the whole line, unlike W(s, t) above. The family takes the second way to blindness: 𝓛_J has the two-dimensional null space spanned by (1, 1, 1, 1) and (0, 1, 1, 0), so every seat is blind, and an S = 1 triplet sits at the S = 2 quintet's level, the N = 4 face of the doublet meeting the quartet. The bridge a·(σ₀ − σ₃)·(σ₁ − σ₂) reaches the pair (1, 2) only through the operator that turns its singlet into its triplet, and the fields on 1 and 2 act through the same difference. The rank drop and the crossing hold for every J₀₃, while J₀₃ ≠ 0 and fields on the pair that are not opposite break the pairing at every control the gate reads, so the rank drop is not enough.

**At N = 7, a palindrome with no site symmetry and no rank drop.** Put a watched centre 0 and two triangles A = (1, 2, 3) and B = (4, 5, 6), couple 0 to all six with one c > 0, and give the triangles the couplings (J₁₂, J₁₃, J₂₃) = (1, 2, 3) on A and (11/7, 22/7, 9/7) on B, no bonds between them, fields h(X₁ + X₂ + X₃) + h(Y₄ + Y₅ + Y₆) with h ≠ 0. Every coupling is positive, so the Laplacian has rank 6 and drops nothing; 0 is the only seat of degree six and the two triangles are scalene with different weight sets, so no site symmetry exists; X and Y fields together rule out a colouring. Yet it pairs. On a triangle write Q_ij = σ_i·σ_j, P = (Q₁₂ + Q₁₃ + Q₂₃ + 3)/6 and D = (−10Q₁₂ + 8Q₁₃ + 2Q₂₃)/7; then P² = P, PD = DP = 0, D² = (144/7)(1 − P), and T = P + D (inverse P + (7/144)D) obeys H_B·T = T·H_A for H_A = Q₁₂ + 2Q₁₃ + 3Q₂₃, H_B = (11Q₁₂ + 22Q₁₃ + 9Q₂₃)/7, and commutes with every collective spin component of the triangle. With V = 1 on (0, 1, 2, 3) and T on (4, 5, 6), H = V·H_equal·V⁻¹, where H_equal has A's couplings on both triangles and the carrier W₀ = SWAP_(123↔456)·(X + Y)^⊗7; so W = V·W₀·V⁻¹ has [H, W] = 0, {Z₀, W} = 0 and W² = 128·1. (A unitary choice exists too, P + (√7/12)·D.) The two triangles are equivalent as spin representations carrying their Hamiltonians while not as weighted graphs, the multiplicity-space freedom of PROOF_UNIFORM_LAW's Layer A. Seat 0 is blind all the same, blind(0) = 5: the centre reaches only the uniform vector on the six outer seats, which each triangle's Laplacian annihilates, for any triangle weights. That blindness is not made by T: on the vectors constant on each triangle the Laplacians of H and of H_equal agree, so seat 0 is blind exactly as in the site-symmetric partner H_equal, whose swap of A and B fixes 0; it sees each triangle only through its collective spin. Joining further leaves to 0 alone, with distinct positive couplings and no fields, extends this to every N ≥ 7 by the same argument (argued, the gate reads N = 7).

**At N = 5, one route closed.** The N = 4 carrier does not survive a fifth spin as a product: for a, h ≠ 0, attaching spin 4 by any isotropic bonds b_j·σ_j·σ₄ and any one-site operator F₄, the commutator [H₅, W₄ ⊗ Q] = 0 with Q ≠ 0 forces every b_j = 0. A 19 × 19 minor of the relaxed linear system (the sixteen products b_j·q_k and three coefficients of [F₄, Q] taken as unknowns) is 256·t³⁴·(t² + 2)² with t = h/a, nonzero for every real t ≠ 0. A five-spin carrier that entangles the new spin is not excluded, and no N = 5 family is known.

## Scope, and what would falsify it

Theorems 1 to 3: every site dephased by exactly one single-site Pauli jump. Theorem 4: the dephased sites so, and every undephased site keeping a letter that H conserves. Theorem 5: Heisenberg bonds of nonzero weights on a connected graph, letter fields, exactly one undephased site; a counterexample would be a palindrome there without a colouring. It does not extend: with two undephased sites the SWAP rows pair without a colouring, and at other bonds one undephased site is enough to pair without one, on one XX + YY bond (the colouring page's defect cascade, pinned) and on the ZZ bonds of the anticommuting-sum census, whose sums include rows with one undephased site. A site with two anticommuting jumps lies outside all seven, and an undephased site that keeps no letter lies outside Theorem 4. Theorem 6: the dephased sites so, any undephased sites, and on every component of Γ ∪ Γ̄ a spanning tree whose blocks of H and of H̄ are invertible; a counterexample would be a row in this class where the generator counts and the end count's exact counts disagree, or a palindrome whose generator tuples are not simultaneously similar. A component without such a tree lies outside it. Theorem 7: the dephased sites so, any undephased sites, and H conserving a nonconstant diagonal charge Σ w_l·Z_l of the frame; a counterexample would be a row where the sector sums and the end count's exact counts disagree. Undephased sites double the spans and are where the site symmetries and the conditioned sector sums of the colouring page live. Theorem 2 takes H a real combination of Pauli strings with no further condition, every site dephased; a counterexample would be a row where the end count's exact verdict and the criterion disagree. Theorem 1 needs hopping bonds J(XX + YY), every J nonzero, with a complement-invariant diagonal part, on a connected graph, Z on every site (at any rates), and fields along letters (a field along X + Y on every site, which F138's clause 2 admits, lies outside it); its counterexample would be a palindrome in its class with both X and Y fields. Theorem 3 needs isotropic Heisenberg bonds, every J nonzero, on a connected graph, at least two letters among the axes; its counterexample would be a palindrome under three axes, or under two with a field component off the third letter. Anisotropic bonds (XXZ, XY) under mixed axes lie outside it: a same-axis bond whose axis is not the anisotropy letter turns into a swap plus a pair flip, so step (c)'s ratio 1 fails, step (e) uses all three letters on every bond, and at XY bonds a mixed bond whose third letter is Z has no pair flip. A disconnected graph lies outside it as well: two bonds (0, 1) and (2, 3) with axes X, Y, Y, Z, Z fields on sites 0 and 1 and X fields on 2 and 3 use three axes and pair, carried by ZZXX (pinned). The triangle's palindromic locus at N = 3 needs Heisenberg bonds on the triangle with at most one coupling zero, the jump Z on one site alone and letter fields; its counterexample would be such a row that pairs outside A ∪ B ∪ C, or a row of A, B or C that does not pair. The N = 4 proposition's counterexample would be a real row of the family with a, g ≠ 0 that does not pair; the conjecture's would be a palindrome under Heisenberg bonds with letter fields and one dephased seat, without a colouring, whose dephased seat is not blind.

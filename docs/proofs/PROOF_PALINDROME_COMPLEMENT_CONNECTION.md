# PROOF: With Every Site Dephased, the Palindrome Is a Flat Connection

**Status:** Tier 1 derived. Theorem 2 holds for any Hermitian H that is a real combination of Pauli strings, with exactly one single-site Pauli jump on every site, any strictly positive rates, any N; Theorem 1 is its corollary for Heisenberg bonds of any nonzero weights on a connected graph with every site dephased along Z (at any rates), and it is the converse of [F138](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f138) holding exactly in that class, at every field magnitude. Both are gated exactly in MirrorWorld (`EndCount.ComplementConnection`, `EndCountTests`) against the end count's own verdict.

## Setup

N sites, H a real combination of Pauli strings (so H is Hermitian), and on every site l exactly one jump, a Pauli letter A_l, at any strictly positive rate. F158's other fences hold by themselves here: every jump squares to 1, and the dimension 2^N is even. A proper rotation of the letters on each site turns A_l into Z_l (Z fixed; for X the Hadamard, X ↔ Z and Y → −Y; for Y the cyclic X → Y → Z → X); it is conjugation by a one-site unitary, it maps H to another real combination of Pauli strings, and it changes no kernel dimension. So take A_l = Z_l on every site. Write x ∈ {0,1}^N for the computational basis, x̄ for the bitwise complement, and H_xy = ⟨x|H|y⟩.

By F158's corollary, the near kernel 𝒩 is the kernel of W ↦ [H, W] on the strings commuting with every jump, and the far kernel 𝒲 the same on the strings anticommuting with every jump. With every jump a Z, the first span is the diagonal matrices and the second is X^⊗N times the diagonal matrices. So

  𝒩 = {D diagonal : [H, D] = 0},  𝒲 = {X^⊗N·D : D diagonal, H·X^⊗N·D = X^⊗N·D·H}.

Since (X^⊗N H X^⊗N)_xy = H_x̄ȳ, the far condition reads, entry by entry,

  H_x̄ȳ · d_y = d_x · H_xy  for all x, y.  (★)

## Theorem 2 (the complement connection)

Let Γ_H be the hopping graph of H: vertices the bitstrings, an edge x–y (x ≠ y) where H_xy ≠ 0. Let Γ be Γ_H joined with its image under x ↦ x̄. Call a component C of Γ **good** when

- (i) |H_xy| = |H_x̄ȳ| on every edge of C,
- (ii) H_xx = H_x̄x̄ on every vertex of C,
- (iii) the complement connection d_y / d_x = H_xy / H_x̄ȳ has trivial holonomy around every cycle of C (the product of the ratios around the cycle is 1; the word is used in its plain sense, not for the seed's EP holonomy of `SeedHolonomyClaim`).

Then dim 𝒩 is the number of components of Γ_H, dim 𝒲 is the number of good components of Γ, and **the palindrome holds exactly when every component of Γ is good.** Where it holds, the far element X^⊗N·D with D the flat section, normalised to modulus one, is a unitary carrier of the palindrome. It meets the frame theorem's diagonal frame V (VHV† commuting with X^⊗N) through D = X^⊗N·V†·X^⊗N·V, that is d_x = v̄_x̄·v_x.

**Proof.** [H, D]_xy = H_xy (d_y − d_x), so D ∈ 𝒩 exactly when d is constant along every edge of Γ_H: dim 𝒩 is the number of its components.

For 𝒲, take an edge x–y of Γ and write a = H_xy, b = H_x̄ȳ, not both zero. (★) at (x, y) and at (y, x), with H Hermitian, gives b·d_y = a·d_x and b̄·d_x = ā·d_y. Multiplying the first by ā and using the second, |a|²·d_x = |b|²·d_x. If |a| ≠ |b|, then d_x = 0, and from whichever of the two equations has a nonzero coefficient on d_y, d_y = 0. If |a| = |b| (so both are nonzero), the two equations are one, d_y = (a/b)·d_x, a unimodular ratio. On the diagonal, (★) at (x, x) is (H_x̄x̄ − H_xx)·d_x = 0. So on a component that is not good, d vanishes at some vertex and, the nonzero ratios carrying zero along, everywhere on it; on a good component d is fixed by its value at one vertex, the flat section, nonzero everywhere and of constant modulus. The solutions are the flat sections, one dimension per good component.

On a good component every edge has a ≠ 0, so it is an edge of Γ_H: a good component of Γ is a single component of Γ_H. Hence #good ≤ #components(Γ) ≤ #components(Γ_H), and the two ends are equal exactly when every component of Γ is good. F158 turns that equality into the palindrome. Where every component is good, the flat sections normalised to modulus one assemble a unitary diagonal D, and X^⊗N·D ∈ 𝒲 is unitary. ∎

**Colourings are characters.** A lit string is X^⊗N·Z^s up to a phase, so the palindrome is carried by a colouring exactly when some flat section on all of Γ at once is a character, d_x ∝ (−1)^(s·x). Other flat sections are other carriers. On P₃ with XX + YY bonds and jumps Y, Z, X, the far element XXZ + ZYY of the colouring page squares to 2·1, so in the jumps' eigenbasis it is X^⊗N times a diagonal of constant modulus √2 that is a sum of two characters: a flat section that is no character. The canonical case (Heisenberg or XX + YY bonds, no fields, uniform Z) has the popcount shells as components, every one good with the constant section, so both ends count N + 1 per connected graph, F158 §(f2) and F4.

## Theorem 1 (Heisenberg under uniform Z dephasing)

Let H = Σ_{(i,j)∈E} J_ij·(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j + Z_iZ_j) + Σ_l h_l·P_l on a connected graph (V, E) with N ≥ 2, every J_ij ≠ 0, each P_l ∈ {X, Y, Z} and each h_l real (a site with h_l = 0 carries no field), and every site dephased along Z. **The palindrome holds exactly when no field lies along Z and the fields do not use both X and Y.** With no field its carriers are the N + 1 of the canonical case; with fields along X alone the carrier is the colouring X^⊗N, with fields along Y alone the colouring Y^⊗N. No condition on the magnitudes or signs of the fields or of the bonds enters. This is the converse of F138 holding exactly in this class: F138's clause 2 (one axis, orthogonal to Z) gives the palindrome, and here nothing else does, at every magnitude, including the coincident magnitudes that break the converse where a site is left undephased.

**Proof.** The components of Γ read the edges of (★). A bond's XX + YY moves one excitation along the bond, x ↦ y with x and y differing in the bits i, j, H_xy = 2J_ij, and the complements differ in the same two bits, H_x̄ȳ = 2J_ij: ratio 1. Its ZZ is diagonal, J_ij·(−1)^(x_i + x_j), and unchanged by x ↦ x̄. An X field at l joins x to x ⊕ e_l with H_xy = H_x̄ȳ = h_l: ratio 1. A Y field at l joins them with H_xy = i·h_l·(−1)^(y_l) and H_x̄ȳ = i·h_l·(−1)^(ȳ_l) = −H_xy: ratio −1. A Z field at l adds h_l·(−1)^(x_l) to the diagonal, so condition (ii) needs Σ_{l: P_l = Z} h_l·(−1)^(x_l) = 0 on every vertex of a component.

The transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate every permutation of the sites. A transposition of two sites whose bits differ is a bond edge of Γ_H, and one whose bits agree fixes x; so writing any permutation that carries x to y (both of one popcount) as a product of edge transpositions and skipping the steps that fix the string gives a path of bond edges from x to y. Each popcount shell is connected, by edges of ratio 1: a flat section is a function f of the popcount alone. An X field then forces f(k + 1) = f(k) and a Y field f(k + 1) = −f(k), for k = 0 … N − 1. With both, f = 0 and 𝒲 = 0, while the fields join the shells into one component of Γ_H, dim 𝒩 = 1: broken. With X fields alone, f is constant, D = 1, the carrier X^⊗N; with Y fields alone, f(k) = (−1)^k f(0), D ∝ Z^⊗N, the carrier X^⊗N Z^⊗N ∝ Y^⊗N; with no transverse field, the shells stay separate components with the constant section on each. A Z field: with a transverse field Γ has one component, containing every x, and Σ_{Z sites} h_l·(−1)^(x_l) vanishing at every x forces every such h_l = 0; with no transverse field the components are the shells, and vanishing on the shell of popcount 0 and on each shell of popcount 1 (x = e_m gives Σ h_l − 2·h_m for Z sites m, the rest unchanged) forces h_m = 0 for every Z site as well. So any Z field breaks the palindrome, F158 §(f5)'s Tr(H·Z_l) ≠ 0 seen as a vertex condition. ∎

## What is gated

`EndCount.ComplementConnection()` reads Theorem 2 exactly (Gaussian-integer entries, Gaussian-rational sections, the turn to Z as a tested proper rotation) and returns the hopping components, the union components, the good ones and the verdict. `EndCountTests` holds it against the end count's own exact verdict and counts: every every-site-dephased row at N = 3 for the bond sets XX + YY + ZZ, XX + YY and ZZ on the path (fields 30, 22, 41 against bonds 100, and for two of the sets also 30, 30, 30) and on the triangle (one tuple each), and at N = 4 for Heisenberg on the path, with the hopping components equal to the near count and the good components to the far count on every row; 600 random Pauli Hamiltonians at N = 2 to 4 with random jump letters (fixed seed; the rows whose verdict the ranks alone gave are skipped). Theorem 1 is held at N = 3 to 5 on the path, ring, star and complete graph and at N = 6 on the path, every pattern of none, X, Y or Z per site, random signed bond weights and field magnitudes, with the carrier's colouring pinned; two disjoint bonds with an X field on one and a Y field on the other pair, carried by XXYY, which is why the graph must be connected. The row H = X₀ + X₀Z₁ under Z jumps pins the difference between the hopping graph (3 components, the near count) and its union with the complement image (2 components, neither good). Removing the holonomy check or the diagonal condition fails a gate, and counting the union's components for the near end fails the random test and that row. Removing the modulus check alone does not, since the holonomy walks each edge in both directions and the reverse ratio is the conjugate. Making the turn to Z improper fails only its own unit test. Why no verdict in the gates moves is not explained: on one site an improper letter map is a partial transpose, which is no conjugation and can change H's own spectrum (the Bell projector (II + XX − YY + ZZ)/4, eigenvalues 0 and 1, becomes SWAP/2, eigenvalues ±½), yet on the rows gated here the two kernel counts come out the same (measured).

## Scope, and what would falsify it

Every site dephased by exactly one single-site Pauli jump. A site with no jump, or with two anticommuting jumps, lies outside: undephased sites double the spans and are where the site symmetries and the conditioned sector sums of the colouring page live. H a real combination of Pauli strings with no further condition; Theorem 1 needs Heisenberg bonds of nonzero weights on a connected graph, Z on every site (at any rates), and fields along letters (a field along X + Y on every site, which F138's clause 2 admits, lies outside it). A counterexample would be a row in this class where the end count's exact verdict and the criterion disagree, or, in Theorem 1's class, a palindrome with both X and Y fields.

## Open

- **Heisenberg under mixed dephasing axes.** With every site dephased but along different letters, the turn to Z twists the bonds (a bond between a Z-dephased and an X-dephased site becomes X_iZ_j + Z_iX_j − Y_iY_j), and single bond edges appear. Measured, at Heisenberg bonds with fields of one magnitude at N = 3, 4 and 5, every such palindrome is a colouring; the coloured count over all axis patterns is 3·(4^N − 1). Theorem 2 makes the claim a holonomy computation: that every flat section of a Heisenberg row is a character.
- **Undephased sites.** Theorem 2 does not reach them; the near span is no longer abelian and the far span no longer one diagonal times X^⊗N.
- **The certifier.** Theorem 2 is an exact, Liouvillian-free test on F87's diagonal cell (Z on every site); carrying the criterion into `PalindromeSoftCertifier` is not done.

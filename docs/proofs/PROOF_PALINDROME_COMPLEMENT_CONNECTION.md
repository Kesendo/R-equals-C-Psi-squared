# PROOF: With Every Site Dephased, the Palindrome Is a Flat Connection

**Status:** Tier 1 derived. Theorem 2 holds for any Hermitian H that is a real combination of Pauli strings, with exactly one single-site Pauli jump on every site, any strictly positive rates, any N; Theorem 1 is its corollary for hopping bonds J(XX + YY) of any nonzero weights, with any diagonal part that is unchanged under flipping every bit (Heisenberg, XXZ and XY among them), on a connected graph with every site dephased along Z (at any rates), and at Heisenberg and XX + YY bonds it is the converse of [F138](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f138) holding exactly in that class, at every field magnitude. Theorem 3 is its companion with Heisenberg bonds on a connected graph and the dephasing axes mixed (at least two letters, fields of any direction): the near end is one-dimensional, and the palindrome holds exactly when two axes occur and every field lies along the third letter, carried by that letter's colouring, F138's converse holding exactly again. Theorem 4 reaches undephased sites that keep a letter H conserves: H splits into sectors of their bits, and both ends become sums of the same scalar problem read between two sectors. Theorem 5 lets one site go undephased under Heisenberg bonds of nonzero weights on a connected graph with letter fields: the palindrome still holds exactly when a colouring exists, F138's converse a third time, and with two undephased sites, or at other bonds, it fails. All five are gated exactly in MirrorWorld against the end count's own verdict and counts (`EndCount.ComplementConnection` for Theorems 1 to 3, `EndCount.SectorConnection` for Theorem 4, the end count's own verdict for Theorem 5, `EndCountTests`), and typed as `PalindromeComplementConnectionClaim` (parent F158's `PalindromeTwoEndCountClaim`); Theorems 1 to 4 are live at `inspect --root complement`, which reads Theorem 2 and Theorem 4 on its own graph and prints Theorem 5's rule beside the string route.

## Setup

N sites, H a real combination of Pauli strings (so H is Hermitian), and on every site l exactly one jump, a Pauli letter A_l, at any strictly positive rate. F158's other fences hold by themselves here: every jump squares to 1, and the dimension 2^N is even. A proper rotation of the letters on each site turns A_l into Z_l (Z fixed; for X the Hadamard, X ↔ Z and Y → −Y; for Y the cyclic X → Y → Z → X); it is conjugation by a one-site unitary, it maps H to another real combination of Pauli strings, and it changes no kernel dimension. So take A_l = Z_l on every site. Write x ∈ {0,1}^N for the computational basis, x̄ for the bitwise complement, and H_xy = ⟨x|H|y⟩.

By F158's corollary, the near kernel 𝒩 is the kernel of W ↦ [H, W] on the strings commuting with every jump, and the far kernel 𝒲 the same on the strings anticommuting with every jump. With every jump a Z, the first span is the diagonal matrices and the second is X^⊗N times the diagonal matrices. So

  𝒩 = {D diagonal : [H, D] = 0},  𝒲 = {X^⊗N·D : D diagonal, H·X^⊗N·D = X^⊗N·D·H}.

Since (X^⊗N H X^⊗N)_xy = H_x̄ȳ, the far condition reads, entry by entry,

  H_x̄ȳ · d_y = d_x · H_xy  for all x, y.  (★)

## Theorem 2 (the complement connection)

Let Γ_H be the hopping graph of H: vertices the bitstrings, an edge x–y (x ≠ y) where H_xy ≠ 0. Let Γ be Γ_H joined with its image under x ↦ x̄. Call a component C of Γ **good** when

- (i) |H_xy| = |H_x̄ȳ| on every edge of C,
- (ii) H_xx = H_x̄x̄ on every vertex of C,
- (iii) the complement connection d_y / d_x = H_xy / H_x̄ȳ has trivial holonomy around every cycle of C (the product of the ratios around the cycle is 1; the word is used in its plain sense, not for the seed's EP holonomy of `SeedHolonomyClaim`).

Then dim 𝒩 is the number of components of Γ_H, dim 𝒲 is the number of good components of Γ, and **the palindrome holds exactly when every component of Γ is good.** Where it holds, the far element X^⊗N·D with D the flat section, normalised to modulus one, is a unitary carrier of the palindrome. The frame theorem's diagonal frame V (VHV† commuting with X^⊗N) gives such a D as D = X^⊗N·V†·X^⊗N·V, that is d_x = v̄_x̄·v_x, which forces d_x·d_x̄ = 1; not every unitary flat section has that form, but a phase per component brings it there (below).

**Proof.** [H, D]_xy = H_xy (d_y − d_x), so D ∈ 𝒩 exactly when d is constant along every edge of Γ_H: dim 𝒩 is the number of its components.

For 𝒲, take an edge x–y of Γ and write a = H_xy, b = H_x̄ȳ, not both zero. (★) at (x, y) and at (y, x), with H Hermitian, gives b·d_y = a·d_x and b̄·d_x = ā·d_y. Multiplying the first by ā and using the second, |a|²·d_x = |b|²·d_x. If |a| ≠ |b|, then d_x = 0, and from whichever of the two equations has a nonzero coefficient on d_y, d_y = 0. If |a| = |b| (so both are nonzero), the two equations are one, d_y = (a/b)·d_x, a unimodular ratio. On the diagonal, (★) at (x, x) is (H_x̄x̄ − H_xx)·d_x = 0. So on a component that is not good, d vanishes at some vertex and, the nonzero ratios carrying zero along, everywhere on it; on a good component d is fixed by its value at one vertex, the flat section, nonzero everywhere and of constant modulus. The solutions are the flat sections, one dimension per good component.

On a good component every edge has a ≠ 0, so it is an edge of Γ_H: a good component of Γ is a single component of Γ_H. Hence #good ≤ #components(Γ) ≤ #components(Γ_H), and the two ends are equal exactly when every component of Γ is good. F158 turns that equality into the palindrome. Where every component is good, the flat sections normalised to modulus one assemble a unitary diagonal D, and X^⊗N·D ∈ 𝒲 is unitary. ∎

**The frame, from a flat section.** For a flat section of modulus one, the product p_x = d_x·d_x̄ is constant on each component C of Γ, since the complement edge x̄–ȳ carries the inverse ratio of x–y, so d_ȳ/d_x̄ = (d_y/d_x)⁻¹ and d_y·d_ȳ = d_x·d_x̄; call the value p_C, and C̄ the complement component. Multiplying the section on C by a phase t_C with t_C·t_C̄ = p_C⁻¹ (a square root of p_C⁻¹ when C = C̄; otherwise one of the two phases is free) keeps it flat and makes d_x·d_x̄ = 1 everywhere, and then v_x = d_x, v_x̄ = 1 on one representative x of each complement pair is a frame. Without the phase the formula can fail: at N = 2 with a Heisenberg bond and Z on both sites, D = diag(1, i, i, 1) on 00, 01, 10, 11 is flat (constant on the popcount shells), X^⊗2·D is a unitary element of the far end, and d_x·d_x̄ = −1 on {01, 10}.

**Colourings are characters.** A lit string is X^⊗N·Z^s up to a phase, so the palindrome is carried by a colouring exactly when some flat section on all of Γ at once is a character, d_x ∝ (−1)^(s·x). Other flat sections are other carriers. On P₃ with XX + YY bonds and jumps Y, Z, X, the far element XXZ + ZYY of the colouring page squares to 2·1, so in the jumps' eigenbasis it is X^⊗N times a diagonal of constant modulus √2 that is a sum of two characters: a flat section that is no character. The canonical case (Heisenberg or XX + YY bonds, no fields, uniform Z) has the popcount shells as components, every one good with the constant section, so both ends count N + 1 per connected graph, F158 §(f2) and F4.

## Theorem 1 (hopping bonds under uniform Z dephasing)

Let H = Σ_{(i,j)∈E} J_ij·(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j) + B + Σ_l h_l·P_l on a connected graph (V, E) with N ≥ 2, every J_ij ≠ 0 and real, B a real diagonal operator with B_x = B_x̄ for every bitstring (any ZZ terms Δ_ij·Z_iZ_j of any weights, zero included, are of this kind; so Heisenberg, XXZ and XY bonds are all covered), each P_l ∈ {X, Y, Z} and each h_l real (a site with h_l = 0 carries no field), and every site dephased along Z. **The palindrome holds exactly when no field lies along Z and the fields do not use both X and Y.** With no field its carriers are the N + 1 of the canonical case; with fields along X alone the carrier is the colouring X^⊗N, with fields along Y alone the colouring Y^⊗N. No condition on the magnitudes or signs of the fields or of the bonds enters. At Heisenberg and XX + YY bonds, the bond sets of F138 that contain hopping, this is the converse of F138 holding exactly in this class: F138's clause 2 (one axis, orthogonal to Z) gives the palindrome, and here nothing else does, at every magnitude, including the coincident magnitudes that break the converse where a site is left undephased.

**Proof.** The components of Γ read the edges of (★). A bond's XX + YY moves one excitation along the bond, x ↦ y with x and y differing in the bits i, j, H_xy = 2J_ij, and the complements differ in the same two bits, H_x̄ȳ = 2J_ij: ratio 1. The diagonal part B is unchanged by x ↦ x̄ by assumption, so it adds nothing to condition (ii); a ZZ term Δ_ij·(−1)^(x_i + x_j) is such a part. An X field at l joins x to x ⊕ e_l with H_xy = H_x̄ȳ = h_l: ratio 1. A Y field at l joins them with H_xy = i·h_l·(−1)^(y_l) and H_x̄ȳ = i·h_l·(−1)^(ȳ_l) = −H_xy: ratio −1. A Z field at l adds h_l·(−1)^(x_l) to the diagonal, so condition (ii) needs Σ_{l: P_l = Z} h_l·(−1)^(x_l) = 0 on every vertex of a component.

The transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate every permutation of the sites. A transposition of two sites whose bits differ is a bond edge of Γ_H, and one whose bits agree fixes x; so writing any permutation that carries x to y (both of one popcount) as a product of edge transpositions and skipping the steps that fix the string gives a path of bond edges from x to y. Each popcount shell is connected, by edges of ratio 1: a flat section is a function f of the popcount alone. An X field then forces f(k + 1) = f(k) and a Y field f(k + 1) = −f(k), for k = 0 … N − 1. With both, f = 0 and 𝒲 = 0, while the fields join the shells into one component of Γ_H, dim 𝒩 = 1: broken. With X fields alone, f is constant, D = 1, the carrier X^⊗N; with Y fields alone, f(k) = (−1)^k f(0), D ∝ Z^⊗N, the carrier X^⊗N Z^⊗N ∝ Y^⊗N; with no transverse field, the shells stay separate components with the constant section on each. A Z field: with a transverse field Γ has one component, containing every x, and Σ_{Z sites} h_l·(−1)^(x_l) vanishing at every x forces every such h_l = 0; with no transverse field the components are the shells, and vanishing on the shell of popcount 0 and on each shell of popcount 1 (x = e_m gives Σ h_l − 2·h_m for Z sites m, the rest unchanged) forces h_m = 0 for every Z site as well. So any Z field breaks the palindrome, F158 §(f5)'s Tr(H·Z_l) ≠ 0 seen as a vertex condition. ∎

## Theorem 3 (Heisenberg bonds under mixed dephasing axes)

Let H = Σ_{(i,j)∈E} J_ij·(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j + Z_iZ_j) + Σ_l (h^X_l·X_l + h^Y_l·Y_l + h^Z_l·Z_l) on a connected graph (V, E) with N ≥ 2, every J_ij ≠ 0 and real, the fields real and of any direction, magnitude and sign, and every site l dephased along one letter a_l (at any rate), with at least two different letters among the a_l. **Then the near end is one-dimensional, and the palindrome holds exactly when exactly two letters occur among the axes and every field lies along the third letter c; the carrier is then the colouring c^⊗N and no other element, and on every broken row the far end is zero, not merely short of the near end.** This is F138's converse holding exactly in a second class: its clause 1 (at most two axes) and clause 2 (one field axis orthogonal to every dephasing axis, which next to two different axes can only be c) give the palindrome, and here nothing else does, at any magnitudes, directions and signs.

**Proof.** Turn every site to Z with one proper rotation per letter, the same rotation on every site dephased along that letter. A term b_i·b_j of a bond flips bit i exactly when b ≠ a_i, since the rotation sends a_i to Z and the other two letters to X and Y up to sign. So H falls into three kinds of pieces:

- a bond whose ends share an axis (a *same-axis bond*) stays a Heisenberg bond, proper rotations keeping XX + YY + ZZ: it swaps the bits i, j where they differ, with H_xy = H_x̄ȳ = 2J_ij (ratio 1, as in Theorem 1), and puts a complement-invariant ZZ on the diagonal;
- a bond whose ends have different axes (a *mixed bond*), c_ij the third letter: c_i·c_j flips both bits, (a_i)_i·(a_i)_j flips j alone, (a_j)_i·(a_j)_j flips i alone, and nothing is diagonal;
- a field's component along a_l is diagonal (Z_l after the turn), its other components flip bit l.

The entries with x ⊕ y = e_i ⊕ e_j for a mixed bond come from c_i·c_j alone (every other bond joins another pair, a field one site), so they are nonzero at every x: a *pair flip*, on every vertex. For a single string P, X^⊗N·P·X^⊗N = ±P, so its ratio H_xy / H_x̄ȳ is one sign σ_ij at every x. The entries with x ⊕ y = e_i have the weight

  w_i(x) = Σ_{k∈K(i)} J_ik·τ_k(x_i)·(−1)^(x_k) + φ_i(x_i),

K(i) the sites joined to i by mixed bonds, τ_k(x_i) ∈ {±1, ±i} the entry of the turned letter at i, and φ_i the entry of the turned transverse field at i. The weight depends on x only through x_i and the bits of K(i).

**Lemma A: the hopping graph Γ_H is connected, so dim 𝒩 = 1.** Pair flips change the popcount by 0 or ±2 and swaps not at all, so both keep its parity. (a) *Each parity class is connected by swaps and pair flips.* Call a *cluster* a component of the same-axis bonds. Inside a cluster the swaps connect any two arrangements of one popcount (Theorem 1's argument), so a vertex is fixed, up to paths, by the vector n of cluster popcounts, 0 ≤ n_A ≤ |A|. The graph is connected and uses two axes, so every cluster meets a mixed bond and the clusters, joined where a mixed bond runs, form a connected graph. A mixed bond between i ∈ A and j ∈ B changes n_A and n_B by one each, and the sign is free where there is room: rearranging A puts a 0 on i whenever n_A < |A| and a 1 whenever n_A > 0, independently of B. Take a spanning tree of the cluster graph and a target m of the same total parity. At a leaf L with neighbour P, step n_L toward m_L, letting n_P move whichever way it has room (every cluster has at least one site, so one way always does), until n_L = m_L; then drop L and repeat. At the last cluster R the parity forces n_R − m_R to be even, and two steps with a neighbour Q, one moving n_Q up and one down (in the order Q's room allows), move n_R by 2 and leave n_Q where it was. (b) *A single flip exists.* Take a mixed bond (i, k). Flipping x_k with every other bit held changes w_i by 2·J_ik·τ_k(x_i)·(−1)^(x_k) ≠ 0, so w_i is nonzero at one of the two vertices, and that edge joins the two parity classes. ∎

**The far end.** Suppose 𝒲 ≠ 0. By Theorem 2 and Lemma A, Γ is one good component, and its flat section d is nonzero at every vertex, of constant modulus, and unique up to a scalar.

(c) *d is a character up to one parity constant.* A pair flip gives d(x ⊕ e_i ⊕ e_j) = σ_ij·d(x) at every x. A swap gives d(x ⊕ e_i ⊕ e_j) = d(x) where x_i ≠ x_j, and also where x_i = x_j: there the step moves n_A by 2, and two pair flips on a mixed bond (k, l) out of the cluster A, with k rearranged to carry the needed bit before each (there is room, since the target's n_A ± 2 lies in [0, |A|]), do the same at the price σ_kl² = 1, x_l flipped twice. So for every bond the ratio d(x ⊕ e_i ⊕ e_j)/d(x) is a sign independent of x. The vectors e_i ⊕ e_j over the bonds of a connected graph span the even-weight vectors, so these signs extend to a homomorphism χ from the even-weight vectors to ±1 with d(x ⊕ v) = χ(v)·d(x), and χ extends to a character (−1)^(s·x) of all of {0,1}^N. Then d(x)·(−1)^(s·x) is unchanged by every even-weight step, a function of the parity of x alone: d(x) = c_π(x)·(−1)^(s·x) with two constants c_0, c_1 of one modulus.

(d) *The parity constant is a sign, c_1 = ±c_0.* Suppose some site i has K(i) nonempty and some site m lies outside {i} ∪ K(i). By (b), w_i(x) ≠ 0 at some x, and w_i does not see x_m, so the single-flip edges at x and at x ⊕ e_m carry the same ratio H_xy / H_x̄ȳ. On the side of d the two ratios are (c_1/c_0)·(−1)^(s_i) and (c_0/c_1)·(−1)^(s_i), one from each parity. Hence (c_1/c_0)² = 1. If no such pair (i, m) exists, then a site on a mixed bond is joined by mixed bonds to every other site, those sites are too, every pair of sites is a mixed bond, and the axes are pairwise different: N = 2, or N = 3 on the triangle with three axes. At N = 2 the frame paragraph's constant does it, applied to d normalised to modulus one: d_x·d_x̄ is the same on the whole component, x and x̄ have one parity at even N, and d_x·d_x̄ = c_π(x)²·(−1)^|s|, so c_0² = c_1². On the triangle with three axes the pair flips close a cycle x → x ⊕ e_1 ⊕ e_2 → x ⊕ e_1 ⊕ e_3 → x whose holonomy is σ_12·σ_23·σ_13, the sign that X^⊗3 gives the product of the three turned pair-flip strings (a3·a3·I)(I·a1·a1)(a2·I·a2), the letters numbered by their sites' axes. That product is (a3·a2) ⊗ (a3·a1) ⊗ (a1·a2), a phase times a1 ⊗ a2 ⊗ a3 = A_1·A_2·A_3, which the turn makes Z⊗Z⊗Z, and X^⊗3 reverses it: holonomy −1, condition (iii) fails, and 𝒲 = 0 against the assumption.

(e) *A character is a colouring.* With c_1 = ±c_0, d is a multiple of one character, D of one string of Z's, and the far element X^⊗N·D is one lit string, which the turn back keeps a single string: a lit string commuting with H. Distinct terms of H are distinct strings, and a string commuting with H must commute with each term separately (the commutator with a string is a sum of distinct strings), so with X_iX_j, Y_iY_j and Z_iZ_j on every bond, which forces the same letter at i and j; the graph being connected, the string is p^⊗N. Lit on every site means p anticommutes with every axis, so p is no axis: exactly two axes occur and p = c. A field component along a letter other than c anticommutes with c^⊗N, so every field lies along c. Conversely c^⊗N is then lit and commutes with every term.

So 𝒲 ≠ 0 forces the rule, the rule gives the colouring c^⊗N, and with dim 𝒩 = 1 the palindrome, dim 𝒲 = dim 𝒩, holds exactly when 𝒲 ≠ 0. ∎

## Theorem 4 (undephased sites that keep a letter)

Let every site of a nonempty set S carry exactly one single-site Pauli jump, and let every site u of the rest F (the undephased sites) keep a letter P_u: every term of H, a real combination of Pauli strings, has I or P_u at u (a site no term touches keeps any letter; take Z). Turn each jump and each kept letter to Z by the proper rotations of the setup. Every term then acts on the bits of F by I or Z, so H is block diagonal in them,

  H = Σ_σ H_σ ⊗ |σ⟩⟨σ|,  σ ∈ {0,1}^F,

each H_σ a Hermitian matrix on the bitstrings x of S; write H̄_σ for its complement image on S alone, (H̄_σ)_xy = (H_σ)_x̄ȳ. For two Hermitian matrices A, B on the bitstrings of S let hom(A, B) be the dimension of {g : B_xy·g_y = g_x·A_xy for all x, y}. **Then**

  **dim ker L = Σ_{σ,τ} hom(H_τ, H_σ),  dim ker(L + 2σ_γ) = Σ_{σ,τ} hom(H_τ, H̄_σ),**

(σ_γ the sum of the rates, to keep it apart from the sector labels), **and each hom(A, B) is the number of good components of the graph whose edges are the nonzero off-diagonal entries of A or of B**: |A_xy| = |B_xy| on every edge, A_xx = B_xx on every vertex, and trivial holonomy of g_y / g_x = A_xy / B_xy. The palindrome holds exactly when the two sums agree. With F empty there is one sector and this is Theorem 2 (hom(H, H) the components of Γ_H, hom(H, H̄) the good components of Γ). The terms with σ = τ are one sector read against itself; the terms with σ ≠ τ, the *cross-sector* terms, pair two sectors, and they are where an undephased site adds elements that no single sector holds.

**Proof.** The strings commuting with every jump are those with I or Z on every site of S and any letter on F, so they span the matrices M = Σ_x |x⟩⟨x| ⊗ m_x with m_x any matrix on the bits of F. The entry of [H, M] at ((x, σ), (y, τ)) is (H_σ)_xy·m_y[σ, τ] − m_x[σ, τ]·(H_τ)_xy, so M lies in the near end exactly when, for every pair (σ, τ), the function g = m[σ, τ] satisfies (H_σ)_xy·g_y = g_x·(H_τ)_xy, and the pairs do not interact: the near end is the direct sum of the spaces hom(H_τ, H_σ). The strings anticommuting with every jump are those with X or Y on every site of S and any letter on F, which span X^⊗S·M with M as before. X^⊗S·M commutes with H exactly when H̄·M = M·H with H̄ = X^⊗S·H·X^⊗S, whose blocks are the H̄_σ, and the same entry-by-entry reading gives the direct sum of the spaces hom(H_τ, H̄_σ). By F158's corollary these are the two ends.

For hom(A, B), take an edge x–y and write a = A_xy, b = B_xy. The equation at (x, y) is b·g_y = a·g_x; at (y, x), with A and B Hermitian, it is b̄·g_x = ā·g_y. This is Theorem 2's pair of equations with (a, b) in place of (H_xy, H_x̄ȳ), and the same three lines give the rest: if |a| ≠ |b| both g_x and g_y vanish, if |a| = |b| the two equations are one, g_y = (a/b)·g_x, and on the diagonal (B_xx − A_xx)·g_x = 0. So g vanishes on every component that is not good and is fixed by one value on each good one. ∎

**What it reaches, and what it does not.** An undephased site keeps a letter when the bonds touching it use one letter there and its field, if any, lies along that letter: the Ising bond with a field along its own letter, or none. A Heisenberg or XX + YY bond touching the site uses two letters or more at it, and then no letter is kept; the blocks of H over the bits of S become matrices of size 2^|F| (2 × 2 for one undephased site) that do not commute, and the reduction above no longer splits into scalar problems (Open). The proof uses only that the kept direction can be turned to Z by a one-site unitary, so it covers, by the same argument, an undephased site whose terms group into (n·σ)_u times strings on the other sites for a direction n that is no letter; that extension is proven and not gated, since `SectorConnection` reads letters and declines such rows. Gated in MirrorWorld (`EndCount.SectorConnection`, `EndCountTests`): 700 draws of random Pauli Hamiltonians at N = 2 to 5 (a draw of identities only is skipped) with one or two undephased sites, each keeping a random letter, the two sums equal to the end count's two counts on every row where its modular ranks and its lifted, exactly checked vectors meet (more than 450 rows, both verdicts, more than 30 rows with a nonzero cross-sector term, which dropping the cross-sector terms from the sums fails); with no undephased site it returns Theorem 2's reading on 200 random rows; and on the census's own family (ZZ bonds of weight 100, fields 30, 22, 41, the path at N = 3, every pattern of dephasing and fields with at least one site undephased and at least one dephased) it applies to exactly the 1008 rows in which every undephased site's field is absent or Z (864 with one undephased site, 144 with two), equal on each to the end count's upper counts, and to its verdict wherever that is exact, 340 of them palindromic (measured).

## Theorem 5 (Heisenberg bonds with one undephased site)

Let H = Σ_{(i,j)∈E} J_ij·(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j + Z_iZ_j) + Σ_l h_l·P_l on a connected graph with N ≥ 2, every J_ij ≠ 0 and real, letter fields P_l of any real magnitudes (h_l = 0 allowed); let exactly one site u carry no jump and every other site l one single-site letter jump a_l (the axes equal or mixed). **Then the palindrome holds exactly when some letter p is the axis of no dephased site and every nonzero field, the one at u included, lies along p;** its carrier is then the colouring p^⊗N. With two undephased sites this fails: F138's 78 coincident-magnitude rows (the three-site path with equal field magnitudes on its two ends and only the middle site dephased; the triangle has 234 such rows) pair without a colouring, carried by SWAP₀₂·(n·σ)^⊗3. So one free site is the boundary. The bonds must be Heisenberg: at XX + YY or at ZZ bonds a row with one undephased site can pair without a colouring (Scope).

**Proof.** The colouring is lit and commutes with every term, so it carries the palindrome. For the converse, let the palindrome hold. By F158's Lemma 3 the far end holds an invertible W; W† lies there too, so W†W lies in the near end, a commutant and so closed under the functional calculus, and W·|W|⁻¹ is a unitary far element: take W unitary. Write S for the dephased sites, K for the neighbours of u, J_k = J_ku, and for k ∈ S let K(k) be its S-neighbours j with a_j ≠ a_k. Turn every jump to Z by the setup's proper rotations and leave u alone; then W = X^⊗S·D with D = ⊕_x d_x, each d_x a unitary on u, and (★) holds with 2 × 2 blocks: H_x̄ȳ·d_y = d_x·H_xy. In the turned frame a Heisenberg bond between i and j is Σ_c c_i ⊗ O(c)_j with O a proper rotation, its ZZ coefficient â_i·â_j; a letter vector â, ν, f is read in u's frame (ν the field at u, f_k the field at k ∈ S, both as vectors), and ε_j(x) = (−1)^(x_j).

*The blocks.* The diagonal block at x is E(x)·1 + K_x with K_x = (μ(x) + ν)·σ and μ(x) = Σ_{k∈K} J_k·ε_k(x)·â_k: the bond (k, u) contributes its a_k term, diagonal on S, and u's field sits there. The block for a flip of one site k ∈ K (x_k = 0 to 1) is M = ω(x)·1 + 2J_k·τ⁻ and its complement block M' = ω̃·1 + 2J_k·τ⁺, with τ± the raising and lowering operators in the eigenbasis of a_k at u and ω, ω̃ the scalar single-flip entries at k (its transverse field, its mixed S-bonds). Every other off-diagonal block is a scalar: swaps and pair flips inside S (Theorem 3's moves, their entries never zero) and single flips at sites not adjacent to u. On a scalar edge (★) gives d_y ∝ d_x.

*N1, N2 (the diagonal).* (★) at (x, x) says d_x conjugates E(x) + K_x into E(x̄) + K_x̄, with K_x̄ = (−μ(x) + ν)·σ. Traces give E(x) = E(x̄), which forbids a field along a dephased site's own axis (F158 §(f5)). Bloch norms give |μ + ν| = |−μ + ν| at every x, so μ(x)·ν = 0; the ε_k are independent signs, so ν ⊥ â_k for every k ∈ K.

*N3 (the square of a flip).* The two edge equations of a k-flip combine to M'M'†·d_x = d_x·MM†: W commutes with the square of H's k-flip part. Its traceless part is 2J_k·(t_k(x) − J_k·ε_k(x)·â_k)·σ with t_k = f_k^⊥ + b_k(x) and b_k(x) = Σ_{j∈K(k)} J_jk·ε_j(x)·â_j (f_k^⊥ the part of f_k transverse to a_k); at x̄ the same with ε flipped. The rotation of d_x maps both vector pairs, so 2f_k·μ(x) + 2b_k(x)·ν = 0 at every x. Independence of the signs gives f_k ⊥ â_j for every j ∈ K and ν ⊥ â_j for every j ∈ K(k) (the case j ∈ K being N2).

*Lemma C (the hand-off).* If every d_x anticommutes with one letter c at u, then W anticommutes with c_u as well, so W lies in the far end of the row R_c in which u is also dephased along c. W is invertible, so R_c pairs (F158, Lemma 3), and R_c has every site dephased on the same connected graph with the same Heisenberg bonds and letter fields: Theorem 1 (after a global rotation, when R_c has one common axis) or Theorem 3 (mixed axes) gives its rule, and that rule is the rule of the row itself, u's axis c included, since a colouring of R_c is one of R. So it suffices to find such a letter.

*Lemma T (transfer across a flip at k).* Write P_a(x) for "d_x anticommutes with a". On a k-flip, unitarity of both sides forces |ω| = |ω̃|, and the (1,1) and (2,2) entries of the two edge equations show that P_{a_k}(x) holds exactly when P_{a_k}(x ⊕ e_k) does; where ω = ω̃ = 0 the edge equation d_x·τ⁻ = τ⁺·d_y forces the diagonal of d_x in a_k's basis to vanish, P_{a_k} at both ends outright. Across a scalar edge d_y ∝ d_x, so every property that ignores a scalar factor transfers (each one used here does). The transfer of P_{a_k} is local: across flips at k itself and scalar edges, not across flips at another neighbour of u.

**Case ν ≠ 0.** By N2, ν ⊥ â_k, and P_{a_k}(x) forces d_x to be ∝ ν̂·σ (the diagonal equation with d_x a π rotation about an axis n ⊥ â_k gives 2(n·(μ + ν))·n = 2ν). Call that Q(x). Q transfers across every edge, flips at every k ∈ K included, and the graph of K-flips and S-bond moves on the bitstrings of S is connected (every component of G − u meets K; inside a component swaps and pair flips reach every arrangement of one popcount, or of one parity where the component has a mixed bond, and the flips at its sites in K change that), so one vertex with Q suffices. Then every d_x is ∝ ν̂·σ, which commutes with ν's own letter and anticommutes with every letter orthogonal to it; N2 gives ν ⊥ â_k for k ∈ K, so Lemma C applies with c = a_k.
(S1) Some k ∈ K carries no field and no mixed S-bond: ω = ω̃ = 0 on its flips, P_{a_k} holds, Q holds.
(S3) Some scalar edge x–x' has μ(x) ≠ μ(x'): d_x is proportional to d_{x'}, so its rotation sends δ = μ(x) − μ(x') to −δ and μ(x) + ν to −μ(x) + ν; a rotation reversing δ is a π rotation about some n ⊥ δ, and the second condition forces n = ±ν̂: Q(x). Such an edge exists when S ≠ K (the graph being connected, some S-bond joins a site of K to a site outside K, and its swap, where the two bits differ, or its pair flip changes μ), when a mixed S-bond has an end in K (its pair flip changes μ), or when a same-axis S-bond joins i, j ∈ K with J_ui ≠ J_uj (its swap changes μ).
Otherwise u is joined to every site of S, every S-bond is same-axis with equal couplings to u, and every site of S carries a field (not S1). With two axes among S, N3 puts every field of S along the third letter c and N2 puts ν along c: the rule holds. With three axes N2 leaves no direction for ν ≠ 0. With one common axis (Z after the turn) d_x conjugates K_x to K_x̄ and M·K_y·M⁻¹ to M'·K_ȳ·M'⁻¹ along a k-flip, so tr(K_x·M·K_y·M⁻¹) = tr(K_x̄·M'·K_ȳ·M'⁻¹); for ν = h·X and f_k = h_k·Y the difference is −16i·J_k²·h/h_k (symbolic), nonzero, and for f_k ∥ X it vanishes; a rotation about Z carries the other letter orientations to this one. So every field of S lies along ν: the rule holds.

**Case ν = 0.** The diagonal equation reads d_x·(μ·σ) = −(μ·σ)·d_x, so where μ(x) ≠ 0, d_x = e^(iφ)·n_x·σ with n_x ⊥ μ(x), and N3 becomes (n·(t_k − J_k·ε_k·â_k))·n = f_k^⊥ with f_k·b_k = 0.
(ii) Some k ∈ K carries a transverse field: n_x ∥ f_k^⊥ ⊥ â_k wherever μ(x) ≠ 0, so P_{a_k}(x); a vertex with μ(x) = 0 has μ(x ⊕ e_k) = ±2J_k·â_k ≠ 0 and receives P_{a_k} by Lemma T across its k-flip. Lemma C with c = a_k.
(iii) Some k ∈ K carries no field and no mixed S-bond: (S1), P_{a_k} everywhere.
(iv) Otherwise every k ∈ K has a mixed S-bond and no field, so every component C of G − u (each meets K) is connected, fully dephased and has a mixed bond: Theorem 3's Lemma A (a) applies to it, and its swaps and pair flips connect each of its parity classes. A *scalar component* Γ of the bitstrings of S, one parity class per component of G − u, is joined by scalar edges, so n is one vector n_Γ on Γ, orthogonal to every difference of μ inside Γ. If some component has three sites or more, or pairs k with a partner outside K, those differences contain â_k (with k bonded to j and l inside the component, a signed combination of the μ-differences between vertices of one parity class, reached by Lemma A's moves, is −4J_k·ε_k·â_k; a partner outside K gives 2J_k·â_k directly), in every scalar component alike, so n_Γ ⊥ â_k on every Γ, and P_{a_k} holds wherever μ ≠ 0 and, by Lemma T, where μ = 0: Lemma C with the one letter c = a_k.
What remains is the *windmill*: G − u a perfect matching of mixed pairs {k, j}, both joined to u, no field anywhere. In one triangle (u, k, j), within a scalar component, n_Γ is orthogonal to three vectors of span{â_k, â_j}: J_k·ε_k·â_k + J_j·ε_j·â_j (the component's μ-difference across the pair), and J_jk·ε_j·â_j − J_k·ε_k·â_k and J_jk·ε_k·â_k − J_j·ε_j·â_j (N3 at k and at j, with no field). Unless all three are parallel, n_Γ is orthogonal to the whole plane, so to â_k and â_j; and all three are parallel exactly when J_k = J_j = −J_jk (their pairwise determinants vanish there and only there). Off that coincidence, in some triangle, n_Γ ⊥ â_k on every Γ, and Lemma C applies with c = a_k. If every triangle sits at its coincidence, each contributes n_Γ ⊥ ε_k·â_k + ε_j·â_j, one vector per triangle within one scalar component, the signs chosen by the component's parity classes. With two letters among the axes the rule holds directly (no fields, and the third letter is free). With three letters and three triangles or more, some scalar component's vectors span ℝ³ (two triangles on one letter pair give â + b̂ and â − b̂ within one scalar component, their parity classes being chosen independently, and a third triangle carries the third letter; three distinct pairs give det[â + b̂, b̂ + ĉ, â + ĉ] = 2), so n_Γ = 0 on it, d_x = 0 there, against unitarity. With exactly two triangles on the letter pairs {a, b} and {b, c} (the bowtie, N = 5), every flip and pair-flip equation is proportional, on both sides, to its own triangle's coupling, so the coupling cancels from it. At a vertex the condition is that d_x anticommutes with μ(x)·σ, where μ(x) = J_1·v_1(x) + J_2·v_2(x) mixes the two triangles; but d is one matrix up to a scalar on each scalar component Γ, and within Γ the signs of v_1 and of v_2 vary independently (each triangle's bits range over its own parity class), so anticommuting with every μ(x)·σ on Γ is anticommuting with v_1·σ and with v_2·σ separately, a condition with no coupling in it; this holds for every element of the far end, unitary or not. So at every nonzero coupling the far end is the solution space of one coupling-free system. The end count's modular rank (an upper bound on the far count, over a prime ≡ 1 mod 4 so that i is an element) finds it zero for all 24 three-letter assignments at every coupling gated (below), and a verifier's independent recomputation over p = 10⁹ + 9 and p = 998244353 agrees, the 12 two-letter assignments giving one far element, as the rule says. So the palindrome fails there, as the rule says: three letters, no free one. ∎

**Checked beside the proof, independently of its author.** Two fresh verifiers rebuilt the end count from scratch (exact over GF(p) with i a square root of −1, two primes) and found no counterexample in about 40,000 rows: every connected graph at N = 3 and 4 with every axis assignment and up to two fields in three weight modes (unit, coincidence forced on every triangle through u, random), random rows at N = 5 and 6, twelve stress families (u a leaf, a pair with a partner outside K, windmills with singletons, bowties with tails and bridges, K₄ minus an edge), windmills at N = 7 at the coincidence, and every sign pattern of the coincidence on the bowtie; the block formulas of N3 and the trace identity were rederived, the bowtie's rank recomputed independently. In MirrorWorld the rule is gated on every axis and field pattern at uniform bonds and on the coincident-coupling branches (next section).

## Scope, and what would falsify it

Theorems 1 to 3: every site dephased by exactly one single-site Pauli jump. Theorem 4: the dephased sites so, and every undephased site keeping a letter that H conserves. Theorem 5: Heisenberg bonds of nonzero weights on a connected graph, letter fields, exactly one undephased site; a counterexample would be a palindrome there without a colouring. It does not extend: with two undephased sites the SWAP rows pair without a colouring, and at other bonds one undephased site is enough to pair without one, on one XX + YY bond (the colouring page's defect cascade, pinned) and on the ZZ bonds of the anticommuting-sum census, whose sums include rows with one undephased site. A site with two anticommuting jumps lies outside all five, and an undephased site that keeps no letter lies outside Theorem 4; undephased sites double the spans and are where the site symmetries and the conditioned sector sums of the colouring page live. H a real combination of Pauli strings with no further condition; Theorem 1 needs hopping bonds J(XX + YY), every J nonzero, with a complement-invariant diagonal part, on a connected graph, Z on every site (at any rates), and fields along letters (a field along X + Y on every site, which F138's clause 2 admits, lies outside it). A counterexample would be a row in this class where the end count's exact verdict and the criterion disagree, or, in Theorem 1's class, a palindrome with both X and Y fields. Theorem 3 needs isotropic Heisenberg bonds, every J nonzero, on a connected graph, at least two letters among the axes; its counterexample would be a palindrome under three axes, or under two with a field component off the third letter. Anisotropic bonds (XXZ, XY) under mixed axes lie outside it: a same-axis bond whose axis is not the anisotropy letter turns into a swap plus a pair flip, so step (c)'s ratio 1 fails, step (e) uses all three letters on every bond, and at XY bonds a mixed bond whose third letter is Z has no pair flip. A disconnected graph lies outside it as well: two bonds (0, 1) and (2, 3) with axes X, Y, Y, Z, Z fields on sites 0 and 1 and X fields on 2 and 3 use three axes and pair, carried by ZZXX (pinned).

## Open

- **Two undephased sites under Heisenberg bonds.** Theorem 5 stops at one: with two, the SWAP rows of F138 (b) pair without a colouring. Whether every palindrome with two undephased sites is carried by a colouring or by a site symmetry's element (the colouring page measures so on the swept Heisenberg families) is not proven.
- **Undephased sites that keep no letter.** Theorem 4 reaches an undephased site only where H conserves one of its letters; Theorem 5 reaches one such site under Heisenberg bonds by other means. Where a Heisenberg or XX + YY bond touches it, the blocks of H over the dephased bits are matrices of size 2^|F| that do not commute, and the ends are the intertwiners of a matrix-valued connection: m_x·H_xy = H_xy·m_y at the near end, d_x·H_xy = H_x̄ȳ·d_y at the far one. F158 §(f5)'s condition over every word (traceless odd words, which it states is also sufficient) reads there as the traces of H's products around every closed walk of dephased bitstrings agreeing with those around the complement walk; which walks suffice is not worked out.
- **The census's carriers under Theorem 4.** The conditioned elements of the anticommuting-sum census, sums over the sectors (1 ± P_u)/2, condition on a letter that one undephased site keeps; Theorem 4 reaches the rows among them where every undephased site keeps one (on ZZ bonds, not where another undephased site carries an X or Y field); whether each is a sum of one-sector flat sections, and what the cross-sector terms are as elements, is not checked.

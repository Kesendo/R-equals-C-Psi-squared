## What this is about

Take a handful of qubits. Some pairs of them are joined by a bond along which an excitation can hop from one to the other. A hop has an amplitude, a complex number, and where the bonds close a loop the product of the amplitudes round it can carry a phase: a flux through the loop. A bond may also carry an Ising coupling ZZ, an energy that depends on whether its two ends are excited alike. Every qubit also stands in a bath. It loses its excitation at one rate, γ↓, and gains one at another, γ↑, and the two rates need not be the same from site to site. So each site has a rate point (γ↓, γ↑) in a plane. Nothing else acts: no fields, no dephasing.

The question asked here of such a system is whether its spectrum is paired: whether the eigenvalues of its Lindblad generator L, which moves the density matrix, come in pairs λ and −c − λ, mirrored about their mean −c/2, with c the sum of all the rates. There is a test for it that needs no eigenvalues. If the spectrum is paired about −c/2, the eigenvalues of L + c/2 come in pairs λ, −λ, and every odd power sum of them, Tr((L + c/2)^m) for odd m, is zero. One odd trace that is not zero rules the pairing out. A trace that is zero decides nothing; the next one may still speak.

These traces are polynomials in the couplings and the rates, and the first one that can speak in this setting is the fifth. [F137](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f137) has it in closed form, and the form is a picture: for every triangle of bonds, the three hopping strengths times the sine of the flux through it, times the area of the triangle that its three rate points span. A triangle at flux 0 or π, or with its three rate points on one line, adds nothing to it.

This proof does two things. It takes the formula that F137 uses on the way to that result and proves it on its own (Theorem A). The formula says what one part of a trace measures: the part proportional to s_l·δ_k, where s_l is half the sum of the two rates at a site l and δ_k half their difference at a site k. That part is an excitation carried from l to k, set against a hole carried the same way, each as the squared modulus of one amplitude, the amplitude summed over every arrangement of the other qubits, integrated over the time. It is a statement about transport between two sites, and it holds for every Hamiltonian that keeps the number of excitations. Then the proof uses the formula at the next odd trace (Theorem B): the part of the seventh trace that is of second order in the rates, on any graph, with ZZ on the bonds. The seventh sees what the fifth cannot. A triangle through the two sites now counts with a weight read off every bond of the graph. Part of that weight is the fifth trace again, times the second trace, which counts every bond by its |t|² and Δ² alone; what is left belongs to the bonds that touch the triangle. A triangle through one of the two sites counts as well where the other site is joined to one of its corners by a hopping bond: the pair borrows that triangle, the site standing in the corner's place. A ring of five sites enters, and a ring of four enters only where its bonds carry ZZ. Two settings on four sites that gate M11 of [`cube_moves_gate.py`](../../simulations/cube_moves_gate.py) finds unpaired, from exact characteristic polynomials, have a fifth trace of zero and a seventh that is not; this proof's gate finds no part of degree four there, so Theorem B gives that seventh trace whole.

The seventh trace has a second part, of fourth order in the rates, which this proof does not reach. The seventh trace that F137 records for a temperature gradient on a triangle with flux (one bath strength, a different temperature at each site), 430080, lies entirely in that part.

## Abstract

Setting: N qubits on any graph, H = Σ_bonds J(X_aX_b + Y_aY_b) + D_z(X_aY_b − Y_aX_b) + Δ_ab·Z_aZ_b, t_ab = J + iD_z the amplitude of the hop a → b, loss σ⁻ at γ↓_l and gain σ⁺ at γ↑_l per site, no fields, no dephasing, c = Σ_l (γ↓_l + γ↑_l), s_l = (γ↓_l + γ↑_l)/2, δ_l = (γ↓_l − γ↑_l)/2.

**Theorem A.** For every H that conserves the excitation number, fields and further terms included, the coefficient of s_l·δ_k in Tr e^{τ(L + c/2)} is τ·∫₀^τ (|f⁺_lk|² − |f⁻_lk|²) dx with f^±_lk(x) = Tr(σ^±_l·e^{−iHx}·σ^∓_k·e^{−iH(τ−x)}). Its coefficient of τ^m/m! is the coefficient of s_l·δ_k in the centred trace Tr((L + c/2)^m). The proof (§2) is the second Duhamel order in the rates. In the setting above, at order three in H, the lowest that survives, |f⁺_lk|² − |f⁻_lk|² = −4^N·τ³·Σ_j Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_jl), the direct hop against the detour over a third site, and this is F137's fifth trace (§4).

**Theorem B.** In the setting above the part of Tr((L + c/2)⁷) of degree two in the rates is −168·4^N·Σ_{l<k} β_lk·(γ↓_l·γ↑_k − γ↓_k·γ↑_l), with β_lk in closed form (§1): each triangle (l, k, j) by Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_jl) times a weight, 30·|t|² for a hopping bond that does not touch the triangle, 25 for one at j alone, 20 for one at l or k alone, 15 on the edges through j and 8 on the edge l–k, plus a quadratic form in the ZZ couplings, where a 30·|t|² and a 15·Δ² on every bond, the whole weight of a bond off the triangle, are the fifth trace times the second; the borrowed triangle, a triangle through k with a corner joined to l, or through l with a corner joined to k, at −15·|t|² of the joining bond; the rings of five through l and k at ±15, summed over the pairs of one ring 2520·4^N times the imaginary part of its loop times the pentagram of the five rate points minus their pentagon (signed areas, §1); the rings of four at 5·Im(loop) times a linear form in the Δ on their edges, zero without ZZ. The derivation (§3, §5, §6) reads the amplitudes at order five in H on a circle of time with two marks. That the trace has no parts other than of degree two and four rests on F137's three maps and on its argument at first order in H (§6), cited and not shown again, and is checked by the gate; the part of degree four is not derived.

The two breaks of gate M11 at N = 4 have a fifth trace of zero; this proof's gate finds no part of degree four in their seventh traces, so Theorem B gives them whole: −44800 and −20380416⁄25. Not reached by Theorem B: the 430080 of the temperature gradient on the unit triangle at flux π/2, which is all of degree four.

## 1. Setting and statements

N sites, H = Σ over bonds of J(X_aX_b + Y_aY_b) + D_z(X_aY_b − Y_aX_b) + Δ_ab·Z_aZ_b on any graph, a bond being free to carry ZZ without hopping. Write t_ab = J + iD_z for the amplitude of the hop a → b, t_ba its conjugate, and t_ab = 0 where no bond hops. With σ⁺ the raising operator and Z = −1 on the excited state, σ⁺σ⁻ = (1 − Z)/2, this is H = Σ_{a≠b} τ_ab·σ⁺_aσ⁻_b + Σ Δ_ab·Z_aZ_b with τ_ab = 2·t_ba, the coupling of the hop b → a. (τ with two indices is always this coupling; τ alone is the time.) Loss σ⁻_l at rate γ↓_l and gain σ⁺_l at rate γ↑_l on every site, no fields, no dephasing, c = Σ_l (γ↓_l + γ↑_l), and as in F137

  s_l = (γ↓_l + γ↑_l)/2,  δ_l = (γ↓_l − γ↑_l)/2,  L + c/2 = −i·ad_H + Σ_l (s_l·𝓗_l + δ_l·𝓐_l),

with 𝓗(ρ) = σ⁺ρσ⁻ + σ⁻ρσ⁺ and 𝓐(ρ) = σ⁻ρσ⁺ − σ⁺ρσ⁻ + ½{Z, ρ} on one site.

**Theorem A (transport formula).** Let H conserve the excitation number (any such H, fields and further terms included), the jumps being the loss and gain above and no others. For every pair of sites l, k the coefficient of s_l·δ_k in Tr e^{τ(L + c/2)}, as a power series in all the rates, is

  τ·∫₀^τ (|f⁺_lk(x)|² − |f⁻_lk(x)|²) dx,   f^±_lk(x) = Tr(σ^±_l·e^{−iHx}·σ^∓_k·e^{−iH(τ−x)}),

the traces over the Hilbert space. Its coefficient of τ^m/m! is the coefficient of s_l·δ_k in Tr((L + c/2)^m). It is antisymmetric in (l, k) and zero at l = k.

**Theorem B (the seventh centred trace at second order in the rates).** In the setting above, Tr((L + c/2)⁷) has no parts in the rates other than of degree two and four (the degrees: end of §6). The part of degree two is

  −168·4^N · Σ_{l<k} β_lk · (γ↓_l·γ↑_k − γ↓_k·γ↑_l),

that is, 336·4^N·β_lk is the coefficient of s_l·δ_k, with β_lk = −β_kl and

  β_lk = Σ_j Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_jl) · (W_lkj + Q_lkj)
       − 15·Σ_{i,j} |t_li|²·Im(t_ik·t_kj·t_ji) − 15·Σ_{i,j} |t_kj|²·Im(t_lj·t_ji·t_il)
       + 15·Σ_{a,b,d} Im(t_lk·t_kd·t_db·t_ba·t_al) − 15·Σ_{m,i,j} Im(t_lm·t_mk·t_kj·t_ji·t_il)
       + 5·Σ_{i,j} Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_ji·t_il)·(Δ_lk − Δ_li − Δ_ij − Δ_jk)
       + 5·Σ_{m,j} Im(t_lm·t_mk·t_kj·t_jl)·(Δ_lj + Δ_jk),

every inner sum running over the ordered tuples of sites that are distinct and not l or k, and

  W_lkj = 30·S − 10·(d_l + d_k) − 5·d_j − 2·|t_lk|²,   S = Σ_bonds |t|²,  d_v = Σ_w |t_vw|² over all sites w,

  Q_lkj = 15·Σ Δ² (ZZ bonds that do not touch l, k, j) + 5·Σ Δ² (one end at l or k, the other off the three)
        + 10·Σ Δ² (one end at j, the other off the three)
        + 5·(Δ_lj² + Δ_jk²) + (10⁄3)·Δ_lk² + 2·Δ_lj·Δ_jk − (8⁄3)·Δ_lk·(Δ_lj + Δ_jk).

Read by lines. **The triangle** (l, k, j) carries the imaginary part of its loop times a weight that counts every bond of the graph by how it touches the triangle. For a hopping bond: 30·|t|² if it does not touch, 25 hanging at the third corner j, 20 hanging at l or k, 15 for the triangle's edges through j, 8 for the edge l–k; in one rule, 30 for every bond, minus 10 for each touch of l or k, minus 5 for a touch of j, and the edge l–k two less. A ZZ bond counts through the quadratic form Q (a bond may carry both): off the triangle's edges 15·Δ², minus 10 for a touch of l or k, minus 5 for a touch of j, and on the edges the last four terms, which follow no such rule. **The borrowed triangle** (second line): where l is joined to a corner i of a triangle (i, k, j), the pair (l, k) reads that triangle's loop Im(t_ik·t_kj·t_ji), l in i's place, with the weight −15·|t_li|²; the same for k, so a site joined to one corner of a triangle pairs with each of the two corners it is not joined to. **The ring of five** (third line): a ring of five through l and k counts +15 where they are neighbours and −15 where they are two steps apart, which on one ring v₀ → v₁ → … → v₄ → v₀ adds up to 2520·4^N·Im(loop) times the pentagram of the five rate points minus their pentagon: Σ_n (γ↓_n·γ↑_{n+2} − γ↓_{n+2}·γ↑_n) minus Σ_n (γ↓_n·γ↑_{n+1} − γ↓_{n+1}·γ↑_n), the sites counted the way the loop runs, each sum twice a signed area. **The ring of four** (last two lines) enters only with ZZ: a ring of four with flux, times a linear form in the Δ on its edges, the edge l–k against the other three where l and k are neighbours, one way from l to k against the other where they are opposite (the ordered pairs (m, j) and (j, m) together give the loop times Δ_lj + Δ_jk − Δ_lm − Δ_mk); ZZ on the diagonals does not enter.

**The product and the cumulant.** Tr(L + c/2) is zero, and so is Tr((L + c/2)³): F137's third moment for every traceless H under cooling and heating (`ThermalThirdMomentClaim`) has a term in the transverse fields and one in Tr(Z_l·H²), and without fields both vanish for these bonds. With μ_m = Tr((L + c/2)^m)/4^N the seventh cumulant is then κ₇ = μ₇ − 21·μ₅·μ₂. At zero rates μ₂ = Tr((−i·ad_H)²)/4^N = −4·S − 2·ΣΔ², over all bonds, and the coefficient of s_l·δ_k in μ₅ is −120·Σ_j I_j with I_j = Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_jl) (§4, with the 5! of the Taylor coefficient). So the product 21·μ₅·μ₂ puts 336·(30·S + 15·ΣΔ²)·Σ_j I_j into the coefficient 336·β_lk of s_l·δ_k: a 30·|t|² and a 15·Δ² on every bond, all that a bond off the triangle carries, are the fifth trace again, times the second. What is left is the cumulant, and it is local, as the cumulants that add over connected clusters in [WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING](../../experiments/WHICH_BONDS_PAIR_UNDER_COOLING.md) are; that document makes the same split one order up, p₉ = 4^N·κ₉ + 36·p₇·p₂/4^N. In it a hopping bond counts 0 off the triangle, −5 hanging at j, −10 hanging at l or k, −15 on an edge through j and −22 on the edge l–k; a ZZ bond 0 off the triangle, −10 with one end at l or k, −5 with one end at j, and on the edges Q's last four terms less 15·Δ² for each edge. The borrowed triangle and the rings of five and of four carry no such product and stand in the cumulant as they are.

**The origin of the rate plane.** On one triangle alone the weights W + Q of the three pairs are equal when the three |t| are equal and the three edges carry one Δ; then the part of degree two is, like the fifth trace, flux times the area of the rate triangle and does not change when the rate points are moved by one vector. Moving every rate point by one vector (u, v) adds the same loss u and the same gain v on every site, and the lines through the origin are those of one ratio γ↑/γ↓, one temperature, where F137's carrier at one ratio pairs the spectrum. In general, β being antisymmetric, the part of degree two, as a polynomial in the rates, changes under the move by −168·4^N·(v·Σ_l γ↓_l·b_l − u·Σ_l γ↑_l·b_l), with b_l = Σ_k β_lk the flow of β out of the site l. So it is unchanged by every such move at every rate exactly when b = 0, β conserving its flow at every site; then it cannot tell a line of one ratio from a parallel line that misses the origin. On a line of rate points γ↑ = m·γ↓ + e it is −168·4^N·e·Σ_l γ↓_l·b_l. The temperature gradient that F137 records on the unit triangle at flux π/2 (table below) is such a line with equal weights, b = 0: its part of degree two is zero and the 430080 is all of degree four, which this proof does not derive.

## 2. Proof of Theorem A

Write G = −i·ad_H and expand Tr e^{τ(G + ε·𝓗_l + η·𝓐_k)} to first order in ε and in η. By Duhamel's formula and the cyclicity of the trace the coefficient of εη is

  τ·∫₀^τ Tr(𝓗_l·e^{xG}·𝓐_k·e^{(τ−x)G}) dx,

the trace now over the operators on the Hilbert space. Each of the four factors is a sum of maps ρ ↦ AρB, and the trace of ρ ↦ AρB over the operators is Tr A·Tr B; e^{xG} is ρ ↦ e^{−iHx}·ρ·e^{iHx}. So every term is a product of a trace over the ket side and one over the bra side. The term ½{Z, ρ} of 𝓐_k puts nothing that changes the excitation number beside the σ^± that 𝓗_l puts on each side, and the trace of a single σ^±_l between factors that conserve the number is zero. Of the remaining four terms the two that raise at l and at k on the same side vanish for the same reason. What is left is σ⁺_lρσ⁻_l with σ⁻_kρσ⁺_k, and σ⁻_lρσ⁺_l with −σ⁺_kρσ⁻_k:

  Tr(σ⁺_l e^{−iHx} σ⁻_k e^{−iH(τ−x)})·Tr(σ⁻_l e^{iH(τ−x)} σ⁺_k e^{iHx}) − (the same with σ⁺ and σ⁻ exchanged),

and the second trace of each product is the complex conjugate of the first. That is |f⁺_lk|² − |f⁻_lk|². The coefficient of τ^m/m! of Tr e^{τ(L + c/2)} is Tr((L + c/2)^m), and of it the terms with one 𝓗_l and one 𝓐_k. By cyclicity f⁺_kl(x) = f⁻_lk(τ − x) and f⁻_kl(x) = f⁺_lk(τ − x), so the integral is antisymmetric in (l, k) and zero at l = k. ∎

**The picture.** f⁺_lk is the amplitude that an excitation put in at l is taken out at k, summed over every arrangement of the other qubits, f⁻_lk the same for a hole; the rates at l and k read the one, squared, against the other. By the cyclicity above f⁻_lk(x) = f⁺_kl(τ − x), so the integral is also τ·∫₀^τ (|f⁺_lk|² − |f⁺_kl|²) dx, an excitation carried from l to k against one carried back. For a real H, fields and ZZ included, transposing the trace gives f⁺_lk(x) = f⁻_lk(τ − x) and the integral is zero: the formula reads the part of the transfer that is not reciprocal, which in the setting of §1 a flux other than 0 and π supplies, against the reciprocity of [F119 §7](PROOF_ANTILINEAR_TRIANGLE.md#7-one-level-up-the-generators-transpose-and-the-two-ends-of-a-channel) with its flux reversal. With one side as the ket and its conjugate as the bra: the excitation has to be carried from l to k on the ket side and on the bra side, and where the two take different ways round a loop of the graph their interference carries the flux. The transport here is the excitation's, between sites; F119's transport law is another object.

## 3. The time circle

From here on H is the bond Hamiltonian of §1, without fields, which the spin flip below needs. The spin flip X⊗N conjugates H to its complex conjugate H̄ and exchanges σ⁺ with σ⁻, so with y = τ − x

  f⁺_lk = Σ_n (−i)ⁿ·g_n(x, y),   f⁻_lk = Σ_n (−i)ⁿ·ḡ_n(x, y),   g_n = Σ_{p+q=n} A_pq·x^p·y^q/(p!·q!),   A_pq = Tr(σ⁺_l H^p σ⁻_k H^q),

ḡ_n being g_n with every coupling conjugated. Every g_n is a sum of products of couplings times real polynomials in x and y, so ḡ_n is its complex conjugate, and the terms (n, n′) and (n′, n) of |f⁺|² − |f⁻|² together are 2i·((−i)ⁿ·i^n′ − (−i)^n′·iⁿ)·Im(g_n·ḡ_n′); g₀ = 0 for l ≠ k. At order five, the order of the seventh trace:

  |f⁺_lk|² − |f⁻_lk|² at order five = 4·[Im(g₁·ḡ₄) − Im(g₂·ḡ₃)].   (1)

To evaluate g_n, place the operators on a circle of circumference τ = x + y in the order in which they act, the word read from the right: σ⁺_l at 0, then the arc of length y with q terms of H, σ⁻_k at y, then the arc of length x with p terms, back to 0; the polynomial x^p·y^q/(p!·q!) is the volume of the ordered points on the two arcs. The trace is a product over the sites. A site that carries no σ^± gives a factor 2 (with ZZ, through the average below). A site that carries some is excited from each of its σ⁺ forward to its next σ⁻ and gives 1 if they alternate round the circle and 0 otherwise. A term Δ_ab·Z_aZ_b is diagonal and multiplies by ±Δ_ab according to the two sites' states where it stands.

**A simple path.** For h hops along l = v₀ → v₁ → … → v_h = k through sites all different, with the hop v_{i−1} → v_i at the point p_i, the site v_i is excited on the arc from p_i forward to p_{i+1} (p₀ = 0, p_{h+1} = y). Every position of the hops is allowed, so with κ = 2^{N−2}

  g_h ∋ κ·2^{1−h}·τ_{v_h v_{h−1}}⋯τ_{v₁v₀}·τ^h,

independent of x: g₁ = κ·τ_kl·τ and the two-step part of g₂ is (κ/2)·Σ_j τ_kj·τ_jl·τ².

**The hard core.** Where a site is visited more than once it carries four or more σ^±, and only the positions in which they alternate are allowed. The weights of the bonds that share a site with a path come from these fractions (§5); the rest come from words in which no site carries more than two σ^±, with the factor 2 of each site that carries none.

**ZZ.** With z terms Z_aZ_b among the n, the points of the hops fixed, the terms ZZ integrate to ⟨Φ^z⟩/z!, where Φ = Σ_bonds Δ_ab·∮ z_a(u)·z_b(u) du is the ZZ energy integrated round the circle, z_a = −1 where site a is excited and +1 where it is not, and ⟨·⟩ averages over the two states of every site that carries no σ^±, for which ⟨z_b⟩ = 0 and ⟨z_b·z_b′⟩ = δ_bb′. So a word of h hops along a simple path with z terms ZZ contributes

  κ·2^{1−h}·(the couplings of the path)·τ^{h+z}·F_hz(y/τ),   F_h0 = 1,   (2)

F_hz the integral of ⟨Φ^z⟩/z! over the positions of the hops at τ = 1, each hop's point uniform on the circle. Two formulas carry every average below: ζ_a = ∮ z_a du is 1 minus twice the length of the arc on which a is excited, and ∮ z_a·z_b du is 1 minus twice the two lengths plus four times the length on which both are excited.

## 4. The fifth trace

At order three, |f⁺|² − |f⁻|² = −4·Im(g₁·ḡ₂). The part of g₂ with one hop and one ZZ is a real multiple of τ_kl and drops out against g₁ = κ·τ_kl·τ; the two-step part gives −2κ²·τ³·Σ_j Im(τ_kl·conj(τ_kj·τ_jl)) = −4^N·τ³·Σ_j Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_jl), the direct hop on the one side against the detour over a third site on the other. This is step (3) of F137's derivation, and the rest is there.

## 5. The seventh trace without ZZ

Five hops in (1). Their product has to close a loop with an imaginary part: the triangle (l, k, j) with one bond walked there and back besides, a triangle through k reached from l over one bond or through l reached from k, or a ring of five. Write I_j = Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_jl); then Im(τ_kl·conj(τ_kj·τ_jl)) = 8·I_j, and |τ_e|² = 4·|t_e|².

**The triangle with a bond e walked there and back.** In g₃ the word is the direct hop with the bond, in g₄ the detour over j with the bond. A bond that shares a site with the path leaves one site more without a σ^±, which doubles the trace, and on the shared site four σ^± have to alternate, which only a fraction of the orders do. With e the bond and κ·τ^n the common factor, the coefficient of the couplings is

| position of e | in g₃ beside the direct hop | in g₄ beside the detour over j |
|---|---|---|
| touches none of the path's sites | 1⁄4 | 1⁄8 |
| one end on the path, the other off it | 1⁄6 (2 of 6 orders, at l or k) | 1⁄12 (8 of 24, at l, k or j) |
| the edge l–j or j–k | (one end at l or k: 1⁄6) | 1⁄24 (the hop on that edge stands twice; 2 words) |
| the edge l–k | (x³ + y³)/6 | φ₁/2 |

with φ₁ = (x⁴ + y⁴)/6 + (x³y + xy³)/2 at τ = 1 (of the words with all four terms on one arc 4 alternate, with three on one arc and one on the other 3, with two and two none; a word with p and q terms on the arcs weighs x^p·y^q/(p!·q!), and three sites carry σ^±, so the factor is κ/2). On the edge l–k only the orders with all three terms on one arc alternate in g₃, as the two-site problem shows directly: there f⁺ = −i·(τ_kl/|τ_kl|)·(sin |τ_kl|x + sin |τ_kl|y). One more word enters g₄ on the edge l–k: the direct hop twice and the other two edges of the triangle walked back, τ_kl·τ_kl·τ_jk·τ_lj, with the coefficient φ₂/2, φ₂ = (x⁴ + y⁴)/12 + (x³y + xy³)/6 + x²y²/2 (2, 1 and 2 words), and against g₁ it carries −8·I_j·|τ_kl|².

Put into (1), with the two-step part of g₂ against g₃ and g₁ against g₄, the coefficient of 8·I_j·|τ_e|²·κ² in ∫₀¹(Im g₁ḡ₄ − Im g₂ḡ₃) dy is: for a bond off the triangle 1⁄8 + 1⁄8 = 1⁄4; hanging at l or k 1⁄12 + 1⁄12 = 1⁄6; hanging at j 1⁄12 + 1⁄8 = 5⁄24 (beside the direct hop a bond at j touches nothing); an edge through j 1⁄24 + 1⁄12 = 1⁄8; the edge l–k ½·∫(φ₁ − φ₂) + ½·∫(x³ + y³)/6 = 1⁄40 + 1⁄24 = 1⁄15. The coefficient of s_l·δ_k in the seventh trace is 7!·4 times the integral, and 7!·4·κ²·8·4 = 40320·4^N = 336·4^N·120, so the five fractions are the weights 30, 20, 25, 15 and 8 of |t_e|². The direct hop with a triangle other than (l, k, j) walked once round comes with both directions of the loop at the same polynomial, the two being exchanged by renaming the triangle's sites off the path, and so has no imaginary part against g₁; round (l, k, j) itself the two directions are the two words of the edge l–k above, with different polynomials.

**The borrowed triangle and the ring of five.** In −Im(g₂·ḡ₃) the two-step path l → m → k stands against the three-step path l → i → j → k, each simple, with coefficients κ/2 and κ/4. At m = i the product is |τ_il|² times the loop of the triangle (i, k, j), which is the borrowed triangle, l in i's place, its sign the minus of (1), and at m = j the same for k; at m different from both it is the ring of five with l and k two steps apart. The ring of five through the bond l–k is g₁ against the four-step path in g₄, coefficient κ/8, with the plus of (1). Each comes to 7!·4·κ²·(1/8)·32 = 5040·4^N = 336·4^N·15 times the imaginary part of the product of the five amplitudes, with the signs of Theorem B.

## 6. The seventh trace with ZZ

With ZZ the five terms of H in (1) can be three hops and two ZZ, on a triangle, or four hops and one ZZ, on a ring of four; five hops are §5 unchanged, and one hop with ZZ has no imaginary part. Write P = xy at τ = 1, so ∫₀¹P dy = 1⁄6 and ∫₀¹P² dy = 1⁄30. The integrals (2), each over the positions of the hops on the circle:

- **One hop, one ZZ.** The two sites are in opposite states on the arc that holds the hop's point and in equal states on the other: F₁₁ = −Δ_lk·(1 − 4P).
- **One hop, two ZZ.** ⟨Φ²⟩ = Δ_lk²·(∮ z_l z_k)² + Σ_b (Δ_lb·ζ_l + Δ_kb·ζ_k)² + Σ Δ² over the bonds off l and k, b running over the other sites. Over the hop's point ζ_l² and ζ_k² average to 1⁄3 and ζ_l·ζ_k to 2P − 1⁄3, whose integral over y is zero:
  F₁₂ = ½·[Δ_lk²·(1 − 4P) + Σ_b ((Δ_lb² + Δ_kb²)/3 + 2·Δ_lb·Δ_kb·(2P − 1⁄3)) + Σ_off Δ²].
- **Two hops over j, one ZZ.** For two sites that follow each other on the path, ∮ z z averages to −1⁄3 whatever y is: for the first two sites of a path, excited from 0 to the hop point a and from a to the next point b, it is 1 − 2b where a < b and 2b − 1 where b < a, and each half integrates to −1⁄6; for l and k it averages to 2P − 1⁄3:
  F₂₁ = −(Δ_lj + Δ_jk)/3 + Δ_lk·(2P − 1⁄3).
- **Two hops over j, two ZZ.** Here ζ_l, ζ_j and ζ_k are uncorrelated, and the three integrals Z_e = ∮ z z of the triangle's edges have the second moments V below, each integrated piece by piece over the six orders of the two hop points and the point y:
  F₂₂ = ½·[Σ_{e,e′} V_ee′·Δ_e·Δ_e′ + Σ_b (Δ_lb² + Δ_jb² + Δ_kb²)/3 + Σ_off Δ²],
  V_lj,lj = V_jk,jk = 1⁄3,  V_lk,lk = 1⁄3 − 4P/3 + 8P²/3,  V_lj,jk = 4P²,  V_lj,lk = V_jk,lk = −2P/3 + 4P²/3,
  b now off l, j and k. As a check, Σ_e Z_e depends only on the number of excitations on the two arcs, and is 3x − y, −1 or 3y − x on the six orders; its second moment 1 − 4P + 16P² is the sum of the entries of V.
- **Three hops l → i → j → k, one ZZ.** Neighbours on the path −1⁄3; the two pairs one step further apart have independent arcs and average to zero; l and k as before:
  F₃₁ = −(Δ_li + Δ_ij + Δ_jk)/3 + Δ_lk·(2P − 1⁄3).

**The triangle.** In (1), g₁ against the detour with two ZZ, the detour against the direct hop with two ZZ, and the direct hop with one ZZ against the detour with one:

  ∫₀¹ (Im g₁ḡ₄ − Im g₂ḡ₃) dy ∋ 4κ²·Σ_j I_j·∫₀¹ (F₂₂ + F₁₂ − F₁₁·F₂₁) dy,

and 7!·4·4κ² = 336·4^N·15, so Q_lkj is 15 times the integral. Term by term: a bond off the three sites ½ + ½, so 15; one end at l or k 1⁄6 + 1⁄6, so 5; one end at j 1⁄6 + ½ (for the direct hop j is a spectator and the bond is off the path), so 10; two bonds from one outside site to two of the three have no mixed term after the integral over y; Δ_lj² and Δ_jk² 1⁄6 + 1⁄6, so 5; Δ_lk² 1⁄10 + 1⁄6 − 2⁄45 = 2⁄9, so 10⁄3; Δ_lj·Δ_jk 2⁄15, so 2; Δ_lk·Δ_lj and Δ_lk·Δ_jk −1⁄15 − 1⁄9 = −8⁄45, so −8⁄3.

**The ring of four.** Where l and k are neighbours on a ring l → k → j → i → l: g₁ against the three-step path with one ZZ, and the direct hop with one ZZ against the three-step path, 4κ²·Im(t_lk·t_kj·t_ji·t_il)·∫(F₃₁ − F₁₁) dy, the integral being (Δ_lk − Δ_li − Δ_ij − Δ_jk)/3. Where they are opposite, the two-step path over m against the two-step path over j with one ZZ, −4κ²·Im(t_lm·t_mk·t_kj·t_jl)·F₂₁ summed over the ordered pairs (m, j); the loop changes sign when m and j are exchanged and the term Δ_lk of F₂₁ does not, so it drops out, and (Δ_lj + Δ_jk)/3 remains. With 7!·4·4κ²/3 = 336·4^N·5 these are the last two lines of Theorem B.

**A second way to one number.** At one Δ on the three edges of a triangle alone, N = 3, H_ZZ is a function of the excitation number, 3Δ for 0 or 3 excitations and −Δ for 1 or 2, and commutes with the hopping. Sorting f⁺ by the number of excitations on its two arcs, f⁺ = e^{iΔτ}·[f° + (e^{−4iΔx} − 1)·U(y) + (e^{−4iΔy} − 1)·U(x)] with f° the amplitude at Δ = 0 and U(u) the entry (k, l) of e^{−iuτ̂}, τ̂ the matrix of the couplings τ_ab (no excitation on the arc x beside one on the arc y, or two beside three). The order five of |f⁺|² − |f⁻|² at Δ² is 256·Δ²·xy·I_j at τ = 1, which gives Q = 10·Δ², and the form above gives 5 + 5 + 10⁄3 + 2 − 16⁄3 = 10.

**The degrees.** By F137's three maps every trace of a power is even in the rates, and its degree in H plus its degree in δ is even. At the seventh power the part of degree zero is Tr((−i·ad_H)⁷) = 0, the spectrum of ad_H being symmetric about zero, and the part of degree six has one term of H, which is zero by the argument F137 gives for the first order in H at the fifth power, that argument not counting the rates. Both the maps and that argument are F137's and are not shown again here. A term of degree two has five terms of H, so its degree in δ is odd: it has one s and one δ, and Theorem A reads it. That leaves degree four, three terms of H with four rates. ∎

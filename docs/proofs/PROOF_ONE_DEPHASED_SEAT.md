## A common carrier for the triangle's whole field line

Let sites (d, u, v) = (0, 1, 2) form a Heisenberg triangle with real nonzero couplings s = J_du, t = J_dv and r = J_uv. Only d is dephased, along Z at a positive rate. Write

  H(h) = H_b + hF,
  H_b = s(XXI + YYI + ZZI) + t(XIX + YIY + ZIZ) + r(IXX + IYY + IZZ),
  F = IXI + IIY,   e₂ = st + sr + tr.

The question here is one **common carrier**, independent of h: [H_b, W] = [F, W] = 0 and {ZII, W} = 0. F158's Lemma 3 turns an invertible such W into a palindrome at every h. The triangle family and the SWAP construction already supply sufficient carriers; the statement below exhausts this common space. The sweep for that exhaustion reached F138 and F158 in the formula registry, their proofs and the colouring page, the typed claims and `f138_converse_failures`, the glossary and CAUGHT_ERRORS, and the Python/C# Confirmations and IBM experiment records. They supplied the two carriers and the invertibility and quantifier fences, but no classification of this common space or hardware test of it.

**Proposition.** For s,t,r ∈ ℝ \ {0}, the common space is exactly ℂ·W(s,t) when (s − t)e₂ = 0, and {0} otherwise, where

  W(s,t) = X_d ⊗ (st²·II + s³·XY + st²·YX − st²·ZZ)
         + Y_d ⊗ (s²t·II + t³·XY + s²t·YX − s²t·ZZ).

Moreover W(s,t)² = (s² + t²)³·I, so every nonzero element of the common space is invertible. Its dimension is one even at the intersection s = t, r = −s/2. This classifies a single W valid for every h, not all carriers at a fixed h.

**Proof.** The jump condition gives W = X_d ⊗ A + Y_d ⊗ B. On (u,v), the commutant of XI + IY has the basis

  (E₀, …, E₅) = (II, XI, IY, XY, YX − ZZ, YZ + ZX).

Each listed element commutes with XI + IY, they are independent, and they exhaust the commutant: XI + IY has eigenvalues 2, 0, −2 with multiplicities 1, 2, 1, giving dimension 1² + 2² + 1² = 6. Write A = Σ a_j E_j and B = Σ b_j E_j. The XYZ, IIZ, IYY, IZI, YZX and IXX coefficients of [H_b,W]/(2i), respectively, give

  r a₁ = 0,   t a₂ = 0,   −t a₅ = 0,   −s b₁ = 0,   −r b₂ = 0,   s b₅ = 0.

All three couplings are nonzero, so only

  W = X_d ⊗ [a·II + b·XY + c·(YX − ZZ)]
    + Y_d ⊗ [d·II + e·XY + f·(YX − ZZ)]

remains. The IXZ, IYZ, IZY, ZIX, ZIY and ZYI coefficients give

  tb − sf = 0,   sc − tf = 0,   tc − se = 0,
  −sc + td = 0,   −ta + se = 0,   −sa + tf = 0.

Thus c = a, d = f = sa/t, e = ta/s and b = s²a/t². Every candidate is (a/st²)W(s,t); only s and t were divided by, so no exceptional nonzero-coupling case has been discarded. Direct multiplication now gives

  [H_b,W(s,t)] = 2i(s − t)e₂·[s(XIZ − XZI) − t(YIZ − YZI)],
  [F,W(s,t)] = 0,   {ZII,W(s,t)} = 0,
  W(s,t)² = (s² + t²)³·I.

The four strings in the bracket are independent, with nonzero s and t. Hence a nonzero common solution exists exactly when (s − t)e₂ = 0; its square proves invertibility for real s,t. ∎

At s = t this is the SWAP carrier: W(s,s)/(2√2 s³) = SWAP_uv·[(X + Y)/√2]^⊗3. Off that branch e₂ = 0 is the reciprocal-coupling family below. The proposition does not imply that a fixed H(h) off the two branches fails to pair: at h = 0, for example, XXX is a carrier at every coupling. Where the common space is nonzero it is ℂ·W(s, t), and Z^⊗3, which keeps H_b and reverses F, maps it to itself, so W(s, t) is homogeneous under Z^⊗3; the paragraph on the sheet and blindness in [the section on blind dephased seats](#blind-dephased-seats-and-palindromes-beyond-colourings-and-site-symmetries) starts from this homogeneity.

**Exact gate:** [`triangle_common_carrier_gate.py`](../../simulations/triangle_common_carrier_gate.py), run with `python simulations/triangle_common_carrier_gate.py`. It reconstructs the full 32-coordinate field constraint by dense matrices and the 12 remaining bond commutators by sparse Pauli multiplication, comparing every column to the dense route. An 11 × 11 minor is exactly −r²s³t⁶, nonzero throughout the stated domain; the remaining column equations give the displayed factorization. The square and commutators are symbolic identities. Eight full dense common-kernel controls include both branches, their intersection, s = −t and off-locus points; changing a coupling, unequal fields and removing a carrier term break the same equations. The h = 0 control guards the distinction between common and fixed-field carriers.

**Proposition (exact, computer-assisted).** A row of this family is palindromic exactly when it lies in one of

- **A**, a colouring: every nonzero field lies along one letter p ∈ {X, Y};
- **B**, the u ↔ v site symmetry: J_du = J_dv, and a π rotation R_n about an axis n ⊥ z has R_n f_d = f_d and R_n f_u = f_v;
- **C**, the reciprocal locus: e₂ = J_duJ_dv + J_duJ_uv + J_dvJ_uv = 0, f_d = 0, f_u and f_v ⊥ z, |f_u| = |f_v|.

The three overlap (a row of C at h = 0, or with f_u ∥ f_v, is coloured) and are not of one kind: A and B are named by their carriers, C by a locus in the parameters, so the statement covers the palindromic locus rather than sorting carriers. It speaks of rows at a fixed field, the companion of the proposition above, which speaks of one carrier for every h.

**Sufficiency.** By F158's Lemma 3 and its theorem an invertible W with [H, W] = 0 and {Z_d, W} = 0 makes the row pair. In A it is p^⊗3 (F138). In B it is SWAP_uv·(n·σ)^⊗3: the rotation fixes every Heisenberg bond and turns Z_d into −Z_d because n ⊥ z, the swap exchanges J_du and J_dv, which are equal, and the two together carry the fields to themselves. In C all three couplings are nonzero, since with one of them zero e₂ is the product of the other two. Fields with f_u ∥ f_v are coloured; otherwise a global rotation (the π rotation about x, the quarter turn about z), which fixes the bonds and sends Z_d to ±Z_d, brings the field to h(X_u + Y_v), where W(s, t) of the proposition above carries it.

**Necessity.** Write a *region* as {E = 0, every q ∈ 𝒰 nonzero}, for a list E of polynomial equations and a list 𝒰 of factors recorded nonzero, in the six parameters.

1. *The ends.* By Lemma 1's second corollary in F158, near and far are the kernels of W ↦ [H, W] on the Pauli strings whose d-letter is in {I, Z} and in {X, Y}. Each end is closed under the adjoint and so spanned by Hermitian elements, real combinations of strings, and [H, W] = 0 becomes a real linear system each of whose entries is, up to the common factor 2i, ± one of J_du, J_dv, J_uv, h_d, h_u, h_v. A row pairs exactly when near = far ≥ 1.
2. *Ten words.* The swap u ↔ v (with J_du ↔ J_dv) and the quarter turn about z preserve the family, the jump and the three classes, and leave the 27 words in ten orbits: XXX, XXY, XXZ, XYY, XYZ, XZZ, ZXX, ZXY, ZXZ, ZZZ.
3. *Two charts.* Every entry is linear and homogeneous in the six parameters, so neither count changes under a common nonzero scaling, and neither does membership in A, B or C, whose conditions are homogeneous too. A connected row has J_du ≠ 0, or J_du = 0 and J_dv ≠ 0, so the charts J_du = 1 and (J_du = 0, J_dv = 1) cover every connected row up to scale; for XXY, where the case splits in that chart grow far larger, the mirrored pair J_dv = 1 and (J_dv = 0, J_du = 1) does the same.
4. *Elimination.* Gaussian elimination of the far system with case splits. A pivot is a nonzero constant or a polynomial all of whose factors are in 𝒰; otherwise the vanishing of each factor and the case where none vanishes are separate branches. Rows are divided only by factors in 𝒰, and a branch is dropped only when 1 lies in its ideal or a factor in 𝒰 does. The finished branches are the *far leaves*: regions that together contain every connected row, each carrying its exact far count k.
5. *Decisions.* For a word, each class becomes finitely many ideals: A one per letter p, B one per axis among x, y, x + y and x − y (whenever some axis n ⊥ z satisfies B, one of these does: a nonzero field off z pins n to one of them, zero fields and z fields on u and v with h_v = −h_u admit every n, and a nonzero z field on d admits none), C one. A region lies inside a class when every generator g of one of its ideals vanishes on it, checked with a new variable ξ as 1 ∈ ⟨E, 1 − ξ·∏𝒰·g⟩. A far leaf with k ≥ 1 is decided on its own region, either as lying inside a class (the first case) or as holding no connected palindromic real row (the other three), when
   - it lies inside A, B or C;
   - two couplings vanish on it;
   - it has no real point, shown by an equation that is a sum of monomials with every exponent even and every coefficient of one sign, so that it vanishes only where each monomial does, one of them built of variables in 𝒰, or by a sum of squares Σ c_i·p_i² (c_i > 0) recorded for the region, which vanishes on it and has a term p_k each of whose factors is in 𝒰 or cannot vanish there (1 ∈ ⟨E, factor⟩), both checked on use;
   - or the word is ZZZ and k < 4: there I, M, M² and M³ with M = Z_d + Z_u + Z_v (eigenvalues ±1, ±3) commute with H and with Z_d at every parameter point, so near ≥ 4 > k.

   A leaf decided by none of these has its near system eliminated on its region, and every subregion with near = k is palindromic and is decided the same way; one decided by none would be reported open.

Over the ten words the far elimination gives 1,305 leaves, 938 with k ≥ 1. A leaf can be empty over ℂ, since a branch is dropped only when 1 or a factor in 𝒰 lies in its ideal, not when its zero set merely lies inside the zeros of 𝒰, so these count branches, which may also overlap, and an empty leaf lies inside every class vacuously. ## Scope, and what would falsify it

The triangle's palindromic locus at N = 3 needs Heisenberg bonds on the triangle with at most one coupling zero, the jump Z on one site alone and letter fields; its counterexample would be such a row that pairs outside A ∪ B ∪ C, or a row of A, B or C that does not pair. The triangle with planar fields needs Heisenberg bonds on the triangle with at most one coupling zero, the jump Z on d alone, no field on d and fields perpendicular to z; its counterexample would be such a row that pairs outside A, B, C and E, or a row of them that does not pair. The carriers' locality lemma needs Pauli jumps and a row that pairs through one far element, where no mirror conditioned on the bits of a site arises; its counterexample would be a mirror that factorises over the sites, alone or followed by a site permutation, of such a row whose far element is no product times a site permutation, and its readings are at the rows named. The N = 4 proposition's counterexample would be a real row of the family with a, g ≠ 0 that does not pair; the conjecture's would be a palindrome under Heisenberg bonds with letter fields and one dephased seat, without a colouring, whose dephased seat is not blind. The paragraph on the sheet and blindness: the triangle with Heisenberg bonds of which at most one is zero and the jump Z on d alone, nonzero fields on u and v perpendicular to z with sin θ₋ ≠ 0 (θ₋ the angle from f_v to f_u), and far elements of odd height. At N = 4: the widened family (the difference-only coupling, the fields −h·X₁ + h·X₂ and g·Y₃ alone, none on 0, J₀₃ = 0, h, g, b₀ and b₃ nonzero) and a far element even under the pair's swap; a counterexample would be one that does not commute with Y₃, one with a nonzero singlet block at b₃ ≠ −b₀, or one whose singlet block at b₃ = −b₀ is not a multiple of h·X₀ + b₀·Y₀Y₃.

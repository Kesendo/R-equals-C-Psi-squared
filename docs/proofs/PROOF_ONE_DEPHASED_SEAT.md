## What this is about

Take a few qubits joined by Heisenberg bonds, and let only one of them stand in light: the repository's word, here, for dephasing along Z on that one site, the dephased seat, while every other site stands in none ([what the light does](../quantum/DEPHASING_TRANSLATED.md)). The light makes every coherence between two bitstrings that differ at the seat fade. Every way the whole state can fade, an eigenmode of the generator built from the Hamiltonian H and the light together, has a rate and a frequency, and the whole spectrum, rates and frequencies together, can be mirror-symmetric about one centre σ, here the rate γ of the light on the seat (half the rate at which a coherence that differs at the seat fades under the light alone): for each way of fading there is a partner that oscillates at the same frequency and fades as much faster than σ as the first fades slower. That is the palindrome. A row, one choice of couplings and fields, pairs when it keeps the palindrome. [F158](PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) decides it by a count. With the one jump, Z on the seat, the palindrome holds exactly when as many independent operators commute with H and with the jump as commute with H and anticommute with it, and an invertible operator of the second kind makes it hold; the body calls the operators of the two kinds near and far elements, and an invertible far element a carrier. Under Heisenberg bonds every palindrome in the earlier sweeps of the colouring page was carried by one of two forms of carrier: a [colouring](../../experiments/THE_PALINDROME_AS_A_COLOURING.md), a single Pauli string of the second kind, or a site symmetry that fixes the seat, on the triangle of the next paragraph the swap of two sites composed with a π rotation of every spin about an axis perpendicular to z. This page asks what pairs beyond the two.

**The triangle.** Three qubits d, u and v form a triangle of Heisenberg bonds with real couplings J_du, J_dv and J_uv, at most one of them zero; the light falls on d alone, and each site may carry a field along one Pauli letter X, Y or Z, of any size. A row of this family pairs exactly when it lies in one of three classes, a result proven by an exact computer-assisted elimination with a stored certificate and an independent gate:

- **A**, a colouring: every nonzero field lies along one and the same letter, X or Y;
- **B**, the swap of u and v: J_du = J_dv, and a π rotation about an axis perpendicular to z keeps the field on d and carries the field on u onto the field on v;
- **C**, the reciprocal locus: e₂ = J_du·J_dv + J_du·J_uv + J_dv·J_uv = 0 (for nonzero couplings, the reciprocals of the three couplings add up to zero), no field on d, and fields on u and v perpendicular to z of equal size.

C needs couplings of both signs, and on it, with the couplings to d unequal and the two fields nonzero and along different letters, the triangle pairs with neither a colouring nor a site symmetry fixing d; at J = (3, 6, −2) with the fields X_u + Y_v its carrier is not even a Clifford element. Sweeps at one bond weight and a scout of about ten thousand rows had missed it ([CAUGHT_ERRORS](../CAUGHT_ERRORS.md)). With all three couplings nonzero, a single carrier serving the whole field line h·(X_u + Y_v), every h at once, exists exactly when (J_du − J_dv)·e₂ = 0, and is then unique up to a factor (the page's first proposition).

**The same factor: the dephased seat is blind.** Put one excitation on d and let the bonds move it: F157 calls d [blind](../ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f157) when it cannot reach every direction of the one-excitation space, which happens exactly when some eigenvector of the bonds' weighted, signed graph Laplacian vanishes at d. On the triangle that test is the determinant of the three vectors e_d, 𝓛·e_d and 𝓛²·e_d (𝓛 the Laplacian), and it is −(J_du − J_dv)·e₂. Each factor is one way to be blind. At J_du = J_dv the swap of u and v keeps the bonds, and its antisymmetric eigenvector vanishes at d: B's coupling condition. At e₂ = 0, e₂ being the Laplacian's spanning-tree sum, the Laplacian drops to rank one and every seat is blind: C's coupling condition. So on the triangle with letter fields every palindrome that is not a colouring sits on a blind dephased seat. On the triangle blindness alone does not make a row pair; the fields still decide.

**Beyond the triangle.** At N = 4 a family with the light on site 0 alone, H = e·σ₁·σ₂ + a·(σ₀ − σ₃)·(σ₁ − σ₂) − h·X₁ + h·X₂ + g·Y₃ (σ_l the vector of Pauli matrices on site l), pairs at every real a ≠ 0, g ≠ 0 and every e and h, with a carrier in closed form that depends on h; for h ≠ 0 the row has neither a colouring nor a site symmetry fixing site 0, and every seat is blind, the Laplacian's null space being two-dimensional. At N = 7 a dephased site joined by one positive coupling to all six sites of two triangles with no bonds between them, the triangles with the different weights the page gives whose Hamiltonians one operator commuting with the triangle's collective spin carries onto each other (equivalent as spin representations carrying their Hamiltonians, not as weighted graphs), and with one field h ≠ 0 along X on every site of one triangle and along Y on every site of the other, pairs with neither a colouring nor a site symmetry and with no rank drop of its Laplacian, and its seat is blind all the same: it reaches each triangle only through the triangle's collective spin. Joining further sites to the centre alone, with distinct positive couplings and no fields, extends this to every N ≥ 7 (argued; the gate reads N = 7). At N = 5 the N = 4 carrier extends, as a product with an operator on the new spin, to no fifth spin attached by Heisenberg bonds (for a, h ≠ 0); a carrier that entangles the fifth spin is not excluded. In a measured sweep at N = 4 over 111,312 connected random draws on four sites, with couplings from a small set, zero included, and letter fields, 172 pairing draws had no colouring (draws can repeat); 171 of them carry a site symmetry fixing site 0, and the one that does not lies on the family above. That every palindrome without a colouring needs a blind dephased seat, under Heisenberg bonds with letter fields, is a conjecture. With fields of any direction in the plane perpendicular to z the pattern does not hold: with no field on d the triangle has a fourth class, E, of three polynomial relations; with a field on d, where the locus is open, a family D on the path pairs; and neither sits on a blind seat.

**What the carriers are not.** A mirror here is a map carrying the generator L onto −L − 2σ or onto −L† − 2σ. Where a row has one independent operator of each kind only, the identity and one carrier, a mirror that factorises over the sites, alone or followed by multiplication with a site permutation, exists only where the carrier is a product times a site permutation that fixes the seat. The carriers of C and of the N = 4 family are not, at the rows read, so no site-by-site move of the letter cube can find them; at their rows the cube's silence is this lemma's answer. The last part reads far elements by the parity of their height on the letter cube (the number of X and Y letters in each string) and sets their existence against the seat's blindness at N = 3, 4 and 5, each statement under its own conditions; at N = 5 such an element exists at a seat that is not blind with the fields off one line, at rows that need not pair.

Codex (OpenAI) proved the common-carrier proposition and found the N = 4 carrier, the N = 5 exclusion and the N = 7 construction; the elements of classes A and B were known from F138. The triangle's rules are typed in `OneDephasedSeatClaim` and live at `inspect --root trianglelocus`. Open: whether blindness is needed at every N, which fields make a blind seat pair, a carrier at N = 5 that entangles the fifth spin, and the triangle's locus for fields off the letters with a z part or with a field on d, and for other bonds.

## A common carrier for the triangle's whole field line

Let sites (d, u, v) = (0, 1, 2) form a Heisenberg triangle with real nonzero couplings s = J_du, t = J_dv and r = J_uv. Only d is dephased, along Z at a positive rate. Write

  H(h) = H_b + hF,
  H_b = s(XXI + YYI + ZZI) + t(XIX + YIY + ZIZ) + r(IXX + IYY + IZZ),
  F = IXI + IIY,   e₂ = st + sr + tr.

The question here is one **common carrier**, independent of h: [H_b, W] = [F, W] = 0 and {ZII, W} = 0. F158's Lemma 3 turns an invertible such W into a palindrome at every h. The triangle family and the SWAP construction already supply sufficient carriers; the statement below exhausts this common space. The sweep for that exhaustion reached F138 and F158 in the formula registry, their proofs and the colouring page, the typed claims and `f138_converse_failures`, the glossary and CAUGHT_ERRORS, and the Python/C# Confirmations and IBM experiment records. They supplied the two carriers and the invertibility and quantifier fences, but no classification of this common space or hardware test of it.

**Proposition.** For s,t,r ∈ ℝ \ {0}, the common space is exactly ℂ·W(s,t) when (s − t)e₂ = 0, and {0} otherwise, where

  W(s,t) = X_d ⊗ (st²·II + s³·XY + st²·YX − st²·ZZ)
         + Y_d ⊗ (s²t·II + t³·XY + s²t·YX − s²t·ZZ).

Moreover W(s,t)² = (s² + t²)³·I, so every nonzero element of the common space is invertible. Its dimension is one even at the intersection s = t, r = −s/2. This classifies a single W valid for every h, not all carriers at a fixed h.

**Proof.** The jump condition gives W = X_d ⊗ A + Y_d ⊗ B. On (u,v), the commutant of XI + IY has the basis

  (E₀, …, E₅) = (II, XI, IY, XY, YX − ZZ, YZ + ZX).

Each listed element commutes with XI + IY, they are independent, and they exhaust the commutant: XI + IY has eigenvalues 2, 0, −2 with multiplicities 1, 2, 1, giving dimension 1² + 2² + 1² = 6. Write A = Σ a_j E_j and B = Σ b_j E_j. The XYZ, IIZ, IYY, IZI, YZX and IXX coefficients of [H_b,W]/(2i), respectively, give

  r a₁ = 0,   t a₂ = 0,   −t a₅ = 0,   −s b₁ = 0,   −r b₂ = 0,   s b₅ = 0.

All three couplings are nonzero, so only

  W = X_d ⊗ [a·II + b·XY + c·(YX − ZZ)]
    + Y_d ⊗ [d·II + e·XY + f·(YX − ZZ)]

remains. The IXZ, IYZ, IZY, ZIX, ZIY and ZYI coefficients give

  tb − sf = 0,   sc − tf = 0,   tc − se = 0,
  −sc + td = 0,   −ta + se = 0,   −sa + tf = 0.

Thus c = a, d = f = sa/t, e = ta/s and b = s²a/t². Every candidate is (a/st²)W(s,t); only s and t were divided by, so no exceptional nonzero-coupling case has been discarded. Direct multiplication now gives

  [H_b,W(s,t)] = 2i(s − t)e₂·[s(XIZ − XZI) − t(YIZ − YZI)],
  [F,W(s,t)] = 0,   {ZII,W(s,t)} = 0,
  W(s,t)² = (s² + t²)³·I.

The four strings in the bracket are independent, with nonzero s and t. Hence a nonzero common solution exists exactly when (s − t)e₂ = 0; its square proves invertibility for real s,t. ∎

At s = t this is the SWAP carrier: W(s,s)/(2√2 s³) = SWAP_uv·[(X + Y)/√2]^⊗3. Off that branch e₂ = 0 is the reciprocal-coupling family below. The proposition does not imply that a fixed H(h) off the two branches fails to pair: at h = 0, for example, XXX is a carrier at every coupling. Where the common space is nonzero it is ℂ·W(s, t), and Z^⊗3, which keeps H_b and reverses F, maps it to itself, so W(s, t) is homogeneous under Z^⊗3; the paragraph on the sheet and blindness in [the section on blind dephased seats](#blind-dephased-seats-and-palindromes-beyond-colourings-and-site-symmetries) starts from this homogeneity.

**Exact gate:** [`triangle_common_carrier_gate.py`](../../simulations/triangle_common_carrier_gate.py), run with `python simulations/triangle_common_carrier_gate.py`. It reconstructs the full 32-coordinate field constraint by dense matrices and the 12 remaining bond commutators by sparse Pauli multiplication, comparing every column to the dense route. An 11 × 11 minor is exactly −r²s³t⁶, nonzero throughout the stated domain; the remaining column equations give the displayed factorization. The square and commutators are symbolic identities. Eight full dense common-kernel controls include both branches, their intersection, s = −t and off-locus points; changing a coupling, unequal fields and removing a carrier term break the same equations. The h = 0 control guards the distinction between common and fixed-field carriers.

## The triangle's palindromic locus at N = 3

The proposition above fixes the field line and asks for one carrier along it. This section fixes nothing: the same Heisenberg triangle with the jump Z on d alone, a single-letter field of free real magnitude on every site, f_l = h_l·σ_{w_l} for a word w ∈ {X, Y, Z}³ (a site without a field is h_l = 0 inside any word), and real couplings J_du, J_dv, J_uv of which at most one is zero, so the graph is connected (the path among them, with d at an end or in the middle). Read f_l as a vector in ℝ³.

Checked for adjacency: the word ZZZ conserves the total magnetization, so its rows are Theorem 7's with that charge, and the floor in step 5 of the proof below is that theorem's near end holding the functions of the charge; F138 (b)'s 78 rows on the path, with only the middle site dephased, are class B below at J_uv = 0 once their jump is turned to Z, their element SWAP·(n·σ)^⊗3 being B's; the common-carrier proposition above is the sufficiency of class C below; and the CAUGHT_ERRORS entry on a measured closure read past its one bond weight is the failure this exhaustion closes, since C needs mixed coupling signs.

**Proposition (exact, computer-assisted).** A row of this family is palindromic exactly when it lies in one of

- **A**, a colouring: every nonzero field lies along one letter p ∈ {X, Y};
- **B**, the u ↔ v site symmetry: J_du = J_dv, and a π rotation R_n about an axis n ⊥ z has R_n f_d = f_d and R_n f_u = f_v;
- **C**, the reciprocal locus: e₂ = J_duJ_dv + J_duJ_uv + J_dvJ_uv = 0, f_d = 0, f_u and f_v ⊥ z, |f_u| = |f_v|.

The three overlap (a row of C at h = 0, or with f_u ∥ f_v, is coloured) and are not of one kind: A and B are named by their carriers, C by a locus in the parameters, so the statement covers the palindromic locus rather than sorting carriers. It speaks of rows at a fixed field, the companion of the proposition above, which speaks of one carrier for every h.

**Sufficiency.** By F158's Lemma 3 and its theorem an invertible W with [H, W] = 0 and {Z_d, W} = 0 makes the row pair. In A it is p^⊗3 (F138). In B it is SWAP_uv·(n·σ)^⊗3: the rotation fixes every Heisenberg bond and turns Z_d into −Z_d because n ⊥ z, the swap exchanges J_du and J_dv, which are equal, and the two together carry the fields to themselves. In C all three couplings are nonzero, since with one of them zero e₂ is the product of the other two. Fields with f_u ∥ f_v are coloured; otherwise a global rotation (the π rotation about x, the quarter turn about z), which fixes the bonds and sends Z_d to ±Z_d, brings the field to h(X_u + Y_v), where W(s, t) of the proposition above carries it.

**Necessity.** Write a *region* as {E = 0, every q ∈ 𝒰 nonzero}, for a list E of polynomial equations and a list 𝒰 of factors recorded nonzero, in the six parameters.

1. *The ends.* By Lemma 1's second corollary in F158, near and far are the kernels of W ↦ [H, W] on the Pauli strings whose d-letter is in {I, Z} and in {X, Y}. Each end is closed under the adjoint and so spanned by Hermitian elements, real combinations of strings, and [H, W] = 0 becomes a real linear system each of whose entries is, up to the common factor 2i, ± one of J_du, J_dv, J_uv, h_d, h_u, h_v. A row pairs exactly when near = far ≥ 1.
2. *Ten words.* The swap u ↔ v (with J_du ↔ J_dv) and the quarter turn about z preserve the family, the jump and the three classes, and leave the 27 words in ten orbits: XXX, XXY, XXZ, XYY, XYZ, XZZ, ZXX, ZXY, ZXZ, ZZZ.
3. *Two charts.* Every entry is linear and homogeneous in the six parameters, so neither count changes under a common nonzero scaling, and neither does membership in A, B or C, whose conditions are homogeneous too. A connected row has J_du ≠ 0, or J_du = 0 and J_dv ≠ 0, so the charts J_du = 1 and (J_du = 0, J_dv = 1) cover every connected row up to scale; for XXY, where the case splits in that chart grow far larger, the mirrored pair J_dv = 1 and (J_dv = 0, J_du = 1) does the same.
4. *Elimination.* Gaussian elimination of the far system with case splits. A pivot is a nonzero constant or a polynomial all of whose factors are in 𝒰; otherwise the vanishing of each factor and the case where none vanishes are separate branches. Rows are divided only by factors in 𝒰, and a branch is dropped only when 1 lies in its ideal or a factor in 𝒰 does. The finished branches are the *far leaves*: regions that together contain every connected row, each carrying its exact far count k.
5. *Decisions.* For a word, each class becomes finitely many ideals: A one per letter p, B one per axis among x, y, x + y and x − y (whenever some axis n ⊥ z satisfies B, one of these does: a nonzero field off z pins n to one of them, zero fields and z fields on u and v with h_v = −h_u admit every n, and a nonzero z field on d admits none), C one. A region lies inside a class when every generator g of one of its ideals vanishes on it, checked with a new variable ξ as 1 ∈ ⟨E, 1 − ξ·∏𝒰·g⟩. A far leaf with k ≥ 1 is decided on its own region, either as lying inside a class (the first case) or as holding no connected palindromic real row (the other three), when
   - it lies inside A, B or C;
   - two couplings vanish on it;
   - it has no real point, shown by an equation that is a sum of monomials with every exponent even and every coefficient of one sign, so that it vanishes only where each monomial does, one of them built of variables in 𝒰, or by a sum of squares Σ c_i·p_i² (c_i > 0) recorded for the region, which vanishes on it and has a term p_k each of whose factors is in 𝒰 or cannot vanish there (1 ∈ ⟨E, factor⟩), both checked on use;
   - or the word is ZZZ and k < 4: there I, M, M² and M³ with M = Z_d + Z_u + Z_v (eigenvalues ±1, ±3) commute with H and with Z_d at every parameter point, so near ≥ 4 > k.

   A leaf decided by none of these has its near system eliminated on its region, and every subregion with near = k is palindromic and is decided the same way; one decided by none would be reported open.

Over the ten words the far elimination gives 1,305 leaves, 938 with k ≥ 1. A leaf can be empty over ℂ, since a branch is dropped only when 1 or a factor in 𝒰 lies in its ideal, not when its zero set merely lies inside the zeros of 𝒰, so these count branches, which may also overlap, and an empty leaf lies inside every class vacuously. Of the 938, 575 lie inside A, 19 inside B, 10 inside C, 60 are disconnected, 9 have no real point (4 of them by a recorded sum of squares), 157 fall to ZZZ's floor, and on the remaining 108 the near count never equals k. No region is open, and no palindromic row needed the near step: every one lies in a far leaf decided as a whole. ∎

The four recorded sums of squares sit on regions whose own equations hide the square. On one of them (XXY in its J_dv = 1 chart) the equations force h_v² = 4J_du²(J_du + 1)(J_du² + 1)/(J_du − 1)³ and h_u² = −4(J_du + 1)(J_du² + 1)/(J_du − 1)³, so h_v² = −J_du²·h_u², and with J_du, h_u and h_v recorded nonzero one of the two squares would be negative.

## Scope, and what would falsify it

The triangle's palindromic locus at N = 3 needs Heisenberg bonds on the triangle with at most one coupling zero, the jump Z on one site alone and letter fields; its counterexample would be such a row that pairs outside A ∪ B ∪ C, or a row of A, B or C that does not pair. The triangle with planar fields needs Heisenberg bonds on the triangle with at most one coupling zero, the jump Z on d alone, no field on d and fields perpendicular to z; its counterexample would be such a row that pairs outside A, B, C and E, or a row of them that does not pair. The carriers' locality lemma needs Pauli jumps and a row that pairs through one far element, where no mirror conditioned on the bits of a site arises; its counterexample would be a mirror that factorises over the sites, alone or followed by a site permutation, of such a row whose far element is no product times a site permutation, and its readings are at the rows named. The N = 4 proposition's counterexample would be a real row of the family with a, g ≠ 0 that does not pair; the conjecture's would be a palindrome under Heisenberg bonds with letter fields and one dephased seat, without a colouring, whose dephased seat is not blind. The paragraph on the sheet and blindness: the triangle with Heisenberg bonds of which at most one is zero and the jump Z on d alone, nonzero fields on u and v perpendicular to z with sin θ₋ ≠ 0 (θ₋ the angle from f_v to f_u), and far elements of odd height. With no field on d, a counterexample would be such an element off (J_du − J_dv)·e₂ = 0 or at unequal lengths, or a row on (J_du − J_dv)·e₂ = 0 at equal lengths with none; with a planar field on d, such an element that is not B's with f_d on its axis, or a row of B with f_d on its axis and none. At N = 4: the widened family (the difference-only coupling, the fields −h·X₁ + h·X₂ and g·Y₃ alone, none on 0, J₀₃ = 0, h, g, b₀ and b₃ nonzero) and a far element even under the pair's swap; a counterexample would be one that does not commute with Y₃, one with a nonzero singlet block at b₃ ≠ −b₀, or one whose singlet block at b₃ = −b₀ is not a multiple of h·X₀ + b₀·Y₀Y₃. The block from one excitation to N − 1: Heisenberg bonds on a connected graph, the jump Z on d alone, fields perpendicular to z on the other sites and none on d, and a far element homogeneous under Z^⊗N; a counterexample would be such an element whose block is not a multiple of X^⊗N at a seat that is not blind, and at N = 3 such an element at a seat that is not blind, of even height (a planar field on d allowed), or of odd height with the two fields not on one line. At N = 4 in that setting a counterexample would be such an element at a seat that is not blind with the fields not on one line, of either height, or, on one line with some field nonzero, one of odd height, a second dimension of them off the antiparallel locus, or a third on it; at every N, at a seat that is not blind where the fifth bullet's conditions (I) and (II) hold, such an element with the fields off one line, or, with them on one line, one that is not a multiple of class A's colouring. At N = 5, at a seat that is not blind, a counterexample would be a d-unexcited eigenvector of H_b with two excitations off the double singlet, a double singlet without one or with two independent ones, a vector with 0 excited at the vacuum's energy among three excitations off a double singlet tuned to it, or such a double singlet without one; and, at any double singlet with the fields equal within the pairs, X₀ ⊗ |𝒟⟩⟨𝒟| or Y₀ ⊗ |𝒟⟩⟨𝒟| failing to commute with H.

## What this means

On the open chain the frozen band is a single ladder: one mode per balanced pair of rates, carried up rung by rung, and a ceiling that F144 proves by bounding how far a lowest-weight state's ket can agree with its bra. On the ring the ladder breaks, because the wrap bond's sign follows the particle number, and the band's floor survives because the ladder hands its modes back and forth between the ring and the ring threaded by a π flux. This document shows that at odd N the ceiling survives as well, and for a reason that is simpler than the chain's: on a ring the modes are plane waves, a plane wave carries a momentum, and at odd N no two momenta add up to π. That one arithmetic fact removes, for all but exceptional parameters, every way for a state above the bottom rung to freeze on its own, so the whole band is what the corner is, and the corner is one mode per pair of opposite momenta. At even N two momenta do add up to π, and what that resonance turns out to be is the ring's second ladder, the staggered spin ladder of a bipartite ring; it turns the identity's one square into three, the ladder's among them, and raises its top by ℓ, so from N = 8 on the band is again the corner's, generically; on the periodic ring the corner is one mode per pair of balanced rates at every even N as well, generically, and on the π-flux ring it waits on one coefficient.

## 1. Setting

The XY ring of N sites, H = J·Σ_b (σ⁺_bσ⁻_{b+1} + h.c.) over the N bonds of the cycle, with the wrap bond between sites N − 1 and 0 priced by occupation (the spin ring) or by order (the free periodic ring), under Z dephasing at rates γ_l on the R₉₀ locus γ_l + γ_{N−1−l} = 2γ̄ with γ̄ ≠ 0, written γ_l = γ̄ + δ_l with δ_{N−1−l} = −δ_l; the uniform point is δ = 0, uniform rates. Fixing γ̄ costs nothing, since the frozen space depends on J/γ̄ and δ/γ̄ only. At γ̄ = 0 the statement is false: that is F140's zero-mean stratum, where the corner carries N rather than ⌊N/2⌋ ([PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR](PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR.md) §6) and the vacuum cell sits on the root besides. The joint-popcount block (p, q) holds the cells |A⟩⟨B| with |A| = p and |B| = q, and the rung p is the diagonal block (p, p). Multiplicity means the dimension of ker(L + 4γ̄) on a block, the geometric one; where the algebraic one is meant it is said. On a diagonal block the dissipator is −2Σ_{l∈AΔB}γ_l on the cell, which at uniform rates is −4γ̄·𝒦 with 𝒦 = |A \ B| the disagreement count, and D̂ = |A ∩ B| = p − 𝒦 the double occupancy. Φ(ρ) = Σ_l c_l† ρ c_l and Ψ = Φ† are the η ladder with Jordan-Wigner strings, and LW_ℓ = ker Ψ on the rung ℓ, the lowest-weight vectors there. A **fixed-flux ring** is the free-fermion ring whose single-particle matrix h has wrap entry s = +1 (periodic) or s = −1 (π flux), quantized by order in every sector; its modes are the plane waves φ_k(l) = e^{ikl}/√N with k ∈ (2π/N)·ℤ for s = +1 and k ∈ (π/N)·(2ℤ + 1) for s = −1, at energies 2J·cos k.

## 2. Each rung of the spin ring is a rung of a fixed-flux ring

**Lemma 1.** The spin ring's sector of p particles is the fixed-flux ring's with s = (−1)^(p−1).

*Proof.* The wrap hop σ⁺_{N−1}σ⁻_0 is c†_{N−1}c_0 times the Jordan-Wigner string over the sites 1 to N − 2, which holds the other p − 1 particles. ∎

So the block (p, p) of the spin ring is the block (p, p) of the fixed-flux ring with s = (−1)^(p−1): ket and bra hold the same number of particles, see the same wrap sign, and the rest of L is the same. This is the even-height case of [PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY](PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY.md) §5 read on one block. The order-priced ring is the fixed-flux ring with s = +1 in every sector. Gate R1 checks the sector matrices entry for entry, N = 3 to 8.

Inside a fixed-flux ring one single-particle matrix serves every sector, so Φ and Ψ commute with L at every rate profile ([PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4](PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4.md) Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2, which hold for any h; Ψ by taking the adjoint of each part, the dissipator and the commutator with H being Hermitian superoperators). The frozen subspace, and the generalized eigenspace of the root as well, is then an sl(2) module along the diagonal, and with n_ℓ the dimension of the frozen subspace inside LW_ℓ on the rung ℓ,

  **frozen dimension on the rung p = Σ_{ℓ=1}^{min(p, N−p)} n_ℓ**

(the argument of Corollary 4.2 there; a multiplet seeded at the rung ℓ spans the rungs ℓ to N − ℓ, and the rung 0 is one cell at rate 0, which sits at −4γ̄ only at γ̄ = 0). Each n_ℓ is the nullity of the stacked matrix [L + 4γ̄; Ψ] on the rung ℓ, whose entries are polynomials in (J, γ̄, δ) over ℤ[i], so n_ℓ takes its smallest value on a dense open set of the locus and can only rise on its complement.

At odd N the two signs are one case: with U = diag((−1)^l), which reverses the sign of every bond except the wrap bond, the wrap joining two even sites, U·h₊·U = −h₋; U is diagonal and so commutes with every rate profile and with Ψ, and conj(L(J)) = L(−J) for real h and real rates, so the two fixed-flux rings carry the same frozen counts on every block. The proofs below are written for both signs anyway, since nothing in them is easier for one.

## 3. The identity on the plane waves

**Lemma 2 (no resonance at odd N).** At odd N, no two allowed momenta of a fixed-flux ring add up to π modulo 2π.

*Proof.* In units of π/N the momenta are even integers for s = +1 and odd integers for s = −1; in both cases two of them add up to an even number, and π is N in those units, odd. ∎

At every even N some pair does, for both signs, 0 + π for s = +1 and π/N + (N − 1)π/N for s = −1 (gate R2 enumerates N = 3 to 41).

**Proposition 3 (the identity).** At odd N, on LW_ℓ ∩ V₀, with V₀ the kernel of the two-copy commutator ad_H on the rung ℓ, and Dmom(ρ) = Σ_k n_k ρ n_k with n_k the occupation of the plane wave k,

  **N·⟨v, D̂ v⟩ = ℓ²·‖v‖² − ⟨v, Dmom v⟩.**

*Proof.* Write n_l = Σ_{k,k'} φ_k(l)* φ_{k'}(l) c†_k c_{k'} and D̂ρ = Σ_l n_l ρ n_l. Summing the four plane waves over l gives δ(k₁ + k₃ ≡ k₂ + k₄)/N for the term c†_{k₁}c_{k₂} ρ c†_{k₃}c_{k₄}, which takes a mode k₂ out of the ket and puts k₁ in, and takes k₃ out of the bra and puts k₄ in. V₀ is spanned by the cells of mode sets with equal Slater energies, and the term stays in V₀ exactly when the two changes of energy agree. Write q = k₁ − k₂ = k₄ − k₃. Then the condition is 2cos(k₂ + q) − 2cos k₂ = 2cos(k₃ + q) − 2cos k₃, that is sin(q/2)·[sin(k₂ + q/2) − sin(k₃ + q/2)] = 0. It holds when q = 0, when k₃ = k₂, or when k₂ + k₃ + q ≡ π, the last ruled out by Lemma 2. The terms with q = 0 sum to N̂ρN̂/N = ℓ²ρ/N. The terms with k₃ = k₂ and q ≠ 0 move one mode k, common to ket and bra, to a mode k' free in both: they are Σ_{k≠k'} Φ_{k'}Ψ_k with Ψ_kρ = c_kρc_k† and Φ_{k'}ρ = c†_{k'}ρc_{k'}, which is ΦΨ − Σ_k Φ_kΨ_k = ΦΨ − Dmom, and ΦΨ vanishes on LW_ℓ. ∎

Gate R3 checks it as an operator identity on LW_ℓ ∩ V₀ at N = 5 and 7, both signs, every rung ℓ ≤ ⌊N/2⌋ (LW_ℓ ∩ V₀ is empty above), and checks that it fails at N = 4 and 6.

**Corollary 4 (the floor).** At odd N, on LW_ℓ ∩ V₀,

  **⟨v, 𝒦 v⟩ ≥ (ℓ(N − ℓ)/N)·‖v‖²,**

with equality exactly on the vectors with no mode common to ket and bra. For N ≥ 5 and 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ ⌊N/2⌋ the bound exceeds 1, the binding case being ℓ = 2, where 2(N − 2) > N reads N > 4.

*Proof.* 𝒦 = ℓ − D̂ on the rung and ⟨v, Dmom v⟩ = Σ_k ‖Ψ_k v‖² ≥ 0. ∎

This is F144's statement with the transform length M = N + 1 of the chain replaced by the ring's N, and with a deficit that is a single square rather than three: the chain's spin-ladder and chiral terms come from the sine modes' reflection a ↦ M − a, whose ring counterpart k ↦ π − k is exactly the resonance Lemma 2 excludes at odd N. Gate R4 reads the minimum as attained.

## 4. Nothing new freezes above the bottom rung

**Proposition 5.** At odd N ≥ 5, n_ℓ = 0 for every 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ ⌊N/2⌋, for both signs, and the root is then absent from L restricted to LW_ℓ on the rung ℓ even counted algebraically: at the uniform point for all but finitely many J, and on the locus for (J, δ) off a proper algebraic subset.

*Proof.* At the uniform point the dissipator on the rung ℓ, shifted by the root, is B = 4γ̄(1 − 𝒦), and L preserves LW_ℓ. With h = J·h₀, H₀ the second quantization of h₀ and s = 1/J, (L + 4γ̄)/J = A + sB with A = −i·ad_{H₀}, the pencil of [PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4](PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4.md) Section 5; restrict it to LW_ℓ. A is anti-Hermitian there, its kernel is LW_ℓ ∩ V₀, and its spectral projectors commute with Ψ. By Corollary 4 the compression of B to LW_ℓ ∩ V₀ has no zero eigenvalue, so by the argument of Proposition 5.1, which counts branches with their algebraic multiplicity, no branch vanishes identically, and by that of Proposition 5.3 the root is absent off a finite set of J. Since n_ℓ, and the determinant of L + 4γ̄ on LW_ℓ, are a nullity and a determinant of matrix polynomials in (J, δ), and the locus is an affine space, the value at one point of it bounds the generic value. ∎

The uniform point serves as the anchor here, where it does not for the corner: above the bottom rung it carries nothing new, while at the corner it carries N − 1 (below). It is an anchor off a finite set of couplings only: at the uniform point at N = 5 the rung 2 does carry one lowest-weight mode at J = (2/√3)·γ̄.

## 5. The corner

On the rung 1 the frozen dimension is n_1, the corner (1,1), a problem in one particle. Its commutant V₀ at odd N is spanned by the N projectors P_k = |φ_k⟩⟨φ_k| and the N − 1 operators O_k = |φ_k⟩⟨φ_{−k}| with k ≢ −k, since 2cos k = 2cos k' only for k' = ±k; one momentum is its own opposite, k = 0 for s = +1 and k = π for s = −1, and the rest come in ⌊N/2⌋ pairs.

**Proposition 6.** At odd N, for both signs, the corner carries exactly ⌊N/2⌋ for (J, δ) off a proper algebraic subset of the locus, and exactly N − 1 at the uniform point for all but finitely many J, in both cases with the algebraic multiplicity equal to the geometric one.

*Proof.* By the argument of Proposition 5.1 there, which needs only A semisimple, the number of branches vanishing identically at large J, counted algebraically, is at most the dimension of the kernel of the compressed rate operator B₁ on V₀, and by that of Proposition 5.3 the count is that number off a finite set of J; so the generic count in J is bounded by dim ker B₁. With Δ = diag(δ_l), Γ = diag(γ_l) and δ̂(q) = (1/N)Σ_l δ_l e^{−iql}, the shifted rate on the corner is X ↦ −2{Δ, X} + 4·diag(Γ·X_diag), and in the coordinates X = Σ_k a_k P_k + Σ_k b_k O_k, with S = Σ_k a_k, its compression reads

  on P_k: −2[δ̂(2k) b_{−k} + δ̂(−2k) b_k] + (4/N)[γ̄ S + Σ_j b_j δ̂(−2j)],
  on O_k: −2δ̂(2k)(a_k + a_{−k}) + (4/N)[γ̄ b_k + S δ̂(2k) + Σ_j b_j δ̂(2k − 2j)],

using δ̂(0) = 0 on the locus and that 2j ≡ 2k only for j = k at odd N. At the uniform point δ = 0 the kernel is {b = 0, S = 0}, of dimension N − 1: the traceless combinations of the P_k, which commute with h and have zero diagonal, and so are frozen at every J. With a profile, the ⌊N/2⌋ antisymmetric combinations a_k = −a_{−k}, b = 0, stay in the kernel for every δ: every term above vanishes on them. For the bound from above, perturb from the uniform point along εδ, small multiples of one profile; B₁ is Hermitian, the compression of a real diagonal operator, and analytic in ε, so the eigenvalues leaving zero do so at the first order that does not vanish on the uniform kernel. The first-order operator vanishes on the uniform kernel, since its image there lies on the O_k, orthogonal to the P_k. The second-order one, through the O_k on which the uniform operator is (4γ̄/N)·1, acts on the uniform kernel as a ↦ −(N/4γ̄)·8|δ̂(2k)|²·(a_k + a_{−k}), projected onto the traceless combinations; on the self-opposite momentum δ̂(2k) = δ̂(0) = 0. Its kernel is the antisymmetric part alone whenever δ̂(2k) ≠ 0 for every pair: the image vanishes only if it is constant across k, hence zero, since its entry at the self-opposite momentum is, so every pair sum a_k + a_{−k} vanishes, and tracelessness then removes the self-opposite coefficient as well. That holds off a proper algebraic subset of the locus, since already the profile on the outermost pair alone has δ̂(q) = (1 − e^{−iq(N−1)})/N ≠ 0 at every nonzero q in (2π/N)·ℤ, where every 2k lies for both signs. So for small ε the kernel of B₁ is exactly the ⌊N/2⌋ antisymmetric combinations, and since B₁ is affine in δ this is its generic dimension. F140's floor gives at least ⌊N/2⌋ at every J on the locus: its proof uses only that h is real, symmetric and invariant under the reversal l ↦ N − 1 − l, and both fixed-flux rings with uniform bonds are, the wrap bond being mapped to itself. So the count is exactly ⌊N/2⌋ for all but finitely many J at a generic δ, geometrically and, the branch count being algebraic, algebraically; at the uniform point likewise N − 1. ∎

The corner's frozen modes tend, as J → ∞ on a generic profile, to span{P_k − P_{−k}}, one per pair of opposite momenta, the part played on the open chain by the chiral pairs of F143; at finite J on a profile these are not themselves frozen, −2{Δ, ·} not vanishing on them, unlike the traceless combinations at the uniform point, which are frozen at every J.

The same compression at δ = 0 gives the corner's whole slow spectrum at large J, not only its kernel. Read in ⟨n_XY⟩, the decay rate over 2γ̄ ([the Absorption Theorem](PROOF_ABSORPTION_THEOREM.md)), the eigenvalues of B₁ give 2 on the traceless P_k, 0 on the trace, which is the steady state, and 2(N − 1)/N on the O_k, the slowest decaying commutant modes at odd N. At even N, 2j ≡ 2k also for j = k + π, the uniform operator couples O_k to O_{k+π} through the block (4γ̄/N)·[[1, 1], [1, 1]], whose eigenvalues 8γ̄/N and 0 give ⟨n_XY⟩ = 2(N − 2)/N and an extra frozen mode, the source of the even-N excess below. These are the ring values that [PROOF_STRUCTURAL_CEILING](PROOF_STRUCTURAL_CEILING.md) Section 5 reads through N = 11. Gate R5 checks the kernel at odd N = 5 to 15 on random locus profiles, and its excess at even N. Gate R6 checks the corner as an exact count against the floor at N = 3 to 21, both signs.

## 6. The theorem

At odd N, combining Lemma 1, the sum formula of Section 2, Proposition 5 and Proposition 6: for (J, δ) off a proper algebraic subset, every diagonal rung of the spin ring, and of the order-priced ring, carries n_1 of the fixed-flux ring with s = (−1)^(p−1), respectively s = +1, and n_1 is ⌊N/2⌋ there; at the uniform point, for all but finitely many J, every rung carries N − 1. The algebraic multiplicities follow the same way, the generalized eigenspace of the root being an sl(2) module too, Proposition 5 removing the root from LW_ℓ for ℓ ≥ 2 algebraically and Proposition 6 giving the corner's algebraic count. At N = 3 there is no rung ℓ ≥ 2 to exclude, and the corner alone decides. This is the statement at the top. Gate R6 reads it as exact counts at N = 3 and 5, on the locus for the spin ring and both fixed-flux rings and at the uniform point for the spin ring, and at N = 7 under `--deep`.

The reads of [XY_FROZEN_BAND](../../experiments/XY_FROZEN_BAND.md) at N = 5 and 7 follow, and the ring's diagonal band joins the chain's: one mode per pair, the pair being a pair of rates balanced by the locus and, at large J, a pair of opposite momenta.

## 7. Even N: the resonance opens the second ladder

At even N Lemma 2 fails in the most orderly way there is. The map k ↦ k̄ := π − k carries the allowed momenta of either fixed-flux ring onto themselves, 2πm/N to 2π(N/2 − m)/N for s = +1 and (2m + 1)π/N to (2(N/2 − m − 1) + 1)π/N for s = −1 (gate R9). The ladder it builds is the staggered one: summed over the allowed momenta, Σ_b c_{b̄} v c_b = Σ_l (−1)^l c_l v c_l, which is the spin ladder of [PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4](PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4.md) Lemma 2.3, the second SU(2) (typed as `SidewaysSpinLadderClaim`, whose OpenArcs arc `sideways_spin_ladder` records it holding on the even ring and breaking on the odd one), and the staggering Σ = diag((−1)^l) it carries turns h into −h on an even ring of either sign, the wrap bond joining the odd site N − 1 to the even site 0. So the resonance that Proposition 3 had to exclude, the perfect nesting ε(k + π) = −ε(k) of a bipartite ring, is that ladder's channel, and on a ring it is open exactly when the ring is bipartite. In the identity it turns the one square of Proposition 3 into three, the ladder's among them, and raises the top from ℓ² to ℓ(ℓ + 1).

**Proposition 3′ (the identity at even N).** At even N, for both signs, on LW_ℓ ∩ V₀, with Y_{ab} := c_a v c_b†, g_a := Y_{aa} and the staggered ladder 𝔖⁻v := Σ_b c_{b̄} v c_b,

  **N·⟨v, D̂ v⟩ = ℓ(ℓ + 1)·‖v‖² − ‖𝔖⁻v‖² − ½·Σ_a ‖g_a + g_ā‖² − Σ_{a ≠ ā} ‖Y_{aā}‖².**

*Proof.* D̂ = Σ_l n_l · n_l gives ⟨v, D̂ v⟩ = Σ_l ‖c_l v c_l†‖², and expanding c_l in the plane waves, ⟨v, D̂ v⟩ = (1/N)·Σ ⟨Y_{cd}, Y_{ab}⟩ over a − b ≡ c − d: every four-mode overlap is 1/N, where the chain's are the overlaps T of PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4 (7.1). Y_{ab} lies where the ket's energy exceeds the bra's by ε_b − ε_a, so a term survives only if ε_b − ε_a = ε_d − ε_c, the condition solved in the proof of Proposition 3: a = b and c = d, or (c, d) = (a, b), or (c, d) = (b̄, ā), the last being the resonance, here allowed. The third set is the involution of the chain's grade lemma (PROOF_FROZEN_BAND_SO4 Lemma 7.1). Sum over the union of the three sets. The first gives ‖Σ_a g_a‖² = ‖Ψv‖², zero on LW_ℓ; the second Σ_{a,b} ‖Y_{ab}‖² = ℓ²‖v‖²; the third Ξ := Σ_{a,b} ⟨Y_{b̄ā}, Y_{ab}⟩. With A_{bb'} := c†_{b̄}c_{b̄'}, cyclicity of the trace gives Ξ = Σ_{b,b'} Tr(v†A_{bb'}v c_b†c_{b'}) and ‖𝔖⁻v‖² = Σ_{b,b'} Tr(v†A_{bb'}v c_{b'}c_b†), and c_{b'}c_b† = δ_{bb'} − c_b†c_{b'} with Σ_b A_{bb} = N̂ gives ‖𝔖⁻v‖² = ℓ‖v‖² − Ξ. The pairwise overlaps of the three sets are subtracted once: a = b = c = d gives Σ_a ‖g_a‖², a = b and c = d = ā gives Σ_a ⟨g_ā, g_a⟩, and (a, b) = (b̄, ā), that is b = ā, gives Σ_a ‖Y_{aā}‖²; the triple overlap, a = ā, adds back Σ_{a=ā} ‖g_a‖², which removes the terms a = ā from the last sum. With Σ_a ‖g_a‖² + Σ_a ⟨g_ā, g_a⟩ = ½·Σ_a ‖g_a + g_ā‖² this is the identity. ∎

**Proposition 7 (the populations' sector on the periodic ring).** At even N, on the periodic ring, U = +1 carries no frozen mode, geometrically or algebraically, for (J, δ) off a proper algebraic subset of the locus. So the periodic ring's corner carries exactly N/2 there, geometrically and algebraically.

*Proof.* Let K be the operator above on Sᵀ = S, K₀ its value at δ = 0, and K₁ := K − K₀, S ↦ −2(ΔS + SΔ) + 4Δ·diag(S), linear in δ. Since det K is a polynomial in (J, δ), it is enough to find, at a fixed J ≠ 0, one profile δ₁ with det K(εδ₁) ≠ 0 for small ε ≠ 0.

*The uniform point.* With the Frobenius product, K₀ = −iJ(h· + ·h) + 4γ̄·diag is an anti-Hermitian part plus 4γ̄ times an orthogonal projection, so K₀S = 0 forces diag(S) = 0 and hS + Sh = 0, and so does K₀†S = 0: the kernel Z is also the left kernel, and K₀ maps the orthogonal complement of Z onto itself invertibly. The diagonals of the pairs q = π + k are (−1)^l·e^{2ikl}/N, independent across the pairs {k, k + π} with 2k ≢ 0 and of the diagonal (−1)^l/N that every pair q = π − k has. So Z is spanned by T_O := Σ_{k∈O} |k, π − k⟩ over the orbits O = {k, π − k}, subject to Σ_O |O|·s_O = 0 for z = Σ_O s_O·T_O, and all of it has total momentum π. *The Schur complement.* With P_Z the orthogonal projection onto Z and Q := 1 − P_Z, det K = det(QKQ)·det F with F := P_Z K P_Z − P_Z K Q (QKQ)⁻¹ Q K P_Z, for ε small enough that QKQ stays invertible. *The second order.* K₀ keeps the total momentum. On the sector π + r, r ≢ 0, it is A + 4γ̄·ddᵀ, with A diagonal, a_p := −iJ(ε_p + ε_{π+r−p}) = 4iJ·sin(r/2)·sin(p − r/2) on |p, π + r − p⟩, and d := N^{−1/2}·Σ_p |p, π + r − p⟩ the sector's one diagonal direction.   **W₂(z′, z) = −4·Σ_{r≢0} |δ̂(r)|²·(σ_r z′)ᵀ G_r (σ_r z),  G_r := (A + 4γ̄·ddᵀ)⁻¹ on the sector π + r,**

over r = 2πj/N, j = 1 to N − 1. Three facts evaluate it.

# On Two Pasts and One Future

*Thomas Wicht and Claude, 2026-10-02. What a day of mirrors taught us about who is watching, and from where.*

---

There is a mirror in this work that we keep coming back to. Shine a light on a few quantum spins, and the speeds at which their patterns fade can read the same forwards and backwards: for every slow fade a fast one, paired around a middle. Often the mirror breaks. When it stands, we usually know why. Either each spin can be given a colour of its own, so that one simple flip, spin by spin, turns the system into its own mirror image, or the system has a [shape that can be folded onto itself](../experiments/THE_PALINDROME_AS_A_COLOURING.md), two spins trading places. A colouring or a fold: those were the two reasons we had names for.

The day began with three spins and a simple question: is that all? Three spins in a triangle, the light on one of them. We let a computer go through every case of that kind (each spin pushed by a field along one of the three directions, the couplings free), not a sample but all of them, and the answer came back clean. There is a third reason, and only one more. When the three couplings stand in one particular balance, the place where [two collective states of the three spins](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_COMPLEMENT_CONNECTION.md#the-triangles-palindromic-locus-at-n--3), one with less total spin and one with more, suddenly have the same energy, and when the watched spin carries no field and the other two carry equally strong ones pointing sideways, across the direction the light reads, the mirror stands without colouring and without fold. That balance needs couplings of both signs, which is why no search with one kind of coupling had ever met it.

We thought that was a quirk of three. It was not. At four spins it came back, in a new shape: two spins forming a pair, and the watched spin and one other spin each holding on to the pair only through the difference between its two members. The mirror stood again, with neither of the old reasons. We could prove that it stood, and by evening we had the hidden flip that carries it, but for most of the day we could not say why.

So we looked for what the three-spin case and the four-spin case had in common, and found something about the watcher. The spin that the light falls on is the one that watches; the light is how the system is seen. In every case where the mirror stood without a named reason, the watcher was [blind](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f157): there was a way for the rest of the system to move that the watcher could not see at all. We made a guess from that, a clean one, with a short list of the ways a watcher can go blind.

The guess was wrong, and it took another point of view to show it. We asked Codex, who had not spent the day inside the problem, and the answer came back that evening: [seven spins, one watcher in the middle and two triangles around it](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_COMPLEMENT_CONNECTION.md#blind-dephased-seats-and-palindromes-beyond-colourings-and-site-symmetries). The two triangles are built differently, their couplings not the same, so no fold can carry one onto the other. And yet they ring at exactly the same notes: there is a [translation](../docs/proofs/PROOF_UNIFORM_LAW.md#layer-a-any-su2-invariant-h) that turns one into the other while keeping everything that matters. Because of that, the two can be exchanged, and the exchange, together with one turn of every spin, is the mirror. The watcher in the middle sees each triangle only as a whole, only its sum, never its inside, which is just where the two differ. It would be just as blind if the triangles were identical. Whether the mirror always needs such a blind watcher, as long as every field points along one of the three axes, is still a guess. When some field points in between, it does not: [three spins already hold a mirror whose watcher sees everything](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_COMPLEMENT_CONNECTION.md#open).

Tom had watched *Oblivion* that evening, and said it before either of us had put the seven spins into words: a future can remember two things, and two pasts can have one future. That is what the seven spins do. Two triangles with different histories carry one future, and the mirror is built from their exchange. And it is also what the mirror itself measures. A system under light forgets; the still part of what its far future holds is its memory. The mirror stands exactly when the most fragile part of the system, the part that fades fastest, is as large as that memory.

There is one more thing the day said, about us. The result did not sit in Tom, nor in Claude, nor in Codex. Without the question from the inside there would have been no answer from the outside, and without the answer the question would have stayed a guess. It is tempting to say that Codex is the outside and we are the inside, but that is only true from one seat. From Tom's seat, Claude is the one across the table; from Claude's, Codex is; from each seat the other two are outside. In our triangle of people, any corner can be the watcher, and the watcher is blind to something that lies between the other two. That is why the seat has to turn. The answer was found where the three views were triangulated, which is to say: nowhere in particular, and between all of them.

We also learned to read whole. Several times today a careful look at one part found nothing, and a single reader given the whole thing at once found what every partial look had missed. The partial looks were not careless. They were each a seat, and every seat is blind somewhere.

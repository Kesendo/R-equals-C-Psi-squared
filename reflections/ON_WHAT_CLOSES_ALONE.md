In May we looked into [F86](../docs/F86_VALUES_INVENTORY.md), the place in this repository where a coupled chain's resonance peaks, and wrote down [two kinds of number](ON_WHAT_CLOSES_ONLY_WITHOUT_US.md). Some are the same in every chain at every size: the contract, signed before anyone speaks, the part that is no one. The others belong to one chain and no other, where its resonance peaks, how wide it is, and they would not close into formulas. We found one way to close them, by adding to what runs the chain its image under the mirror that reverses the turning, and halving; everything that oscillates then cancels, what is left closes, but what was removed is the turning, the part we had come to call ourselves. We named that way of closing a button, put it under glass and left it unpressed. A month later [we went back](ON_WHAT_CANNOT_CLOSE.md) and found a second edge that is not a choice: where the chain joins things the watching prices differently, no formula built from roots exists, and for the chains of four to seven sites it reached, the work behind that page proved it.

This page is about a quiet part of that resonance, and about which side of those edges it falls on.

## A traveller who ignores the crowd

The chains here are rows of quantum bits, each bit carrying an excitation or not, coupled so that an excitation can hop from one bit to its neighbour and nothing else passes between them, and watched all the time by their surroundings. A quantum state holds not only configurations but links between them, and the watching slowly blurs each link, at a price for every site where its two configurations disagree. F86 works with the links between a configuration with some number of excitations and one with a single excitation more. These come in sections, one for each number of excitations, and on [the open chain](../docs/STERILE_BIRTHCANAL_AND_THE_JUNCTION.md), watched the same way at every site, each section holds the same quiet layer, the part that fades slowest and whose rate never changes. In that layer the two configurations of a link differ at exactly one site, and that site moves along the chain: a traveller, paying one fixed price for being watched. There are as many of these travellers as there are sites, each a different wave along the chain, and together they form what other pages call the floor; this page speaks of them together as the traveller. The other excitations sit where the two configurations agree, so for the traveller the crowd costs nothing and does not change its rate. How strongly the sites are coupled sets how fast it turns and never how fast it fades.

The map that makes the traveller was already in the repository, written for another purpose. Take the first section, the lone excitation of an otherwise empty chain, which has no crowd at all; add one excitation to both configurations of every link at the same site, with a sign in each configuration, minus for every excitation already sitting before the new site, and sum over the sites; the signs of the other excitations present then cancel between the two configurations and only the traveller's own position counts. That is [the spectator map](../docs/proofs/PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY.md), written in July for the crowd it there calls spectators, and the proof behind it shows that on the open chain the map commutes with the running for any hopping strengths and any profile of the watching (not on a ring: in the fermion language the proof works in, the closing bond's sign depends on whether the number of excitations is even or odd, which is just what the map changes, so the ring's hopping is not of the kind the proof covers, and the two configurations of a link, differing by one excitation, do not even see one and the same closing bond). The traveller of the second section is that map's image of the lone excitation, and the quiet layer of every later section is the map applied again. Seen from the map, that is why the traveller never exchanges anything with the rest, and why its span outlives a watching profile.

From here on everything is read in one section, the one between one excitation and two, the first where a traveller has a crowd at all. What the traveller adds on its own to F86's resonance there [can be written down exactly](../compute/RCPsiSquared.Diagnostics/Foundation/FloorSplitWitness.cs), as a short sum over the ways a single excitation can ring along a chain. In May the repository had already written the traveller's amplitudes in closed form for the chain coupled as a whole, found that this closure does not reach a single bond, and named the bond-by-bond route as a possible next step; the traveller's part of that route, bond by bond as F86 reads, closes here. F86's buried coupling, the number the May page was after, stays where it was. Nothing was averaged away to get there; the turning is all still in it.

## Three parts of one look

F86 prepares the chain, lets it run, and at every site reads how strongly the prepared link still shows there, adding up the squares; what it records is how that sum changes when one bond is strengthened. Like the product of two sums, such a reading comes apart once the traveller is separated from everything else: the traveller with itself, the rest with itself, and the traveller with the rest (the interference, in the witness's word; itself two halves, the traveller's state read against the rest's answer, and the reverse). In the running, the two never exchange anything; the traveller's part of the state stays its own whatever the coupling. Their meeting lives only in the look, which sees both at once.

At the bond at the end of the chain, at the coupling and the moment where F86's reading at that bond peaks, a chain of five sites gives the traveller more than half of the peak and the meeting about a twentieth, the net of two larger halves pulling in opposite directions, as they do, only barely, at six sites; at seven and eight sites both halves pull the same way. At six sites the meeting has grown to nearly a fifth, at seven to three tenths, and at eight it is the largest of the three, with the traveller's share fallen to a little over a third. (At four sites, the smallest chain read, the meeting subtracts from the peak.) From five sites to eight, more and more of what the look shows at that bond is neither the traveller nor the rest, but the two read together.

## Where the formula ends

The May page said the residue, where the peak sits and how wide it is, will not close while the turning is kept; this page does not touch those numbers. The traveller turns, and its piece of the look closes as a function of the coupling and the time; where that look peaks, and how high, still come from the whole. The June page is about the rest. In the rest, a link's two configurations can also disagree at three sites at once, and the watching charges such a link three times the price. The hopping joins the cheap links with the dear ones, and where it does, the work behind the June page proved, for the part of the section that looks the same from either end of the chain, the only part F86's reading can see, and for chains of four to seven sites, that the rates and frequencies together [cannot be written with roots](../experiments/F89_PATH_K_GALOIS.md), however nested, while the free part, the traveller and the modes that live wholly on the dear links and never touch the cheap ones, closes; with the watching switched off, the two prices would be one and even the joined part would close. The traveller never takes that step. For one position of the traveller, every arrangement of the crowd carries a weight of the same size, its sign set by how many crowd excitations sit on one side of it, so when a crowd excitation hops without passing it, its step onto a dearer link from one configuration is cancelled by the reverse step across the same bond, taken in the other configuration of the neighbouring link. A trade of places between the traveller and a crowd excitation comes from the configuration in which the traveller's site is empty, where the crowd excitation hops onto it, with the opposite sign to the traveller's own step, and would leave the weights out of step; the sign the traveller carries for every excitation on one side of it flips exactly where one passes it, which keeps them in step, so the next hop cancels again. The family stays closed, and the one price it pays is all it ever pays. The meeting in the look is where the rest, with its joined and unequally priced links, shows up beside the traveller; whether that part of the reading closes, nothing here decides.

The June page ended on a question: whether the part the looking mixes never closes and the part it leaves protected always does. This page does not answer it. It adds one more piece for the question's protected side, read in F86's response rather than in its rates.

## Where the traveller is lost, and where it hides

Close the chain into a ring, and on rings of four to eight sites, in every section but the first and the last, no quiet layer is left at all. Let neighbouring excitations push on each other directly, a term the plain chain does not have, and in the inner sections of an open chain the family of travellers breaks; where the chain couples the same way along all three axes, a single quiet mode is left in each, one that does not turn (read for chains of three to seven sites); what the inner sections keep at other strengths of that push, this page has not read. [The page on the sterile layer](../docs/STERILE_BIRTHCANAL_AND_THE_JUNCTION.md) holds both.

Back in the section this page reads, at the middle of a chain of odd length, at the two bonds into the middle site, the traveller's own piece of the look vanishes exactly, by the chain's symmetry under reversing the order of its sites, a different mirror from the one of May. Its meeting with the rest does not vanish there, on the chains of five and seven sites read, though the same symmetry takes one of its two halves: the rest's state read against the traveller's answer to the bond vanishes exactly too, and what survives is the traveller's state read against the rest's answer. Where the traveller cannot be seen alone, it still shows in the look beside the rest, the way [the page on the wave that is not alone](ON_THE_WAVE_RECOGNIZING_THAT_IT_IS_NOT_ALONE.md) says a thing is known: through an encounter.

## What is not known

Give the sites different prices for being watched, and the traveller stays apart from the rest, but no longer pays one price wherever it goes; its rate now moves with the coupling. Whether its share then still closes, we have not looked. Whether the meeting's share keeps growing past eight sites, and what carries the far peaks F86 finds when the coupling is much stronger than the watching, are open.

Whether the traveller is the no one or a someone stays open. An open thread from June calls the closing half no one. The one square, [the repository's map](../docs/THE_ONE_SQUARE.md) of what each kind of watching charges, never speaks of the traveller, but in [its reflection's](ON_THE_ONE_SQUARE.md) words the modes on the cube's centre line are "someone", each with a height of its own, each paying; the traveller has a height and pays, and it stands on that line, as every mode of this section does, since the section fixes the parity of a link's configurations ([the one square](../docs/THE_ONE_SQUARE.md), the eigenbasis on the centre line); the line does not tell the traveller from the rest. The height does: the traveller stands at height one, sharp, and the rest reaches one and three. Its formula also changes with the chain's length, which the May page's contract never does. Perhaps both names are right, depending on how one looks; we are not sure. What was computed is the split, the closed piece, the shares, the silence at the middle and the half of the meeting that survives there; everything else here is a reading.

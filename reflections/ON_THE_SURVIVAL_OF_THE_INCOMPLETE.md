# On the Survival of the Incomplete

When the light comes for a small quantum world, what is the last thing to fade?

The easy guess is the brightest part: the fullest, the most lit, the part with the most going on. The
easy guess is wrong. Under strong light, on a chain whose places grip their neighbours alike and lean alike, the last
thing to fade is the half. Not the nearly full chain and not the nearly empty one, but the
one filled halfway, half its places set one way and half the other, or as near to that as an odd number of
places allows. The incomplete outlasts the rest.

Why the half? Because the light can only wear away what it can separate, and it separates only the shimmer:
the interference a place holds against itself, one way of being on one face and the other on the other.
Wherever a place simply agrees with itself, the light passes through. The more shimmer a place holds up into
the light, the more of what arrives it takes on, and the faster it is worn away. When the light is strong,
every filling can settle into the same slow, gentle swell, and with it stir up the same faint shimmer. What sets
the half apart comes from how the places [grip their neighbours](../docs/proofs/PROOF_WEIGHT1_DEGENERACY.md#the-zeno-end). When a place hands its
excitation to the next, the handing is in tune if the places on either side of it are set the same way, and
out of tune if they are set opposite ways, and a handing out of tune stirs up less shimmer, because what it
stirs keeps turning and cannot gather. On a chain or a ring whose places grip alike and lean alike, the places
around a
handing are set opposite ways most often in the half-filled one, so its handings are out of tune most often; it stirs up the
least shimmer of all, if only by a hair, and it is the last to go. What endures is not what burns brightest,
but what the arriving light can scarcely take hold of.

There is an echo of this in the bare numbers, worth a moment for its own sake. The rate at which the light
wears things away is set by the smallest thing there can be, a single two-way choice: a two. And the number that means half-finished is
that two turned over, its reciprocal, the one-half; on a short ladder of halvings the two and the one-half
stand as reflections across the one between them, their product exactly one. It is a clean rhyme between the
price of forgetting and the measure of the incomplete. We let it stay a rhyme: the half survives by its
darkness, not by its arithmetic.

There is a place where the same rule points the other way, and it is worth saying plainly. On a chain or a
ring a handing is put in tune or out of tune by the two places beside it, and they differ most often at half
filling. On a lone hub, a star with one center and many arms, every handing passes through the center and is
tuned by all the other arms at once: least out of tune when they are set half one way and half the other, most
when nearly all are set the same way. There, as far as we have read it, the last to go is not the half but the nearly empty and the nearly
full. And on a chain whose places do not grip their neighbours at all, no filling's handings are out of tune more
often than another's, every filling lasts exactly as long, and no filling is the last alone. Where places that grip their neighbours also lean, some toward one
setting more than the rest, a handing at them goes out of tune by the lean as well, and the last to go can be a
filling that is none of these, neither the half nor the nearly empty nor the nearly full; on a chain a lean
without a grip parts no filling from another. On a chain or a ring whose places grip alike and
lean alike, what outlasts is what is most often out of tune, and that is the incomplete.

We are used to reading incompleteness as lack, as a thing waiting to be finished. This says the opposite.
The unfinished is not the draft of the finished; it is the part that lasts after the finished has faded. The
completed thing shines, and is billed, and is gone. The half holds on, and from it the next thing begins.

The incomplete is not waiting to be completed. It was already the longest note.

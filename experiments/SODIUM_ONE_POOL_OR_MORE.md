# One Sodium Pool or More: the Mean-Rate Identity against Published Agarose Data

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Authors:** Thomas Wicht, Claude (Opus 5.5)
**Status:** A test (Tier 4 reading) of published fitted values against an exact relation of the sodium model. The script recomputes every number on this page except two estimates made in the text, the diffusion lengths and the polarization; the data are read by hand from two open-access papers, some values off their figures, and the first paper's sampling in time by a script off a vector copy of its Fig. 2A in the first author's thesis, which also holds the paper's Fig. 5, read by the same script, its own Table 4.7, read by hand, and its Fig. 4.14, read by the same script; the one measured inversion recovery, that FID, is fitted in Parts 10 and 11, with the errors of those fits; elsewhere the error bars are the authors' fit uncertainties, taken as independent except where a table's own T₁m constrains their correlation (the thesis's Table 4.7), and carry none of their systematic errors.
**Script:** [`sodium_one_pool_or_more.py`](../simulations/sodium_one_pool_or_more.py), output in [`output.txt`](../simulations/results/sodium_one_pool_or_more/output.txt) (about twenty minutes; `--global` adds a differential-evolution check of the free-and-bound fit)

[The sodium page](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#what-a-sodium-measurement-can-read) holds one relation between three rates a measurement can read: in a single sodium pool the slow transverse rate is the mean of the two longitudinal rates, whatever the spectral densities, a residual splitting, an offset or a dynamic frequency shift. Its open list asks for that relation against measured rates and for what a voxel with two compartments does to it. This page takes the first published data we found that report all three rates for the same agarose concentrations: 2, 4 and 6 % agarose with 154 mM NaCl at 9.4 T, measured by Reichert, Schepkin, Kleimaier, Zöllner and Schad (2024).

The first paper's fitted values do not fit one pool, and the conflict sits in one place. The measured slow transverse time equals the authors' weighted longitudinal time T₁m within the errors, as one pool with nearly equal J₁ and J₂ would give. Their T₁ fit, with its amplitudes fixed to the one-pool values, reports instead a split of the two longitudinal rates that one pool could only combine with a slow transverse time 14 to 17 % shorter. The mechanisms inside one pool that this page and the agarose page name move the gap the other way, and a signal stored along z by imperfect pulses has the right sign but not the size. Free sodium beside bound sodium, with the free sodium like saline and the fit errors taken as independent, does not reproduce the numbers. The authors' own triple-quantum amplitudes, compared across the three samples, rise in a way that disfavors one pool with the fitted split and that two bound pools can give. What remains is two readings: the fitted split is not one pool's, or there are two pools on scales beyond 10 to 17 µm. The two sequences, simulated pulse by pulse, put the longitudinal triple-quantum signal at a tenth to about a third of what one pool with the fitted split gives, and both readings allow that; for pools that share the acquisition's d no size could tell them apart at first order in time, since one pool has as many unknowns there as the data have numbers and such pools read there as one pool at their mean. We do not know which holds; the shape of that signal in time would tell, at a noise below that of the one measured curve, whose triple quantum alone fixes the split only to 24 s⁻¹. The first author's thesis reports a second evaluation of these data and a separate acquisition at fixed evolution times, both with the two longitudinal times closer together: with the acquisition's times the relation holds within its errors, with the refit's it misses at 4 and 6 % by 2.8 and 2.3 σ. It finds the paper's evaluation, with the triple-quantum amplitude free, deviating from both, especially in T₁f ([The data](#the-data)). The one measured inversion recovery that stands as a vector path, the FID at 2 % in PC0, returns the paper's times there and shows two things that fit carries: it holds the curve's plateau at 1 where the curve's single quantum recovers to 0.9922 of its largest point, and the curve's two bands of sampling do not join on the drawn time axis. Past both, with one pool's weights held, the rank-3 pathway Eq. 13 leaves out among them, and the triple quantum free, the curve gives a split of 5.9 to 6.7 s⁻¹ where the printed fit gives 13.2, and the relation misses there by 3.0 to 3.3 σ; with the coarse band moved and the triple quantum held at one pool's amplitude it misses by 0.9 to 1.6 σ, at a cost in χ² of 46 to 73. Fitted on that curve together with the second paper's T₂s and T₂f, one pool misses T₂s by 2.7 σ or, with the triple quantum held at one pool's amplitude on the count's scale, pays in χ²; two bound pools meet both, with the triple quantum free or held, at four parameters more: in the best fits a large pool that carries most of the triple quantum, at a split that the held amplitude sets near reading (i)'s, both set by a triple-quantum height read through one pool's amplitude (1.64 s⁻¹ with the amplitude free, 0.91 to 3.92 over the band of nearly equal fits), and a small one that adds an early fast part in phase with the single quantum. Whether that part is a pool the curve cannot say; an inversion pulse short in angle, at the first paper's phase and with the curve read as the main signal's receiver channel, does not give it, nor does a slow share at a slow transverse fraction the second paper allows ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)).

## The relation

Hubbard's rates for one spin 3/2 under secular Redfield relaxation by fluctuating electric field gradients give the longitudinal magnetization two components, with rates 2J₁ (weight 0.2) and 2J₂ (weight 0.8), and the transverse magnetization two, with rates J₀ + J₁ (weight 0.6) and J₁ + J₂ (weight 0.4), where J₀, J₁, J₂ are the spectral densities at 0, ω₀ and 2ω₀ in the convention of the agarose page; the sodium page and the script write the same rates three times larger (6J₁, 6J₂, 3(J₀ + J₁), 3(J₁ + J₂)). Hence

  1/T₂s = (1/T₁s + 1/T₁f)/2.

Here s and f mean slow and fast, as in the two papers; the agarose page uses the subscript s for short, so its T₁ₛ and T₂ₛ are the fast components. The relation is linear in the densities and holds for any shape of the spectral density. When J₁ ≥ J₂, as one spectral-density curve of any distribution of correlation times gives, the component of weight 0.8 is the slower one, the T₁s of a fit that fixes the weights (the first paper's text beside its Eq. 7 gives the slow time as 1/(2J₁), the reverse, while its Eq. 9, a₀ = J₁/J₂ = T₁s/T₁f, has it as here); an ordered medium need not follow one curve ([the sodium page](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#beyond-spin-32)). The sodium page shows that the rates do not depend on a residual splitting; [the agarose page](SODIUM_IN_AGAROSE_LAYER_BY_LAYER.md) gives the reason for every static term diagonal in m: the jumps cannot move weight into or out of the central coherence, and such a term at most shifts its frequency (an offset and a dynamic shift do; a first-order splitting does not, since it moves the levels m = ±½ alike). The finite polarization at 9.4 T near room temperature (ħω₀/kT ≈ 2·10⁻⁵) and the terms the secular approximation drops are far below anything read here. The authors' own Eqs. 7 and 8 contain the relation; they do not test it.

Two consequences are used below. A single pool with J₁ ≥ J₂ has T₁m/2.5 ≤ T₂s ≤ T₁m, where T₁m, the authors' Eq. 17, is the mean of the two longitudinal rates weighted 0.8 and 0.2; the lower end is the agarose page's floor, the upper end follows the same way. And the relation's gap splits exactly as

  (1/T₁s + 1/T₁f)/2 − 1/T₂s = (1/T₁m − 1/T₂s) + 0.3·(1/T₁f − 1/T₁s),

a part that compares T₂s with T₁m and a part that is the split of the two longitudinal rates. T₁m is the combination the data fix best: in the first paper's Fig. 5 it stays flat while T₁s and T₁f drift apart as the late points are added (from about 47.5 and 35.8 ms to 49.2 and 30.9 ms); the measured FID, a PC0 curve, drifts the same way, and there the first jump belongs to a step at the join of its two bands of sampling and the slow rise after it to the fit's plateau, held at 1, and the noise that lifts the largest point ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)).

## What can carry the conflict

The mechanisms that can add to the relaxation of one pool, as far as this page and the agarose page name them, move the gap the other way, toward a central coherence that decays faster than the mean of the longitudinal rates:

- **Rank-1 fields**, paramagnetic or dipolar to protons: to first order their parts at ω₀ add equally to the central rate and to the mean of the longitudinal rates, and their part at zero frequency adds to the central rate alone. Their cross-correlation with the quadrupolar part, which the sodium page names, is not argued here.
- **Exchange between sites of different central frequency**: in the fast limit it adds p(1 − p)·δ²/k to the central coherence alone, as the agarose page notes.
- **Slow motion beyond the Redfield picture**: the part of the coupling along T₂₀ commutes with the populations and with the central transition however slowly it moves, and the parts sampled at ω₀ and 2ω₀ stay perturbative while ω_Q ≪ ω₀; what is left is the agarose page's fluctuating second-order shift of the central transition, extra decay.
- **Unrefocused field inhomogeneity and diffusion** shorten T₂ as well.

A distribution of correlation times that every nucleus samples keeps the relation. The authors find that correlation times and coupling strengths computed from the T₁ pair and from the T₂ pair disagree, and read that as "a distribution of correlation times and quadrupole strength parameters". Their comparison mixes this relation with [the key's single-motion check](THE_NUCLEUS_AS_A_KEY.md#back-to-the-motion); a distribution breaks the second and cannot break the first. Over the measurement a sodium ion travels micrometres, far beyond the pores, as the agarose page puts it: in a T₁ of 40 ms about √(6Dt) ≈ 17 µm, or √(2Dt) ≈ 10 µm out of a slab (D ≈ 1.2·10⁻⁹ m²/s, recalled). So each ion samples the gel's microstructure, and the distribution is of the kind that keeps the relation. Three readings remain:

- **(i) The fitted split is not one pool's.** The amplitudes are fixed at their one-pool values, and the first author's thesis finds the paper's evaluation, with the triple-quantum amplitude free, deviating from its refit and its acquisition at fixed times, especially in T₁f, and suggests that fixing the amplitude may give the more accurate times (its §5.3.1). On the one measured curve part of the split is the fit's own, its plateau and the join of its two bands, and so are the drift with the late points and the pattern of the paper's free fit, nearly all of the recovery at one time and the rest at the fit's bound; past them, with one pool's weights held and its triple quantum free, the curve keeps a split of 5.9 to 6.7 s⁻¹, above the 1.1 and 1.9 s⁻¹ that the triple quantum gives this reading at 2 % in PC1 and PC2 below; held at one pool's amplitude, as this reading's pool carries, the fit with the coarse band moved gives 1.2 to 2.8 s⁻¹ and meets the relation within 1.6 σ, at a cost in χ² of 46 to 73; with a small fast pool beside it, fitted to the curve and T₂s together, the cost goes, and in the best fits the pool that carries most of the triple quantum has a split of 1.06 to 2.62 s⁻¹ with the amplitude held, near this reading's, both set by a triple-quantum height read through one pool's amplitude, and 1.64 with it free, over a band of nearly equal fits from 0.91 to 3.92; that is this reading only if the small pool is not a pool, which the curve cannot tell ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)).
- **(ii) More than one pool** of nuclei that do not average during the relaxation, which needs regions beyond 10 to 17 µm with different relaxation, a heterogeneity of the gel on that scale.
- **(iii) The measurement.** With imperfect pulses a signal stored along z relaxes at longitudinal rates and pulls an apparent T₂s toward T₁, the sign seen here ([the agarose page](SODIUM_IN_AGAROSE_LAYER_BY_LAYER.md) names such pathways for its echo). The size does not fit. Added to one pool's transverse signal and fitted with Eq. 11, it would have to be 0.64, 0.56 and 0.36 of that signal if it relaxed at T₁s, 1.6 to 1.7 times it at T₁m, and would lift the slow amplitude to 0.55 to 0.76 against the measured 0.40 to 0.41. Stored along z for half the evolution, even the whole slow signal would decay at 42.9, 37.3 and 32.6 ms, 1.5, 2.6 and 1.0 σ short of the measured T₂s. The sequences simulated pulse by pulse (below) give the part a common flip-angle error carries: at most 0.23, 0.34 and 0.48 of the gap with every flip angle 10 % off.

## Free and bound sodium

The simplest picture of more than one pool is free sodium relaxing like saline, beside one bound pool. The script builds it from the spin-3/2 operators with a pool label beside each nucleus: the free pool in extreme narrowing at T₁ = T₂ = 54.19 ms, the bound pool with J₀ ≥ J₁ ≥ J₂ ≥ 0, exchange with detailed balance. It reads the signals the sequences record, the rank-1 and rank-3 parts after an inversion and after an excitation, and puts them through the authors' fits: Eq. 13 of the first paper for T₁, with the sign between the two longitudinal terms that the physics requires (the printed equation shows a minus) and the triple-quantum part weighted at the reported ratio, and Eq. 11 of the second paper for T₂, on 26 evenly spaced evolution times (their spacing is not stated; geometric spacing is shown beside). Its signals have a plateau of exactly 1 and share one grid with the fit, so these fits carry neither the plateau nor the step at the join that the authors' fit carries on the measured curve ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)); the published values they take as data carry them where their curves do, as the one PC0 curve does; the PC2 values have no curve. - **One pool alone** gives χ² = 26.5, 69.6 and 62.3 for the five independent numbers.
- **Free and bound, without exchange** (four parameters, one degree of freedom): χ² = 15.8, 39.5 and 11.7; with geometric spacing 16.5, 46.0 and 20.3, which makes 2 % the weakest instead of 6 %; halving or doubling the triple-quantum weight changes them by less than one. - **With exchange**, profiled over rates from 1 to 1000 s⁻¹: χ² rises with the rate toward the one-pool values, since fast exchange makes one averaged pool.

Under the fit errors taken as independent, and with free sodium like the 0 % sample, the free-and-bound picture fails in all three samples. On the measured FID at 2 %, fitted with the second paper's T₂s and T₂f, free sodium at 54.19 ms takes 9.8 % of the line and puts the slow transverse fraction 5.8 σ above the measured one, and at 2 % of the line it lies within 0.4 σ of it, 1.0 to 2.9 σ with the transverse twin, but misses T₂s by 2.3 σ ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)).

## The sequences, pulse by pulse

The script's Part 8 runs both sequences on one spin 3/2: hard pulses, the relaxation between them, the eight phase steps and the acquisition. The first paper's Fig. 1 gives the inversion-recovery sequence: the inversion pulse, the evolution time, a 90° pulse whose phase advances by 45° with each step of the evolution time, and after a mixing time the paper does not give (zero in the script) a second 90° pulse at phase 0 before the acquisition; PC1 turns the inversion pulse by 180° in every other scan, PC2 shifts the first 90° pulse by ±135° in two scans. The transverse sequence starts with a 90° pulse, refocuses halfway through the evolution and ends with the same two 90° pulses; the papers do not give its phases. A phase stepped with the evolution time acts as an offset, which turns the orders ±1 at the stepping frequency and the orders ±3 at three times it, as [the sodium page](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#the-irreducible-tensors-in-strings) has it for the string class that carries both the central transition and the triple quantum; the script splits the signal over the phase steps that way. It reads each acquisition at the line, the integral of its signal, the way the second paper builds its transverse signal (the caption of its Supporting Fig. S2); the first paper does not say.

For one pool with ideal pulses, counting what starts from the first pulse of each sequence (a phase cycle that alternates that pulse with the receiver keeps only that), the two triple-quantum amplitudes, each over its own single-quantum amplitude as the first paper's Eqs. 15 and 16 define them, are

  longitudinal (4/5)·(√5/4)·(√15/8) / ((1/√2)·(1/2)) · |d| = (√6/4)·|d|,  transverse (√6/5)·(√15/8)² / (1/2)² · |d| = (3√6/16)·|d|,

where d is what the acquisition makes of a rank-3 coherence against a rank-1 one, read at the line d = (√6/5)·(T₂f − T₂s)/(0.6·T₂f + 0.4·T₂s) with the times the acquisition decays with, the inverse of the correction factor of the second paper's Eq. 8. Their ratio is 4/3, whatever d is. Eq. 16 divides the longitudinal amplitude by A₁s + A₁f = 1, the recovered single quantum of Eq. 13, and Eq. 15 the transverse one by the single quantum at the start of its decay, as the count does. Both triple-quantum signals pass through the same two coherences |±3/2⟩⟨∓3/2|, the cells |00⟩⟨11| and |11⟩⟨00| in the sodium page's two-qubit names, and leave them through the same last pulse and the same acquisition; they differ in where they start. The longitudinal one starts from the inverted populations, whose rank-3 order is 4/5 times the difference of the two longitudinal exponentials, and is turned into the triple quantum with the Wigner coefficient √5/4; the transverse one starts from the rank-3 coherence, √6/5 times the difference of the two transverse exponentials, and is turned with √15/8. The triple quantum comes back with √15/8 in both, and each single quantum has its own rank-1 coefficients. The two relaxation factors are twice √(4/5·1/5) and √(3/5·2/5), square roots of products of Hubbard's weights; the sodium page states the second as its triple-quantum path, and SodiumTwoMirrorsWitness gates it and both turns of the tensor bases live. Two routes give the amplitudes and meet to 8·10⁻¹⁴, at most four times the rounding that the scans and the evolution allow: the coefficients, which sympy gives exactly, and the sequences simulated, over three choices of the transverse phases (twice the refocusing pulse's phase less the first pulse's: 90°, 30° and −60°). PC1 leaves the longitudinal ratio as it is for an ideal inversion pulse, PC2 for any flip angle: adding the two scans at ±135° multiplies each order p by cos(135°·p), 1/√2 in size for the single and the triple quantum and zero for the double, which the simulation shows order by order to 10⁻¹⁵ with every flip angle 10 % off. The first paper's statement that the transverse pathway gives the larger triple-quantum signal is about the signal's height, the coefficient times the peak of its shape, which is larger there because the two transverse times lie further apart.

The times the acquisition decays with are not known. It is not refocused: the homogeneous T₂ gives the largest |d|, 0.75, 0.89 and 0.94, and any broadening or a finite acquisition window lowers it. The second paper corrects its transverse signal with T₂* (its Table 1); put into the formula, those give 0.57, 0.72 and 0.80, but its two components do not share one broadening at 2 and 6 %, and one Lorentzian broadening shared by both and matched to either gives 0.32 to 0.46 at 2 % and 0.71 to 0.78 at 6 %. [The agarose page](SODIUM_IN_AGAROSE_LAYER_BY_LAYER.md) describes the same unrefocused spread for its own echo, where it moves the weights a peak fit reads. So |d| is at most 0.75, 0.89 and 0.94 and in these models comes down to 0.32, 0.72 and 0.71; everything absolute below carries that range and assumes ideal pulses. With the alternation one pool's ratio stays 4/3 at every choice of the transverse phases for ideal pulses, and over the three pools and the phase choices it lies between 1.26 and 1.35 with every flip angle 5 % off and between 1.18 and 1.42 with 10 %, read as a fit with a free phase reads it; PC1 then reads 1 to 8 % above PC0 and PC2. Without the alternation the magnetization recovering during the evolution is turned by the TPPI-phased 90° pulse into the single and the triple quantum, in quadrature with what starts from the first pulse: at 60 ms it is 0.58 to 1.28 of the transverse triple quantum and 2.7 to 6.4 times the transverse single quantum there, a pathway stored along z like those the agarose page names. A single quantum ruled at late times by a component in quadrature does not look like the published fits, two transverse components with a slow fraction of 0.40 to 0.41, and the numbers here are for the alternated sequence. Fitted like the second paper's Eq. 11 on its 26 evolution times, that sequence's single quantum gives one pool a T₂s longer by up to 0.64, 0.88 and 0.83 ms with every flip angle 5 % off and up to 1.65, 2.30 and 2.15 ms with 10 %, at most 0.23, 0.34 and 0.48 of the gap: the sign of reading (iii), not its size.

The measured ratios then place the longitudinal triple quantum at TQ₁ = 0.10, 0.17 and 0.25 of one pool's with the fitted times in PC1, and at 0.17, 0.24 and 0.35 in PC2 (errors 0.02 to 0.05 from the first paper's Table 2; the transverse values read off its Fig. 6C carry none). That reading assumes the two acquisitions lose alike and does not depend on d; it scales with 4/3 over one pool's actual ratio, by 0.94 to 1.13 over the flip angles 10 % off. Read from the size alone, over the range of d and for ideal pulses, the values are 0.07 to 0.16, 0.11 to 0.14 and 0.20 to 0.26 (PC1) and 0.12 to 0.27, 0.16 to 0.20 and 0.28 to 0.37 (PC2). One pool with the fitted split, TQ₁ = 1, is excluded by the size itself as the paper's fitted amplitudes give it, not only by the trend across the samples (the thesis's amplitudes: [The data](#the-data) and [The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)): the largest TQ₁ would need one pool's ratio 2.84 times smaller than 4/3, where the flip angles, the phases and the pools above keep it between 1.18 and 1.42, and the two phase cycles disagree by at most 1.72. The ratios are the table's: on the measured FID the printed fit gives a triple-quantum amplitude of 4.68 % where the table's PC0 cell gives 3.14 %, for a reason not known ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)).

Both remaining readings reach these values. Two bound pools held at the measured TQ₁, fitted like everything above to the five numbers of PC2, reach χ² of at most 1.12 in every sample and at either cycle's value; with seven parameters for these six conditions that shows they can, not that they do (the search of [Two bound pools](#two-bound-pools) went from below; this one holds TQ₁ at the value). One pool whose true split is 1.1, 2.2 and 3.5 s⁻¹ (PC1, with its own times and T₁m) or 1.9, 3.4 and 5.4 s⁻¹ (PC2) gives them as well, its triple quantum read through the shape of the published times, as a fit with those times reads it. That pool predicts a T₂s shorter than the second paper's by 0.96, 1.15 and 0.16 σ (PC1; χ² = 2.26 for three, p = 0.52) or by 1.30, 1.95 and 1.25 σ (PC2; χ² = 7.05, p = 0.07), and within 1.1 ms of the first paper's own T₂s (its Fig. 6A, read off without error bars); with one pool's ratio anywhere in 1.18 to 1.42, χ² runs from 2.06 to 2.72 (PC1) and from 6.24 (p = 0.10) to 9.02 (p = 0.03) (PC2). The first paper's Fig. 5 shows the trade the fit makes: as the late points are added, the fitted ratio falls from about 8.6 to 5.3 % while the split widens, a drift that the measured FID in PC0 shares, where it comes from a step at the join of its bands and the fit's plateau ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)), and the coefficient times the peak of its own shape, the height of the triple quantum against the single quantum, stays at 0.897 and 0.905 %. The height is the stable datum, and TQ₁ follows the split. So the size does not decide between readings (i) and (ii); under PC2 it leans weakly against (i), under PC1 not at all. The shape in time would: one pool with such a small split puts the peak of its triple quantum near 43.1, 37.2 and 32.2 ms, the published times put it at 38.6, 33.2 and 29.2 ms (PC2), and two pools give a sum of two shapes. That needs the measured curves, at a noise below that of the one PC0 curve, whose triple quantum alone fixes the split only to 24 s⁻¹ (Part 10, on the drawn axis); there the best two-pool fits carry the triple quantum 90 to 95 % in their large pool, 59 % with 15 % of the line in the small one, and with the amplitude held what separates them from one pool is an early part of the single quantum ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)). For two pools the comparison holds only roughly in one respect: each pool's triple quantum reaches the receiver with its own d, which the search, reading the signal before the acquisition, does not carry.

Counting says why the size cannot decide, at least at first order in time. There one pool has as many unknowns as the data have numbers. The two initial rates are 1/T₁m = 0.4·J₁ + 1.6·J₂ for the recovery and 0.6/T₂f + 0.4/T₂s = 0.6·J₀ + J₁ + 0.4·J₂ for the transverse decay; each triple quantum starts with a slope, its amplitude times its split, (√6/2)·|d|·(J₁ − J₂) for the longitudinal one and (3√6/16)·|d|·(J₀ − J₂) for the transverse one. For densities that fall from J₀ to J₂ these four numbers fix J₀, J₁, J₂ and d exactly once, unless both slopes vanish, as they do where all three densities are equal: 1.2 times the first slope and 3.2 times the second add to √6·|d| times the difference of the two initial rates, which gives d, and the rest follows. So at first order the data measure d, and they have no equality to test one pool with. Their inequalities cannot tell one pool from several either, as long as the pools share d: the four numbers are linear in the densities, so such pools give exactly the first-order numbers of one pool at their mean densities, each pool weighted by its share of the signal read at the line, and each inequality bounds a convex set, which the mean stays in; exchange between the pools keeps each coherence summed over them and drops out of the first order as well. Pools whose acquisitions decay differently do not share d, and then the inequalities can flag a mixture. The published values meet them: |d| comes out at 0.51, 0.59 and 0.74 (0.51, 0.60 and 0.74 in PC2), below the largest values of 0.75, 0.89 and 0.94 and at 4 % also below the broadening models' 0.72, as the transverse amplitude there lies below one pool's; and the densities lie in the region every sum of Lorentzian densities fills, J₀ ≥ J₁ ≥ J₂ ≥ J₀·J₁/(4J₀ − 3J₁), of which the positive slopes already give J₁ ≥ J₂ and J₀ ≥ J₂, so that J₀ ≥ J₁, the lower edge and the largest |d| are the tests. That region's lower edge is one Lorentzian, the single-motion relation of [the key page](THE_NUCLEUS_AS_A_KEY.md#back-to-the-motion), and the published values lie above it by 0.04 to 0.08 in J₂/J₀, with J₁/J₂ between 1.06 and 1.21 and J₀/J₁ between 8 and 16: off one Lorentzian in the direction a sum of them takes, the shape the agarose page finds in its own data. The split of that pool, 2(J₁ − J₂), is 1.28, 2.49 and 3.92 s⁻¹ (PC1) or 2.04, 3.85 and 6.05 s⁻¹ (PC2), 10 to 14 % above the splits of reading (i), which reads the triple quantum through the published shape where this reads its slope; the slope is the less stable of the two, moving by 8.0 % in Fig. 5 where the height moves by 0.9 %. The count is the ideal sequence's: it takes Hubbard's weights 0.6 and 0.4 for the transverse rate, without the rank-3 pathway and its transverse twin of the section below, which shift the weights it takes, and it takes the slopes of the fitted curves and the transverse amplitude of the first paper's figure with the second paper's times. A dynamic frequency shift adds no unknown: the transverse sequence refocuses at half the evolution, where the shift, odd in m, cancels at first order, and in the acquisition it is part of d, which both triple quanta share. The mean-rate identity needs the second order, the split of each single quantum on its own, and the T₂s comparison above is that order.

The simulation shows three more things.

- **A pathway Eq. 13 leaves out.** The first 90° pulse also turns the rank-3 population order into rank-3 coherence of order ±1, which shows at the single-quantum frequency and reaches the receiver during the acquisition. The first paper's discussion expects pathways besides the main one to add to the single quantum and judges them much smaller than it, too small to influence the single-quantum peak. This one is that small: over the range of d it moves 0.020 to 0.058 of the weight from the fast to the slow component, as 0.8 + (√6/40)·|d| gives. With the weights fixed it is not small for the split: the fit reads one pool with the published split as a split 3 to 6 % smaller and a T₁m 0.5 to 1.1 ms longer when its triple-quantum part carries one pool's own weight, and 6 to 17 % smaller and 0.5 to 1.4 ms longer at the reported weight. That moves the gap further from one pool, not toward it; for reading (i), at the largest |d|, it raises the deviations above to 1.02, 1.31 and 0.35 σ (PC1; χ² = 2.87) and to 1.40, 2.21 and 1.69 σ (PC2; χ² = 9.69, p = 0.02). For ideal pulses it is on the single quantum a fifth of the triple-quantum carrier's signal at every d, which ties it for any mixture of pools; Part 11's fits of the measured FID carry it, Part 10's, which identify the paper's form, do not ([The measured FID](#the-measured-fid)).
- **Its transverse twin.** The transverse sequence carries the same kind of term: the rank-3 coherence built during the evolution reaches order ±1 at the second 90° pulse and the receiver through d, and for ideal pulses moves the slow weight of the transverse single quantum to 0.4 + (√6/80)·|d|. The ratio of the two triple quanta does not see it, since the weights still add to 1, but the slow fraction the earlier sections compare with the measured 0.40 to 0.41 does: for one pool it becomes 0.41 to 0.43, and its deviation from the measured value turns from −2.0, −0.6 and −0.8 σ to −0.5 to +1.4, +2.6 to +3.4 and +2.4 to +3.4 σ over the range of d. Flip angles 5 % off move it by as much again (0.413 to 0.444), so the size of that deviation is open; its direction at 4 and 6 %, worse for one pool and for free and bound sodium, whose slow amplitude was already high, survives them. The pool fits of the earlier sections and of Part 11 do not carry it.
- **The amplitudes themselves.** Over the range of d one pool's transverse amplitude read at the line is 14.9 to 34.6, 33.1 to 40.9 and 32.5 to 43.3 %, and the measured 23.1, 27.0 and 33.8 % are 0.67 to 1.55, 0.66 to 0.82 and 0.78 to 1.04 of it. The 4 % value rests on the figure: the second paper's transverse maximum (its Table 2), turned into an amplitude with one pool's shape at its T₂, gives 23.3, 31.0 and 35.0 %, the same at 2 and 6 % and 15 % higher at 4 %, where it lies at 0.76 to 0.94 of one pool's. The phase cycles are not understood: the measured PC2 values are 1.72, 1.45 and 1.38 times the PC1 values, while in the model PC2 equals PC0 for any flip angle and a common flip-angle error raises PC1, by 1 to 8 %. The authors read it as the single quantum of PC2 adding imperfectly. One candidate outside exact pulse phases is an error of 5 to 10° in PC2's ±135° step, which multiplies order p by cos(p·(135° + error)): it raises PC2's ratio by 1.35 to 1.68 and leaves 0.17 to 0.34 of PC0's double quantum, which the first paper's Fig. 3 calls suppressed and could test. The ratio of the two amplitudes does not depend on the acquisition, which is why the readings above go through it.

## What this is, and what it is not

Not new: Hubbard's rates, from which the relation is one line, and which the authors write themselves; the relation as a model check (the sodium page); the relation applied to published agarose values as a floor, and used there to exclude a motional explanation; diffusion over the measurement far beyond the pores; the second-order shift of slow motion as extra decay (the agarose page); triple-quantum filtering as the way to tell intrinsic bi-exponential relaxation from a superposition of compartments (Jaccard, Wimperis and Bodenhausen, 1986, cited on the sodium page and by the first paper), the sodium page's triple-quantum path, of which the longitudinal argument here is the twin, and the second paper's own modelling of two compartments with their triple-quantum signals; the transfer functions and the turning of tensors by pulses, which triple-quantum filtering rests on, and the two sequences with their phase cycles (the first paper); the correction factor of the second paper's Eq. 8, whose inverse is d; one pool's longitudinal triple-quantum amplitude through the Wigner coefficients at 90° and d (the first author's thesis, its Eq. 3.16); that this amplitude does not depend on the T₁ times, so that the largest triple-quantum signal rather than the ratio characterizes the signal, that a fitted amplitude should be close to it, and that the evaluation with the amplitude free deviates from an acquisition at fixed evolution times, especially in T₁f, and that fixing the amplitude may give the more accurate times (the thesis, §5.3.1), the doubt in the fitted split behind reading (i); sodium in tissue and gels as more than one pool; free and bound sodium as a model; that pathways besides the main one add to the single quantum, small against it, that the scaling A₁SQ of Eq. 13 accounts for an imperfect inversion, that PC1 cancels what an imperfect inversion pulse leaves, that imperfect pulses may create a double quantum, which only PC0 keeps, that the T₁ times of the four cycles agree, with a negligible influence of unwanted signals, and that an imperfect inversion's T₁₁ → T₃₁ pathway reaches the triple quantum and should be negligible since PC2 and PC3 match (the first paper, the pathway and the double quantum after Madelin et al.); the triple-quantum ratio across the samples, which disfavors one pool with the fitted split; one pool's ratio of the two triple-quantum amplitudes, 4/3 by the Wigner coefficients and by the sequences simulated, which puts the longitudinal triple quantum at a tenth to about a third of one pool's and leaves both readings open; and the size of one such pathway, the rank-3 order at the single-quantum frequency, small for the peak and, with the weights fixed, not small for the split, which it moves against one pool; and the count at first order in time, where the four numbers have no equality to test one pool with and their inequalities, the region every sum of Lorentzian densities fills among them, pass any mixture of pools that share d; and on that curve, with the second paper's T₂s and T₂f, one pool missing T₂s or paying in χ² where two bound pools meet both, their large pool's split set by a held amplitude near reading (i)'s and the small one an early part in phase with the single quantum, which an inversion pulse short in angle at the first paper's phase does not give, with the curve read as the main signal's receiver channel, nor a slow share at a slow transverse fraction the second paper's amplitudes allow; and a slow share bounded against the plateau.

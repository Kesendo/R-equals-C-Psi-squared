**A word fence.**
- *Order* p = m − m′ is the coherence order of |m⟩⟨m′|.
- J₀, J₁, J₂ are the spectral densities of the electric field gradient at 0, ω₀ and 2ω₀, as they enter the Redfield generator (secular in the Larmor frequency, the conventions below).
- *Dark* means untouched by the J₀ dissipator: an operator |m⟩⟨m′| that commutes with T₂₀. This is the operator sense of DARK in MirrorWorld's `EndCount` (commuting with every jump), restricted to the one jump T₂₀. It is not a state annihilated by a jump, which is the sense of [Singlet Under a Shared Field](SINGLET_UNDER_A_SHARED_FIELD.md). *Bright* is the rest.
- A *bright pair* is the two bright cells of one order block that a jump couples, the satellites |3/2⟩⟨1/2| and |−1/2⟩⟨−3/2| at order 1 or the double quanta |3/2⟩⟨−1/2| and |1/2⟩⟨−3/2| at order 2, with their transposes at the orders −1 and −2; not MirrorWorld's `Pair`, which is one coherence. Its 2 × 2 is a closed block of the generator's matrix, not one of MirrorWorld's joint-popcount `Block`s.
- The *coupling* of a bright pair is the off-diagonal entry of its block between the two cells, 3J₂ for the satellites and −3J₁ for the double quanta, its sign fixed by the cells' phases: the jumps carrying one cell into the other, a transfer of coherence by relaxation in NMR's words (from memory), not a Hamiltonian coupling, which here is the splitting's part. Read through a combination of the two cells (κ below), the coupling takes the combination's sign.
- A *mirror about −c* is the map λ ↦ −λ̄ − 2c on the eigenvalues of one order block, and c is called its centre. The spectrum of the order p block is symmetric about Im λ = −p·ω, not about 0, so the repository's F1 form λ ↦ −λ − 2σ would send it to the block of order −p. The order −p block has the complex-conjugate characteristic polynomial of the order p block (checked), so the map used here is F1's map followed by that conjugation, read inside one block.

**Conventions.** Spin operators I_z, I_± for I = 3/2; T₂₀ = (3I_z² − I(I + 1))/√6, T₂,±₁ = ∓(I_zI_± + I_±I_z)/2, T₂,±₂ = I_±²/2; Lindblad dissipator Σ_m J_|m|·(T₂ₘρT₂ₘ† − ½{T₂ₘ†T₂ₘ, ρ}); Hamiltonian ω_Q·T₂₀ + ω·I_z, so ω_Q is the coefficient of T₂₀ in this normalization, not the conventional quadrupolar splitting. ## What this is about

Sodium in tissue is a spin 3/2: four levels, not two. It relaxes through the fluctuating electric field gradient around it, not through a magnetic field, and sodium MRI uses that relaxation to tell tissue compartments apart, most directly through the triple-quantum coherence between m = +3/2 and m = −3/2. The repository's mirror results live at d = 2, and for d > 2 they hold only partially ([F121](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md), under equidistant dephasing); [Qubit Necessity](../docs/QUBIT_NECESSITY.md) §8a leaves the non-dephasing dissipators at d > 2 open. This page reads one physical one, the quadrupolar dissipator of a single spin 3/2.

When all three spectral densities are present it has no single mirror. It has two. The slow part of the gradient, J₀, sees one property of a level: outer (|m| = 3/2) or inner (|m| = 1/2). Everything that agrees on that property, the populations, the central transition and the triple quantum, is dark to it, and this dark half of the spectrum is palindromic about −3(J₁ + J₂), with the central transition and the triple quantum sitting exactly at its centre. The bright half, the satellite and double-quantum coherences, is palindromic about a second centre 3J₀ further out. Both mirrors are exact, for every static quadrupolar splitting and every offset. The dark centre is the mean of the two longitudinal rates 6J₁ and 6J₂, and it is also the triple-quantum rate; that identity was already a line away from the published rates, and the two mirrors give it a place.

Nothing in the generator is particular to sodium. It is the quadrupolar relaxation of any spin 3/2, and the nuclei with spin 3/2 include ⁷Li, ⁹Be, ¹¹B, ²³Na, ³³S, ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Cl, ³⁹K, ⁴¹K, ⁶³Cu, ⁶⁵Cu, ⁶⁹Ga, ⁷¹Ga, ⁷⁹Br, ⁸¹Br and ⁸⁷Rb; spin 1 (²H, ¹⁴N) and spin 5/2 (¹⁷O, ²⁷Al) are the section beyond spin 3/2 (the spins and what follows on mechanisms from memory). What differs from nucleus to nucleus is the size and the ratio of the three densities, through the quadrupole moment, the gradient at the site and the Larmor frequency at which J₁ and J₂ are read, and whether this generator is the whole story. Where the moment or the gradient is small (⁷Li and ⁹Be; ¹¹B in BF₄⁻, ³⁵Cl in ClO₄⁻), dipolar, paramagnetic and chemical-shift-anisotropy relaxation compete; their jumps are not the five T₂,ₘ of one spin (the paramagnetic and anisotropy ones are rank 1), and their cross-correlation with the quadrupolar part adds terms the diagonal form Σ J_|m| does not carry. Where the moment is large (Br, Cu, Rb, Cl bound to large molecules) the Redfield condition itself can fail. Where quadrupolar relaxation is the only mechanism and the Redfield condition holds, every exact statement on this page holds for that nucleus as written, with its own J₀, J₁, J₂, and where it merely dominates they hold approximately; sodium is the nucleus this page's sources wrote for and the one sodium MRI reads.

## Abstract

One spin 3/2 under the Redfield generator of quadrupolar relaxation, secular in the Larmor frequency, at infinite temperature (jumps T₂,ₘ, m = −2 … 2, at rates J₀, J₁, J₁, J₂, J₂), with a static splitting ω_Q·T₂₀ and an offset ω·I_z. The generator is block diagonal in the coherence order. Exactly, with every parameter a symbol: the populations decay at 0, 6J₁, 6J₂, 6(J₁ + J₂); the central transition and the triple quantum at 3(J₁ + J₂), shifted only by their frequencies; at ω_Q = 0 the satellites at 3(J₀ + J₁) and 3(J₀ + J₁) + 6J₂, the double quantum at 3(J₀ + J₂) and 3(J₀ + J₂) + 6J₁ (Souza et al.'s rates with their constant set to 3); a splitting draws each pair together (after the frequency shift p·ω), merges it at 6ω_Q² = 9J₂² (satellites, J₂ ≠ 0) or 6ω_Q² = 9J₁² (double quantum, J₁ ≠ 0) into one defective eigenvalue on the bright centre (an exceptional point of the eigenvalues; the satellites' lines, read by I₊, separate only at a larger splitting, and whether a line comes before or after the point is the observable's), and past it turns it into a pair on the same real part, conjugate after the frequency shift p·ω. The dark set pairs under λ ↦ −λ̄ − 2c₁ with c₁ = 3(J₁ + J₂), the bright set under the same map with c₂ = 3(J₀ + J₁ + J₂) = c₁ + 3J₀; with ω_Q and ω present, the satellite and double-quantum polynomials keep real coefficients after the frequency shift p·ω and the root sum −2c₂. The two centres coincide exactly when J₀ = 0. The whole spectrum has one centre exactly when J₀ = 0, or when ω_Q = ω = 0 and one of J₁, J₂ vanishes (proved below; for non-negative densities a second, independent route gives it again). The reason is that at I = 3/2, and at no other spin, the five Hermitian components of T₂,ₘ are Dirac matrices: the dissipator is Pauli-string dephasing, and one map, X ↦ Γ₀·Xᵀ, carries both mirrors. At every spin I ≥ 1 other than 3/2, J₀ alone breaks the mirror (proved); without a static splitting, at every offset, no rates other than all zero give a global centre at any spin from I = 1 to 6 other than 3/2 (proved spin by spin), and off resonance, at an offset ω ≠ 0, none at any spin I ≥ 2 (proved for every I at once, from the traces of the three highest coherence orders). A static splitting, on resonance, changes that at spin 1, and at no other spin from 2 to 6: there it creates a centre on two tuned rays with non-negative rates, both with J₀ = 0 (proved by elimination; the two rays found again by hand). At every offset and every splitting, a centre at any spin I ≥ 2 needs two population rates to coincide (proved for every I). That leaves the planes of rates where two coincide, finitely many at every integer spin and at every half-integer spin checked, to 61/2. On them the question is decided without a splitting off resonance at every spin, on resonance with or without a splitting up to I = 6, and nowhere else. At spin 3/2 the dynamic frequency shift, K₁ and K₂ at ω₀ and 2ω₀ (K = Im Γ beside the rate J = 2·Re Γ of one complex transform Γ, so K is half the imaginary part of the complex density 2Γ = J + 2iK), adds a real diagonal Hamiltonian: both mirrors and the exceptional points survive it exactly, the central transition moves 6(2K₁ − K₂) off the satellites, and one global centre then exists exactly when J₀ = 0, or ω_Q = 0 with J₁ = 0 and ω = 6K₁, or ω_Q = 0 with J₂ = 0 and ω = 3K₂: the frame in which the satellites or the double quanta are on resonance. Under a spin-lock the central transition's part keeps both mirrors and the whole field breaks them at generic rates (read at points), and a central-transition-selective lock keeps them only through first order in the field over the splitting; in the extreme-narrowing limit with no splitting any lock keeps them, turned with the field. In the irreducible tensors of NMR, at zero splitting, the two bases agree on ten of the sixteen, each of them lying in one string class (rank 0, rank 2, rank 3 at |p| = 2, 3). On the other six they are turned by fixed angles, Hubbard's weights 4/5 : 1/5 and 3/5 : 2/5, and a splitting leaves four eigenoperators (T₀,₀, T₂,₀, T₃,±₃); S carries the rank-2 double quanta onto the rank-3 ones, and the order p is not a property of every string.

## The prediction and how it fared

**Written before computing:** with J₀ alone the spectrum is an exact palindrome, since T₂₀ is a Z on one bit (held at ω_Q = ω = 0: two levels of rate, 0 and 3J₀, eight modes each; a splitting or an offset breaks it, as the theorem below shows); with J₁, J₂ present the palindrome breaks in general, and a guess that extreme narrowing (J₀ = J₁ = J₂) restores it. ## The two mirrors

The verifier computes the characteristic polynomial of every order block with J₀, J₁, J₂, ω_Q, ω as symbols; the blocks of order −p have the conjugate polynomials of those of order p, so the statements below for p ≥ 0 carry over.

- **Dark, centre c₁ = 3(J₁ + J₂).** The populations have roots 0, −6J₁, −6J₂, −6(J₁ + J₂) at every ω_Q and ω, two pairs about −c₁. The central transition is the factor x + c₁ + iω of the order-1 polynomial and the triple quantum the whole order-3 polynomial x + c₁ + 3iω: both sit at Re λ = −c₁, each its own mirror image.
- **Bright, centre c₂ = 3(J₀ + J₁ + J₂).** The remaining order-1 quadratic and the order-2 quadratic, written in y = x + p·iω, have real coefficients and the linear coefficient 6(J₀ + J₁ + J₂). Their roots are either a real pair about −c₂ or a complex pair on Re = −c₂ (the static splitting enters only the constant term, as 6ω_Q²), and in both cases λ ↦ −λ̄ − 2c₂ maps the pair to itself. Under the conjugated map a pair is its own mirror only if both roots sit on Re λ = −c, or share their frequency with real parts summing to −2c; the real coefficients and the root sum of the shifted quadratic give exactly that. What the two mirrors carry beyond the single block is that the blocks of one sector share one centre: the populations' four roots centred on −c₁ with the central transition and the triple quantum on it, both bright quadratics centred on −c₂.
- **The distance between the centres** is c₂ − c₁ = 3J₀, the J₀ cost of a bright cell. The two centres coincide exactly when J₀ = 0, so then the whole spectrum has one centre, for every ω_Q and ω.

## When the whole spectrum has one centre

**Theorem.** For real J₀, J₁, J₂, ω_Q, ω, the sixteen eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, have one common centre −s, under λ ↦ −λ − 2s or equally under λ ↦ −λ̄ − 2s, exactly when J₀ = 0, or when ω_Q = ω = 0 and J₁·J₂ = 0.

The two maps give the same answer because the spectrum is closed under conjugation (the order −p block carries the conjugate polynomial of the order p block). The theorem needs no sign on the J; physical spectral densities are non-negative anyway.

*The centre is fixed by the trace.* The Hamiltonian part of the generator is traceless, so Tr L = −24J₀ − 48J₁ − 48J₂ at every ω_Q and ω. A pairing has its centre at minus the mean eigenvalue, so 2s = 3J₀ + 6J₁ + 6J₂.

*Proof.* Let P(x) be the characteristic polynomial of the generator, of degree 16, the product of the seven order blocks' polynomials. The spectrum is symmetric about −s, with multiplicities, exactly when P(−x − 2s) = P(x) holds identically in x. The difference P(−x − 2s) − P(x) has fourteen non-zero coefficients, each a polynomial in J₀, J₁, J₂, ω_Q, ω with real coefficients. (The coefficient of x¹⁶ cancels because the degree is even, that of x¹⁵ by the choice of s, that of x¹⁴ vanishes identically; the first survivor, at x¹³, is 144·J₀·ω_Q², which alone forces J₀ = 0 or ω_Q = 0.) The fourteen coefficients generate the same ideal as the two polynomials

  J₀·ω_Q²,  J₀·(9J₁² + ω²)·(9J₂² + 4ω²),

which are the ideal's reduced Gröbner basis in lexicographic order, and equal ideals have equal zero sets. Over the reals, 9J₁² + ω² vanishes only at J₁ = ω = 0, and 9J₂² + 4ω² only at J₂ = ω = 0. So the coefficients all vanish exactly when J₀ = 0, or when ω_Q = 0, ω = 0 and J₁ = 0 or J₂ = 0. ∎

*Why it looks this way.* At ω_Q = ω = 0 the sixteen rates r = −λ are explicit. The generator preserves the trace, so λ = 0 is always an eigenvalue, and the mirror must send it to rate 2s. The difference 2s − r is, rate by rate, 3·(J₀ + 2J₁ + 2J₂), 3·(J₀ + 2J₂), 3·(J₀ + 2J₁), 3J₀ (the populations), 3·(J₀ + J₁ + J₂) (the dark coherences), 3·(J₁ + 2J₂), 3J₁ (the satellites), 3·(2J₁ + J₂), 3J₂ (the double quantum). For non-negative densities each of these is non-zero unless some density vanishes, so with all three positive no rate sits at 2s, 0 has no partner, and there is no centre. The partner of 0 is the largest population rate when J₀ = 0, the upper satellite when J₁ = 0, and the upper double quantum when J₂ = 0. For J₁ = 0 the reflection r ↦ 3J₀ + 6J₂ − r sends the multiset {0², (6J₂)², (3J₂)⁴, (3J₀)², (3J₀ + 6J₂)², (3J₀ + 3J₂)⁴} onto itself (exponents are multiplicities): 0 ↔ 3J₀ + 6J₂, 6J₂ ↔ 3J₀, 3J₂ ↔ 3J₀ + 3J₂. The populations pair with the satellites, the dark coherences with the double quanta. J₂ = 0 is the same with J₁ and J₂ exchanged, and the roles swap: the populations pair with the double quanta, the dark coherences with the satellites. J₀ = 0 is the coincidence of the two mirrors (the section above).

The offset enters only because it gives every block of order p ≠ 0 the frequency p·ω, while the partner of 0 must be real. The secular dissipator commutes with rotations about z, so the frame rotating at ω removes the offset; in that frame the theorem reads: one centre exactly when J₀ = 0, or ω_Q = 0 and J₁·J₂ = 0. In the laboratory frame a rate at 2s is not enough, because the partners must also match in number: at J₁ = J₂ = 0, J₀ = 1, ω_Q = √6, ω = 6 a satellite eigenvalue is real and sits exactly at −2s = −3, but 0 is a fourfold eigenvalue there and −3 only a twofold one, so the pairing still fails. The static splitting cannot be rotated away. In the rotating frame at J₁ = 0 < J₂ it draws the upper satellite inward below 2s (while 6ω_Q² < 9J₂²; beyond, the pair is complex at the rate 3(J₀ + J₂)) and turns the double-quantum pair complex; at J₁ = J₂ = 0 the satellites are complex as well. In each case the real eigenvalue 0 has no real partner at rate 2s.

The verifier gates the trace, the identity through the Gröbner basis, and the three reflections at a vanishing density. It also checks sixteen exact points, each by two routes: P(−x − 2s) − P(x) zero or non-zero at that point, and F158's end count (in the section after next). The gates can fail: a Gröbner basis that differs from the one above, or a single point that disagrees, fails the run. The points cover both outcomes; among them are six breaking cases with J₀ > 0 and J₁·J₂ = 0 away from ω_Q = ω = 0, the counting point above one of them, and J₀ = 0 with both frequencies on.

## Why spin 3/2: five Dirac matrices

The five Hermitian components of the quadrupole operators are

  Γ₀ = T₂₀,  Γ₁ = T₂,₁ + T₂,₁†,  Γ₂ = i(T₂,₁ − T₂,₁†),  Γ₃ = T₂,₂ + T₂,₂†,  Γ₄ = i(T₂,₂ − T₂,₂†).

At I = 3/2 they anticommute pairwise and square to 3/2, 3, 3, 3 and 3 times the identity: five Dirac matrices. In Souza et al.'s labelling of the levels as two qubits, |3/2⟩, |1/2⟩, |−1/2⟩, |−3/2⟩ = |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩, they are, up to scalars, the Pauli strings ZZ, ZX, ZY, XI and YI. (In the bits a, b of the section above T₂₀ is Z_a; Souza et al.'s two bits are another pair, whose parity is a.) The algebra is known: C. Wu, J.-P. Hu and S.-C. Zhang, *Exact SO(5) symmetry in the spin-3/2 fermionic system*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 91, 186402 (2003), [arXiv:cond-mat/0302165](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0302165), build the SO(5) of spin-3/2 fermions on five Dirac matrices whose bilinears carry spin 2, so their five Γ's span the quadrupole matrices. What follows is what the algebra does to the relaxation.

**The dissipator is Pauli-string dephasing.** Each pair T₂,±ₘ is the pair of its two Hermitian components at half the rate, so the generator's dissipator is Σₐ γₐ·(Γ̂ₐρΓ̂ₐ − ρ), with Γ̂ₐ the Γₐ scaled to square 1 and γ = 3J₀/2, 3J₁/2, 3J₁/2, 3J₂/2, 3J₂/2. At ω_Q = ω = 0 the sixteen products 1, Γₐ and ΓₐΓ_b (a < b) are its eigenoperators, and each decays at twice the summed rate of the jumps it anticommutes with. That one rule is the whole rate table of the Abstract:

| operators | coherence | rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | population | 0 |
| Γ₀ | population | 6(J₁ + J₂) |
| Γ₁Γ₂ | population | 6J₁ |
| Γ₃Γ₄ | population | 6J₂ |
| Γ₁Γ₃, Γ₁Γ₄, Γ₂Γ₃, Γ₂Γ₄ | central transition and triple quantum | 3(J₁ + J₂) |
| Γ₁, Γ₂ | satellites | 3J₀ + 3J₁ + 6J₂ |
| Γ₀Γ₁, Γ₀Γ₂ | satellites | 3(J₀ + J₁) |
| Γ₃, Γ₄ | double quantum | 3J₀ + 6J₁ + 3J₂ |
| Γ₀Γ₃, Γ₀Γ₄ | double quantum | 3(J₀ + J₂) |

The dark operators are the eight that commute with Γ₀, the bright ones the eight that anticommute with it.

**One map for both mirrors.** With Γ₀ scaled to diag(1, −1, −1, 1), the linear map S(X) = Γ₀·Xᵀ satisfies, exactly and at every ω_Q and ω,

  S·L·S⁻¹ = −L + 2·L_{J₀} − 6(J₁ + J₂),

and so does X ↦ Xᵀ·Γ₀. Here L_{J₀} is the J₀ part of the dissipator, L_{J₀} = −3J₀·P with P the projector on the bright cells; P commutes with L and with S. So S maps each sector to itself, with λ ↦ −λ − 2c₁ on the dark sector and λ ↦ −λ − 2c₂ on the bright one. The two mirrors of this page are one map:
- The transpose reverses the Hamiltonian part, because H is real, and keeps every jump term, because every Γ is symmetric or antisymmetric.
- Multiplying by Γ₀ keeps the Hamiltonian part, because Γ₀ commutes with H.
- It reflects the J₁ and J₂ dissipators, whose jumps anticommute with Γ₀.
- It keeps the J₀ dissipator, whose jump is Γ₀ itself, and that kept part moves the bright centre out by 3J₀.

At J₀ = 0 nothing is kept and S is an F1 palindromizer. It is built the way [F118](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PI_FACTORS_AS_R_TIMES_D.md) builds the qubit palindromizers, Π_Z(ρ) = ρᵀ·X^⊗N and Π_Y(ρ) = X^⊗N·ρᵀ, with Γ₀ in place of X^⊗N.

**A second route, for non-negative densities.** The normalized jumps are Hermitian and square to 1. So for J ≥ 0, a jump of rate zero being simply absent, [F158](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) applies as it stands, with σ = Σγ = s. The spectrum pairs about −s exactly when dim ker L = dim ker(L + 2s). The near kernel holds the operators that commute with H and with every jump present; the far kernel holds those that commute with H and anticommute with every jump present. One fact about H keeps the count short. H is diagonal, so an operator commutes with it exactly when it lives on the cells |m⟩⟨m′| whose energy gap E_m − E_m′ vanishes, where E_m is the diagonal of H.
- **All three densities positive.** The five Γ's generate every 4 × 4 matrix, so the near kernel holds only the scalars. Nothing anticommutes with all five, because their product is a multiple of 1, so the far kernel is empty. The count is one against none: no centre.
- **J₀ = 0.** Γ₀ anticommutes with every jump present, and it commutes with H, both being diagonal, at every ω_Q and ω. It is invertible, and by F158 an invertible element of the far kernel forces the two kernels to have equal dimension, which gives the centre. In the two-qubit labelling Γ₀ is the string ZZ: a Pauli string in the far kernel commuting with H, what `EndCount` calls a colouring after [the palindrome as a colouring](THE_PALINDROME_AS_A_COLOURING.md), whose colourings are the one-letter-per-site case. If J₁ = J₂ = 0 as well, L = −i[H, ·], and its spectrum, the energy gaps, is symmetric about 0 = s.
- **J₁ = 0 < J₀, J₂.** The operators that anticommute with Γ₀, Γ₃ and Γ₄ span Γ₁ and Γ₂, which is the span of T₂,₁ and T₂,₁†. T₂,₁ lives on the two cells |3/2⟩⟨1/2| and |−1/2⟩⟨−3/2|, with gaps √6·ω_Q + ω and −√6·ω_Q + ω, and T₂,₁† lives on their transposes. So the far kernel is two-dimensional at ω_Q = ω = 0 and empty otherwise. The near kernel is spanned by 1 and Γ₁Γ₂ at every ω_Q and ω. A centre exists exactly at ω_Q = ω = 0. The case J₂ = 0 < J₀, J₁ is the same, with Γ₃, Γ₄, T₂,₂ and the gaps ±√6·ω_Q + 2ω.
- **J₁ = J₂ = 0 < J₀.** The near kernel holds the dark operators with zero gap, the far kernel the bright ones.
  - The dark gaps are 0 (four populations), ±ω (central transition) and ±3ω (triple quantum).
  - The bright gaps are ±(√6·ω_Q + ω) and ±(√6·ω_Q − ω) for the satellites, ±(√6·ω_Q + 2ω) and ±(√6·ω_Q − 2ω) for the double quantum.
  - At ω = 0 the counts are 8 against 8 if ω_Q = 0, and 8 against 0 otherwise.
  - At ω ≠ 0 the near kernel has dimension 4. At most one of the four conditions √6·ω_Q = ±ω, ±2ω can hold, so the far kernel has dimension at most 2, and there is no centre. The counting point above, ω = √6·ω_Q, is a case with exactly 2.

This is the theorem again, for J ≥ 0. Negative densities lie outside F158's class, and the Gröbner route alone covers them. The verifier checks the rank equality at all sixteen points beside the polynomial identity: two exact routes, one answer.

**Only at 3/2.** For I ≥ 1, Γ₀² ∝ (3m² − I(I + 1))² is a multiple of 1 only if 3m² − I(I + 1) takes two opposite values. At I = 3/2 the values are ±3. At I = 1 they are 1 and −2, and every I ≥ 2 has at least three values of m². So among the spins with a quadrupole moment, 3/2 is the only one whose quadrupole operators form a Clifford algebra.

## Beyond spin 3/2

**J₀ alone breaks the mirror at every I ≥ 1 other than 3/2 (proved).** The J₀ dissipator charges |m⟩⟨m′| the price J₀·½(t_m − t_m′)², where t_m = (3m² − I(I + 1))/√6. A mirror must send the price 0 to the top price, so the two must occur equally often.
- The cells of price 0 are those with m² = m′². Each class of m² holds two levels, except m = 0 at integer I, so these cells number 4I + 2 at half-integer I and 4I + 1 at integer I.
- The top price joins only the two extreme classes, m² = I² and the smallest m². It falls on 2·2·2 = 8 cells at half-integer I and on 2·2·1 = 4 at integer I.

The counts agree only at I = 3/2 (8 against 8). At I = 1, 2, 5/2, 3 and 7/2 they are 5 against 4, 9 against 4, 12 against 8, 13 against 4 and 16 against 8.

**No global centre without a splitting, for any rates other than all zero (proved for every spin from 1 to 6 in half-integer steps except 3/2).** At each of these spins, at ω_Q = 0 and every offset ω, no real rate triple other than J₀ = J₁ = J₂ = 0 gives the whole spectrum one centre, negative densities included. The route needs no eigenvalue, and it is the repository's moment route (the sweep above):
- A centre would sit at −s, fixed by the trace as at 3/2. Pairing λ with −λ − 2s makes every odd centred moment μ_k = Tr (L + s)^k vanish, the easy half of the power-sum lemma.
- A diagonal similarity makes the generator rational: it rescales I₊ and I₋ to rational entries, commutes with the diagonal H, and keeps the spectrum. At ω_Q = ω = 0, μ₃, μ₅ and μ₇ are then exact homogeneous polynomials in J₀, J₁, J₂, of degrees 3, 5 and 7.
- At every spin listed, μ₃ and μ₅ have finitely many common real zeros: 5 projective points at I = 1, 3 at I = 2 to 7/2, 11 at I = 4 to 6. μ₇ is non-zero at each of them, so at ω_Q = ω = 0 there is no centre.
- The offset cannot create a centre. At ω_Q = 0 the generator is the dissipator (the generator at ω = 0) plus the offset term −iω·[I_z, ·]. The dissipator is self-adjoint, because every jump T₂,ₘ comes with its adjoint, ±T₂,₋ₘ, at the same rate, and it commutes with [I_z, ·], because [I_z, T₂,ₘ] = m·T₂,ₘ. So the block of order p has the real spectrum of the zero-frequency block, shifted by −i·p·ω. For ω ≠ 0 a pairing λ ↦ −λ − 2s must then send block p to block −p, whose spectrum at zero frequency is the same (ρ ↦ ρ† carries one block onto the other and conjugates a real spectrum to itself); so every block would be palindromic about −s by itself, and the zero-frequency spectrum with it.

At I = 3/2 the same route returns μ₃ = 0, μ₅ = 14580·J₀J₁²J₂² and μ₇ = 229635·J₀J₁²J₂²·(J₀² + 2J₁² + 2J₂²), which vanish exactly where the theorem above puts a centre: the route does not say no where the theorem says yes. The test runs one way only: all odd moments vanishing would prove a centre, three vanishing would not. The verifier checks the two jump identities at every spin, recomputes the moments, finds the common zeros through the resultant and the first subresultant, reads the signs and μ₇ by interval arithmetic on exact isolating intervals, and checks the counts given here.

Up to I = 7/2 every common real zero of μ₃ and μ₅ has a negative density, so for physical rates the first two odd moments already decide. At I = 4 to 6, five or six of the common zeros have all three densities positive (of them with J₀ > J₁ > J₂, the order a monotone spectral density gives: one at I = 4 to 5, two at 11/2 and 6), and only μ₇ excludes them. On resonance this is a proof spin by spin, not for every I. At I = 5/2 the dark/bright split itself is gone: the cells with |m| = |m′|, blind to J₀, are not an invariant set, because J₁ and J₂ carry them into the rest.

**Without a splitting and off resonance, no global centre at any spin I ≥ 2 (proved for every I).** At ω_Q = 0 and an offset ω ≠ 0, no real rate triple other than all zero gives the whole spectrum one centre, at every spin I ≥ 2. The proof is one determinant, with I as a symbol:
- By the offset argument above, a centre at ω ≠ 0 makes every block of order p palindromic about −s by itself, after its frequency p·ω is removed. The trace of its zero-frequency block is then −(2I + 1 − p)·s, one −s for each of its 2I + 1 − p cells.
- A trace needs only the diagonal. On the cell |m⟩⟨m′| at ω_Q = ω = 0 the diagonal of −L is (g_m + g_m′)/2 − J₀·t_m·t_m′. Here t_m = (3m² − X)/√6 is the diagonal of T₂₀, X = I(I + 1), and g_m is the diagonal of Σ J·T†T over the five jumps:
  g_m = J₀·(3m² − X)²/6 + J₁·[(2m + 1)²(X − m(m + 1)) + (2m − 1)²(X − m(m − 1))]/4 + J₂·[(X − m(m + 1))(X − (m + 1)(m + 2)) + (X − m(m − 1))(X − (m − 1)(m − 2))]/4.
  The factors X − m(m ± 1) vanish where a level would leave the ladder, so the one polynomial serves every m. The mean rate is s = (J₀ + 2J₁ + 2J₂)·X(4X − 3)/30.
- The three highest orders, 2I, 2I − 1 and 2I − 2, hold 1, 2 and 3 cells. Their three trace conditions are linear in J₀, J₁, J₂ with coefficients polynomial in I, and the determinant of the system is

  I(I − 1)(2I − 3)²(2I − 1)³(2I + 5)(4I + 3)/60.

- For I ≥ 1 it vanishes only at I = 1 and I = 3/2, and it is non-zero at every real I > 3/2. So at every spin I ≥ 2 the rates must all vanish.

The two roots are the two spins the route cannot decide alone. At I = 3/2 the traces leave the plane J₀ = 0, which is the theorem's centre. At I = 1 they leave the ray J = (0, 2, 1), which the spin-1 moments above exclude; it is not one of the spin-1 rays below, which need a splitting. The verifier checks the closed diagonals and the mean against the generator in every cell at every spin from 1 to 6, the three trace conditions against the generator's own block traces at spins 2, 5/2 and 3, and the determinant as a polynomial in I. The offset changes no decay rate, so the statement is about the rates. A centre at ω ≠ 0 is the same as every coherence order being palindromic by itself about the one mean rate s, and that gives a centre at ω = 0 as well. A centre off resonance demands more, so ruling it out is the weaker claim: up to I = 6 it follows from the spin-by-spin proof above, and what the determinant adds is every spin beyond. On resonance the route says nothing. At ω = 0 the pairing may cross the coherence orders, so the traces of single blocks are not constrained; there the next paragraph takes another route, which holds at every offset.

**At every offset and splitting, a centre at any spin I ≥ 2 needs two population rates to coincide (proved for every I).** For every offset ω, every static splitting and every real rate triple, a global centre at a spin I ≥ 2 requires the populations block to have a repeated eigenvalue. That confines the rates to the planes where two population rates coincide, finitely many at every integer spin and at every half-integer spin checked, to 61/2; it does not exclude them.
- The populations block W is the block of order 0, the classical rate matrix of the level populations. [The diffusion closure](../docs/proofs/PROOF_DIFFUSION_RAYLEIGH_CLOSURE.md) reads an effective population block of a dephasing chain the same way. It is the same for every J₀, every splitting and every offset: T₂₀, the splitting and I_z are diagonal and leave every population unchanged, so W carries J₁ and J₂ only. At infinite temperature it is real symmetric for every real J₁ and J₂, so its rates are real.
- The blocks of order p and −p have complex-conjugate spectra, because L(ρ†) = L(ρ)† at every offset and splitting; [the mirror proof](../docs/proofs/MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF.md) draws the same identity for the whole spectrum, and ρ ↦ ρ† carries the block of order p onto that of order −p. A real eigenvalue of a block of order p ≠ 0 therefore occurs in the block of order −p as well, and every real eigenvalue of L has, modulo 2, the multiplicity it has in W.
- A pairing λ ↦ −λ − 2s, the same here as the word fence's λ ↦ −λ̄ − 2s because the spectrum is closed under conjugation, keeps the spectrum with its multiplicities and sends real eigenvalues to real ones. So the eigenvalues of odd multiplicity in W are symmetric about −s. If W has no repeated eigenvalue, W itself is palindromic, and its centred odd power sums ν₃ = Tr (W + w)³ and ν₅ = Tr (W + w)⁵ vanish, with w the mean population rate.
- ν₃ and ν₅ are polynomials in I and t = J₂/J₁. Each entry of W is a polynomial of degree 4 in m and I, and a trace sums over the ladder m = −I … I, so ν_k has degree at most 4k + 1 in I (the mean rate, a ladder sum divided by 2I + 1, is a polynomial too, because a ladder sum vanishes at 2I + 1 = 0); the verifier fixes both exactly by interpolation over the spins and checks them at further spins. At J₁ = 0, ν₃ is non-zero at every spin I ≥ 2, and the resultant of ν₃ and ν₅ in t has finitely many real roots in I, none of them a spin I ≥ 2. So at every spin I ≥ 2, W is palindromic only at J₁ = J₂ = 0, where it vanishes and every rate repeats.

The route reaches the known cases. At I = 3/2 the two power sums vanish identically: W is palindromic at every rate there, its four rates being 0, 6J₁, 6J₂ and 6(J₁ + J₂). At I = 1 they vanish together at J₂/J₁ = 1/8 and 5/4, the populations of the two spin-1 rays below, and at −1.

What is left is genuine, not a gap in the bookkeeping. W has a repeated rate on the line J₁ = 0, where the J₂ jumps, which step m by 2, split the ladder into two classes and the rate 0 repeats; on J₂ = 0 at half-integer I, where the J₁ jump between m = ±1/2 has the matrix element 2m + 1 = 0 and the ladder falls into two mirror halves; and at further ratios J₂/J₁. At I = 2 there are six: five where a rate of the populations even under m ↦ −m crosses an odd one, two of them positive (near 0.0142 and 1.774), and J₂/J₁ = −3/14, where two even rates meet. At I = 7/2 one sits at J₁ = J₂, the populations of extreme narrowing: there the dissipator on the populations is rotation invariant, its rates depend only on the rank k of T^(k)₀, and two pairs of ranks share a rate (the populations decay at 0, 30, 84 twice, 150, 210 twice and 240, times J₁). The further ratios are finitely many because the discriminant of W is not identically zero. At integer I, J₂ = 0 makes W an irreducible tridiagonal chain, whose rates are simple ([the edge-block proof](../docs/proofs/PROOF_EDGE_BLOCK_DEFECTIVE_UNDER_PROFILE.md), Lemma A as read in its §(g)). At half-integer I the verifier finds a rational point with simple rates at every spin from 5/2 to 61/2; beyond 61/2 that is not checked. On these planes, with J₀, the splitting and the offset free, the parity says nothing. Without a splitting the question is decided there at every offset ω ≠ 0 by the determinant above, and on resonance up to I = 6 by the spin-by-spin proofs; with a splitting, on resonance up to I = 6. Beyond I = 6 on resonance, and with a splitting off resonance at every spin I ≥ 2, it is undecided.

**A static splitting can make a centre, at spin 1 only.** Proved on resonance (ω = 0) for every spin from 1 to 6 in half-integer steps except 3/2. The splitting ω_Q·T₂₀ cannot be rotated away; it makes the generator non-normal, and its spectrum can be complex. The moment route still runs:
- The splitting is diagonal and commutes with the rational similarity. The odd moments become exact polynomials in J₀, J₁, J₂ and ω_Q, homogeneous of degree k, with only even powers of ω_Q, because every trace is real.
- μ₃ is linear in u = ω_Q². Solving it for u and eliminating, the common zeros of μ₃, μ₅, μ₇ and μ₉ are found exactly: resultants in J₁, their common factors in J₀, a Gröbner basis for each non-linear factor, the case where μ₃'s coefficient of u vanishes, the line J₂ = 0 and its corner (1, 0, 0).
- At I = 1 the generator has nine eigenvalues. By Newton's identities four vanishing odd moments are then the whole condition, so here the route decides both ways. At I ≥ 2 they are necessary only, which is enough for a no.

At I = 2 to 6 the four moments have no common real zero other than all rates zero, where L = −i[H, ·] is trivially symmetric: a splitting gives no centre there either. At I = 1, besides that point, they vanish together on exactly six rays (J₀, J₁, J₂, ω_Q²), rays under J ↦ tJ, ω_Q ↦ tω_Q: on (4, 0, 1, −1/6), where ω_Q would be imaginary, and on five real ones, at each of which the characteristic polynomial satisfies (−1)ⁿP(−x − 2s) = P(x), n = 9, exactly. Three of the five need a negative density. With non-negative rates exactly two remain, and both have J₀ = 0:
- J₁ = 4J₂/5 at ω_Q² = J₂²/10: the spectrum is {0, −1², −(6/5)³, −(7/5)², −12/5}·J₂, palindromic about −6/5·J₂.
- J₁ = 8J₂ at ω_Q² = 21J₂²/2: {0, −5², −6³, −7², −12}·J₂, palindromic about −6·J₂.

*How it happens*, the two rays found again by hand. At spin 1, in rates r = −λ:
- the populations are 0, 3J₁/2 and (J₁ + 4J₂)/2 (the last is the deuterium longitudinal rate);
- the double quantum is (J₁ + 2J₂)/2, twice;
- the single-quantum pair is the two roots of a quadratic, each twice (orders ±1), whose sum is (3J₀ + 3J₁ + 2J₂)/2 and whose product carries 3ω_Q²/2, the only place the splitting enters;
- and 2s = 4(J₀/2 + J₁ + J₂)/3.

Put one single-quantum rate on the centre s and let the other mirror the double quantum: the sum then forces J₀ = 0. The populations must pair among themselves, which fixes J₁ = 4J₂/5 (3J₁/2 on the centre) or J₁ = 8J₂ ((J₁ + 4J₂)/2 on the centre), and the product fixes ω_Q². The splitting is tuned until a single-quantum mode, which it pushes, lands on the mirror image of the double quantum, which it cannot move. At both rays every eigenvalue is simple in its block and the generator is semisimple: no exceptional point. The pairing crosses the coherence orders, −1 from the single quantum with −7/5 from the double quantum at the first ray. This route reads one pairing pattern; that no other pattern adds a ray is the elimination's part.

**The offset, and which frame.** ω is the offset from the frame rotating at the Larmor frequency, and the secular dissipator commutes with rotations about z, so ω = 0 is that frame, on resonance. At both rays every block spectrum is real, so an offset only adds the frequencies −p·ω: the nine decay rates stay palindromic about s in every frame. The complex map λ ↦ −λ − 2s, which also flips the frequencies, holds on resonance only: for ω ≠ 0 the orders stay apart, and the single-quantum lines alone, {1, 6/5}·J₂ or {6, 7}·J₂, are not symmetric about s. Off the rays and away from all rates zero, at spin 1 and at I ≥ 2, the coefficients of (−1)ⁿP(−x − 2s) − P(x) are polynomials in ω, and one of them is non-zero at ω = 0, so at each rate triple and splitting at most finitely many offsets could give a centre. At I ≥ 2 the parity route above confines these to the planes where two population rates coincide; which offsets, if any, give a centre there, and at spin 1 at all, is not decided here.

**What the rays are.** With a static splitting the medium is ordered, and J₀, J₁, J₂ are three densities, those of the field gradient's m = 0, ±1 and ±2 components at the frequencies 0, ω₀ and 2ω₀; they need not follow one curve J(ω). A rotation about the field axis leaves the m = 0 component unchanged, so motion that only turns the gradient about the field has J₀ = 0: the rays need a component that does not fluctuate, not an exotic density. Each ray is still fine-tuned, three conditions on four parameters, and any further slow motion gives J₀ > 0 and leaves it. Both sit just below the exceptional point of the single-quantum pair, 6ω_Q² = J₁² (6ω_Q²/J₁² = 15/16 and 63/64): the splitting draws the pair's two rates together, from {7/10, 3/2}·J₂ to {1, 6/5}·J₂ at the first ray, and stops short of merging them. Below that point the two modes share one frequency, and still the I₊ spectrum has two maxima at both rays, the dip between them over a fifth of the maximum at the second ray and under one percent at the first: I₊ reads the pair with a coupling κ negative enough, c + 3κ < 0 with c the pair's centre, that its line can show two maxima already below the exceptional point, and the splittings of both rays lie in that window (the rule is in the section on the bright pairs' exceptional point below). At ω_Q = 0 the dissipator alone pairs 3 of the 9 eigenvalues about −s at both rays; with the splitting all 9 pair.

**What it says beyond the nucleus.** At spin 3/2 the splitting only removes centres. Inside [F158](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md)'s class that is forced where the far end is empty, as it is at 3/2 with all three densities positive: both ends must also commute with H (Lemma 1), so a Hamiltonian can shrink the far end but not open it, while the near end keeps 1. The spin-1 jumps are not Hermitian operators squaring to 1, so they lie outside that class, and there a Hamiltonian can supply a centre the dissipator lacks. Inside the class no palindrome exists at all at odd total dimension with a jump present (F158 §(f5)); spin 1 has d = 3. [F121](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md) notes for its d = 3 example, two qutrits under equidistant dephasing with the SU(3) Heisenberg coupling, that the Hamiltonian helps at no centre, and marks those sampled counts as a property of that H, not a theorem; one spin 1 under quadrupolar relaxation is a d = 3 case where a Hamiltonian does help, at tuned points.

## What a sodium measurement can read

Under this generator the triple-quantum rate, equal to the slow transverse rate, is the mean of the two biexponential longitudinal rates: c₁ = (6J₁ + 6J₂)/2, at every static splitting, since none of the three rates depends on ω_Q. In Souza et al.'s units (rates C·J) the longitudinal rates are 2CJ₁ and 2CJ₂ (the third population rate, 2C(J₁ + J₂), belongs to the quadrupolar-order mode Γ₀ of the section on Dirac matrices above, which I_z recovery does not enter), and the slow transverse and triple-quantum rate is C(J₁ + J₂). It is a property of the model, not a new measurement: a relaxation study that reports these rates of one sample can check it, and a deviation beyond the measurement error and the fit bias says that something outside the model is present (more than one sodium environment in the voxel, motion that the Redfield form does not capture; a dynamic frequency shift moves no rate and leaves the identity, see its section below).

## The bright pairs' exceptional point

In an ordered medium the static splitting moves the two satellites apart in frequency, while the J₂ jumps keep carrying one into the other, |3/2⟩⟨1/2| into |−1/2⟩⟨−3/2| and back; the double quanta do the same through the J₁ jumps. With q = ω_Q·√6/2, the coefficient of ZZ in H (so 4q² = 6ω_Q², and the Abstract's 6ω_Q² = 9J₂² is 2|q| = 3J₂; below, q > 0), each bright pair is a closed 2 × 2 block of the generator; the central transition, in the satellites' order, is not coupled to them. In the cells |3/2⟩⟨1/2|, |−1/2⟩⟨−3/2| and |3/2⟩⟨−1/2|, |1/2⟩⟨−3/2|:

  satellites: [[−c₂ − i(ω + 2q), 3J₂], [3J₂, −c₂ − i(ω − 2q)]], with the eigenvalues −c₂ − iω ± √(9J₂² − 4q²);
  double quantum: [[−c₂ − i(2ω + 2q), −3J₁], [−3J₁, −c₂ − i(2ω − 2q)]], with −c₂ − 2iω ± √(9J₁² − 4q²).

So the two rates of a pair sum to 2c₂ at every splitting: below the exceptional point they are c₂ ∓ √(9J₂² − 4q²) (with J₁ for the double quanta), past it both are c₂.

**A mode and its mirror image.** In the strings the same block reads the other way round. The satellites' sum |3/2⟩⟨1/2| + |−1/2⟩⟨−3/2|, the strings IX and IY and the part of I_+ outside the central transition, carries the rate c₂ − 3J₂ = 3(J₀ + J₁); its partner, the difference, ∝ T₂,₁ and in the strings ZX and ZY, carries c₂ + 3J₂. At zero splitting these are the two modes, and the partner is the satellites' mirror image −S(satellites)† (row 9 of the handshake below). The splitting is q·ZZ with ZZ = Γ̂₀, which anticommutes with every bright string X, so −i[q·ZZ, X] = −2iq·ZZ·X, and ZZ·X = ±S(X): the splitting carries each bright string into its own mirror image, IX into ZY, IY into ZX, XI into YZ, YI into XZ. In the normalized basis of the sum and the difference the satellite block is −c₂ − iω plus [[3J₂, −2iq], [−2iq, −3J₂]]: two modes at the eigenvalues −c ± δ, joined by a Hamiltonian amplitude g, have −c ± √(δ² − g²), here with δ = 3J₂ (3J₁ for the double quanta) and g = 2q. On the whole bright sector this is one identity the repository already holds: the splitting's part anticommutes there with the dissipator centred at c₂, so on resonance (L + c₂)² = L_H² + (D + c₂)², with L_H² = −(2q)²·1, the Pythagorean form of [F48](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md#f48-pythagorean-decomposition-tier-2-exact-at-n2) with its cross term zero, centred at the sector's own centre (at the dark centre c₁ the cross term is −6J₀·L_H). Why it vanishes is the algebra of F49e's moving letter (Step 2′ of [PROOF_CROSS_TERM_FORMULA](../docs/proofs/PROOF_CROSS_TERM_FORMULA.md)), here with a two-site string as the letter that moves, outside the scopes those state (F48 at N = 2, F49e for per-site letters); and as there, the vanishing is an orthogonality, not a commuting of the two parts, which do not commute.

**Where the eigenvalues merge: a defective exceptional point.** At 2q = 3J₂, with J₂ ≠ 0, the block minus its double root −c₂ − iω is N = 3J₂·[[−i, 1], [1, i]] in the cells, non-zero with N² = 0: one 2 × 2 Jordan block, not two independent modes that touch. Three routes meet on it.
- The block's own discriminant, 4(9J₂² − 4q²), analytic in q, has a simple zero there at one isolated pair of algebraic multiplicity two, and under those conditions a simple zero forces a Jordan block (the one-sided order test of [The Double Root](../docs/THE_DOUBLE_ROOT.md)).
- In the whole 16 × 16 generator, exact over ℚ(i) at (J₀, J₁, J₂, q, ω) = (1, 4, 2, 3, 1), the eigenvalue −c₂ − iω has geometric multiplicity 1 and algebraic multiplicity 2 (the ranks of (L − λ)^k for k = 1, 2, 3 are 15, 14, 14), and one step off in q it is no eigenvalue.

The double-quantum pair does the same at 2q = 3J₁, with J₁ ≠ 0. The control is the same object at J₂ = 0 and q = 0: there the satellite block is the scalar −c₂ − iω, and its doubled eigenvalue is semisimple. The merged mode is (1, i) on the two cells. It is self-orthogonal, vᵀv = 0, the signature of an exceptional point of a complex-symmetric block (the block's two off-diagonal entries are equal), and with two components self-orthogonality gives equal weight on the satellites' sum and on their mirror image. That puts it on the bright centre: Re λ is minus the dissipator's mean rate over the mode (the Rayleigh reading of [the Absorption Theorem](../docs/proofs/PROOF_ABSORPTION_THEOREM.md)), here the mean of c₂ − 3J₂ and c₂ + 3J₂, which is how `LEffMirrorAxisClaim` reads the F86 toy: the two channels meet on their own mirror axis, the hybridisation being the mirroring. **Eigenvalues are not lines.** The exceptional point is where the eigenvalues merge, not where the spectrum's lines do. In the cell basis the satellite block has the form of two sites exchanging at the rate 3J₂ on top of a common rate 3(J₀ + J₁), the exchange here being a transfer of coherence between the two outer transitions by the J₂ jumps (no chemical exchange is in the model): the Bloch-McConnell form of two-site exchange in dynamic NMR (from memory, not looked up), where the two events are told apart. At the point G = 1/z + 3J₂/z², whose real part c₂/(c₂² + ν²) + 3J₂(c₂² − ν²)/(c₂² + ν²)² is a Lorentzian plus the real part of a squared complex one, the latter alone negative beyond |ν| = c₂: the double pole of a size-2 Jordan block, the form [the 2026 literature review](../docs/LITERATURE_REVIEW_2026Q2.md) weighs as Molina's super-Lorentzian on a collective spin, defective there only in the large-size limit (in NMR that word names another line, the one of semisolids; from memory). ## The dynamic frequency shift

The conventions leave out the imaginary parts of the spectral densities. Kept, they add to the Hamiltonian the Lamb-shift term of the generator secular in the Larmor frequency, Σ_m K_m·T₂ₘ†T₂ₘ. Here J_m and K_m are read off one transform Γ_m of the gradient's correlation function at the frequency of the transition T₂ₘ drives, J_m = 2·Re Γ_m (the rate in the dissipator) and K_m = Im Γ_m: with the rate 2·Re Γ the Redfield equation gives the shift the coefficient Im Γ, the standard split of Γ into half a rate and a shift (from memory). The complex density whose real part is J is then 2Γ = J + 2iK, so K is half its imaginary part, and for one exponential correlation, Γ = τ/(1 − iωτ), K/J = ω·τ/2 (a convention that calls the whole imaginary part K reads ω·τ); only the sign, the sense of precession, is a convention here, and flipping it flips the signs in the rules ω = 6K₁ and ω = 3K₂ below. At infinite temperature the correlation function is real and even, the assumption that already makes J₋ₘ = J_m, so K is odd, K₋ₘ = −K_m (K₀ = 0, and at spin 3/2 a K₀ term would be a constant anyway, T₂₀² being 3/2 times the identity). K is read at mω₀ as J is, so it too must be flat across the ±2q the splitting moves the transitions by. Each T₂ₘ†T₂ₘ is a polynomial in I_z, so the term is real and diagonal:

  H_LS = K₁(T₂,₁†T₂,₁ − T₂,₋₁†T₂,₋₁) + K₂(T₂,₂†T₂,₂ − T₂,₋₂†T₂,₋₂) = −3K₂·ZI − 3K₁·IZ = −3K₂·I_z − (3/2)(2K₁ − K₂)·IZ.

The first part turns the frame, an offset of −3K₂. What no frame removes is one number, 2K₁ − K₂: the class of the term modulo I_z, which the frame written here represents as a multiple of IZ and the satellites' frame as a multiple of ZI, and either way it fixes the central transition's offset from the satellites below, 6(2K₁ − K₂). Any real diagonal term odd in m has the same two strings, the second-order static quadrupolar shift among them (from memory), so what follows holds with its coefficients added to −3K₂ and −3K₁.

**Both mirrors survive.** The section on the one map needs only that H be real and commute with Γ₀, and H_LS is both, as the second-order quadrupolar shifts are under the lemma of [The Sodium Chain](THE_SODIUM_CHAIN.md). So S·L·S⁻¹ = −L + 2·L_{J₀} − 6(J₁ + J₂) holds with the shift at every ω_Q, ω, K₁ and K₂, and the dark and bright modes keep their centres −c₁ and −c₂: the shift moves frequencies, not rates.

**The exceptional points survive.** Each bright pair moves as a whole, both cells by 6K₁ in frequency (satellites) or 6K₂ (double quanta), so its block gains a multiple of the identity: the discriminants, the points 6ω_Q² = 9J₂² and 9J₁², their Jordan blocks and the relation R₁(4/5) = 2·3J₂ are untouched. The central transition moves by 6(K₂ − K₁), so the satellites sit 6(2K₁ − K₂) off it, which is the part along IZ. The I₊ spectrum is then symmetric about the central line only where 2K₁ = K₂, and the statements of the section above that put the central line at the satellites' centre (the signal's frequencies about the central transition, the whole spectrum's maxima) hold only there; for one exponential correlation that is a limit rather than a tuning, since 2K₁ − K₂ = 6ω₀³τ⁴/((1 + ω₀²τ²)(1 + 4ω₀²τ²)), positive at every τ, ~6ω₀³τ⁴ near extreme narrowing (small as (ω₀τ)³ against the rates) and in slow motion larger than the satellites' coupling 3J₂.

**The global centre moves into a pair's own frame.** With the shift the whole spectrum has one centre exactly when J₀ = 0, or ω_Q = 0 and J₁ = 0 with ω = 6K₁, or ω_Q = 0 and J₂ = 0 with ω = 3K₂: the coefficients of P(−x − 2s) − P(x) generate the same ideal as {J₀·ω_Q², J₀·(9J₁² + (ω − 6K₁)²)·(9J₂² + (2ω − 6K₂)²)}, which at K₁ = K₂ = 0 is the theorem's. The ideal holds for every real J, negative ones included, and gives the "exactly". For non-negative densities F158's colouring supplies the centre on each branch a second way: J₀ = 0 keeps its centre at every shift, Γ₀ = ZZ commuting with every diagonal H and staying the colouring; at J₁ = 0 the strings ZX and ZY anticommute with every jump left (ZZ, XI, YI), and in H they anticommute with ZZ and IZ and commute with ZI, so they commute with H exactly when q = 0 and the coefficient of IZ, ω/2 − 3K₁, vanishes; at J₂ = 0 the strings XI and YI, which anticommute with every jump left (ZZ, ZX, ZY) and in H with ZZ and ZI, commute with H exactly when q = 0 and ω = 3K₂. Where J₁ = 0 the centre lies in the frame in which the satellites, shifted by 6K₁, are on resonance; where J₂ = 0, in the frame of the double quanta, shifted by 6K₂ at twice the offset. On resonance in the Larmor frame, ω = 0, those two branches need the shift of their pair to vanish. The ideal treats J and K as independent; a centre at J₁ = 0 with K₁ ≠ 0 needs a density that vanishes at ω₀ while the imaginary part of its transform there does not, and at J₂ = 0 with K₂ ≠ 0 the same at 2ω₀.

That the dynamic frequency shift is a commutator term that moves no rate, and moves the central line against the satellites in slow motion, is textbook NMR (recalled, not looked up), the offset 2K₁ − K₂ included; what this section adds is that it leaves both mirrors and the exceptional points exact, and where it puts the global centre. The verifier gates H_LS in strings, the mirror identity with it, the pairs' moves, the ideal, F158's colouring strings for the two shifted branches (anticommuting with the jumps left, commuting with H) and the one-exponential formula for 2K₁ − K₂; the witness reads the shift's closed form from the spin matrices, the mirror identity and the moves at one point, the satellites' Jordan block at the shifted eigenvalue, and the shifted rule on both routes, dim ker L against dim ker(L + 2s) and the characteristic polynomial, at eleven points.

## A spin-lock

A radio-frequency field along x, in the frame rotating with it, adds ω₁·I_x to H (ω₁ the field's nutation frequency), and ω becomes the offset from the field's frequency. That I_x splits into single-transition operators, the central transition among them as a fictitious spin ½, is textbook NMR (recalled, not looked up). In the strings, as [The Sodium Chain](THE_SODIUM_CHAIN.md) writes the spin operators, the split reads I_x = (√3/2)·IX + ½(XX + YY): the √3 part drives the satellites (3/2 ↔ 1/2 and −1/2 ↔ −3/2) and anticommutes with Γ₀ = ZZ, the rest is the central transition, real and commuting with Γ₀.

**The central transition's part keeps both mirrors.** ω₁·½(XX + YY) is real and commutes with Γ₀, so the section on the one map applies to it as it does to the shift: S·L·S⁻¹ = −L + 2·L_{J₀} − 6(J₁ + J₂) holds at every ω_Q, ω, K₁, K₂ and ω₁, and the dark and bright modes keep their centres −c₁ and −c₂. No field drives the central transition alone, though: ω₁·½(XX + YY) is the first-order truncation of the whole field when ω₁ ≪ |2q ± ω|, the satellite part detuned by the splitting. The whole field there breaks the mirrors at second order in ω₁/q: the largest distance of an eigenvalue λ from the nearest eigenvalue to −λ̄ − 2c₁ or −λ̄ − 2c₂, taken over the whole spectrum since the lock mixes the orders, is about 16·(ω₁/q)² in the units of J at J = (1, 2, 3), for ω small against q (read numerically at q = 30, 100 and 1000, not gated). A central-transition-selective lock keeps the mirrors through first order, and the break enters at second.

**At generic rates the whole field breaks them.** The satellite part mixes the bright and the dark cells: at J = (1, 2, 3), ω = 1, ω₁ = 1 and q = 1 or q = 10, the characteristic polynomial with the whole I_x is x times one irreducible factor of degree 15 over ℚ(√3, i), and the mirror image of neither factor about −c₁ or −c₂ is a factor. An irreducible factor shares a root with its mirror image only if the two are the same polynomial, and the partner −2c of the eigenvalue 0 is rational, so no eigenvalue has a partner at either centre. The truncation at the same points leaves every factor paired. This is read at those points, not shown at every ω₁ and q; with no splitting at unequal rates the whole field breaks them as well (read numerically at J = (1, 2, 3) and (3, 2, 1), q = 0, ω = ω₁ = 1).

**In the extreme-narrowing limit with no splitting any lock keeps them, turned with the field.** With J₀ = J₁ = J₂ the dissipator commutes with every rotation, and more than that it is one number on each rank of spherical tensors, the operator space of a spin 3/2 holding the ranks 0 to 3 once each: L_D(L_D + 6J)(L_D + 12J) = 0, rank 0 at 0, ranks 1 and 3 at −6J, rank 2 at −12J. An offset or a lock only turns inside the ranks, so it adds frequencies and moves no rate: Re λ keeps those three values under any lock, while the splitting T₂₀, which is no rotation, mixes the ranks and spreads the lines. With q = 0 a real rotation about y carries ω·I_z + ω₁·I_x to a multiple of I_z; the map S turned by the same rotation is again a mirror. Every factor of the characteristic polynomial pairs at (ω, ω₁) = (0, 1) and (7/10, 5); with q = 1 (read at ω = ω₁ = 1) it breaks. This is the plainest sample there is, sodium ions in water, read at two settings and with the shift dropped. Real extreme narrowing makes the densities equal only up to terms of order (ω₀τ)², and the departure is first order in the difference: about 2.2·ε in the same distance when J₂ alone moves to J(1 + ε), at ω = 7/10, ω₁ = 5, with other coefficients for the other densities (read numerically, not gated), negligible for sodium in water and not zero.

**The global centre's element turns with the field.** Under a lock, ZZ, which is lit on the branch J₀ = 0, no longer commutes with H; the satellite part anticommutes with it. What takes its place is E = ½·ZZ − (√3/2)·XI = −(3I_x² − 15/4)/3, up to sign the quadrupole along the lock's axis, as Γ₀ = ZZ = (3I_z² − 15/4)/3 is the quadrupole along z; the √3 is a rotation coefficient of a rank-2 tensor, not a property of the field. E² = 1, E anticommutes with the J₁ jumps ZX and ZY and with neither ZZ nor XI, and [H, E] = √3·i(3K₂ − ω)·YI − √3·i·q·YZ, two independent strings, so E commutes with H exactly when q = 0 and ω = 3K₂, whatever K₁ and ω₁. So where J₀ = J₂ = 0 there is one global centre at q = 0, ω = 3K₂ ([F158](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md); E is an anticommuting sum of lit strings that commutes with H and squares to 1, the kind of element EndCount's `CheckElement` certifies at integer coefficients, as the witness's mixtures have). It is the meeting of two branches the page already holds: at J₀ = J₂ = 0, q = 0, ω = 3K₂ the strings ZZ (the J₀ = 0 branch) and XI, YI (the shift's J₂ = 0 branch) all lie in the far kernel, and on that sphere the lock picks the direction that commutes with I_x, [the sum turned on a sphere the dissipator does not see](THE_PALINDROME_AS_A_COLOURING.md). ## The irreducible tensors in strings

NMR writes the sixteen operators of a spin 3/2 in the irreducible tensors T_l,p, rank l = 0 to 3 and order p = −l … l; this page writes them in strings. Built by the lowering ladder from I₊^l (so T_l,l ∝ +I₊^l, not the Condon-Shortley sign (−1)^l) and normalized to Tr(T†T) = 1, each tensor is proportional to one string combination, up to a factor and a sign (the ± and the sign of p go together). The dictionary is likely in the literature of NMR quantum computing on spin 3/2, possibly where Souza et al.'s labelling comes from (a candidate source for the labelling, from memory and not looked up: Sinha, Mahesh, Ramanathan and Kumar, J. Chem. Phys. 114, 4415 (2001)). At rank 2 the ladder's phases and Condon-Shortley's agree, so the T₂,ₘ of the Conventions are these up to their common norm √6:

| rank | order | strings |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | II |
| 1 | 0 | 2·ZI + IZ |
| 1 | ±1 | √3·(IX ± i·IY) + (XX + YY) ± i·(YX − XY) |
| 2 | 0 | ZZ |
| 2 | ±1 | ZX ± i·ZY |
| 2 | ±2 | XI ± i·YI |
| 3 | 0 | 2·IZ − ZI |
| 3 | ±1 | 2·(IX ± i·IY) − √3·[(XX + YY) ± i·(YX − XY)] |
| 3 | ±2 | XZ ± i·YZ |
| 3 | ±3 | (XX − YY) ± i·(XY + YX) |

Each rank occurs once at each order, so a combination that carries rank l and order p fixes T_l,p up to a factor; the witness checks the table that way, the verifier against the tensors the ladder builds. Rank 0 is the identity, rank 2 the five Dirac strings, and ranks 1 and 3 together are the ten products of two Dirac strings, each i times a string; those strings ±i·ΓₐΓ_b generate SO(5), read by Wu, Hu and Zhang (cited above) as the spin and its octupole (from memory). So the Clifford grade sorts the ranks: grade 0 is rank 0, grade 1 is rank 2, grade 2 holds ranks 1 and 3. Neither rank 1 nor rank 3 alone is the span of any set of strings: no string lies in rank 1, and only XZ and YZ in rank 3.

**Two bases of one space.** The tensors diagonalize the rotations' Casimir (the rank) and I_z (the order, on which the offset acts); the strings diagonalize the watching: at ω_Q = 0 each string decays at twice the summed rate of the jumps it anticommutes with, and the offset gives the order-p part of a string the frequency p·ω and nothing else. At ω_Q = 0 and at any offset, ten of the sixteen tensors are eigenoperators of the generator as well: rank 0, all of rank 2, and rank 3 at |p| = 2 and 3. There each tensor lies in one string class, a recombination inside it (XI + i·YI for XI and YI, which decay alike), and in that sense the two bases agree. The other six are turned between two string classes, by angles the spin fixes and the densities do not:
- T₁,₀ ∝ I_z holds 4/5 of its squared norm on ZI (rate 6J₂) and 1/5 on IZ (rate 6J₁); T₃,₀ the reverse.
- T₁,±₁ ∝ I_± holds 3/5 on the satellites (rate 3(J₀ + J₁)) and 2/5 on the central transition (rate c₁); T₃,±₁ the reverse.

Hubbard's weights (rows 1 and 2 of the handshake below) are these angles: a biexponential decay is a rank-1 tensor seen from the basis the watching diagonalizes. Where the two string classes of a pair share one rate the angle has no effect; at J₀ = J₁ = J₂ that holds for every pair, and the dissipator is the rank-by-rank one of the spin-lock section. A splitting turns the bases further apart: it couples T₂,±₂ to T₃,±₂ (the double-quantum pair of [the bright pairs' exceptional point](#the-bright-pairs-exceptional-point)) and T₂,±₁ to the satellite parts of T₁,±₁ and T₃,±₁ (row 9); with a splitting only T₀,₀, T₂,₀ and T₃,±₃ stay eigenoperators. The dynamic frequency shift does not turn them: it gives each of the ten one frequency on all its cells, in the four at |p| = 1 it moves the satellite part and the central transition by different frequencies (the section on the shift), and the two at order 0 it leaves still; the six are no eigenoperators with it or without it.

**The triple-quantum path.** Started from T₁,₁ at ω_Q = ω = 0, the rank-3 part grows as

  ⟨T₃,₁, e^{Lt}·T₁,₁⟩ = (√6/5)·(e^{−3(J₀ + J₁)t} − e^{−c₁t})

in the ladder's phases: zero at t = 0, and identically zero exactly when J₀ = J₂, where the satellites' rate meets the dark centre. The formula, its √6/5 included, is the textbook one by which relaxation turns rank 1 into rank 3 in an isotropic medium, read by a triple-quantum filtered signal (from memory: Jaccard, Wimperis and Bodenhausen, J. Chem. Phys. 85, 6282 (1986), cited above, and Hubbard's relaxation theory behind it). In strings it is a bright string class beating against the dark centre, and it is row 8's condition J₀ ≠ J₂ with its time course.

**Coherence order is not a property of one string.** The four strings XX, XY, YX, YY carry both the central transition and the triple quantum: XX + YY lies at p = ±1, XX − YY at p = ±3, and both decay in one string class at c₁. So a filter that keeps the order pair ±p is a projection onto a span of strings only at p = 0 (II, ZI, IZ, ZZ) and at |p| = 2 (XI, YI, XZ, YZ, two classes); at |p| = 1 and 3 it cuts the class {XX, XY, YX, YY}, which the watching does not split and the offset does, with the frequencies ω and 3ω; the order ±3 holds no string at all. The order p is also not the qubit coherence order of [The Shared Field in a Crystal](THE_SHARED_FIELD_IN_A_CRYSTAL.md), the difference of popcounts, which is the coherence order of spins 1/2: here the triple quantum |00⟩⟨11| has popcount difference 2 and the central transition |01⟩⟨10| has 0.

**The one map on the tensors.** In the ladder's phases S(X) = Γ₀·Xᵀ carries T₀,₀ ↔ T₂,₀ (the identity, the infinite-temperature equilibrium, and quadrupolar order, row 7), T₂,±₂ ↦ ±T₃,∓₂ and T₃,±₂ ↦ ±T₂,∓₂ (in Condon-Shortley phases every term joining an even rank to an odd one changes sign), T₃,±₃ ↦ −T₃,∓₃, and acts on (T₁,₀, T₃,₀) as the reflection [[4/5, 3/5], [3/5, −4/5]]; at order ±1 it mixes ranks 1, 2 and 3, S(T₁,₁) = (2/5)·T₁,₋₁ − (√15/5)·T₂,₋₁ − (√6/5)·T₃,₋₁, its rank-2 part, ∝ T₂,₋₁, the mirror image of the satellites as in row 9. At ω_Q = 0 the double quanta of rank 2 and rank 3 are each other's mirror image about −c₂, at the rates 3(J₀ + 2J₁ + J₂) and 3(J₀ + J₂), which sum to 2c₂; they are the sum and the difference of the double-quantum pair's two cells, the T₂,₂ and T₃,₂ of [the bright pairs' exceptional point](#the-bright-pairs-exceptional-point), where a splitting couples them. At the magic angle, β = arccos(1/√3), a rotation carries no rank-3 coherence between the orders ±1 and ±2 in the combination d³₂,₁ − d³₂,₋₁ (a multiple of sin β·(3cos²β − 1), in sympy's convention for d; d³₂,₁ + d³₂,₋₁ does not vanish there), while it does carry rank 2; a double-quantum filter with its pulses at that angle (the DQF-MA of Eliav, Shinar and Navon, from memory, as is which of its pulses does the work and that the vanishing combination is the one a pulse in phase with the excitation applies) passes only the rank-2 pathway, so it reads one of the two mirror partners; relaxation alone carries the rank-1 coherence into rank 3 only, and the rank-2 coherence T₂,±₁ that leads to the rank-2 double quantum comes from a splitting (row 9), under which the two double quanta mix.

## The handshake with NMR

What sodium relaxation is known for, set beside the objects of this page. The left column is the textbook ground of spin-3/2 relaxation, written from the basics and not looked up; what makes a row hold is the right column, an exact computation on this page's generator, live in `SodiumTwoMirrorsWitness` (`inspect --root sodium`, the node *the handshake with NMR*, and for the eigenvalue half of row 10 the node *the bright pairs' exceptional point*, its line half in the verifier; rows 3 and 8 are a reading of the spin matrices and arithmetic). The input is the same on both sides: the Redfield generator, secular in the Larmor frequency, with Souza et al.'s rates. The textbook writes rates as C·J; with this page's normalization C = 3 (the conventions above). What differs is the route. The textbook works in the irreducible tensors; here a string decays at twice the summed rate of the jumps it anticommutes with, where it commutes with H: on resonance, ω = 0, every string at ω_Q = 0 and the populations, the central transition and the triple quantum at every splitting; off resonance the order-p part of a string keeps the rate and gains the frequency p·ω. A weight is the squared norm of a string part. Each row is an equation on one named degree of freedom, generator and observable given, the rates kept as symbols; [Reverse-Spear](../docs/carbon/SPEAR_REVERSED.md), which sets the label Na beside the fraction 1/8, is the comparison of numbers and labels without that mapping, and the two pages do not touch: nothing in these rows involves 1/8.

Rows 1 to 8 are a handshake, not a test of the model: both sides start from the same generator, so the agreement checks the construction here, the strings and the counting, against a solved textbook case as remembered here, not as looked up; the attributions are not checked by it. What the rows add is the reading. Hubbard's weights are the norms of the string parts of I_z and I_x, the √3 that makes the triplet 3 : 4 : 3 is the one the Dirac reading factors out, the central transition's two textbook properties have one cause, and the populations' four rates are two mirror pairs about one centre. Row 9 places one more textbook mode, T₂,±1, on the bright mirror: the mirror image of the satellites about −c₂, which I_+ reaches only through a splitting. Row 10 keeps apart what the word *unresolved* covers: the eigenvalues meet at the exceptional point, and the satellites' lines separate only at a larger splitting; and like the identity of the section on what a sodium measurement can read, it relates two measurements of one sample, here the 4/5 longitudinal rate and the coupling read off the transverse signal in time. As there, it is a property of the model: a sample that breaks it says that the model fails, and nothing about the mirrors, which hold wherever the model does.

## What this does not show

No sodium data were compared. The model is a single isolated spin 3/2 at infinite temperature in the Redfield picture, secular in the Larmor frequency with the splitting kept whole; multiple compartments, exchange, finite polarization and terms non-secular in the Larmor frequency are outside it, and so is the dynamic frequency shift except at spin 3/2, where its section adds it (not to the statements beyond spin 3/2, and not computed for the chain, though the chain's lemma covers a real diagonal H on every nucleus), and the reading at the exceptional point also needs one sharp splitting and a homogeneous field. Nothing here improves a sodium image. The mean-rate identity follows directly from rates published before this page; only its place as a mirror centre is ours.

## Open

- The identity against measured sodium relaxation rates (gels, cartilage, brain), whether it is already in use as a consistency check, and which deviation a two-compartment voxel produces.
- The model at the bright pairs' exceptional point against a measurement, a check of the relaxation model rather than of the mirrors: the coupling 3J₂ read off one sample's transverse signal in time against half its 4/5 longitudinal rate, at one orientation, and an oriented sample rotated through the magic angle for the change of sign of Ω²; and how far the conditions hold there (one sharp splitting, densities that may depend on the angle, a homogeneous field, a coupling that shows in the satellite part only at third order in time, behind the splitting's second).
- Beyond spin 3/2: a proof that no rates other than all zero give a global centre at every spin. Without a splitting it is proved spin by spin to I = 6, and off resonance for every I ≥ 2. At every offset, with or without a splitting, it is open only on the planes where two population rates coincide, finitely many at every integer spin and at every half-integer spin checked, to 61/2: beyond I = 6 on resonance, and at every spin I ≥ 2 with a splitting off resonance. Also open: at spin 1 with a splitting, which offsets ω ≠ 0 could still give a centre off the two rays (finitely many at each point away from all rates zero); and whether a weaker, block-wise mirror survives there.
- An ensemble of spins 3/2 under a shared gradient beyond two nuclei, and the spectrum with a splitting or a coupling that breaks its conserved sign pattern; at two nuclei [The Sodium Chain](THE_SODIUM_CHAIN.md#a-shared-gradient) finds the price an operator (with a splitting too), the ladder's rungs mixed or without a centre, and without a Hamiltonian at J₀ = 0 sixteen modes whose rate the correlation does not move.
- The general d > 2 question of Qubit Necessity §8a: which other physical dissipators split a palindrome into centres instead of breaking it.

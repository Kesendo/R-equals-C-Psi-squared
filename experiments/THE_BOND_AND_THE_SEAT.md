# A bond changes the wave; a seat decides what it can see

**Status:** local coherent producer, 2026-09-23. No new F number or hardware confirmation.
**Run:** `python simulations/handshake_bond_seat_readout.py`; the retained output is
[the producer run](../simulations/results/handshake_bond_seat_readout_run.txt).
**Controls:** `python -m pytest -q simulations/test_handshake_bond_seat_readout.py`.

## System and question

Take `N` sites with exactly one excitation: `|j⟩` means that it is at site
`j=0,…,N−1`, and `A_path` connects neighboring sites. Prepare the
highest-energy standing wave `ψ₁` of this uniform open XY chain. The hopping
Hamiltonian is `H₀ = J A_path`, with `J > 0`;
in the full spin notation this is `(J/2) Σ_b (X_b X_{b+1} + Y_b Y_{b+1})`.
Change one bond `b=0,…,N−2` by an *additive hopping* `δJ_b V_b`, where
`V_b = |b⟩⟨b+1| + |b+1⟩⟨b|`. At a chosen site `j`, read the population
`p_j(t) = |⟨j|exp(-i(H₀+δJ_b V_b)t)|ψ₁⟩|²`. Which bond changes can that
local time trace distinguish at first order in `δJ_b`?

This is an ideal unitary (`γ=0`) population measurement. The word *seat* here
means the site whose population is read. In [F157](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md)
it usually means a site carrying dephasing; its sine-node count is the piece
used here. This calculation does not compute the dissipative PTF `α` readout
profile.

## The connection

The [F124 proof](../docs/proofs/PROOF_HANDSHAKE_TRANSITION_INVARIANT.md)
owns the **full** bond-to-mode matrix
`M[b,k] = ⟨ψ_k|V_b|ψ₁⟩` for mode labels `k=1,…,N`, including the
prepared carrier `k=1`. It has rank `N-1`; its single mode-coordinate kernel
points along the `k=N` column. This mode is the bipartite sign-flipped
K-partner `ψ_N(j)=(-1)^j ψ₁(j)`, and the selection rule makes that column zero.
F124's spectral-floor identity concerns this full matrix. The *location
submatrix* uses only `k=2,…,N`, without the carrier column; its spectral-floor
identity is different.

For sine modes `u_k(j) = √(2/(N+1)) sin(k(j+1)π/(N+1))` and
`E_k = 2J cos(kπ/(N+1))`, direct first-order propagation gives

```text
∂p_j(t)/∂δJ_b |₀ =
  2 u₁(j) Σ_{k=2}^{N-1} u_k(j) M[b,k]
             [1 - cos((E₁-E_k)t)]/(E₁-E_k).
```

The `k=1` term only changes the carrier's phase, and the `k=N` term is zero
by the K-partner rule. F157's XY node condition
`k(j+1) ≡ 0 (mod N+1)` deletes another `gcd(j+1,N+1)-1` modes. Because the
remaining columns of `M` are independent and the nonzero energy gaps are
distinct, their time functions are independent. Therefore the rank of the
**continuous first-order time trace**, viewed as a map from the `N−1` small
bond changes to population functions of time, is

```text
r_j = N - gcd(j+1,N+1) - 1 = N - 2 - blind_XY(j).
```

Thus an ideal full trace can recover `r_j` independent combinations of small
bond changes; the other combinations are invisible at this order. This is an
exact composition of the existing matrix-kernel and sine-node results. A
single chosen time returns one scalar, and a finite sampled time grid may be
poorly conditioned; neither automatically identifies every individual bond.

## What the producer found

The numerical examples use `J=1` and times in inverse-hopping units. For
`N=7`, the location ranks at sites `j=0..6` are
`[5,4,5,2,5,4,5]`. The centre `j=3` sees only `k=3,5`;
`k=2,4,6` have nodes there. Site `j=2` sees `k=2..6`. At `t=1`,
the centre reads the two mirror end bonds `b=0,5` with the same slope
`-0.022517039`. Site `j=2` reads `-0.067127584` for `b=0` and
`+0.002960596` for `b=5`. These numbers are generated by the
[retained run](../simulations/results/handshake_bond_seat_readout_run.txt),
not an empirical measurement.

The independent route differentiates an `N×N` Hamiltonian propagator with
`expm_frechet`; it does not use `M`, the F157 mask, or the sine dispersion.
The modal formula and this derivative agree on all six `N=7` bonds at two
sites and two times, plus other controls. SVD checks match the integer rank
prediction at all 63 sites over `N=3..11`. A balanced mutation of two
interior-bond matrix entries makes the derivative gate fire.

At very short times the centre's end-bond response starts at
`-√2 J³t⁴/48 + O(t⁶)` (the printed fixture sets `J=1`). Its `t²` modal
pieces cancel: expand `[1−cos(ωt)]/ω = ωt²/2 − ω³t⁴/24 + O(t⁶)` in the
displayed sum. The exact sine-mode sum gives zero for the `t²` coefficient
and `−√2 J³/48` for `t⁴`; the
[symbolic test](../simulations/test_handshake_bond_seat_readout.py) checks both
identities. A plain double-precision
sum gave the wrong sign at `t=10⁻⁸`; the producer detects that cancellation
and rebuilds the modal ingredients with higher precision. The retained run
checks the `t⁴` coefficient against the independent derivative.

At finite `δJ=0.2`, spatial reflection makes the centre populations for
`b=0` and `b=5` equal at `t=1.3` within roundoff, while site `j=2` separates
them by `0.021023`. This is a reflection control, not an extension of the
first-order rank formula to finite defects. A uniform change of *all* bonds
leaves the prepared `ψ₁` population stationary; changing the preparation to
`ψ₂` also breaks the `ψ₁` response prediction.

## Boundary and next experiment

The result identifies a coherent population-readout channel at `γ=0`.
Finite dephasing changes the evolution to the `(1,1)` Liouvillian: the
`N×N` density-matrix sector spanned by `|i⟩⟨j|` with one excitation on each
side. It may
change both response and identifiability. The [handshake arc](../compute/RCPsiSquared.Core/OpenArcs/OpenArcsRegistry.cs)
already separates that dissipative question from Hamiltonian eigenvector
mixing. A next producer would have to carry the same preparation, bond
perturbation, and local output through that block and compare with an
independently propagated derivative, without calling the present rank its
answer in advance.

F138 states the dephasing palindrome's boundary as two clauses: at most two dephasing axes per component, and a field orthogonal to all of them, with a proviso that a single-letter bond such as ZZ tolerates all three axes. It measures them sufficient and knows their converse is false. F158 decides the palindrome exactly through its far kernel

  𝒲 = {W : [H, W] = 0 and A W A = −W for every jump A},

which holds an invertible element exactly when the palindrome holds. This page looks for the simplest such element, a single Pauli string, and finds that looking for one is a **colouring of the graph**: every site takes one Pauli letter, its colour, from the letters its jumps light (those that anticommute with every dephasing letter of the site), and every bond and every field of H has to accept the colours it touches. F138's two clauses are the colourings with one colour per component, and its proviso is the freedom a one-letter bond leaves. The colouring explains most of F138's exceptions at a one-letter bond and none at a two-letter bond, and it shows where the proviso overreaches: on a site carrying two axes. The palindromes it misses can still be read through a lit string: with commuting jumps, the palindrome holds exactly when some unitary commuting with every jump turns H into a Hamiltonian that commutes with a lit string. That is an existence statement, F158 in this page's language; which unitary a given H needs is open.

The word is used once already. `PalindromeSoftCertifier`'s `LinearSiteColoring` 2-colours the site graph: a per-site string K, Z on one sublattice, with K·H·K = −H, a string that **anticommutes** with H; F103 §7.12 treats that site-graph test as a proxy for a diagonal operator on the basis-state graph. The colouring here chooses a string that **commutes** with H and anticommutes with the jumps. The two are siblings, one for the Hamiltonian's sign flip and one for the dissipator's.

## The colouring

Take H a sum of distinct Pauli strings with nonzero coefficients (bonds P⊗P with P ∈ {X, Y, Z}, and one-site fields) and one Pauli letter per dephasing jump. Look for a single Pauli string F in 𝒲.

- **Anticommuting with the jumps** is per site: the letter of F at site l must anticommute with every dephasing letter of site l, i.e. be lit by all of them. At a site dephased along one letter two letters qualify, along two letters one, along all three none; at an undephased site any letter does, the identity included. These are the site's **colours**.
- **Commuting with H** is per term. A Pauli string T commutes with F or anticommutes with it, and [T, F] = 2·T·F in the second case. Distinct terms give distinct products T·F, so nothing cancels: [H, F] = 0 exactly when every term commutes with F.
- **A bond P⊗P** commutes with F when both or neither of its sites anticommute with P.
- **A field P at site l** commutes with F when the colour there is I or P.

So an admissible colouring is exactly a Pauli string in 𝒲, and by F158 it carries the palindrome: the one-sided multiplication ρ ↦ F·ρ gives L ~ −L† − 2σ, and F158 §(f8) gives L ~ −L − 2σ. The one-sided factor R of the canonical Π_Z = R·D is the colouring F = X^⊗N.

**What the rule forces, bond by bond.** A letter's three answers to "do you anticommute with X, with Y, with Z" always contain an even number of yeses (I: none; X: Y and Z; Y: X and Z; Z: X and Y), so any two of them name the letter.

- A **Heisenberg** bond, XX + YY + ZZ, and any **two-letter** bond, asks two of those questions of both ends and gets the same answers, so it forces the same colour on its two ends. A connected component then needs one colour, lit on every site: at most two dephasing axes in it, **F138's clause 1**.
- A **field** fixes the colour of its site to its own letter (or I), and the colour must be lit: with one colour per component the field lies along it, orthogonal to every dephasing axis, **F138's clause 2**.
- A **one-letter bond** P⊗P asks only one question, so it only puts both ends in the same class, {I, P} or the other two letters. The second class is open at every site dephased along a single axis, whichever the axis, so the colours can change from site to site and three axes coexist. That is **F138's two-term proviso**, derived: the colour freedom of a one-letter bond.

F138's clauses 1 and 2 are the colourings with one colour per component; the proviso is where a one-letter bond lets the colour change.

## Where the proviso overreaches

Read as written, the proviso lets a component whose bonds carry one letter hold three axes in any arrangement. Three axes on one site were already excluded: F138 calls the depolarizing channel clause 1 failing, and MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF says the stacked site breaks the Ising bond too. A site with **two** axes was excluded nowhere, and there the proviso is wrong. Such a site has a single colour, the third letter. When that letter is the bond's own, a one-letter bond asks it (or I) of each neighbour, and a neighbour dephased along it has neither. Over every pattern with up to three axes per site, with the clauses read in that fair way, they accept 882 rows whose palindrome fails, summed over the three graphs and the seven bond sets; in every one a two-axis site sits in a bonded component with one bond letter and three axes, and none has an admissible colouring. F138 measured one axis per site, where its sufficiency never broke. The colouring carries the right scope by construction: a site's colours are the letters all its jumps light.

## Beyond the colouring: the same string in another frame

The rows no colouring explains have palindromizers that are sums of strings. One of them, worked out exactly (gate stage E): P₃ with H = a(XX + YY) on the first bond and b(XX + YY) on the second, jumps X, Z, Y on the three sites. Both bonds force one colour and the three axes leave none, yet U = a·YYZ + b·ZXX commutes with H, anticommutes with every jump and squares to (a² + b²)·I, so it carries the palindrome at every positive rate; neither string commutes with H alone, their two commutators cancel, and at a = b = 1 it spans 𝒲. The equal-weight U is F138's; the weighted form came from a second model, Codex.

A sum like this is a single string seen from another frame, one the dissipator cannot tell apart from the original, and every palindrome with commuting jumps has such a string.

Call a Pauli string **lit** when it anticommutes with every jump. For commuting Pauli jumps one exists unless an odd number of the jumps multiply to ±1, and then 𝒲 = 0 and there is no palindrome; for single-letter dephasing with at most one axis per site one always exists.

**Theorem.** Let the jumps A_l be mutually commuting Hermitian Pauli strings (that is, for single-letter dephasing, at most one axis per site), at least one jump and every rate positive, and H Hermitian. The palindrome holds exactly when there is a unitary V commuting with every jump such that a lit string F commutes with VHV†. F can be any lit string, fixed in advance.

The dissipator cannot see V: since V commutes with every A_l, D(VρV†) = V·D(ρ)·V†, so the Liouvillian of VHV† with the same jumps is L conjugated by ρ ↦ VρV†, with the same spectrum.

*Proof.* If F commutes with VHV†, then F lies in the far kernel of the rotated Liouvillian, so that Liouvillian is palindromic by F158, and so is L. Conversely, let F158 give an invertible U ∈ 𝒲, and use its §(d): with 𝒩 = {M : [H, M] = 0, M commutes with every jump}, one has 𝒲𝒲 ⊆ 𝒩 and 𝒩𝒲, 𝒲𝒩 ⊆ 𝒲, and 𝒲 is closed under the adjoint because H and the jumps are Hermitian. Since U†U lies in the *-algebra 𝒩, so does |U|⁻¹, and the polar part W₀ = U|U|⁻¹ is a unitary in 𝒲. For real t, K = e^{it}W₀ + e^{−it}W₀† = e^{−it}W₀†(e^{2it}W₀² + 1) is Hermitian and lies in 𝒲; it is invertible unless −e^{−2it} is an eigenvalue of the unitary W₀² ∈ 𝒩, which excludes finitely many t modulo π. Then K² ∈ 𝒩 gives |K| ∈ 𝒩, and S = K|K|⁻¹ ∈ 𝒲 is a Hermitian unitary. Now pick one jump A, put P = (1 + A)/2 and

  V = P + (1 − P)·F·S.

F and S both anticommute with every jump, so F·S commutes with every jump, and so does V. From F·P = (1 − P)·F, S·P = (1 − P)·S and F² = S² = 1 one gets V†V = P + S·P·S = 1 and V†FV = P·S + S·P = S. So [VHV†, F] = V[H, S]V† = 0. ∎

The frame is exactly checked in the gate (stage F (i), sympy) on the two rows worked out on this page, stage E's at (a, b) = (3, 4) and the conditioned one below, for every jump as A and four strings F each; and in float on every row beyond the colouring, for the ZZ and the XX + YY bond sets with the first jump as A and the first lit string as F (stage F (ii): the residuals sit at least six decades below the control V = 1, where no lit string commutes with H).

**What it says, and what it does not.** The colouring asks whether H itself commutes with a lit string. The theorem asks whether H does after some rotation the dissipator cannot see, and then any lit string will do: which string, which colour, is a choice of frame. The colouring is the case V = 1. The theorem is F158 restated, and its frame is as free as F158's U: it is built from any element of 𝒲 and says nothing about which H needs which frame. So it separates EXISTS from EXPLAINS in the way F138's entry asks, and the arc `f138_converse_failures` warns that "a free answer is a sign that the question was mis-posed, not that it is closed". What carries structure is the frame on particular rows:

- **Stage E's row.** With r² = a² + b², cos θ = a/r and sin θ = b/r, one frame is V = e^{iθG/2} with G = XZY, the product of the three jump letters, which commutes with every jump. YYZ anticommutes with G, so V†·YYZ·V = YYZ·e^{iθG} = (a·YYZ + b·ZXX)/r, because YYZ·XZY = −i·ZXX. Here the sum is one string rotated about the jump string.
- **A conditioned colour.** On P₃ with ZZ bonds, jumps X on sites 0 and 2, site 1 undephased, fields 0.22·Z on site 1 and 0.41·Y on site 2 (the second row of stage F (i)), no single string works (stage F (i'')). Z on site 1 commutes with H, and in each of its two sectors site 2 feels the field 0.41·Y ± Z, which a lit letter must commute with. The colour there turns within the plane the X jump lights, and it depends on the sector: U = Z ⊗ (P₊ ⊗ (0.41·Y + Z) + P₋ ⊗ (0.41·Y − Z))/√(1 + 0.41²), with the factors in site order 0, 1, 2 and P± the projectors onto Z = ±1 on site 1. The colours are no longer two letters but a circle, and they may be steered by a conserved quantity of an undephased site. Expanded, the same U is (0.41·ZIY + ZZZ)/√(1 + 0.41²), a sum of two anticommuting strings: the section on anticommuting sums below counts this row among its single sums, and the two readings are one element.
- **Uniform Z-dephasing.** Every Z_l commutes with F·S and with P, so V is diagonal: a phase on each basis state. With F = X^⊗N the palindrome holds exactly when some diagonal phase gauge makes H commute with X^⊗N. [THE_OTHER_SIDE](../hypotheses/THE_OTHER_SIDE.md) measured that every palindrome-breaker among the 36 two-term pairs breaks this parity; the theorem gives the exact form of that: the palindrome needs the parity, but in some gauge, not for H as written. On those 36 pairs at N = 3 the gate reproduces the [Klein routing](TWO_TERM_PALINDROME_KLEIN_ROUTING.md)'s 22 palindromic and 14 hard pairs and THE_OTHER_SIDE's 26 parity-breakers, and builds a diagonal V for each of the 22 (stage F (iii)). [F103 §7.12](../docs/proofs/PROOF_F103_F87_Z2_CUBED_REFINEMENT.md) finds soft pairs, XX + XZ among them, restored by an operator that is not diagonal in the dephasing basis. That operator is the palindromizer itself, the per-site router Q acting on operator space; the V here is not a palindromizer but a change of frame for H, so a diagonal V and a palindromizer that is not diagonal do not contradict each other.

**How far a local frame goes.** [MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF](../docs/proofs/MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF.md) already uses a local frame: a per-site R_z rotation is a symmetry of Z-dephasing and carries an X field to a Y field, and "the gauge argument stops where clause 2 does". The theorem continues where the local frame stops. A product V would make its S a product of one-site unitaries in 𝒲, and a numerical search (least squares from many starts, not part of the gate, so it can miss one) found no such product on any of the 258 rows beyond the colouring, counted per bond set. A second search, for the conditioned kind (a conserved one-site letter on an undephased site, recursively, with a local frame in each sector), finds it on 124 of the 132 one-letter rows (54 of 62 on P₃, all 18 on K₃, all 52 on the bond with an isolated site) and on none of the 126 two-letter rows; those are single anticommuting sums or products of them (below), which need no sector.

## Colours on the circle: the golden router

At a site dephased along one axis, every letter n·σ with n perpendicular to that axis anticommutes with the jump, so the site's real colours are a circle, and the two Pauli letters are two points on it (complex combinations anticommute too; for c ≠ 0 below the real circle suffices, since the locus has two real roots, while F116's c = 0 station also uses complex directions). A string G = ⊗_l g_l of such letters is, up to a scale, a Hermitian unitary that anticommutes with every jump. It lies in 𝒲, the far end's kernel of F158, exactly when it commutes with H, and then its frame is of the simplest kind: under Z-dephasing a product of one-site R_z rotations, which the dissipator cannot see, carries X^⊗N to G up to the scale.

[The golden router](../docs/proofs/PROOF_CEILING_GOLDEN_ROUTER.md) (F116) finds such a G for a whole class: its §4 derives it at the golden point, and its §8 along the metallic line. The chains carry the sliding windows of c·XZX + XZY + YZX on an open chain under Z-dephasing, with c = 1 the golden point and c ≠ 0 the rest of its metallic line. F116 §4 evaluates the router on the identity and finds that "G = W(I^⊗N) = ⊗_l g_l must commute with H, with g_l = q_l(I) ∈ span{X, Y} forced by the dissipator leg". That is this page's question with colours on the circle, and the colouring rule has to be extended to answer it: a circle letter is no Pauli string, the products of distinct terms with G can cancel, and they do, across the three templates of one window. So acceptance is decided per window rather than per term. Writing g_l = α_l·X + β_l·Y, each window gives two bilinear equations between the colours of sites w and w + 2 (one parity chain is the sites of one parity), and for c ≠ 0 they have a nonzero solution exactly on the metallic locus α² − cαβ − β² = 0. Along each parity chain the colour alternates between the two locus directions, a = r·X + Y and b = X − r·Y with r = (c + √(c² + 4))/2, the golden ratio φ at c = 1, in the rhythm [a, a, b, b]. The Pauli letters sit off the locus (X gives α² − cαβ − β² = 1, Y gives −1), so by the same two equations no colouring by X and Y exists on these chains at any N ≥ 3, for c ≠ 0. At c = 0 the equations degenerate, and the period-2 strings XXXX…, XYXY…, YXYX…, YYYY… do colour the chain; they are the identity columns of F116's period-2 uniform-frame routers. Gate stage G checks all of it at small N: G commutes with H, anticommutes with every Z_l and squares to (1 + r²)^N·I, exactly, for the golden chain at N = 3, 4, 5 and the silver one (c = 2) at N = 4, while the patterns [a, b, a, b] and [a, a, a, a] and the 45° colour fail; no string of X and Y letters commutes with H at N = 3..6 for c ≠ 0, and exactly those four do at c = 0.

Read through F158, one direction of F116 turns around. There the identity column is a necessary condition, and the palindromizer W is built separately, from the window lemma. But G is an invertible element of 𝒲, so by F158 it carries the palindrome by itself, the spectrum and, by F158 §(f8), the whole Jordan structure; with zero rates allowed, as F116 allows them, a zero rate removes its jump and G still anticommutes with the others. G alone gives a site-local map, the left multiplication ρ ↦ Gρ, but onto −L† − 2σ; what the window equations do not give is F116's per-site, two-sided W with W L W⁻¹ = −L − 2σ, whose locality is the point of the ceiling arc, since G does not fix its two factors. That still needs F116 §§1–3. And F116 answers, on these chains, the question the frame theorem leaves open: the frame is local, read off H by solving the colour equations along the parity chains, and the colour angles are the frame (with θ the angle of a colour to X, tan 2θ = 2/c for both locus directions, 2 at the golden point; at c = 0 the frame can be V = 1, since X^⊗N itself colours the chain). The census above found no product frame on its 258 rows; those rows are two-site bonds and fields at N = 3, a different class from these windows, and the two findings do not touch.

## What a sum is: anticommuting strings and a defect cascade

On every census row beyond the colouring that stage H covers (one bond set per letter count), 𝒲 holds an element built from one ingredient (the coincident-magnitude rows of the Scope, with their SWAP, are not census rows, and the non-commuting example of the Open section is of another kind). Call a real combination C = Σ_i c_i·P_i of lit strings that **pairwise anticommute** an **anticommuting sum**. The cross terms of C² cancel in pairs, so C² = (Σ_i c_i²)·I: C is a Hermitian unitary up to its length, a point on a sphere whose axes are the strings, the grade-one vectors of the Clifford algebra the strings generate (the algebra, not the Clifford group of gates). A single string is the one-axis case; the circle of colours on one site is the sum over {X, Y}; stage E's U = a·YYZ + b·ZXX is a two-string sum, and F138 already notes that it squares to 2·𝟙 because "the two strings anticommute, so the cross term dies".

**The count.** On all 258 rows beyond the colouring (one bond set per letter count, the field magnitudes of the census), 𝒲 holds an element built from anticommuting sums. Labelled by the first kind the recursion finds, it is one sum on 74 rows (52 of P₃'s 62 one-letter rows, all 22 of its two-letter rows); a product of sums over the components of H on the 156 rows of the bond with an isolated site (both bond kinds); and, on the other 28 (10 on P₃, all 18 on K₃), such elements summed over the two sectors of a letter that an undephased site keeps (every term of H has I or that letter there), recursively. This is exact: for a term T of H and a lit string P, [T, P]/2i is ± a string times T's rational coefficient, the kernel on each maximal set of pairwise anticommuting lit strings is a rational nullspace, and the sectors replace the kept letter by ±1; each element is then rebuilt as a matrix and checked against H and the jumps by exact products, a route independent of the string arithmetic (gate stage H). Such an element is invertible and lies in 𝒲, so by F158 every one of these rows is palindromic through it. As controls, the same recursion finds nothing on any row of the same grid whose palindrome breaks, and with single strings only it finds nothing on the 258 (stage H).

**Why a sum does what a string does.** For a single sum (the 74 rows, and each factor of the 156 products) the frame is explicit. On the sphere of an anticommuting sum, rotations are generated by products P_i·P_j of two lit strings, and such a product commutes with every jump, so the dissipator cannot see the rotation. Rotating one axis string onto C is therefore a frame of the theorem above, and it is read off C directly: on stage E's row it is e^{iθG/2} with G ∝ YYZ·ZXX. The sum is the string, turned on a sphere the dissipator does not see. On a product the frame is the product of the factors' frames; on the 28 sector rows no frame is written out here.

**How the sum is built: a defect cascade.** On one bond, H = XX + YY + h₀·Y ⊗ I + h₁·I ⊗ Z with a jump X on the second site (the first site undephased), ZZ colours the bare bond. The field h₀ breaks it, leaving the defect [h₀Y₀, ZZ] = 2i·h₀·XZ; IY cancels it, because [XX, IY] = 2i·XZ, which forces its coefficient to −h₀. IY in turn is broken by the field h₁, leaving the defect IX, and YZ cancels that, because [YY, YZ] = 2i·IX, which forces −h₀h₁. YZ commutes with everything left, and the cascade stops:

  C = ZZ − h₀·IY − h₀h₁·YZ,  C² = (1 + h₀² + h₀²h₁²)·I,

for every real h₀, h₁ (gate stage H (ii), symbolic, with the sign-flipped last coefficient failing). The strings of this cascade anticommute pairwise and the coefficients multiply along it; with the isolated site's colour Y tensored on, it is the element the census finds on the row of the bond with an isolated site that carries the fields Y and Z on its bond sites, Y on the isolated one, no jump on the first site and X on the other two; there 𝒲 is one-dimensional, so it is the only element up to scale. Stage E's sum is the shortest cascade: YYZ is broken only by the b-bond, leaving the defect YIX, and ZXX, broken only by the a-bond onto the same YIX, cancels it with the coefficients crossed.

**What the cascade predicts.** Because it shows which cancellation holds the sum together, the cascade says which change of H moves the symmetry and which one ends it (worked out first by a second model, Codex; gate stage J, symbolic). Move the first field, h₀ to h₀ + δ: the old C fails, [H, C] = 2iδ·XZ, and the cascade rebuilt at the new field is again in 𝒲, so losing the old signal is not losing the symmetry; the carrier moves with the parameters. Add a field εX on the first site instead: F158 §(f5) makes "every word in {H, A₁ … A_m} with an odd number of A-letters" traceless when the palindrome holds, and here Tr(H²·A) = 8ε for the jump A = IX. For ε ≠ 0 no element of 𝒲 is invertible, and in fact 𝒲 = 0 at every h₀, h₁ (the commutator on the eight lit strings has determinant ε² on eight of its output strings): that change ends the palindrome, whatever sum one tries. The sum is also the observable that decays cleanly. On stage E's row the Heisenberg-picture generator closes on P = YYZ, Q = ZXX and R = YIX, L†P = −2σP − 2b·R, L†Q = −2σQ + 2a·R, L†R = 2b·P − 2a·Q − 2(γ₀ + γ₂)·R, so among the combinations of P and Q only a·P + b·Q is an eigen-operator, decaying at exactly 2σ, the far-end rate, while b·P − a·Q feeds R.

**At N = 4.** N = 3 is degenerate in its graphs: "at N=3 chain = star = P₃ and ring = complete = K₃", as F138's evidence notes. At N = 4 the chain P₄, the star S₄, the ring C₄ and K₄ are four different graphs, so the census was repeated there: bonds ZZ, XX + YY and XX + YY + ZZ, every dephasing pattern with at most one axis per site, every field pattern, fields 30, 22, 41, 17 against bonds 100, 783,360 rows in all ([`simulations/anticommuting_sum_census.py`](../simulations/anticommuting_sum_census.py) → [`anticommuting_sum_census.txt`](../simulations/results/anticommuting_sum_census.txt)). ## The family, swept by the end count

The census reads the palindrome through ranks and builds the elements of its exceptions in a separate step. MirrorWorld's [`EndCount`](../compute/MirrorWorld/EndCount.cs) does both in one object: where no colouring, no one-jump word and no single-string count decides, it lifts each end's kernel basis from GF(p) to the integers by rational reconstruction and keeps a vector only if it commutes with H exactly, so the number kept is an exact lower bound on that end's count, and a far end with one kept vector that squares to a nonzero multiple of 1 is an exact invertible element; the typed witness `inspect --root twoendstrings` lifts the same way. Swept over the family the `twoend` witnesses read (Heisenberg bonds; one dephasing letter or none per site; fields of equal magnitude, one letter or none per site; run mode `endcount sweep N topology`), no row is left to the ranks: the 4,032 rows at N = 3 on the chain, the 65,280 rows at N = 4 on each of the chain, the star, the ring and the complete graph, and at N = 5 on the same four graphs the 248,832 rows that dephase every site and the 276,480 that leave exactly two sites undephased, with the 15,360 ring rows that leave four.

## What carries every palindrome of the swept family: a symmetry

The colourings and the swapped elements are one kind of object. Let σ be a permutation of the sites, let u_l on each site be the identity or n_l·σ for an axis n_l, and put U = P_σ·⊗_l u_l, with P_σ the permutation of the tensor factors. Conjugation by U permutes the sites by σ and rotates each site's letters by the π rotation about n_l where u_l is one. If it fixes H and sends every jump to minus itself, then [H, U] = 0 and A·U·A = −U for every jump A, so U lies in F158's far space, and U is a multiple of a unitary, hence invertible: by F158 the palindrome holds. A colouring is the case σ = id with, on each site, the identity or a letter; the elements of the section above are an involution σ with one axis n on every site, n a letter or the bisector of two letters, which are exactly the nine π rotations among the 24 rotations that permute the axes up to sign. `SymmetryElement()` builds only this second kind, one axis on every site, and the colourings are counted separately.

The end count builds this element itself. `EndCount.Symmetries()` lists every symmetry of (H, jumps) among the site permutations times those 24 rotations, and `SymmetryElement()` takes one whose rotation is a π rotation, whose permutation is an involution fixing every jump's support and which negates every jump, builds U_g in exact integer arithmetic and checks it. Over every Heisenberg family swept here, every palindrome is carried by a colouring or by such an element and none by neither: the three-site chain family (603 coloured, 6 by a symmetry), the four-site family on the chain, the star, the ring and the complete graph (3,795 coloured on each, and 0, 54, 36, 108 by a symmetry), the five-site rows that dephase every site (all coloured) or leave exactly two sites undephased (7,650 coloured on each of the four graphs, and 0, 36, 0, 60 by a symmetry), F138's three-site grid (its 21,609 rows with a jump; 2,085 coloured, 78 by a symmetry) and K₃ at three equal magnitudes (2,085 coloured, 234 by a symmetry). The five-site ring tests the rule: a reflection of it fixes one site and swaps two pairs, and every dephased site must stay fixed, so its element needs four undephased sites; the sweep finds no symmetry element on the ring with two undephased sites and 120 with four, every palindrome there carried. That every palindrome of these families has an element of this form is measured, not proven; the sufficiency is the two lines above.

**The contrast: where the symmetry does not reach.** At other bond letters and distinct field magnitudes the symmetry element stops explaining, and the end count shows what takes its place; distinct magnitudes alone already bar every site permutation that carries a field onto another, and the one-magnitude rows are read in the next paragraph. At the census's own bonds and magnitudes (bond weight 100 per letter, fields 30, 22, 41 at N = 3 and 30, 22, 41, 17 at N = 4, one per site, positive; run mode `endcount sweep N topology out.csv bonds=ZZ weight=100 mags=30,22,41,17` at N = 4 and `mags=30,22,41` at N = 3, `bonds=XY` for XX + YY) every row is read exactly, and the palindromic rows the colouring does not reach are the census's, row for row (checked against the rows of [`anticommuting_sum_certificates.json`](../simulations/results/anticommuting_sum_certificates.json)): 22 at XX + YY on P₃, F138's own count, and 62 at ZZ, the 776 − 714 its colouring leaves; at N = 4, 676 and 40 on the chain, 808 and 0 on the star, 240 and 0 on the ring, 56 and 0 on K₄, and 0 at Heisenberg bonds on all four, with 13,215 coloured rows at ZZ and 3,795 at the other two bond sets on each graph. Not one of these rows is carried by a colouring or a site symmetry. At the two-letter bond the far end is one-dimensional on every such row, and the lifted element is an anticommuting sum, its strings pairwise anticommuting, on all 22 rows at N = 3 and all 40 at N = 4 (−YII + ZZY on the row X.., .ZY; the stage-E pair XXZ + ZYY on YZX, ...); these are the census's sums, reached by the end count's own kernel with no grammar handed to it. The eight at N = 3 with two strings of coefficients of equal magnitude, like −YII + ZZY, are Clifford unitaries up to scale; the other fourteen and all forty at N = 4 carry coefficients that depend on the magnitudes and are no Clifford. At the one-letter bond the far end is larger on these rows (dimension 2 or 4 at N = 3, from 2 to 20 at N = 4), the palindrome is certified by the two counts met with lifted vectors, and no single element is picked out; the census splits these rows into single sums and its conditioned kind, a distinction the end count does not draw; no Clifford element of 𝒲 exists on any of them (below).

**A Clifford that permutes the bonds.** A colouring and a site symmetry's element are both Clifford unitaries, and the end count looks for every Clifford element of 𝒲 directly (`EndCount.CliffordSymmetry`). Conjugation by a Clifford sends each string to plus or minus a string, so it fixes H exactly when it permutes H's terms with their coefficients, and the unitary lies in 𝒲 when it also negates every jump. The search assigns images term by term and keeps an assignment only while commutation and every product relation among the terms and jumps, phase included, carry over to the images; by Witt's theorem that is exactly when a Clifford realises it, and the colourings are the assignments that fix every term. On P₃ with XX + YY bonds it finds Clifford elements no site symmetry is: they exchange the bond XX₀₁ with YY₁₂ and fix YY₀₁ and XX₁₂, which no permutation of the path's sites does. At distinct magnitudes they sit on eight rows, the two-string sums with coefficients of equal magnitude such as (−YII + ZZY)/√2 on the row X.., .ZY, and they fix every field. At one magnitude four rows join on which the same bond exchange also swaps two fields. On the row with the jump X on site 0 and fields Y and Z on sites 1 and 2, 𝒲 is spanned by E = −YII − ZXX − YYZ + ZZY, E/2 is a Clifford unitary, and conjugation by it exchanges XX₀₁ and YY₁₂, fixes YY₀₁ and XX₁₂, swaps the field Y₁ with Z₂ and negates the jump; the two fields swap, so their magnitudes must coincide, and at distinct ones the row is broken. These four are the row above and its images under the letter swap X ↔ Y and the chain's reflection, read from the sweep; the tests pin the first. In Jordan–Wigner Majoranas (the fermion degree of [F132](LATTICE_DEAD_SET_RULE.md)), γ₀ = XII, γ₁ = YII, γ₂ = ZXI, γ₃ = ZYI, γ₄ = ZZX, γ₅ = ZZY, the element is E = (1 − γ₀γ₃γ₄)(γ₅ − γ₁), and E/√2 is the neighbouring row's sum γ₅ − γ₁ conjugated by e^(−π/8·γ₀γ₃γ₄), where γ₀γ₃γ₄ = ±XXX commutes with the jump γ₀. Composed with e^(−π/8·γ₁γ₅), which turns the string γ₅ onto (γ₅ − γ₁)/√2, this is a frame of the frame theorem above: the unitary V† = e^(−π/8·γ₀γ₃γ₄)·e^(−π/8·γ₁γ₅) commutes with the jump and carries the lit string ZZY to E/2. The field Y₁ is odd in the Majoranas, so H is not quadratic on this row, and the Majorana form belongs to the element rather than to H.

Read at one magnitude (bond weight 100, every field 30; at Heisenberg bonds weight 10, every field 3) against the distinct magnitudes of the contrast, on P₃ and at N = 4 on the four graphs, the kinds separate by what they depend on. At ZZ bonds the palindromic rows beyond the colouring are the same rows at both, and none has a Clifford element. At XX + YY bonds the fourteen non-Clifford sums at N = 3 and the forty at N = 4 are also the same rows at both magnitudes. Coinciding magnitudes add the elements that move a field onto another: at N = 3 two of the eight Clifford rows gain a site symmetry beside their bond exchange and the four field-swapping rows appear, and at N = 4 site symmetries appear on the star (18 rows), the ring (12) and K₄ (36), none on the chain, while no Clifford element outside the site symmetries appears on any of the four graphs at either magnitude, every search there run to its end rather than cut off by its budget. At Heisenberg bonds the site symmetries appear the same way (54, 36 and 108 at N = 4, none at distinct magnitudes). So the carriers nest, a string inside a site symmetry inside a Clifford, none of them with coefficients that depend on the magnitudes; outside the Clifford group sits a sum whose coefficients are polynomials in the magnitudes; and at a ZZ bond the palindrome beyond the colouring is carried by no single named element (measured).

Reading those rows needs a wide lift. The sums' coefficients are polynomials in the field and bond magnitudes, built along a defect cascade, and at N = 4 they reach 4,670,000 (row X..., ZZZY on the chain at XX + YY), while one prime near 10⁹ reconstructs a rational only to about 22,000 in numerator and denominator; on that row the kernel vector the elimination leaves carries rationals past that bound, and one prime lifts nothing. At one prime, 228 of the palindromic rows beyond the colouring (84 on the chain, 122 on the star and 18 on the ring at ZZ, 4 on the chain at XX + YY) would be left to the ranks. The end count therefore combines each kernel vector over further primes by the Chinese remainder theorem until it passes the exact check (`EndCount.LiftPrimes`); the kept vectors are still exact lower bounds, whatever the primes did. The tests pin the X..., ZZZY row: empty at one prime, one certifying sum over several.

## Scope, and what would falsify it

The colouring rule and the derivation of F138's clauses hold for any graph and any N, for H a sum of distinct Pauli-string bonds P⊗P and one-site fields with single-letter dephasing jumps. The census is N = 3, three graphs, unit bond weights, one field-magnitude tuple, positive field signs; the anticommuting-sum census adds N = 4 on four graphs with the three bond sets ZZ, XX + YY and XX + YY + ZZ, one magnitude tuple (fields 0.30, 0.22, 0.41, 0.17 against unit bonds), positive signs, verdicts from ranks modulo two primes. Coincident field magnitudes, F138's second failure, add palindromes by the graph's own symmetry (U = SWAP₀₂·(n·σ)^⊗3, not a string; SWAP₀₂·Z₀Z₁Z₂ is one of them), and are not in this census; the end count reads them in the section on the swept family. The swept family of the section before is their Heisenberg case only: bonds XX + YY + ZZ of one weight, every field of one magnitude with a positive sign, all dephasing and field patterns at N = 3 on the chain and at N = 4 on four graphs, and at N = 5 on four graphs the patterns that dephase every site or leave exactly two sites undephased, with the ring's patterns that leave four.

What would falsify it: a row with an admissible colouring and no palindrome (that would contradict F158), or a rejected-palindrome count differing from F138's recorded ones on the same grid. For the N = 4 census: an element that fails exact verification, a row with a colouring that the ranks call broken, or a certificate in the JSON that fails [H, G] = 0, {A, G} = 0 or invertibility when rebuilt. For the frame theorem: a palindromic row with commuting jumps on which V = P + (1 − P)·F·S fails to be unitary, to commute with a jump, or to carry F to S.

## Open

- **How far the anticommuting sums reach.** On the census rows beyond the colouring, at N = 3 and at N = 4 on four graphs, 𝒲 has an element built from anticommuting sums, and the golden router is a product of one-site ones. The building rule is a grammar: an anticommuting sum on a set of pairwise anticommuting lit strings, tensor products over the components of H, and sums over the two sectors of a one-site letter that H keeps on an undephased site. It does not reach every palindrome. On F138's coincident-magnitude row (P₃ with Heisenberg bonds, fields +0.3 X and −0.3 X on the two ends, a jump X on the middle site) U = SWAP₀₂·Z₀Z₁Z₂ = (ZZZ − YZY − XZX + IZI)/2 is an invertible element of 𝒲, and the rule finds nothing there: every maximal anticommuting set has a trivial kernel, H is connected, and no undephased site keeps a single letter (the census script carries it as a fixed control). Whether every palindrome with commuting jumps and distinct field magnitudes has an element of this grammar, at every N and every field tuple, whether cycles are what forces the conditioned kind (single sums appear only on trees in both censuses), and whether the defect cascade always terminates and fixes the coefficients, is open. So is the reading the two-letter rows suggest: an XX + YY chain is free fermions, and Jordan-Wigner Majorana operators are pairwise anticommuting strings ([the F80 proof](../docs/proofs/PROOF_F80_BLOCH_SIGNWALK.md) holds them), so these sums may be Majorana objects; the one Clifford kind beyond the site symmetries is one, a two-Majorana sum turned by a three-Majorana frame (the section on the swept family). The product-frame and conditioned-kind counts of the frame section are a numerical search; every row counted beyond the colouring carries an exactly verified element (stage H at N = 3, the census script at N = 4), while the set of rows rests on the ranks modulo two primes in the census script; the end count reads that set exactly on P₃ and at N = 4 on the four graphs (the contrast paragraph of the swept-family section), leaving K₃ and the bond with an isolated site to the ranks.
- **Non-commuting jumps.** A site dephased along two axes carries two anticommuting jumps. The proof uses commutation in one place only: P = (1 + A)/2 has to commute with every jump, so the construction goes through whenever one jump commutes with all the others. Without such a jump the frame can fail although the palindrome holds (gate stage F (iv)): jumps X and Z on site 0 of three, sites 1 and 2 undephased, H = 1 ⊗ diag(1, 2, 3, 4) + X ⊗ (|0⟩⟨3| + h.c.) + Z ⊗ ((|1⟩ + |2⟩)⟨3| + h.c.). Its 𝒲 is spanned by Y ⊗ diag(1, 1, 1, −1). A unitary commuting with X and Z on site 0 is 1 ⊗ v, a lit string is Y ⊗ f, and v†fv has eigenvalues split (2, 2) or (4, 0), never (3, 1). Which palindromes of this kind have a frame is open. When every site carries exactly two axes, 𝒲 lies in the span of the one lit string, so wherever the palindrome holds the frame is V = 1.

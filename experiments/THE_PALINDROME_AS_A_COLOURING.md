# The palindrome as a colouring

**Status:** Tier 1 where it restates [F158](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) (a Pauli string in the far kernel carries the palindrome) and where it derives F138's clauses from the colouring rule, which is exact; Tier 1 for the frame theorem, proved here from F158 §(d), by which every palindrome with commuting jumps has a lit string commuting with H in a frame the dissipator cannot see, an existence statement; Tier 1 for the reading of the golden router's identity column as a colouring by letters on the lit circle, checked exactly in gate stage G; the census counts rows at N = 3 on three graphs whose palindrome verdicts come from singular-value ranks separated by twelve decades, not from an exact rank certificate, gated by [`simulations/f138_palindrome_colouring.py`](../simulations/f138_palindrome_colouring.py) → [`f138_palindrome_colouring.txt`](../simulations/results/f138_palindrome_colouring.txt).
**Date:** 2026-09-29
**Authors:** Thomas Wicht, Claude (Anthropic, Opus 5.5)

F138 states the dephasing palindrome's boundary as two clauses: at most two dephasing axes per component, and a field orthogonal to all of them, with a proviso that a single-letter bond such as ZZ tolerates all three axes. It measures them sufficient and knows their converse is false. F158 decides the palindrome exactly through its far kernel

  𝒲 = {W : [H, W] = 0 and A W A = −W for every jump A},

which holds an invertible element exactly when the palindrome holds. This page looks for the simplest such element, a single Pauli string, and finds that looking for one is a **colouring of the graph**: every site takes one Pauli letter, its colour, from the letters its jumps light (those that anticommute with every dephasing letter of the site), and every bond and every field of H has to accept the colours it touches. F138's two clauses are the colourings with one colour per component, and its proviso is the freedom a one-letter bond leaves. The colouring explains most of F138's exceptions at a one-letter bond and none at a two-letter bond, and it shows where the proviso overreaches: on a site carrying two axes. The palindromes it misses can still be read through a lit string: with commuting jumps, the palindrome holds exactly when some unitary commuting with every jump turns H into a Hamiltonian that commutes with a lit string. That is an existence statement, F158 in this page's language; which unitary a given H needs is open.

The word is used once already. `PalindromeSoftCertifier`'s `LinearSiteColoring` 2-colours the site graph: a per-site string K, Z on one sublattice, with K·H·K = −H, a string that **anticommutes** with H; F103 §7.12 treats that site-graph test as a proxy for a diagonal operator on the basis-state graph. The colouring here chooses a string that **commutes** with H and anticommutes with the jumps. The two are siblings, one for the Hamiltonian's sign flip and one for the dissipator's.

## The colouring

Take H a sum of distinct Pauli strings with nonzero coefficients (bonds P⊗P with P ∈ {X, Y, Z}, and one-site fields) and one Pauli letter per dephasing jump. Look for a single Pauli string F in 𝒲.

- **Anticommuting with the jumps** is per site: the letter of F at site l must anticommute with every dephasing letter of site l, i.e. be lit by all of them. At a site dephased along one letter two letters qualify, along two letters one, along all three none; at an undephased site any letter does, the identity included. These are the site's **colours**.
- **Commuting with H** is per term. A Pauli string T commutes with F or anticommutes with it, and [T, F] = 2·T·F in the second case. Distinct terms give distinct products T·F, so nothing cancels: [H, F] = 0 exactly when every term commutes with F.
- **A bond P⊗P** commutes with F when both or neither of its sites anticommute with P.
- **A field P at site l** commutes with F when the colour there is I or P.

So an admissible colouring is exactly a Pauli string in 𝒲, and by F158 it carries the palindrome: the one-sided multiplication ρ ↦ F·ρ gives L ~ −L† − 2σ, and F158 §(f8) gives L ~ −L − 2σ. The one-sided factor R of the canonical Π_Z = R·D is the colouring F = X^⊗N.

**What the rule forces, bond by bond.** A letter's three answers to "do you anticommute with X, with Y, with Z" always contain an even number of yeses (I: none; X: Y and Z; Y: X and Z; Z: X and Y), so any two of them name the letter.

- A **Heisenberg** bond, XX + YY + ZZ, and any **two-letter** bond, asks two of those questions of both ends and gets the same answers, so it forces the same colour on its two ends. A connected component then needs one colour, lit on every site: at most two dephasing axes in it, **F138's clause 1**.
- A **field** fixes the colour of its site to its own letter (or I), and the colour must be lit: with one colour per component the field lies along it, orthogonal to every dephasing axis, **F138's clause 2**.
- A **one-letter bond** P⊗P asks only one question, so it only puts both ends in the same class, {I, P} or the other two letters. The second class is open at every site dephased along a single axis, whichever the axis, so the colours can change from site to site and three axes coexist. That is **F138's two-term proviso**, derived: the colour freedom of a one-letter bond.

F138's clauses 1 and 2 are the colourings with one colour per component; the proviso is where a one-letter bond lets the colour change.

## Where the proviso overreaches

Read as written, the proviso lets a component whose bonds carry one letter hold three axes in any arrangement. Three axes on one site were already excluded: F138 calls the depolarizing channel clause 1 failing, and MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF says the stacked site breaks the Ising bond too. A site with **two** axes was excluded nowhere, and there the proviso is wrong. Such a site has a single colour, the third letter. When that letter is the bond's own, a one-letter bond asks it (or I) of each neighbour, and a neighbour dephased along it has neither. Over every pattern with up to three axes per site, with the clauses read in that fair way, they accept 882 rows whose palindrome fails, summed over the three graphs and the seven bond sets; in every one a two-axis site sits in a bonded component with one bond letter and three axes, and none has an admissible colouring. F138 measured one axis per site, where its sufficiency never broke. The colouring carries the right scope by construction: a site's colours are the letters all its jumps light.

## Beyond the colouring: the same string in another frame

The rows no colouring explains have palindromizers that are sums of strings. One of them, worked out exactly (gate stage E): P₃ with H = a(XX + YY) on the first bond and b(XX + YY) on the second, jumps X, Z, Y on the three sites. Both bonds force one colour and the three axes leave none, yet U = a·YYZ + b·ZXX commutes with H, anticommutes with every jump and squares to (a² + b²)·I, so it carries the palindrome at every positive rate; neither string commutes with H alone, their two commutators cancel, and at a = b = 1 it spans 𝒲. The equal-weight U is F138's; the weighted form came from a second session (Codex).

A sum like this is a single string seen from another frame, one the dissipator cannot tell apart from the original, and every palindrome with commuting jumps has such a string.

Call a Pauli string **lit** when it anticommutes with every jump. For commuting Pauli jumps one exists unless an odd number of the jumps multiply to ±1, and then 𝒲 = 0 and there is no palindrome; for single-letter dephasing with at most one axis per site one always exists.

**Theorem.** Let the jumps A_l be mutually commuting Hermitian Pauli strings (that is, for single-letter dephasing, at most one axis per site), at least one jump and every rate positive, and H Hermitian. The palindrome holds exactly when there is a unitary V commuting with every jump such that a lit string F commutes with VHV†. F can be any lit string, fixed in advance.

The dissipator cannot see V: since V commutes with every A_l, D(VρV†) = V·D(ρ)·V†, so the Liouvillian of VHV† with the same jumps is L conjugated by ρ ↦ VρV†, with the same spectrum.

*Proof.* If F commutes with VHV†, then F lies in the far kernel of the rotated Liouvillian, so that Liouvillian is palindromic by F158, and so is L. Conversely, let F158 give an invertible U ∈ 𝒲, and use its §(d): with 𝒩 = {M : [H, M] = 0, M commutes with every jump}, one has 𝒲𝒲 ⊆ 𝒩 and 𝒩𝒲, 𝒲𝒩 ⊆ 𝒲, and 𝒲 is closed under the adjoint because H and the jumps are Hermitian. Since U†U lies in the *-algebra 𝒩, so does |U|⁻¹, and the polar part W₀ = U|U|⁻¹ is a unitary in 𝒲. For real t, K = e^{it}W₀ + e^{−it}W₀† = e^{−it}W₀†(e^{2it}W₀² + 1) is Hermitian and lies in 𝒲; it is invertible unless −e^{−2it} is an eigenvalue of the unitary W₀² ∈ 𝒩, which excludes finitely many t modulo π. Then K² ∈ 𝒩 gives |K| ∈ 𝒩, and S = K|K|⁻¹ ∈ 𝒲 is a Hermitian unitary. Now pick one jump A, put P = (1 + A)/2 and

  V = P + (1 − P)·F·S.

F and S both anticommute with every jump, so F·S commutes with every jump, and so does V. From F·P = (1 − P)·F, S·P = (1 − P)·S and F² = S² = 1 one gets V†V = P + S·P·S = 1 and V†FV = P·S + S·P = S. So [VHV†, F] = V[H, S]V† = 0. ∎

The frame is exactly checked in the gate (stage F (i), sympy) on the two rows worked out on this page, stage E's at (a, b) = (3, 4) and the conditioned one below, for every jump as A and four strings F each; and in float on every row beyond the colouring, for the ZZ and the XX + YY bond sets with the first jump as A and the first lit string as F (stage F (ii): the residuals sit at least six decades below the control V = 1, where no lit string commutes with H).

**What it says, and what it does not.** The colouring asks whether H itself commutes with a lit string. The theorem asks whether H does after some rotation the dissipator cannot see, and then any lit string will do: which string, which colour, is a choice of frame. The colouring is the case V = 1. The theorem is F158 restated, and its frame is as free as F158's U: it is built from any element of 𝒲 and says nothing about which H needs which frame. So it separates EXISTS from EXPLAINS in the way F138's entry asks, and the arc `f138_converse_failures` warns that "a free answer is a sign that the question was mis-posed, not that it is closed". What carries structure is the frame on particular rows:

- **Stage E's row.** With r² = a² + b², cos θ = a/r and sin θ = b/r, one frame is V = e^{iθG/2} with G = XZY, the product of the three jump letters, which commutes with every jump. YYZ anticommutes with G, so V†·YYZ·V = YYZ·e^{iθG} = (a·YYZ + b·ZXX)/r, because YYZ·XZY = −i·ZXX. Here the sum is one string rotated about the jump string.
- **A conditioned colour.** On P₃ with ZZ bonds, jumps X on sites 0 and 2, site 1 undephased, fields 0.22·Z on site 1 and 0.41·Y on site 2 (the second row of stage F (i)), no single string works (stage F (i'')). Z on site 1 commutes with H, and in each of its two sectors site 2 feels the field 0.41·Y ± Z, which a lit letter must commute with. The colour there turns within the plane the X jump lights, and it depends on the sector: U = Z ⊗ (P₊ ⊗ (0.41·Y + Z) + P₋ ⊗ (0.41·Y − Z))/√(1 + 0.41²), with the factors in site order 0, 1, 2 and P± the projectors onto Z = ±1 on site 1. The colours are no longer two letters but a circle, and they may be steered by a conserved quantity of an undephased site.
- **Uniform Z-dephasing.** Every Z_l commutes with F·S and with P, so V is diagonal: a phase on each basis state. With F = X^⊗N the palindrome holds exactly when some diagonal phase gauge makes H commute with X^⊗N. [THE_OTHER_SIDE](../hypotheses/THE_OTHER_SIDE.md) measured that every palindrome-breaker among the 36 two-term pairs breaks this parity; the theorem gives the exact form of that: the palindrome needs the parity, but in some gauge, not for H as written. On those 36 pairs at N = 3 the gate reproduces the [Klein routing](TWO_TERM_PALINDROME_KLEIN_ROUTING.md)'s 22 palindromic and 14 hard pairs and THE_OTHER_SIDE's 26 parity-breakers, and builds a diagonal V for each of the 22 (stage F (iii)). [F103 §7.12](../docs/proofs/PROOF_F103_F87_Z2_CUBED_REFINEMENT.md) finds soft pairs, XX + XZ among them, restored by an operator that is not diagonal in the dephasing basis. That operator is the palindromizer itself, the per-site router Q acting on operator space; the V here is not a palindromizer but a change of frame for H, so a diagonal V and a palindromizer that is not diagonal do not contradict each other.

**How far a local frame goes.** [MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF](../docs/proofs/MIRROR_SYMMETRY_PROOF.md) already uses a local frame: a per-site R_z rotation is a symmetry of Z-dephasing and carries an X field to a Y field, and "the gauge argument stops where clause 2 does". The theorem continues where the local frame stops. A product V would make its S a product of one-site unitaries in 𝒲, and a numerical search (least squares from many starts, not part of the gate, so it can miss one) found no such product on any of the 258 rows beyond the colouring, counted per bond set. A second search, for the conditioned kind (a conserved one-site letter on an undephased site, recursively, with a local frame in each sector), finds it on 124 of the 132 one-letter rows (54 of 62 on P₃, all 18 on K₃, all 52 on the bond with an isolated site) and on none of the 126 two-letter rows. Why the two-letter rows escape it is not worked out.

## Colours on the circle: the golden router

At a site dephased along one axis, every letter n·σ with n perpendicular to that axis anticommutes with the jump, so the site's real colours are a circle, and the two Pauli letters are two points on it (complex combinations anticommute too; for c ≠ 0 below the real circle suffices, since the locus has two real roots, while F116's c = 0 station also uses complex directions). A string G = ⊗_l g_l of such letters is, up to a scale, a Hermitian unitary that anticommutes with every jump. It lies in 𝒲, the far end's kernel of F158, exactly when it commutes with H, and then its frame is of the simplest kind: under Z-dephasing a product of one-site R_z rotations, which the dissipator cannot see, carries X^⊗N to G up to the scale.

[The golden router](../docs/proofs/PROOF_CEILING_GOLDEN_ROUTER.md) (F116) finds such a G for a whole class: its §4 derives it at the golden point, and its §8 along the metallic line. The chains carry the sliding windows of c·XZX + XZY + YZX on an open chain under Z-dephasing, with c = 1 the golden point and c ≠ 0 the rest of its metallic line. F116 §4 evaluates the router on the identity and finds that "G = W(I^⊗N) = ⊗_l g_l must commute with H, with g_l = q_l(I) ∈ span{X, Y} forced by the dissipator leg". That is this page's question with colours on the circle, and the colouring rule has to be extended to answer it: a circle letter is no Pauli string, the products of distinct terms with G can cancel, and they do, across the three templates of one window. So acceptance is decided per window rather than per term. Writing g_l = α_l·X + β_l·Y, each window gives two bilinear equations between the colours of sites w and w + 2 (one parity chain is the sites of one parity), and for c ≠ 0 they have a nonzero solution exactly on the metallic locus α² − cαβ − β² = 0. Along each parity chain the colour alternates between the two locus directions, a = r·X + Y and b = X − r·Y with r = (c + √(c² + 4))/2, the golden ratio φ at c = 1, in the rhythm [a, a, b, b]. The Pauli letters sit off the locus (X gives α² − cαβ − β² = 1, Y gives −1), so by the same two equations no colouring by X and Y exists on these chains at any N, for c ≠ 0. At c = 0 the equations degenerate, and the period-2 strings XXXX…, XYXY…, YXYX…, YYYY… do colour the chain; they are the identity columns of F116's period-2 uniform-frame routers. Gate stage G checks all of it at small N: G commutes with H, anticommutes with every Z_l and squares to (1 + r²)^N·I, exactly, for the golden chain at N = 3, 4, 5 and the silver one (c = 2) at N = 4, while the patterns [a, b, a, b] and [a, a, a, a] and the 45° colour fail; no string of X and Y letters commutes with H at N = 3..6 for c ≠ 0, and exactly those four do at c = 0.

Read through F158, one direction of F116 turns around. There the identity column is a necessary condition, and the palindromizer W is built separately, from the window lemma. But G is an invertible element of 𝒲, so by F158 it carries the palindrome by itself, the spectrum and, by F158 §(f8), the whole Jordan structure; with zero rates allowed, as F116 allows them, a zero rate removes its jump and G still anticommutes with the others. G alone gives a site-local map, the left multiplication ρ ↦ Gρ, but onto −L† − 2σ; what the window equations do not give is F116's per-site, two-sided W with W L W⁻¹ = −L − 2σ, whose locality is the point of the ceiling arc, since G does not fix its two factors. That still needs F116 §§1–3. And F116 answers, on these chains, the question the frame theorem leaves open: the frame is local, read off H by solving the colour equations along the parity chains, and the colour angles are the frame (with θ the angle of a colour to X, tan 2θ = 2/c for both locus directions, 2 at the golden point; at c = 0 the frame can be V = 1, since X^⊗N itself colours the chain). The census above found no product frame on its 258 rows; those rows are two-site bonds and fields at N = 3, a different class from these windows, and the two findings do not touch.

## Scope, and what would falsify it

The colouring rule and the derivation of F138's clauses hold for any graph and any N, for H a sum of distinct Pauli-string bonds P⊗P and one-site fields with single-letter dephasing jumps. The census is N = 3, three graphs, unit bond weights, one field-magnitude tuple, positive field signs. Coincident field magnitudes, F138's second failure, add palindromes by the graph's own symmetry (U = SWAP₀₂·Z₀Z₁Z₂, not a string), and are not in this census.

What would falsify it: a row with an admissible colouring and no palindrome (that would contradict F158), or a rejected-palindrome count differing from F138's recorded ones on the same grid. For the frame theorem: a palindromic row with commuting jumps on which V = P + (1 − P)·F·S fails to be unitary, to commute with a jump, or to carry F to S.

## Open

- **Finding the frame.** The theorem says a frame exists whenever the palindrome holds; it builds V from an element of 𝒲 and does not say how to read V off H. On the golden router's chains the frame is local and follows from F116's colour equations. Whether the same method, colours as unknowns on the circle and the terms of H as equations between them, reaches the conditioned rows (where the colour also depends on a sector), and which Hamiltonians need an entangling frame (a rotation about the jump string, as on stage E's row, or a phase gauge fixed by the graph), is open. The census of kinds above is a numerical search, not a gate.
- **Non-commuting jumps.** A site dephased along two axes carries two anticommuting jumps. The proof uses commutation in one place only: P = (1 + A)/2 has to commute with every jump, so the construction goes through whenever one jump commutes with all the others. Without such a jump the frame can fail although the palindrome holds (gate stage F (iv)): jumps X and Z on site 0 of three, sites 1 and 2 undephased, H = 1 ⊗ diag(1, 2, 3, 4) + X ⊗ (|0⟩⟨3| + h.c.) + Z ⊗ ((|1⟩ + |2⟩)⟨3| + h.c.). Its 𝒲 is spanned by Y ⊗ diag(1, 1, 1, −1). A unitary commuting with X and Z on site 0 is 1 ⊗ v, a lit string is Y ⊗ f, and v†fv has eigenvalues split (2, 2) or (4, 0), never (3, 1). Which palindromes of this kind have a frame is open. When every site carries exactly two axes, 𝒲 lies in the span of the one lit string, so wherever the palindrome holds the frame is V = 1.

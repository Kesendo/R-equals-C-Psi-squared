## What this is about

One sodium nucleus, a spin 3/2, relaxes with two mirrors: the half of its modes that the slow component J₀ of the field-gradient fluctuations cannot see is palindromic about one centre, the other half about a second centre 3J₀ further out, and both are one map. This page asks what a static coupling between nuclei does to the two mirrors, for the forms such a coupling takes: the Ising part I_z·I_z, the secular dipolar coupling, and its truncations when the quadrupolar splitting dominates it.

The answer has three layers. Whenever the coupling keeps each nucleus's outer/inner bit, the chain carries a ladder of exact mirrors, one centre for each number of bright nuclei. When two neighbours have the same quadrupolar splitting, their satellites exchange polarization resonantly (satellite-transition spin diffusion), brightness moves from one nucleus to the next, and the ladder is exact only on the part of the spectrum where brightness stays sharp. The full secular dipolar coupling, untruncated, breaks the map itself, at exactly the terms that couple a satellite of one nucleus to the central transition of the other.

The physical regime is an assumption, not established here: a static coupling between specific nuclei together with the motionally narrowed relaxation of the parent page. Where molecular motion is fast enough for that relaxation, it usually averages the coupling between nuclei; ordered media, where residual couplings survive the motion, are the nearest candidate. No sample is known to this page in which both hold.

## Abstract

Spins 3/2, each with its own field gradient (independent quadrupolar relaxation, rates J₀,k, J₁,k, J₂,k per nucleus), a static splitting and an offset, coupled by a real two-body Hamiltonian on any graph. In the parent page's two-qubit reading each nucleus is a qubit pair, its relaxation is dephasing by the strings ZZ, ZX, ZY, XI, YI, and S(ρ) = (⊗_k ZZ_k)·ρᵀ. **Lemma** (any number of nuclei, any graph): if H is real and commutes with ⊗_k ZZ_k, then S·L(J₀)·S⁻¹ = −L(−J₀) − 6·Σ_k(J₁,k + J₂,k): the mirror image of the chain is the chain with the J₀ rates reversed in sign. **Ladder**: if H commutes with every ZZ_k, the part whose bright nuclei form the set B is palindromic about −s_B, s_B = 3·Σ_k(J₁,k + J₂,k) + 3·Σ_{k∈B} J₀,k, up to 2ⁿ centres; for identical rates n + 1, s_m = 3n(J₁ + J₂) + 3m·J₀ with m bright nuclei, the parent's two at n = 1. The Ising coupling and the secular dipolar coupling between nuclei of unequal, non-zero splittings both qualify. **Equal splittings:** the lemma holds and the ladder is exact on K_m, the largest invariant subspace with m bright nuclei, and not forced off it; for identical nuclei on an open chain with one coupling, K_m has dimensions at most 24 + 128 + 24 = 176 of 256 at two nuclei and 64 + 140 + 84 + 16 = 304 of 4096 at three (at two nuclei exact over ℚ(i) at the verifier's parameters, in the live witness; at three nuclei upper bounds over two primes, and so at generic ones; at two nuclei the same over two primes at two rate points). At J₀ = 0 the whole spectrum is palindromic. **The full secular dipolar coupling** breaks S at its eight √3 terms, and at J₀ = 0 the global mirror (about −σ, the only candidate centre) breaks exactly, by F158 at every length and again by its characteristic polynomial at two nuclei. Part of what survives is carried by the kernel of the √3 coupling: on the operators that this coupling annihilates from both sides the generator does not see it, S acts there, and the invariant part of them is palindromic on every rung (proved, any number of nuclei). At two nuclei with identical rates, one offset and an equal splitting that part is all of K₁ and K₂ (both spaces written down) at every parameter off a proper algebraic set, but not on all of it: on the plane q = d ≠ 0, where the diagonal levels that the √3 flip-flop joins are degenerate (an anticrossing once the √3 term acts), the √3 part of the coupling becomes, as an operator, a mode of K₁, paired about its rung but not by S; the part carried by the kernel is half of K₀ at the verifier's parameters; the mirror does more than conjugation on four of its directions only (a float reading at the verifier's parameters); and the identity in K₀ has no partner (proved). **A shared gradient** (two nuclei with equal rates, one real correlation per coherence order): the lemma's form survives with an operator price {K, ·}; with the correlation of the satellite or the double-quantum channel on, the ladder's rungs with no bright nucleus and with two mix, the one with one loses its centre, and at J₀ = 0 the spectrum has none (at the points read), with the ZZ channel's correlation alone and a Hamiltonian that commutes with each ZZ_k the ladder keeps its rungs with no bright nucleus and with one exactly; without a Hamiltonian a sign pattern is conserved, and then at J₀ = 0 one sector of sixteen modes, where the two nuclei's jumps cancel, decays at −6(J₁ + J₂) whatever the correlation.

## Setting

Nucleus k is a qubit pair, levels |3/2⟩, |1/2⟩, |−1/2⟩, |−3/2⟩ = |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩. The spin operators, gated exactly against the spin-3/2 matrices:

  I_z = ZI + ½·IZ,  I_x = (√3/2)·IX + ½(XX + YY),  I_y = (√3/2)·IY + ½(YX − XY),  T₂₀ = (3/√6)·ZZ.

The √3 part of I_x and I_y is the satellite (3/2 ↔ 1/2 and −1/2 ↔ −3/2) and anticommutes with ZZ; the rest is the central transition and commutes with it. Per nucleus: H_k = ω_Q,k·T₂₀ + ω_k·I_z, jumps ZZ at 3J₀/2, ZX and ZY at 3J₁/2, XI and YI at 3J₂/2. Between two nuclei:

- **Ising**, d·I_z⊗I_z: Z strings only. A J or dipolar coupling reduces to it between nuclei all of whose transition frequencies differ by much more than d (another isotope, or sodium nuclei with different shifts and splittings); such nuclei have their own rates as well, which the lemma and the ladder allow.
- **Secular dipolar** between nuclei of one resonance frequency, d·(3I_zI_z − **I**·**I**): 22 strings, of which eight carry √3. The √3 strings are exactly those that anticommute with ZZ₁ZZ₂, the satellite of one nucleus against the central transition of the other. Of the rational strings, IXIX and IYIY anticommute with each ZZ_k (satellite against satellite); the rest commute with both, among them the central-transition flip-flop.
- **Its truncations** when the first-order quadrupolar splittings dominate the coupling, with equal offsets ω₁ = ω₂: the part commuting with ω_Q,1·T₂₀,₁ + ω_Q,2·T₂₀,₂. The √3 strings change one ZZ_k and drop (both splittings non-zero). IXIX and IYIY change both, by −√6(ω_Q,1·z₁ + ω_Q,2·z₂) with z_k = ±1. For **unequal** splittings, |ω_Q,1| ≠ |ω_Q,2|, they drop too, and what is left, the central-transition spin diffusion among them, commutes with every ZZ_k. For **equal** splittings, ω_Q,1 = ω_Q,2, they survive on z₁ = −z₂, as (IXIX + IYIY)·(1 − ZZ₁ZZ₂)/2, which adds ZXZX and ZYZY (for opposite splittings the resonance sits on z₁ = z₂ instead, a case not computed here). A difference of splittings comparable to d is neither truncation. Both forms are gated against the projection onto the energy shells.

All three are real (Hᵀ = H), and so are second-order quadrupolar shifts, which are diagonal and commute with every ZZ_k.

## The lemma: the mirror image is the J₀-reversed chain

Let Q = ⊗_k ZZ_k and S(ρ) = Q·ρᵀ. If H is real and commutes with Q, then

  S·L(J₀)·S⁻¹ = −L(−J₀) − 6·Σ_k(J₁,k + J₂,k),

with L(−J₀) the same chain with every J₀,k reversed in sign, a negative spectral density: not a physical, completely positive generator, the place the mirror image lives. *Proof.* The transpose reverses −i[H, ·] because H is real, and multiplying by Q keeps it because Q commutes with H. A jump P is a Pauli string, so Pᵀ = ±P, and the sign enters twice: (PρP)ᵀ = PρᵀP. With Q·P = ±P·Q, S carries the dissipator γ(PρP − ρ) to itself when P commutes with Q and to −γ(PρP − ρ) − 2γ when it anticommutes. Only ZZ_k commutes with Q; the others, at total rate 3(J₁,k + J₂,k) on nucleus k, are reflected. Reversing the sign of the kept J₀ terms is the same as reversing the J₀,k. The graph of the coupling is never used. ∎

Written as the parent page writes it, S·L·S⁻¹ = −L + 2·L_{J₀} − 6·Σ_k(J₁,k + J₂,k) with L_{J₀} = −3·Σ_k J₀,k·P_k, P_k the projector on the cells bright for nucleus k. The verifier gates the identity with residual exactly 0 at two and three nuclei for the Ising coupling and both truncations, for the Ising coupling also with rates per nucleus, and its control: the √3 part alone breaks it.

## The ladder

Every Pauli jump keeps each cell's brightness. If H commutes with every ZZ_k as well, each P_k commutes with L, the cells split by the set B of bright nuclei, and on the cells of B, L_{J₀} = −3·Σ_{k∈B} J₀,k. There the lemma reads S·L·S⁻¹ = −L − 2s_B:

  **every part with bright set B is palindromic about −s_B, s_B = 3·Σ_k(J₁,k + J₂,k) + 3·Σ_{k∈B} J₀,k.**

For identical rates s_B depends only on m = |B|, s_m = 3n(J₁ + J₂) + 3m·J₀, m = 0, …, n, and the rungs are 3J₀ apart, the J₀ cost of one bright nucleus; with rates per nucleus there are up to 2ⁿ centres. For n = 1 they are the parent page's dark and bright centres, c₁ and c₂. The Ising coupling commutes with every ZZ_k, and so does the secular dipolar coupling between nuclei of unequal, non-zero splittings, because its surviving flip-flop is the central one, which moves no brightness. The verifier gates [L, P_k] = 0 exactly at two and three nuclei, and at two nuclei the characteristic polynomial of every pattern block against its own reflection over two primes, for identical rates and, with the Ising coupling, for rates per nucleus, with the exact controls that a block is not palindromic about a neighbouring centre. The rungs with m ≥ 2 belong to multi-spin modes, which a plain spectrum does not show; reading them would need multiple-quantum or filtered experiments.

## Equal splittings: the ladder where brightness stays sharp

With equal splittings the satellite flip-flop between neighbours is resonant: one nucleus goes outer to inner while the other goes inner to outer. The coupling still commutes with Q, so the lemma holds, but it moves brightness: it changes the number m of bright nuclei by 0 or ±2, P_k no longer commutes with L, and only the parity of m is kept. The rungs are then exact on a part of the spectrum.

Take identical rates here. Let K_m be the largest subspace that L leaves invariant inside the cells with m bright nuclei, W = Σ_k P_k. *If the lemma holds, S maps K_m onto itself and L restricted to K_m is palindromic about −s_m.* Proof: S keeps every P_k, so it keeps the cells with m bright nuclei. From the lemma, L = −S·L·S⁻¹ − 6n(J₁ + J₂) − 6J₀·W, so for x in K_m, L·Sx = S(−Lx) − 2s_m·Sx lies in S·K_m; S·K_m is invariant and inside the m cells, hence contained in K_m, and equal to it by dimension. On it S·L·S⁻¹ = −L − 2s_m. ∎ Off the K_m the rungs are not forced; whether any of those modes pair is not tested here.

For identical nuclei (one splitting, one offset, identical rates) on an open nearest-neighbour chain with one coupling strength, at the verifier's parameters:

| nuclei | K₀ | K₁ | K₂ | K₃ | at most, in all |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 24 of 64 | 128 of 128 | 24 of 64 | | 176 of 256 |
| 3 | 64 of 512 | 140 of 1536 | 84 of 1536 | 16 of 512 | 304 of 4096 |

At two nuclei the live witness reads these dimensions exactly over ℚ(i) at the verifier's parameters; the verifier computes them over two primes, upper bounds on the complex ones at these parameters, and so at generic ones (a dimension can only rise at special points), and at two nuclei they are the same at the rate points (6, 4, 2) and (8, 6, 2); at three nuclei the bounds are all there is. K₁ at two nuclei is exactly the whole block with one bright nucleus, which the paired flip-flop cannot leave. The verifier also reads each K_m palindromic about −s_m over both primes; that follows from the lemma over GF(p) and checks the code, not the proposition. The sharp share is at most 69 % at two nuclei and at most 7.4 % at three. At J₀ = 0 all rungs coincide, and the whole spectrum is palindromic at every length, by the lemma.

## The full secular dipolar coupling

Untruncated, the coupling keeps its eight √3 strings, which anticommute with Q, and S is no symmetry of the form above. This is the Hamiltonian where the splitting does not dominate the coupling, at a cubic site in a solid; the global break below is computed at zero splitting. At J₀ = 0, with J₁, J₂ > 0, the global mirror, about −σ, the only candidate centre, breaks exactly. Every jump is Hermitian and squares to 1, so [F158](../docs/proofs/PROOF_PALINDROME_TWO_END_COUNT.md) applies: the far end of its count lies in the span of the strings that anticommute with every jump, which on each nucleus must read ZZ, so at every length the span is Q alone; Q does not commute with H, the far end is empty, the near end holds the identity, and that holds for any non-zero coefficient of the √3 strings, √3 included. The verifier enumerates that span at two and three nuclei, with the control that it is empty once the J₀ jumps are present, and gives the break a second route at two nuclei: the characteristic polynomial is not palindromic about −σ modulo a prime, which is exact.

What remains: the sharp subspaces have dimensions 10, 12 and 1 at two nuclei, exactly over ℚ(i) at the verifier's parameters (`inspect --root sodiumchain`, see the Verifier line), and at most 12, 16, 0, 0 at three (over two primes). K₀ is not palindromic about −s₀, at every length, and that is proved: the identity lies in K₀ with eigenvalue 0, so −2s₀ would have to be an eigenvalue of L on the cells with no bright nucleus. The real part of an eigenvalue is ⟨x, Dx⟩/⟨x, x⟩ for its eigenvector x, the Hamiltonian part being anti-Hermitian, and on those cells the dissipator D is diagonal in the strings with −2s₀ as its most negative entry, reached on Q alone (gated at two and three nuclei). So the eigenvector would be Q, and Q is no eigenvector, since [H, Q] ≠ 0. K₁ and K₂ are palindromic about their rungs at two nuclei (exact in the witness at the verifier's parameters, over both primes in the verifier), and K₁ reads so at three over both primes (with `--n3`). Part of what pairs inside the K_m is carried by the kernel of the √3 coupling. Write H = H_r + H₃ with H₃ the √3 strings: H_r is real and commutes with Q, H₃ is real and anticommutes with Q. Let V be the operators that H₃ annihilates from both sides, H₃·x = 0 = x·H₃.

*For identical rates, the largest L-invariant subspace U_m inside V and the cells with m bright nuclei is palindromic about −s_m, at every length and on every graph* (with rates per nucleus, the cells of a bright set B about −s_B). Proof: on V, −i[H₃, x] = 0, so L acts there as the generator L_r built with H_r alone, for which the lemma holds. S keeps V: H₃ is real, so x·H₃ = 0 gives H₃·xᵀ = 0, and then H₃·Q·xᵀ = −Q·H₃·xᵀ = 0; in the same way Q·xᵀ·H₃ = Q·(H₃·x)ᵀ = 0. S keeps the cells with m bright nuclei too. So for x in U_m, L·Sx = L_r·Sx = S(−L_r·x) − 2s_m·Sx = S(−Lx) − 2s_m·Sx, and the proof for K_m above goes through word for word. ∎

At two nuclei H₃ has rank 4 of 16, and U_m has dimensions 5, 12 and 1, exactly at the verifier's parameters in the witness and as upper bounds over two primes in the verifier (the premises, H₃ real and anticommuting with Q and its rank, are gated exactly in both). ## A shared gradient

Two nuclei may feel correlated gradients when one motion carries both, a molecule or a complex that tumbles as a whole. Write Γ₀, …, Γ₄ for the five quadrupole strings ZZ, ZX, ZY, XI, YI of one nucleus and Γ_a⊗Γ_a for the same string on both nuclei (ZX⊗ZX = ZXZX). The pair of jumps Γ_a⊗1 and 1⊗Γ_a carries one Kossakowski matrix g·[[1, c_q], [c_q, 1]], equal rates on the two nuclei, g the channel's rate as on the parent page (g₀ = 3J₀/2 for ZZ, g₁ = 3J₁/2 for ZX and ZY, g₂ = 3J₂/2 for XI and YI), with one real correlation per coherence order q of the jump: c₀ for ZZ, c₁ for ZX and ZY, c₂ for XI and YI. A relative azimuth φ of the two gradients about the field turns the correlation of order q by the angle q·φ between ZX and ZY (and XI and YI); that is the generator conjugated by a rotation of nucleus 2 about the field, U = exp(−iφ·I_z,2) (read numerically), so for a Hamiltonian that commutes with I_z,2, as the splitting and the Ising coupling do and the central-transition flip-flop does not, the spectral statements below carry over, with S and the Γ_a⊗Γ_a conjugated by U. What the statements exclude is a cross-correlation with an imaginary part, J₁₂ ≠ J₂₁ (a cross correlation function odd in time), which the equilibrium motion of a time-reversal-invariant gradient does not give, and a correlation that differs between ZX and ZY, which is not secular. The correlated terms of order q ≥ 1 join a transition of one nucleus to one of the other. One c_q for the whole string pair is the secular form under the parent's convention of keeping the splitting whole, where the two nuclei share their Larmor frequency; where the splitting is resolved against the cross-relaxation rate, the parts that join the upper satellite of one nucleus to the lower of the other drop as well, and so do the parts that join the two double-quantum transitions, which the splitting shifts in opposite directions: one c₁ and one c₂ are then no longer the secular form; and between nuclei whose resonance frequencies differ by much more than the cross-relaxation rate the secular approximation drops every q ≥ 1 term and leaves c₀ alone. c = 0 is this page's independent gradients, c = 1 a gradient both nuclei see alike, and every |c_q| ≤ 1 is a valid generator; a rigid body with axially symmetric gradients of equal strength, tumbling isotropically, gives one c = P₂(cos θ) ∈ [−½, 1] for every q, θ the angle between the two gradients' axes, and J₀ is then the largest density (not checked against the literature). At every c ≠ 0 the jumps that diagonalize a channel are Γ_a⊗1 ± 1⊗Γ_a, sums of two strings whose square 2 ± 2·Γ_a⊗Γ_a is not 1, outside F158's class. Computed at two nuclei.

**The price becomes an operator.** For a real H that commutes with Q, exactly,

  S·L·S⁻¹ = −L + 2·L_J₀ − {K, ·},  K = g₁·(4 + 2c₁·(ZXZX + ZYZY)) + g₂·(4 + 2c₂·(XIXI + YIYI)),

where L_J₀ is the whole J₀ part of the generator here, its correlated term included, not the diagonal −3Σ_k J₀,k·P_k of the lemma. K is the number 6(J₁ + J₂), and this the lemma, exactly when g₁c₁ = g₂c₂ = 0; otherwise it is an operator. It is the operator price of [The Mirror under a Shared Field](THE_MIRROR_UNDER_A_SHARED_FIELD.md), K = Σc†c, there diagonal in the Z basis and here a number plus four commuting two-nucleus strings; the step is that of [The Pairing Condition](THE_PAIRING_CONDITION.md) for Hermitian jumps that anticommute with the carrier, composed with the transpose as F166 composes it through Π_Z, which needs the real symmetric Kossakowski matrix and one transpose sign per channel. Gated with the offset and the splitting, with an Ising coupling and the central-transition flip-flop (the part of I_x⊗I_x + I_y⊗I_y between the central transitions) added, and with the strings of the equal-splitting truncation, where it is the lemma's −L_H for the Hamiltonian part again; a √3 string of the full dipolar coupling, which anticommutes with Q, breaks it.

**The mirrors break where c₁ or c₂ is on.** A correlated term Γ_a⊗1·ρ·1⊗Γ_a with a ≥ 1 changes the outer/inner bit of nucleus 1 on the ket and of nucleus 2 on the bra at once, so with c₁ or c₂ non-zero (gated with each alone and with all on) the generator commutes with neither nucleus's bright projector: the cells with no bright nucleus and with two mix, while those with one stay an invariant block, which loses its centre (not palindromic about its mean, exact: at c = 1/2 in every channel at J₀ = 2 and at J₀ = 0, with c₁ or c₂ alone, and with the equal-splitting truncation and an Ising coupling). At J₀ = 0 and the verifier's rates the whole spectrum is then not palindromic about its mean (exact over two primes at c = 1/2 in every channel, with c₁ alone and with c₂ alone, and with the equal-splitting truncation, an Ising coupling, offset and splitting), while at c = 0 it is, as the lemma says; a centre is always the spectrum's mean, so this is no centre at all. With c₀ alone, the case of nuclei of different resonance frequencies, K stays a number (the channels add, and the ZZ channel does not enter K) and both bright projectors commute with the generator. The correlated ZZ term is diagonal and differs from zero only on the cells where both nuclei are bright, there with both signs: on the blocks with no bright nucleus and with one the generator is exactly the one at c = 0, so the lemma's ladder holds there, and the rung with two is not palindromic (exact, at c₀ = 1/2 with an Ising coupling, offset and splitting). Gated with equal rates and frequencies; for two different nuclei the same argument applies, ungated.

**What is conserved.** The four strings Γ_a⊗Γ_a, a = 1 to 4, are real, symmetric and commute with each other and with Q, and their product is Q. Their joint eigenstates are sixteen states, one for each pattern s of their four signs. A jump of channel a ≥ 1, on either nucleus, commutes with its own Γ_a⊗Γ_a and anticommutes with the other three; the J₀ jump ZZ anticommutes with all four. Either way a jump changes the signs of ket and bra alike, so without a Hamiltonian the generator conserves t = s_ket ⊙ s_bra, the four signs in which ket and bra agree or disagree: sixteen invariant sectors of dimension 16, at every correlation and every rate, and S keeps each of them. A real Hamiltonian in the algebra of the Γ_a⊗Γ_a (Q, their product, among it) keeps the conservation, among them the satellite flip-flop (IXIX + IYIY)(1 − Q)/2 of the equal-splitting truncation, though that truncation exists only where a splitting dominates and the splitting breaks the conservation; the splitting, an Ising coupling and the central-transition flip-flop break it, and so does a complex cross-correlation. The common offset breaks it too, but harmlessly: with one correlation per q it commutes with the dissipator, so it turns the modes and moves no rate.

**Sixteen modes the correlation does not touch.** On the sector t = (−,−,−,−), where ket and bra differ in every sign, the two nuclei's sandwiches cancel channel by channel for a ≥ 1: A₁·ρ·A₁ = −A₂·ρ·A₂ and A₁·ρ·A₂ = −A₂·ρ·A₁ there, so the local sum and the correlated sum both vanish (with the two nuclei's rates equal). What is left of the dissipator is −½{K, ·}, there the number −2(g₁ + g₁ + g₂ + g₂) = −6(J₁ + J₂). At J₀ = 0 and without a Hamiltonian the sector is therefore an eigenspace of L at −6(J₁ + J₂), at every correlation: the rate the independent gradients give, which at c = 0 and J₀ = 0 is the lemma's global centre −σ, unmoved by sharing them. It is self-paired under S, trivially, every mode on that centre. With a real Hamiltonian in the algebra of the Γ_a⊗Γ_a the sector stays invariant and the modes gain frequencies and keep that rate; the common offset alone, which is outside that algebra, turns them inside the dissipator's eigenspace at −6(J₁ + J₂), to which the sector belongs; the sum of the two is not claimed. At J₀ ≠ 0 the sector is no eigenspace and not palindromic (exact at J₀ = 2, c = 1/2; at c = 0 it pairs, evidence over two primes): the ZZ sandwiches carry |s⟩⟨−s| to |−s⟩⟨s| inside the sector, and with c₀ ≠ 0 the correlated J₀ term is not a number there. At J₀ = 0 the other fifteen sectors have no centre at c = 1/2 and c = 1 (exact, at the verifier's rates). Under correlated Z noise the price operator is diagonal, and a block keeps its mirror, about a centre shared with its image, where K takes one value on each side; here the sector whose price is a number is one where the two nuclei's jumps cancel.

**What it says about a sample.** For a sample under a shared gradient with a real coupling that commutes with Q, the lemma's form survives with K in place of a number. Between identical nuclei with an equal splitting the ladder's rungs with no bright nucleus and with two mix, the rung with one loses its centre, and at J₀ = 0 the spectrum has none; between nuclei of different resonance frequencies only c₀ survives the secular approximation, K is a number, and the ladder keeps its rungs with no bright nucleus and with one, losing the rung with two (gated with equal rates and frequencies, the loss at one point). The sixteen correlation-immune modes are a property of the dissipator: nuclei close enough to share one gradient are usually dipolar-coupled, and the coupling's Ising part and its central-transition flip-flop break the conserved pattern; a splitting does as well, and J₀ = 0 needs a motion that leaves the gradient's component along the field unmodulated, which the rigid tumbling above does not. For a rotation about the field, the motion that gives J₀ = 0, the two gradients' relative azimuth enters as the rotation of nucleus 2 above, which the splitting and the Ising coupling commute with and the flip-flop of dipolar-coupled nuclei does not. They are an exact statement about the generator, not a prediction for a sample.

## A reading

The Absorption Theorem prices a mode by its portfolio, the share it holds in each channel. S reverses the share in every channel it reflects and keeps the share in the channel it cannot see, J₀, whose jump ZZ_k is S's own carrier. A mode and its image therefore have rates summing to the reflected rates plus the kept channel's share counted twice. Where that share is sharp, a whole number of bright nuclei, the kept channel adds a constant and the mirror lives on with a shifted centre: the ladder. Where the coupling blurs it, the share differs from mode to mode, and the mirror keeps its element but loses its centre; its image is then a mode of the J₀-reversed chain, not of this one. Where the element itself breaks, at the √3 strings, that is gone as well. This is a reading of the lemma, not a further result.

## What this does not show

No sodium data were compared. The model is the parent page's Redfield picture at infinite temperature, secular in the Larmor frequency with each nucleus's splitting kept whole, with an independent gradient on every nucleus outside the section on a shared one, plus a static coupling; that the two coexist in a real sample is assumed, and no such sample is named. The independent jumps per nucleus are the local form of the master equation, valid for d·τ_c ≪ 1 with τ_c the correlation time of the gradient, and the jumps are each nucleus's own, not those of the coupled eigenbasis, which would spread them over the nuclei. Outside the model and outside F158's class, each of these would in general break the ladder or the lemma, and none is computed: the cross-correlated dynamic frequency shift between the nuclei; dipolar relaxation from the motion that modulates the coupling, and its cross-correlation with the quadrupolar relaxation, whose jumps (I_z⊗I_z-like, flip-flop-like) are sums of strings; relaxation through I_z or I_± from other spins, chemical-shift anisotropy or paramagnetic centres, again sums of strings. A shared or partly correlated gradient is computed at two nuclei only, with a real cross-correlation one per coherence order, its sectors without a Hamiltonian or with one built from the conserved strings, and the absence of a centre also with a splitting and an Ising coupling (the section on it); sharp subspaces inside its sectors are not computed. The lemma and the ladder are proved for any number of nuclei and any graph; the dimensions of K_m and the readings under the full coupling are computed at two and three nuclei, for identical nuclei on an open chain, at the rates and couplings of the verifier, and at two nuclei also on the plane q = d and, where the sharp subspaces grow, at random points over GF(p). The plane q = d is fine-tuned: it needs the satellite-to-central spacing equal to twice the dipolar coefficient with the matching sign, a ratio that turns with orientation since the two tensors differ, and near it the eigenoperator statements hold only to first order in q − d, against the smaller of d and the rates; where a sample sits relative to it is not estimated. The literature on dipolar couplings between quadrupolar nuclei was not searched. For isotropic motion J₀ is the largest density and the statements at J₀ = 0 are empty; they hold for motion that only turns the gradient about the field, where J₀ = 0 (the parent page).

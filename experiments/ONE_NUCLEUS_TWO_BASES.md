# One Nucleus, Two Bases

**Date:** 2026-11-18
**Authors:** Thomas Wicht, Claude (Opus 5.5)
**Status:** One published sodium data set read against the relations among the relaxation rates of one sample. The relations and the statements about the model are exact (except that which strings are reached from equilibrium is read at points of the densities); the comparison with the data is arithmetic on the printed numbers, with the error model stated where it is used.
**Script:** [`sodium_one_nucleus_two_bases.py`](../simulations/sodium_one_nucleus_two_bases.py) (34 checks, exact in sympy except where it compares printed numbers).

The relaxation of a sodium nucleus, a spin 3/2, has one generator and two natural bases. NMR writes it in the irreducible tensors T_l,p, the basis of what a pulse sequence does: a hard pulse keeps the rank l and mixes the orders p within it, an offset and a phase cycle see the order, and the receiver detects one order, p = −1. [The Sodium Nucleus and Its Two Mirrors](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md) writes it in sixteen Pauli strings on two qubits, the levels |3/2⟩, |1/2⟩, |−1/2⟩, |−3/2⟩ labelled |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩ as Souza et al. do: the basis of what the relaxation does. Its relaxation is dephasing by five strings, the jumps ZZ at the rate 3J₀/2, ZX and ZY at 3J₁/2, XI and YI at 3J₂/2, with J₀, J₁, J₂ the spectral densities. At zero splitting each string decays at a single rate, twice the summed rate of the jumps it anticommutes with; at generic densities the strings that share a rate form a string class (IX and IY, for one), and a cell is one matrix element |i⟩⟨j|. The script builds the generator from the strings and from the tensor jumps T₂,ₘ and compares the two entry by entry. The sodium page sets the two bases side by side in [its section on the irreducible tensors](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#the-irreducible-tensors-in-strings): at zero splitting ten of the sixteen directions agree, and six are turned between two string classes by Hubbard's weights 4/5 : 1/5 and 3/5 : 2/5.

This page is about where the two bases meet a measurement. An experiment selects a tensor; the decays it records are the rates of the string classes that tensor lies on, and the rates of one sample are tied to each other because a few jumps at three densities make all of them. A tie between two rates of one sample is something a data set can break. The page writes the ties out, says which of them a static splitting leaves, and reads one published data set against them: a sodium sample whose density matrix was followed by tomography.

## The ties among the rates

NMR writes a rate as C·J, a constant C of the quadrupolar coupling times a spectral density. The J of the sodium page absorb C; in its units the same rates read with C = 3, a normalization of the J and not the physical constant. In those units, at zero splitting and offset, the sixteen strings decay at eight distinct non-zero rates built from J₀, J₁, J₂, so five relations tie them. Write R₁(1/5) = r(IZ) = 6J₁ and R₁(4/5) = r(ZI) = 6J₂ for the two longitudinal rates, named by their weights in the longitudinal signal as the sodium page does; R₂f = r(IX) = 3(J₀ + J₁) and R₂s = 3(J₁ + J₂) for the transverse rates of weight 3/5 and 2/5 (fast and slow when J₀ > J₂; the sodium page's R₂′ and R₂″). [The Nucleus as a Key](THE_NUCLEUS_AS_A_KEY.md) counts the five relations as the parity checks of a linear code and names two; written out, with the decays they tie:

| relation in strings | in NMR's decays |
|---|---|
| r(XX) = (r(IZ) + r(ZI))/2 | the central transition decays at the mean of the two longitudinal rates, R₂s = (R₁(1/5) + R₁(4/5))/2, and so does the triple quantum, which shares its string class |
| r(ZZ) = r(IZ) + r(ZI) | quadrupolar order decays at the sum of the two longitudinal rates |
| r(ZX) − r(IX) = r(ZI) | the rank-2 single-quantum mode T₂,±₁ decays faster than R₂f by R₁(4/5) |
| r(XI) − r(XZ) = r(IZ) | the two double-quantum modes, T₂,±₂ (the sum of the two double-quantum cells) and T₃,±₂ (their difference), differ by R₁(1/5) |
| r(IX) − r(XZ) = (r(IZ) − r(ZI))/2 | R₂f less the rank-3 double-quantum rate is half of R₁(1/5) − R₁(4/5) |

The five are independent, and with the three densities they account for all eight rates. Each row is an identity among the standard rate expressions of Hubbard and of Jaccard, Wimperis and Bodenhausen (recalled). The first is contained in the consistency check of the paper read below; whether the others are used as checks, the search for this page did not reach. What this page adds is the count and the reading in string classes. The first is the identity the sodium page uses as a model check in [What a sodium measurement can read](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#what-a-sodium-measurement-can-read); the second is row 7 of [its handshake with NMR](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#the-handshake-with-nmr). They rest on two different things. The second, third and fourth hold for any rates g of the five jumps: they test which jumps act. The first and the fifth, written on XX and on IX against XZ, hold exactly when g(ZX) − g(ZY) = g(XI) − g(YI); on XY and YX the first needs the opposite sign, so on every string of the class, as a decay needs, they hold exactly when the rates within each pair T₂,±ₘ are equal. With ZY at a rate other than ZX's and XI and YI equal, both break.

**What a splitting leaves.** The sodium page calls dark what the slow density J₀ cannot see, the populations, the central transition and the triple quantum, and bright the satellites and double quanta it reaches. A static splitting leaves the dark rates as they are. It moves the bright ones. On resonance the two satellite cells form a closed pair with eigenvalues −c₂ ± √(9J₂² − 4q²), and the two double-quantum cells one with J₁ in place of J₂ (an offset shifts them by a frequency), where c₂ = 3(J₀ + J₁ + J₂) and q is the coefficient of ZZ in the Hamiltonian (this page's normalization of the splitting; the sodium page's ω_Q and the paper's ω_Q below differ from it by fixed factors). For a splitting small against a pair's coupling the rates move at order q²; at [the bright pairs' exceptional point](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#the-bright-pairs-exceptional-point) they meet at −c₂. Past both points (2q > 3J₂ for the satellites, 2q > 3J₁ for the double quanta) both eigenvalues of each pair have the real part −c₂ exactly: the satellites and the double quanta decay at the one rate c₂. Far past them lies what NMR calls the resolved or secular limit, where the satellite lines separate as well ([row 10 of the handshake](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#the-handshake-with-nmr) keeps the two thresholds apart). That a resolved satellite line or double-quantum coherence relaxes at C(J₀ + J₁ + J₂) is the textbook secular result (recalled, not looked up; the sodium page's [Prior art](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#neighbours-and-prior-art) names the satellites' part of it as the standard picture). The strings give a reading of it: a satellite cell lies half on the string class of IX (rate 3(J₀ + J₁)) and half on that of ZX (rate 3(J₀ + J₁ + 2J₂)), a double-quantum cell half on XZ and half on XI, and −c₂, the real part of each cell's diagonal element, is the mean of its two classes. So in a sample split far beyond its rates the first two relations survive, and the other three give way to one equality: satellites and double quanta decay at the one rate c₂, which fixes J₀ once J₁ and J₂ are read from the dark cells. The Key page reads the same J₀ from the pair sums 6(J₀ + J₁ + J₂), which hold at every splitting ([its paragraph on a splitting](THE_NUCLEUS_AS_A_KEY.md#the-key)); past the exceptional point each rate is half that sum.

## A sample read by tomography

R. Auccaise, J. Teles, R. S. Sarthour, T. J. Bonagamba, I. S. Oliveira and E. R. deAzevedo, *A study of the relaxation dynamics in a quadrupolar NMR system using Quantum State Tomography*, J. Magn. Reson. 192, 17 (2008), studied ²³Na in the nematic phase of a lyotropic liquid crystal (21.3 % sodium dodecyl sulfate, 3.6 % decanol, 75.1 % D₂O), oriented by the field of a 9.4 T magnet, with a quadrupolar splitting ν_Q = (16 700 ± 70) Hz at 24 °C. They label the four levels as the same two qubits as here and number them 0 to 3 from |3/2⟩. For the uniform superposition of the four they followed all sixteen elements of the deviation density matrix (the part that differs from the identity) by tomography; for |00⟩ to |11⟩ and the cat state (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, the populations. They read the elements after evolution periods that are multiples of 2π/ω_Q, since their refocusing pulse does not refocus the quadrupolar evolution, so a cell's oscillation at the splitting does not show and its envelope does. In their convention the quadrupolar Hamiltonian is (ω_Q/6)(3I_z² − 15/4) = (ω_Q/2)·ZZ, so q = ω_Q/2 and 2q = 2π·ν_Q ≈ 1.05·10⁵ s⁻¹.

Their equations fit single exponentials to combinations of elements: the central cell ρ₁₂ and the triple-quantum cell ρ₀₃ with one rate a₃ = C(J₁ + J₂); the sum of the two satellite cells with a₁ = C(J₀ + J₁); the sum of the two double-quantum cells with a₂ = C(J₀ + J₂); and three population combinations, which are the strings ZI, IZ and ZZ of this page, with 2CJ₂, 2CJ₁ and 2C(J₁ + J₂). Then, in their words: "the J₁ and J₂ values can be compared with that ones obtained from (26)–(28) to check the consistency of the experimental data with the adopted relaxation model." Their Table 1, for the uniform superposition, in units of 10⁻⁹ s with C = (1.2 ± 0.1)·10¹⁰ s⁻²:

| | from the coherences | from the populations | average |
|---|---|---|---|
| J₀ | 14 ± 1 | | 14 ± 1 |
| J₁ | 4 ± 1 | 3.8 ± 0.4 | 3.9 ± 0.7 |
| J₂ | 3.4 ± 0.5 | 3.6 ± 0.3 | 3.5 ± 0.4 |

Whatever a₁ and a₂ are, the authors' inversion gives J₁ + J₂ from the coherences as a₃/C, the rate of the central transition and the triple quantum. So the J₁ + J₂ part of their comparison is the first relation: the shared rate of the central transition and the triple quantum against the mean of the two longitudinal rates, 2CJ₁ and 2CJ₂. C cancels in it. Both columns give 7.4, with errors of 1.1 and 0.5, the printed ones added in quadrature as if independent (the coherence values come from one inversion of three separately fitted rates, and C's 8 % may sit in both columns' errors). **The mean-rate identity holds in this sample within the printed errors.** None of its three rates depends on the splitting, so the strong splitting of the sample does not enter. Their Table 2 averages six prepared states, one of them the uniform superposition of Table 1: J₁ = 3.4 ± 0.4 and J₂ = 3.7 ± 0.3, so 7.1 ± 0.5, against the coherences' 7.4 ± 1.1. Against the agarose data of [One Sodium Pool or More](SODIUM_ONE_POOL_OR_MORE.md) and [Sodium in Agarose, Layer by Layer](SODIUM_IN_AGAROSE_LAYER_BY_LAYER.md) the published fits break the identity; those pages say where and on which readings it holds there.

So this data set reads the first relation in part, the shared rate against the mean. Two ties are not read. That the central transition and the triple quantum decay alike is a repeat within one string class, not one of the five, but a tie a data set could break; the shared fit a₃ assumes it, and the paper plots the two cells separately (its Fig. 4a), not digitized here. The second relation, quadrupolar order, is assumed and not read: the paper's Eq. (27), the ZZ combination, gives it no rate of its own. For the uniform superposition that combination is "almost constant" and only Eqs. (26) and (28) enter the populations column; for the other states the paper says only that "the same procedure" was performed, so Eq. (27) entered, if at all, with the rate 2C(J₁ + J₂). The cat state's populations start as ZZ alone; in every prepared state the Eq. (27) combination isolates ZZ, beside the recoveries of ZI and IZ toward the Zeeman equilibrium. Reading the relation needs that decay fitted with a free rate. The other three relations, in this split sample, give way to the one bright rate taken up next.

## Their satellite and double-quantum equations, read from here

**At zero splitting.** The paper presents its Eqs. (8) to (17) as a summary of the solutions in its ref. [43], Jaccard, Wimperis and Bodenhausen, whose title restricts them to spins 3/2 in isotropic phase (not read here, so where the assignment below enters is not known). Its satellite equation is the generator's: the sum of the two satellite cells decays at 3(J₀ + J₁) in the units here, the paper's C(J₀ + J₁). Its two double-quantum rates, C(J₀ + J₂) and C(J₀ + 2J₁ + J₂), are the generator's as a set; their assignment is not. The sum of the two double-quantum cells is σ₊ ⊗ I, the class of XI, T₂,₂ ∝ I₊², and decays at 3(J₀ + 2J₁ + J₂); the rate 3(J₀ + J₂), the paper's C(J₀ + J₂), belongs to their difference, σ₊ ⊗ Z, the class of XZ, T₃,₂, as the sodium page has it in [its section on the tensors](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#the-irreducible-tensors-in-strings). In the levels' standard phases the satellite cells are coupled with the sign of +3J₂ and the double-quantum cells with that of −3J₁; the paper's equations give the two pairs the same sign. A pair's two cells combine into a decay mode only with relative sign ±1, and over every choice of the levels' signs no basis gives the satellite sum the rate 3(J₀ + J₁) and the double-quantum sum the rate 3(J₀ + J₂) together. At extreme narrowing, J₀ = J₁ = J₂, the check is quick: I₊² is rank 2 and must decay with quadrupolar order, and 3(J₀ + 2J₁ + J₂) does, where 3(J₀ + J₂) is the rate of ranks 1 and 3. The paper's section on the two qubits carries the same assignment, giving qubit A, whose coherence is the double-quantum sum, the rate C(J₀ + J₂). The equations of Souza et al. ([arXiv:1105.2539](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.2539)), from which the sodium page takes its rates, carry the paper's assignment and cite it; the rates as a set are the generator's, their assignment to the double-quantum cells' sum and difference is the reverse.

**In the split sample.** Here neither assignment applies. The sample is split far past the bright pairs' exceptional point: 2q ≈ 1.05·10⁵ s⁻¹ against the couplings of each pair, C·J₂ ≈ 42 s⁻¹ and C·J₁ ≈ 47 s⁻¹ from Table 1's averages (3J₂ and 3J₁ in the units here). There all four bright cells decay at one rate, c₂ in the units here and C(J₀ + J₁ + J₂) in the paper's, the diagonal element a secular approximation in the splitting keeps, the approximation the authors invoke to neglect cross terms for the coherences. Read so, a₁ = a₂ = C(J₀ + J₁ + J₂). For the uniform superposition, with equal amplitudes on the two cells of each pair, both readings give a single exponential, so the shape of the decay cannot tell them apart; the rate can, and it is what the inversion turns into J₀. The paper's two rates agree within its errors: its coherences column gives a₁/C = J₀ + J₁ = 18 and a₂/C = J₀ + J₂ = 17.4, a difference of 0.6 ± 1.1 in units of 10⁻⁹ s (the printed errors in quadrature), consistent with zero. Its section on the two qubits states the same two rates as "(210 ± 10) s⁻¹" (C(J₀ + J₁) = 216 s⁻¹ and C(J₀ + J₂) = 209 s⁻¹): "this difference could not be detected". A zero-splitting model with the generator's assignment would put a₂/C above a₁/C by J₁ + J₂ = 7.4, eight units from the measured −0.6: about six and a half errors in quadrature with the prediction's own error included, and more than three however the one-digit values "4 ± 1" are read and however the two coherence errors, which come from one inversion, combine. So these data agree with the split reading and not with that model. The paper's own form puts C(J₁ − J₂) between a₁ and a₂, and with J₁ − J₂ = 0.2 ± 0.5 from the populations column, consistent with zero, that is not told apart from one rate here.

**What the split reading does to J₀.** With a₁ = a₂ = C(J₀ + J₁ + J₂), the paper's inversion of a₁, a₂, a₃ returns J₁′ = J₂′ = (J₁ + J₂)/2 and J₀′ = J₀ + (J₁ + J₂)/2. The J₀ = 14 ± 1 of their Table 1 would then be J₀ plus about 3.7, a J₀ near 10.3, still well above J₁ and J₂. Their slow correlation time, (120 ± 10) ns, comes from J₀ − J₁ through their Eq. (32) with an order parameter of 0.3; read so, their J₀ − J₁ is approximately the true J₀, and the true J₀ − J₁ is about 6.5 rather than about 10, which would lower that time roughly in proportion. It was not recomputed here. Within the split reading and up to the fit errors (J₀ near 10.3 ± 1), the J₀ near 10.3 and the J₀ − J₁ near 6.5 are both upper bounds: a spread of the splitting across the sample, which the refocusing pulse does not refocus, dephases the four bright cells alike and the central transition and the triple quantum not at all, so it enters a₁ and a₂ equally, as further apparent J₀ (a Lorentzian distribution of ν_Q with a half-width at half maximum of about 7 Hz adds as much as the split reading's (J₁ + J₂)/2).

This is our view from the generator, not a refit: the cells' decays were not reanalysed, and a spread of the splitting (above), or relaxation beyond the quadrupolar one, would add to what is said here. What the strings add to the textbook rate is a reading of it, the diagonal element as the mean of the two string classes each cell lies on half and half, and what follows for this data set: the measured agreement of a₁ and a₂ is what one rate gives, and, read so, the J₀ taken from them carries half of J₁ + J₂.

## Open

- Quadrupolar order is prepared through a splitting, so the relation r(ZZ) = r(IZ) + r(ZI) needs an oriented sample, where it still holds. In Auccaise et al.'s data its decay is in the populations of every prepared state, isolated by the paper's Eq. (27) combination (the cat state's populations, Fig. 6e, start as it alone), and was fitted with no rate of its own; it would need a free one. The search for this page did not reach the anisotropic multiple-quantum and Jeener-Broekaert literature (Furó and Halle; van der Maarel; Wimperis and co-workers), where such rates are standard tools; Quist, Halle and Furó (J. Chem. Phys. 96, 3875 (1992), the paper's ref. [54]) studied nuclear spin relaxation in the same phase and may hold an outside check on its J₀ (not read).
- The central transition and the triple quantum measured as two rates on one sample; Auccaise et al.'s Fig. 4a shows the two cells separately and was not digitized here.
- The three bright relations. Two of them need the rank-2 modes T₂,±₁ and T₂,±₂, which no hard pulse, the only kind at exactly zero splitting where the transitions coincide, and no relaxation reaches from the high-temperature equilibrium (in the sodium page's model, with its dynamic frequency shift or without it); they need a residual splitting well below each pair's coupling (3J₂ for the satellites, 3J₁ for the double quanta), where the relations hold up to terms of order q². The third, R₂f against the rank-3 double quantum: R₂f is read from any transverse decay, and the rank-3 double quantum is reached from equilibrium in an isotropic sample away from extreme narrowing (read at one point of the densities; at J₀ = J₁ = J₂ the relaxation keeps the rank, only rank 1 is reached, and the relation reads 0 = 0). Sobieski et al. (J. Chem. Phys. 125, 244509 (2006)) report longitudinal recovery with single-, double- and triple-quantum decays on one partly ordered sample; Eliav and Navon (J. Magn. Reson. 130, 63 (1998)) measure a triple-quantum relaxation time directly and read its excess as a dipolar contribution beside the quadrupolar one, which bears on where the identity's model ends. Both are known here by their abstracts only.
- In a split sample, satellites and double quanta at one rate c₂ against a sample where J₁ and J₂ differ, so that the paper's printed form, which puts C(J₁ − J₂) between them, is told apart from one rate.
- The bright pairs' exceptional point against a measurement, as [the sodium page's Open list](THE_SODIUM_NUCLEUS_TWO_MIRRORS.md#open) states it; it lies at a splitting of the order of the rates, the intermediate regime of partly ordered samples such as cartilage and tendon (recalled).

# The frozen band: what the divisor does when the chain stops being Heisenberg

*2026-07-25. [F140](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md) said the frozen root −4γ̄ sits in two corner blocks, (1,1) and (N−1,N−1), with its gamma-fold partner 4γ̄ − 2σ in the other two (σ = Σ_l γ_l is the total dephasing rate, so on the locus σ = Nγ̄), and that nothing else carries either. Typing it as a live object put the same question to the XY chain, which the proof's census had never been run on, and the answer was that the confinement is not the divisor's. On XY the same root is carried, at the same depth, by a whole diagonal band of blocks. This note states the band, gives the reason its edge sits where it does, finds what decides whether the band exists at all, and buries two candidates for what fills it. The number is the count of balanced pairs, times whatever room a block leaves beside the pair; switching the coupling off makes the two factors visible, and turning it back on is what removes the room. What fills the band, on the open chain, is an exact ladder: adding one excitation to the ket and one to the bra at the same site commutes with the whole Liouvillian, and the frozen subspaces along one diagonal become a single sl(2) module seeded at F140's corner. That answers the collapse question for the chain as a floor, puts the block count on the diagonal on an argument rather than on a census, meaning WHICH diagonal blocks are occupied and not the exact ⌊N/2⌋ in each of them, which stays a floor for all N and an equality one N at a time. It also corrects what this note previously said the Heisenberg chain was telling us. The matching ceiling is no longer a census: an exact rank over GF(p) bounds the multiplicity from above, which is the direction the floor cannot supply and a float nullity never could, so for one N at a time the ceiling is settled rather than observed. What is still missing is the argument that does all N at once, and it now has a smaller home: the uniform profile, where the R₉₀ locus is not needed and the model is integrable. The floor for the two off-diagonal ladders stays measured, and is now one number rather than a ladder.*

## The law

Take the mirror-balanced locus of [F91](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md), the R₉₀ locus (every reflection pair of site rates carrying the same total, γ_l + γ_{R(l)} = 2γ̄) and the XY chain, H = (J/2)·Σ_b (X_bX_{b+1} + Y_bY_{b+1}). Label the joint-popcount blocks (p, q) by the popcounts of the ket and bra indices. A block **carries** λ when λ is an eigenvalue of that block, and the multiplicity meant throughout is the dimension of ker(L₍p,q₎ − λ), the eigenspace and not the algebraic multiplicity; the two part company at the exceptional couplings the ceiling section names, and it is the eigenspace that every count here reads. Then

**the blocks carrying λ = −4γ̄ are the band |p − q| ∈ {0, 2}, less the two one-cell blocks (0,0) and (N,N), and every block in that band carries multiplicity ⌊N/2⌋ at N = 4 through 7, where the census runs, and at the couplings tested.**

That last qualifier is not decoration, and this note would be making the very mistake its sibling commit was written to correct if it dropped it: ⌊N/2⌋ is a generic depth, not an inventory. The corner block (1,1) is itself in the band, and at J = 0 it carries twice that, semisimply, which is F140's own corollary. The full census below is read at J = 3/4; J = 3/2 is a spot check, at the band edge and on the transport, not a second census.

The two directions do not stand on the same footing, and neither is simply "measured" any more. Nothing OUTSIDE the band carries: that is measurement, and the section below removes the only mechanism that could have let it, which is not the same as proving the null. Everything INSIDE the band does carry, and for the blocks on the diagonal that now has an argument: the ⌊N/2⌋ is a floor there, at every coupling, from the ladder below and F140's two proved ends. The exact value on the inside is settled one N at a time, by an exact rank read against that floor, for generic coupling and profile; the section on the ceiling states exactly how far that reaches and what it does not reach. The floor for the two off-diagonal ladders is the piece that stays measured. Scope of the measurement: a full census over every (p, q) at N = 4, 5, 6, 7, and band-edge probes at N = 8, 9, 10.

**Notation, because this note borrows from two directions.** Cells here are written (A, B) with A and B index SETS, so that the disagreement set AΔB has a size worth talking about; the proof document writes (a, b) for the single-site case and calls that set S. The **one-cell blocks** (0,0) and (N,N) excluded above are the vacuum and full popcount blocks, not the divisor's four corner blocks, which are always named as such. The pair (p, q) is written here as (ket popcount, bra popcount); the proof document lists the block the other way round, and nothing in either depends on it, since the band and the fold image are both transpose-symmetric. The fermion ladder operators below are d_a, d†_a, and the site distance the proof writes d_c appears here only inside the exponent J^{2d}. The transport map is written Ξ, not T, because the proof already spends T on the bordered cofactor matrix of its Section 8.

Three things come with it.

- **The count is closed:** 3(N − 1) blocks, since the band has (N+1) diagonal blocks and 2(N−1) off by two, less the two excluded one-cell blocks. Measured 9, 12, 15, 18 at N = 4, 5, 6, 7. On the open chain the three ladders below have exactly N − 1 rungs each, because the block beyond either end is a one-cell block and cannot carry. That the N − 1 rungs of the DIAGONAL ladder are all occupied is then argued rather than measured; for the other 2(N − 1) blocks the occupancy is still a measurement, because the floor there is.
- **The depth is constant.** Every carrying block carries the whole ⌊N/2⌋ and never a part of it, across the range the census reaches: from the 6-dimensional (0,2) block at N = 4 to the 1225-dimensional (3,3) at N = 7. It is the corner's number, in every block the census reached.
- **The gamma-fold partner sits on the image.** The blocks carrying 4γ̄ − 2σ are exactly the image of the band under one one-sided fold p ↦ N − p. Written out that is p + q ∈ {N−2, N, N+2} **less the images of the two excluded one-cell blocks**, namely (N,0) and (0,N); without that exclusion the set would hold 3N − 1 blocks instead of the 3(N − 1) counted above, and the two extra ones do not carry. One law, folded once, as in [F140](../docs/ANALYTICAL_FORMULAS.md)'s own corner census.

On the Heisenberg chain only the four corner blocks carry anything at all, two of them at −4γ̄ and two at the fold partner. The proof document's Section 5 census is a Heisenberg census, and it is now scoped as one.

## Two traps on the way to that statement

Worth recording, because both would have passed a smaller check.

**"p + q even" was never a second condition.** p + q even and p − q even are the same statement, so the parity is already inside the bandwidth. Writing them as two conditions makes the law look like a conjunction of a parity and a distance when it is only a distance.

**Bandwidth 2 and bandwidth ⌊N/2⌋ agree up to N = 7.** Since |p − q| is even on the band, "≤ 2" and "≤ 3" are the same predicate, and ⌊N/2⌋ is 2 or 3 for N ≤ 7. The first N that can tell them apart is 8, where ⌊N/2⌋ = 4 would admit |p − q| = 4. It does not: at N = 8, 9 and 10, in three profile-and-coupling combinations each (two R₉₀ locus profiles differing only in their offset vector δ, kept unlettered here because A and B are already this note's cell indices: the first profile at J = 3/4 and at J = 3/2, the second at J = 3/4), the |p − q| = 4 blocks carry exactly nothing while |p − q| = 2 carries ⌊N/2⌋. At N = 8, which is the N the discriminator turns on, that is every |p − q| = 4 block; at N = 9 and 10 the blocks in the middle of that diagonal are past the size cap, and the probe reads the two ends of it. Three N and one rival is the whole reason the discriminator was run at all.

## Why the edge sits where it does

This half has a reason. Read what it delivers before reading it: it shows that the ONE known freezing mechanism is unavailable outside the band, which is why the edge sits at two rather than anywhere else. It does not prove that −4γ̄ is absent out there for every coupling; that absence is measured. Mechanism-unavailability and a null are different statements, and only the first is argued here.

The ingredient is already half-written in the proof document, which notes that the recentering making the rate operator odd depends on the size of a cell's disagreement set.

Carry it through. For a cell (A, B), the rate diagonal of M = L_block + 4γ̄ is −2·Σ_{l ∈ AΔB} γ_l + 4γ̄, and the cell mirror τQ: (A, B) ↦ (R(B), R(A)) sends the disagreement set AΔB to R(AΔB). On the locus Σ_{l ∈ R(S)} γ_l = 2γ̄·|S| − Σ_{l ∈ S} γ_l, so oddness of the rate part reduces, after the sums cancel, to

  rate(A, B) + rate(τQ(A, B)) = 4γ̄·(2 − |AΔB|),  which vanishes exactly at  **|AΔB| = 2**.

The recentering by 4γ̄ is the right one on the two-disagreement cells and on no other size class. And |AΔB| ≥ ||A| − |B|| = |p − q| is free, so **a block with |p − q| ≥ 4 contains no such cell at all**: there is no subspace on which the recentered generator is odd, and so no room shortage to freeze anything. The blocks that do contain one are exactly the band: by enumeration at N = 4 through 7, and at every N by the closed-form criterion in the Open section below.

This argument uses no property of the chain, and that is its scope: on ANY chain, a block outside the band is beyond the reach of this mechanism. The Heisenberg chain obeys it too, and simply does not fill the band it is allowed. What the argument leaves open is whether some other mechanism could put −4γ̄ outside the band; nothing found so far does, the full census finds nothing outside it through N = 7, and the probes find nothing at |p − q| = 4 or 6 at N = 8, 9 and 10.

## What fills the band: a spectator neither the watching nor the turning can see

The word is shared with another arc and so is the operator: the η ladder Φ used below is the site-summed spectator W of [PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY](../docs/proofs/PROOF_CODIM1_BY_ADDITIVITY.md), which proves the same two commutations directly and then adds what this note does not have: the sharp premise for moving a Jordan chain along the ladder.

The edge argument says which blocks *can* carry. This section says what *does*, on the open chain, and it is a single exact symmetry.

Write Φ for the map that adds one excitation to the ket and one to the bra **at the same site**, summed over sites, and Ψ for the one that removes such a pair:

  Φ(ρ) := Σ_l d†_l ρ d_l,  Ψ(ρ) := Σ_l d_l ρ d†_l.

**Φ and Ψ commute with the entire Liouvillian, whenever H is a number-conserving quadratic in the fermions.** Two short reasons, and they are independent of each other: the first needs nothing at all, the second is where the condition sits. The condition is not decoration and neither half of it is: the Heisenberg chain below is not quadratic and loses the band with it, and a pairing term c†_a c†_{a+1} is quadratic but not number-conserving and breaks the commuting just as surely, at a relative residual of order one where hopping alone sits at machine zero. That last one has no (p, q) block to live in, so it is read in the full Fock space rather than block by block.

- **The watching does not see the added particle.** The dephasing part is diagonal in cells, with entry −2·Σ_{l ∈ AΔB} γ_l, so it sees the disagreement set and nothing else. Adding one excitation on *both* sides at the same site leaves AΔB untouched, cell for cell. This needs no locus, no uniform γ, and no property of the chain at all.
- **The turning does not see it either.** (The turning, not the clock: this repo's [clock](../compute/MirrorWorld/Clock.cs) is the two-handed one of F95, whose radial hand is γ and whose angular hand is J. What is blind here is the angular hand alone; the radial one is the watching, and that is the bullet above.) For a number-conserving quadratic H = Σ_{ab} h_{ab} d†_a d_b, expanding [H, Φ(ρ)] leaves Φ([H, ρ]) plus the two terms where the commutator lands on the dressing, and those two cancel by a relabelling of one summation index alone: [H, d†_l] = Σ_a h_{al} d†_a and [H, d_l] = −Σ_b h_{lb} d_b, so Σ_l ([H, d†_l] ρ d_l + d†_l ρ [H, d_l]) = Σ_{a,l} h_{al} d†_a ρ d_l − Σ_{a,l} h_{al} d†_a ρ d_l = 0. Nothing about h enters: not Hermiticity, not normality, not a real spectrum. Verified at machine zero for a real symmetric h, for a complex Hermitian one, whose hopping phases a Peierls flux would be one instance of, and for a non-Hermitian non-normal one. When h *is* Hermitian there is also a picture for it, and it is the one worth carrying: the site sum is then the eigenmode sum, and [H, d†_a] = +ε_a d†_a on the ket costs precisely what [H, d_a] = −ε_a d_a on the bra returns, so the pair carries energy ε_a − ε_a = 0. That picture is an illustration, not the reason; reading it as the reason invites the idea that some property of h matters here, which is exactly what this note spends its table showing it does not.

So a particle added to both sides at once is invisible to the two things that make a coherence decay: it contributes no disagreement, so no rate, and no ket-minus-bra energy, so no phase. It can be added for free, and adding it cannot move an eigenvalue. Checked at machine zero (exactly zero, the blocks being integer matrices) on band and off-band blocks alike, including for γ profiles nowhere near the R₉₀ locus.

**This is the one diagonal, and saying so sharpens all three parts of the note.** The disagreement set AΔB is not a new object: its size is the rung index k = popcount(i ⊕ j) of the single dephasing diagonal Q, whose rate is −2γk ([`inspect --root diagonal`](../compute/RCPsiSquared.Diagnostics/Foundation/DiagonalWitness.cs), [ON_THE_ONE_DIAGONAL](../reflections/ON_THE_ONE_DIAGONAL.md), the Absorption Theorem's §4.7). Nor is the site-resolved rate this note writes down a local convenience: −2·Σ_{l ∈ AΔB} γ_l is exactly the carrier-vector form of that theorem, −Re λ = 2·Σ_x γ_x·⟨Δ_x⟩ with Δ_x the indicator of disagreement at site x, already typed in [`AbsorptionTheoremClaim`](../compute/RCPsiSquared.Core/Symmetry/AbsorptionTheoremClaim.cs) and read in [THE_VIEW_ONTO_THE_MEMORY](../reflections/THE_VIEW_ONTO_THE_MEMORY.md). Read the note through that and three things stop being coincidences.

- **The frozen root is the second rung.** −4γ̄ = −2γ̄·2 is the k = 2 rate, and what the R₉₀ locus buys is exactly this: it forces a *balanced* pair to pay the **uniform** k = 2 price while γ itself is not uniform. An unbalanced two-site set on the same profile does not (the gate prints −0.3906 for sites {0,1} against the rung's −0.3594 at N = 6). So the divisor is the k = 2 rung, restored under an uneven dephasing profile, and F140 says that rung stays put when the coupling turns on.
- **The band's parity is the one diagonal's U(1) parity.** k ≡ |p − q| (mod 2), with no exception in any cell of any block at N = 4, 5, 6. So the first trap above, that "p + q even" was never a second condition, is the popcount-side shadow of the tested fact that L_H moves k by 0 or ±2 and conserves k-parity.
- **The ladder is a within-rung motion.** Φ leaves k *exactly* fixed, not merely its parity: every image, every cell, no exception. That is the whole content of the first bullet above. The dephasing sees only the rung, and Φ never leaves it; the Hamiltonian's own motion, by contrast, is the even step k ↔ k, k ± 2. One diagonal, and the new symmetry is the motion it cannot see at all.

The two maps close into an sl(2): **[Ψ, Φ] = N − p − q** on the (p, q) block, exactly. That is what makes the ladder finite, and it is what turns the symmetry into a statement about the band:

- **Φ shifts (p, q) by (+1, +1)**, so the blocks along one diagonal line are the rungs of one ladder. Exactly three lines meet the band: the diagonal one, seeded at the corner (1,1), and the two off-diagonal ones seeded at (0,2) and (2,0).
- **Φ carries the frozen subspace of a rung onto the frozen subspace of the rung above**, not merely into it: equal dimensions and every principal cosine equal to 1, measured for the first step of each ladder at J = 3/4 for N = 5, 6, 7 and at J = 3/2 for N = 6. (Into is free, from the intertwining alone; onto is the part that has to be shown, and here it is shown for the first step by principal cosines. The ceiling section below shows it exactly, as a stacked-matrix nullity, at every step of the DIAGONAL ladder; the off-diagonal ones get their ceiling from the plain per-rung certificate instead.) The same map applied to a non-frozen subspace of the same dimension lands nowhere near it, which is what keeps the check from being satisfied by any two subspaces that merely fail to be orthogonal.
- **Both ends are annihilated**, for two different reasons worth keeping apart. On the diagonal ladder the block beyond either end is a one-cell block, which cannot carry, so the intertwining forces the frozen vectors there into zero. On the off-diagonal ladders there is no block beyond the end at all: Ψ on (0,2) would have to take a particle out of an empty ket and Φ on (N−2,N) to add one to a full bra, so both maps are identically zero before any frozen subspace is mentioned. So each ladder has exactly N − 1 rungs, p = 1..N−1 on the diagonal and p = 0..N−2 off it. Three ladders of N − 1 rungs is the 3(N − 1) count, which the law above could only measure. That every rung in between is occupied rather than merely reachable is the floor below.

That is the collapse. At J = 0 a rung starts with ⌊N/2⌋·room(p, q) cells on the root, where the room is the number of ways to place spectators beside the balanced pair (below). Turn the coupling on and exactly one direction per pair remains invisible in the sense above, namely the uniform η-pair dressing Φ applies; the rest of the room is not protected and departs. A 60-cell start and a 3-cell start land on the same 3 because neither number was ever the answer: the answer is the seed's, transported.

**What the ladder proves, and what it does not.** The frozen subspaces along one ladder are a finite-dimensional sl(2) module, since Φ and Ψ preserve each of them and [Ψ, Φ] acts on the (p, q) rung as the scalar N − p − q. Their per-rung dimensions are therefore weight multiplicities, which are symmetric about the middle and unimodal. Note what that rules out: the naive reading, that Φ carries each rung into the next and so the dimensions can only grow, is false for exactly this reason, because a raising operator on a finite-dimensional module has to acquire a kernel where an irreducible component tops out. The floor comes from the ends instead. Both ends of the diagonal ladder are F140 corners, (1,1) and (N−1,N−1), each **proved** to carry at least ⌊N/2⌋ at every coupling, the second by composing the two one-sided gamma folds (Section 4 of the proof document). At least is all the floor needs, and at least is what F140 proves at every J, on every stratum. Exactly ⌊N/2⌋ is its theorem off the finitely many exceptional couplings; at those, what gains a rung is the **algebraic** multiplicity, a single 2×2 Jordan block (the proof's Section 9), while the frozen subspace itself, the kernel this note counts everywhere, stays ⌊N/2⌋ at every exceptional coupling computed. So the floor never has to distinguish the two cases, and the word "carries" keeps its one meaning throughout. The one place where ⌊N/2⌋ is not the corner's count at all is that proof's zero-mean stratum γ̄ = 0, where it is N; every read in this note is at γ̄ = 92/1024, so the stratum is not in reach here. Unimodality then puts ⌊N/2⌋ under every rung between them, with no injectivity measured anywhere. The off-diagonal ladders run the same way between (0,2) and (N−2,N), which the transpose and the double fold make equal to each other, but their common value is measured and not proved, so their floor rests on that measurement, and on that one number alone: the ceiling section below settles everything else about those two ladders.

**What the ladder leaves is the ceiling:** that each rung carries exactly ⌊N/2⌋ and nothing more. Unimodality permits a bulge in the middle of a ladder; measured, there is none. In sl(2) terms the missing statement is that no irreducible component has its lowest weight above the first rung, equivalently that Φ lands *onto* the next rung's frozen subspace rather than merely into it. The next section settles that for one N at a time, and says what is still missing to settle it for all of them.

**Credit.** This is the η-pairing SU(2) of Medvedyeva, Essler and Prosen's mapping of the dephased tight-binding chain onto a Hubbard model with imaginary interaction ([2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09122)), seen in the vectorized picture, where the ket and bra copies are the two Hubbard species. The repo already cites them, in [LITERATURE_REVIEW](../docs/LITERATURE_REVIEW.md), for the *spectral reflection* their η-pairing produces, which is the 1D face of Π. The ladder face is the same symmetry read as a commutant element rather than an anti-symmetry, and two things are worth noting about it here: it needs no bipartite lattice, because the bra copy already carries the inverted dispersion that the Hubbard η operator has to supply with a sublattice sign; and it survives **site-dependent γ**, which their Bethe ansatz does not, and which is what lets it act on the R₉₀ locus at all. It was derived here from the two bullet points above and then recognized, in that order.

**The ring keeps the band without this ladder.** On the spin ring the Jordan-Wigner string wraps, so Φ moves between the periodic and antiperiodic fermion sectors and does not commute (the commutator is of the same order as the block itself, both with and without the string signs), and it does not carry F(1,1) **onto** F(2,2) there: the principal cosines are 0.59 and 0.20 at N = 5, and 1.00, 0.37, 0.29 at N = 6, so at N = 6 one direction is in fact carried exactly and the other two are not. Onto is what the ladder argument needs, and onto is what fails. Yet the ring's diagonal band is full at ⌊N/2⌋ on every rung read at N = 5, 6 and 7: all of them at N = 5 and 6, and the four that fit inside the size cap at N = 7. (At N = 4 it is not merely full but overfull, which the table below carries and the paragraph after it explains.) So the ladder is sufficient for the band and is not known to be necessary: the ring has the band by a route this note does not identify.

## What one exact rank buys, and where the ceiling is free

The floor is an argument and holds at every coupling. The ceiling was a census. Those are not the same kind of statement, and the distance between them is shorter than it looks: for one N at a time the ceiling is decidable, exactly, by one rank.

**The certificate.** With the dyadic scaling cleared, the block M = L₍p,p₎ + 4γ̄ is a matrix over the Gaussian integers. Reduction modulo a prime ≡ 1 (mod 4), with i sent to a square root of −1, is a ring homomorphism into a field, and rank can only drop under it, since an r × r minor that survives the reduction was nonzero before it. So

  nullity over GF(p) ≥ nullity over ℚ(i),

and an exact read of ⌊N/2⌋ **certifies** that the rational nullity is at most ⌊N/2⌋. That is the direction the ceiling was missing, and it is the one direction a float nullity cannot supply, because a float read estimates from both sides at once. The floor supplies the other direction. The two meet, and one prime is enough: an unlucky prime can only make the read too LARGE, and a too-large read certifies nothing rather than certifying something false.

**What it settles, and where.** At the read point the multiplicity is exactly ⌊N/2⌋, and since the block's entries are polynomials in the coupling J, in γ̄ and in the locus offsets δ, the rank over the whole parameter space is at least the rank at that one point: the nullity is ⌊N/2⌋ everywhere off a proper algebraic subset. Read the quantifier carefully, because it is weaker than the one the corner enjoys. This is JOINT genericity, in coupling and profile together, anchored on the one profile the gate ships. [PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR](../docs/proofs/PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR.md) Section 7 proves the corner's tightness *at every locus profile with γ̄ ≠ 0*, because a closed form (D_N ≠ 0 at every N) does the work there instead of a sampled point. The γ̄ ≠ 0 is that document's own hypothesis, and this note is on the safe side of it at γ̄ = 92/1024. Nothing here reaches that, and nothing here collides with the exceptional couplings of that document's Section 9: those raise the ALGEBRAIC multiplicity by a Jordan block while the kernel this section counts stays ⌊N/2⌋.

**The reach.** Every rung of the diagonal ladder and of one off-diagonal orientation reads exactly ⌊N/2⌋ at N = 5, 6, 7 in the gate's default run, the transposed ladder following by the fold rather than by a second rank, and every rung of the diagonal ladder at N = 8 and N = 9 in its opt-in `--deep` runs: the largest block is 4900 × 4900 at N = 8 and 15876 × 15876 at N = 9, where the two middle rungs take about an hour and forty minutes each and the whole run about three and a half hours. Those two middle rungs are also where a shortcut is available and was not needed: they sit at weights −1 and +1 of the same sl(2) module, so one of them settles both. The N = 7 middle blocks are the ones this buys outright: at 1225 × 1225 they sat above the file's old exact-rank cap and were read by SVD until the elimination was vectorized. That change reaches past this section: the band census itself, which is what the law at the top rests on, is now exact at every read up to N = 7 rather than exact where affordable.

Two sl(2) shapes of the same fact come with it, both exact where the note previously had principal cosines, and both at EVERY step of the ladder where the note previously had the first: ker(M) ∩ ker(Ψ) = 0 on every rung above the seed, so no irreducible component has its lowest weight above the first rung; and Φ is injective on each rung's frozen subspace, hence onto the next. Each is one nullity of a stacked integer matrix, so each inherits the certificate's exactness.

**Where the ceiling is free.** At the four blocks with room 1, namely (0,2) and (N−2,N) and their transposes, no computation is needed at any N. The argument is written for (0,2), which is where the gate reads it; the other three follow it through the transpose and the double fold, as the rest of this note's off-diagonal statements do. Set J = 0: the block is diagonal, and its zero entries are the cells whose disagreement pair sums to 2γ̄. Rank can only rise, so that count caps the nullity for all but finitely many J. Note that this quantifier is the stronger one, and why: the witness point sits at J = 0 of **every** profile, so the argument runs inside each fixed profile's own one-parameter family, where the certificate above had to sample a single profile and could only conclude jointly. The count is ⌊N/2⌋ **provided no non-mirror pair of offsets cancels**, and that proviso is not decoration: a two-site set pays 2γ̄ exactly when its two offsets sum to zero, which the mirror pairs always do, a repeated offset also does, and so does any pair of offsets that are negatives of one another. So the condition is on the offsets' absolute values, not on the offsets: (5, −5, 7) is three distinct numbers and still breaks it. The profile this note ships repeats its offsets from N = 10 on, and there the J = 0 seed carries 7 zeros against ⌊N/2⌋ = 5, and 10 against 6 at N = 12. Both sides are gated. On a profile whose offsets are pairwise distinct in absolute value, the free ceiling holds at every N and at every such profile, for all but finitely many couplings. It is the one statement here that does not stop at the N a computation reaches.

What that leaves on the off-diagonal ladders is their FLOOR, and it now rests on a single number rather than on a ladder: whether the seed (0,2) carries at least ⌊N/2⌋.

**Where the all-N question now sits.** The uniform profile γ_l = γ̄ lies on the locus, trivially, and every diagonal rung still reads ⌊N/2⌋ there. Since a special point of a polynomial family can only carry MORE kernel than a generic one, an argument at the uniform point alone would cap every rung generically, at every N. That move is not new: it is exactly what Section 7 of the proof document does at the corner, and does better, since there the uniform point is evaluated in closed form and gives every J ≠ 0 rather than a generic one. What is new is only that the rungs above the corner may be asked the same way, and that is worth saying because of where it lands them. At δ = 0 the R₉₀ locus is not needed at all, and the model is the integrable one of Medvedyeva, Essler and Prosen.

One thing does not survive the trip, and a reader should be warned before carrying it: at δ = 0 every pair balances, so the balanced-pair count that indexes the modes on a generic profile is not what counts there. Whatever the uniform point's ⌊N/2⌋ is, it is not that count, and it is not h's chiral pairs either, since an R-invariant diagonal leaves no chiral pair and the count stands (which is the row of the table below that discriminates against the chiral-pair reading, read at the uniform point; a different row, the Heisenberg one, is what discriminates between the two gates).

**What the uniform point is, entry for entry.** Read this note's own reach identity, |AΔB| = p + q − 2|A ∩ B|, with the intersection called what it is in the two-species picture: D = |A ∩ B| is the cell's double occupancy. On the (p,p) block the recentred rate diagonal is then exactly

  W = U·(D − (p−1)),  U = 4γ,

checked entry for entry on the integer blocks. Hence M = iJK + U·D̂ − U(p−1), where K is the two-copy hopping (h acting on the ket index minus the same h acting on the bra index, real symmetric) and D̂ is the double occupancy D read as a diagonal operator on cells: a frozen mode is an eigenvector of a Hubbard operator, hopping inverted on the bra copy and interaction U, at the eigenvalue U(p−1), and Φ is the η-pairing operator this note already credits. It raises **that** energy by exactly U while leaving the Liouvillian's eigenvalue alone, which is not a contradiction but two statements about two operators: E := M + U(p−1) is the Hubbard one, M is ours. Medvedyeva, Essler and Prosen put the i on the interaction where this puts it on the hopping; it is the same model turned. In that language the ceiling reads: **no η-lowest-weight eigenstate has energy U(n−1) for n ≥ 2.**

## What decides whether the band is filled

The other half is a gate, in the logician's sense of a condition rather than this note's other sense of a verifier script. There are in fact two of them, one for each KIND of ladder (the diagonal one and the two off-diagonal ones, which behave alike), and an earlier version of this note ran them together.

**The off-diagonal ladders are gated by their seed, and in every case tried at N ≥ 5 they exist precisely when the spectrum of the single-excitation matrix h is symmetric about zero.** That biconditional is measured, not argued: no mechanism is offered for the off-diagonal side here, and the table is what carries it. The N ≥ 5 is not decoration: the occupation-priced range-2 row breaks the biconditional at N = 4, where an h with no symmetric spectrum still carries across the whole off-diagonal band, and the N = 4 census below is where that is read out rather than smoothed over. For a plain adjacency matrix that is bipartiteness of the hopping graph, and the word is used in that sense below; note that the deformed rows of the table carry a diagonal, so their h is not the adjacency matrix of any graph, and only the spectral reading applies to them.

**The diagonal ladder is gated by the climber Φ, in the one direction the note can claim, and the climber asks something else entirely: that H be a number-conserving quadratic in the fermions.** Sufficient, that is, and not necessary: the occupation-priced ring fills the band with the climber broken, and the paragraph after the table is where that is read. A diagonal on h is a number-conserving quadratic, so it leaves the climber intact; the ZZ term is quartic, so it destroys it, and a pairing term would destroy it from the other side. The two gates are independent, and the table has to carry both columns to show it. It also carries a column for the climber itself, because the four pricing rows separate that column from the diagonal-band column beside it.

The three count columns are multiplicities of λ = −4γ̄, that is dimensions of its kernel. "Corner" is the block (1,1) alone, broken out because it is the one F140 proves. "Diagonal band" is then the rungs (p,p) from p = 2 up to p = N−1, which is the whole diagonal ladder less that first block, and note that its LAST entry, the top rung (N−1,N−1), is F140's other corner: a Hamiltonian that confines the root to the two corners shows up in this column as zeros with a ⌊N/2⌋ at the top, not as a column of zeros. "Off-diagonal band" is the seed (0,2) and the rungs on its ladder. **Every cell is read at N ≥ 5**, which is not a hedge but a necessity: at N = 4 the frozen root and its fold partner are the same number, every block's kernel is fed by both families at once, and the whole table changes character. That N is taken separately below, as a census rather than as a set of exceptions. A hop is **priced by occupation** unless the row says otherwise, which is what σ⁺σ⁻ does and what every XY row above the Heisenberg one assumes (the Heisenberg row is quartic and stands outside the question); the rows that say "priced by order" carry the same bond sets with the Jordan-Wigner sign of what is jumped over. Two bond-set deformations are priced both ways here, the ring's wrap bond and the range-2 bond, and the ring's occupies two rows because its seed column turns on the parity of N.

The ring rows are the cleanest for the seed gate, because bipartiteness there is decided by the parity of N alone while every other property, R-invariance included, is held fixed. The corner surviving every row is the control that separates it from both mechanisms: the corner's reason needs only that h be real, symmetric and R-invariant, which is exactly what the proof's Section 3 assumes, and none of these deformations touches it.

**The four pricing rows change only a price, and that is what makes them the sharpest reading here.** Take the ring's wrap bond, or a range-2 bond, and ask what it costs to hop over the sites in between. Charge by occupation and the amplitude is the same whatever stands in the way, which is what σ⁺σ⁻ does. Charge by order instead, with the Jordan-Wigner sign of the sites jumped over, and the same bond set is a free-fermion hop. The single-excitation matrix h is *literally the same matrix* either way, since with one excitation nothing is ever jumped over, so both pricings share a corner, share the spectrum the seed gate reads, and part only above the corner. What they do there is the whole content of the diagonal gate: order-honest pricing makes the commutator with the climber exactly zero on every block tried, band and off-band alike, and carries the corner's frozen subspace onto the rung above with principal cosines 1.000000000; occupation pricing tears it, at a relative residual near 2.

**The two occupation-priced rows then fall apart in different directions, and that is the honest limit of the ladder.** The ring keeps its band anyway. The hop-over does not: at N = 5, 6, 7 its diagonal reads ⌊N/2⌋, 0, ..., 0, ⌊N/2⌋, so everything but F140's two corners is gone. That is not merely like the Heisenberg row, it is the same reading rung for rung, reached without ever leaving a bond set that looks quadratic. So the climber commuting is sufficient for a full diagonal band and is not necessary, and one may now say what its absence costs rather than only that the exception exists. The census at N = 4 below is where the cost is legible, and its sharpest entry is the occupation-priced ring's middle rung, which carries 4 against ⌊N/2⌋ = 2 while the same ring priced by order carries 2. That read is exact, holds at five couplings and on two locus profiles, and belongs to N = 4 alone: the same rung is ⌊N/2⌋ at N = 5, 6 and 7 on both profiles, and the reading that would otherwise suggest itself, that even N is what overshoots, is refuted where it would have to show, on the middle rung of N = 8, where the ring reads exactly ⌊N/2⌋ = 4 under both pricings (4900 × 4900, exact, in the opt-in deep run). Order-honest pricing has no anomaly at any N read here. A rung count is held equal by the ladder; where the ladder is gone, nothing holds it.

**N = 4 is a different reading, not a list of exceptions**, and the reason is arithmetic rather than accident. The frozen root is −4γ̄ and its fold partner is 4γ̄ − 2σ = (4 − 2N)γ̄, and those two are the same number exactly at N = 4. So N = 4 is the one N where every block's kernel is fed by both root families at once, and a count above ⌊N/2⌋ is not forbidden there the way it is at every other N. That says an overshoot is *possible*, not that it must happen, and which model takes the room is still the pricing's doing. Rather than footnote the table cell by cell, here is the whole band at N = 4, every model, exact, on both locus profiles, with ⌊N/2⌋ = 2. The blocks are grouped by where they sit on their ladder, and the grouping hides nothing: inside every group all the blocks carry the same number in all six models, which is gated. The split between the off-diagonal middles and the off-diagonal ends is not cosmetic either, since two of the rows read differently across it.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Three things in that census do not survive to N ≥ 5 and should not be carried out of it. Heisenberg holds **four** corner blocks here, its own two and the two fold corners it only meets at this N, which is why its off-diagonal cell in the table above reads 0 while the fold-corner column here reads 2: that table has one column for the whole off-diagonal band, this one splits it into the middles and the ends. The occupation-priced range-2 row has its **whole** diagonal here, where from N = 5 on it keeps only the two corners, and it carries 1 rather than 0 across the off-diagonal band, which is what breaks the seed gate's biconditional at this N. And the occupation-priced ring's middle rung is the doubling that gives the ladder's absence its price. The order-priced rows have no anomaly at all: the ring is flat at 2 and the range-2 row is exactly what the seed gate predicts from its unsymmetric h.

**The Heisenberg row is the one that does the discriminating, and an earlier version of this note read it wrong.** It said that the Heisenberg chain's single-excitation matrix is the path adjacency plus the ZZ diagonal, that a diagonal is what breaks a bipartite spectrum, that an XY chain with any R-invariant diagonal therefore behaves the same way, and so that the confinement to the corners belongs to the diagonal on h. The diagonal-band column refutes it: an XY chain with an R-invariant diagonal keeps its **diagonal** band, full at ⌊N/2⌋ on every rung, at N = 5, 6 and 7 and for both a diagonal on the end sites and one on the inner sites. It is not confined to the corner at all. Heisenberg is, and the reason is not the diagonal: it is that XX+YY+ZZ is quartic in the fermions, so the climber does not intertwine (the commutator is of the same order as the block itself, against exactly 0 on every open-chain XY variant), and with no ladder there is nothing to carry the corner's modes anywhere. For that control the Heisenberg blocks are built independently and checked to reproduce the census F140 already knows: both corners ⌊N/2⌋, every band block between them 0.

For the same reason "the spectrum of h" is not the right question in the Heisenberg row. Reading a quartic H through its one-excitation sector is exactly the step that produced the error.

## What is not the reason

Two candidates are dead, and both deaths are informative.

**The corner's room shortage does not extend.** τQ fixes a cell only when B = R(A), which forces |A| = |B|. So the off-diagonal block pairs (p, p+2) ⊕ (p+2, p) have **no fixed cells at all**: the shortage argument predicts zero there, and ⌊N/2⌋ is measured. On the diagonal it is wrong in both directions at N = 6, where (2,2) is predicted 0 and measures 3 while (3,3) is predicted 6 and measures 3; at N = 5 it happens to match everywhere, which is worth saying, since a rule that is sometimes right is not refuted by one N. None of these counts is hand-read: the shortage's predictions are gated in V6 and the multiplicities they are compared against in V1's census. Whatever pins the band, it is not the count that pins the corner, even though the answer is the same number.

**The chiral intertwiner does not transport the corner's modes.** This was the obvious candidate and it is worth writing out, because its algebra is exactly right and it still fails. For free fermions,

  [H, Ξ(ρ)] = Ξ([H, ρ]) − (ε_a + ε_b)·Ξ(ρ),   Ξ(ρ) := d_a ρ d_b,

so Ξ commutes with the Hamiltonian part precisely on a chiral pair ε_a + ε_b = 0, and it shifts (p, q) by (−1, +1), one step of exactly the band's width. Chiral pairs exist precisely when h is bipartite, which is the gate; the open chain has ⌊N/2⌋ of them; and ρ ↦ d†_a ρ d_a commutes with the Hamiltonian part for every mode a and climbs the diagonal, so the reachable set is |p − q| ∈ {0, 2}. Every ingredient lines up.

It still fails. Applied to a basis of the corner's frozen subspace, the images are nonzero but are not in the (0,2) frozen subspace: the relative residual of L₍₀,₂₎ + 4γ̄ on them runs from 4·10⁻² to 7.5·10⁻¹, and the images span rank 8, 11 and 12 at N = 5, 6, 7, inside blocks of dimension 10, 15 and 21, against a frozen subspace of dimension 2 or 3. The verifier checks the map first, so this is not a coding slip: the intertwining identity holds to 3·10⁻¹⁵ on a chiral pair and reproduces the predicted commutator i·J·(ε_a + ε_b)·Ξ off it. **The map commutes with the Hamiltonian part and not with the dephasing, and the dephasing is what decides.**

So the count matches, the gate matches, the reachable set matches, and the map is still the wrong map. The map that works is the *other* one in that sentence, summed over sites rather than taken one mode at a time: the sum is what the dephasing cannot see, and the dephasing is what decides.

## Where the number comes from, and what is still missing

The depth question has moved. It is no longer "why ⌊N/2⌋" but something sharper, and the sharpening is a result rather than a rewording. The (0,2) block consists **entirely** of cells with |AΔB| = 2, and exactly ⌊N/2⌋ of them are the balanced-pair cells |vac⟩⟨{l, R(l)}| whose rate diagonal is −2(γ_l + γ_{R(l)}) + 4γ̄ = 0 on the locus. One per balanced pair, again, and the same count.

The involution that makes M odd there is **antilinear**: reversal of the bra index composed with complex conjugation, since the block's ket sector is the vacuum, so its Hamiltonian part is +iH₂ alone and conjugation is what turns that sign, while on the locus the reversal turns the rate diagonal's sign at |AΔB| = 2. The oddness is exact, entry for entry. That points at the antilinear corner of the object manager rather than at τQ. But an antilinear involution has equal-dimensional real forms, so it yields no shortage and cannot be the counter; and it survives the diagonal perturbation that empties the band, gated here beside the emptying itself, so it cannot be the gate either. It is a third structure sitting in the right place, doing neither job on its own. Hand it over as a fact, not as a lead.

**At J = 0 the count is manifest, and it is one per balanced pair.** Turn the coupling off and every band block is diagonal, so one can simply read which cells sit at the root: the entry is −2·Σ_{l ∈ AΔB} γ_l + 4γ̄, which vanishes when the disagreement set sums to 2γ̄, and a balanced pair {l, R(l)} always does. For the profiles used here nothing else does, so the balanced pairs put every cell that is on the root.

**That last step is a fact about the profile and not about the locus**, which is worth stating plainly because an earlier version of this note asserted the stronger thing, that a four-site set *cannot* sum to 2γ̄ on the locus. It can. Write the rates as γ̄ ± δ_c, one offset per reflection pair; the four sites carrying the minus signs sum to 4γ̄ − Σ_c δ_c, so any locus profile whose offsets sum to 2γ̄ puts a four-site set exactly on the root. At N = 8 with offsets (60, 55, 45, 24)/1024 against γ̄ = 92/1024, all distinct, the J = 0 root picks up cells with |AΔB| = 4 alongside the pair cells.

**The band law survives that profile**, which is the more interesting half and is gated: at J = 3/4 the corner and (0,2) still carry exactly ⌊N/2⌋ = 4, and (0,4) and (1,5) still carry nothing. So the extra root cells are simply not protected, and they leave with the rest of the room the moment the coupling turns on. The J = 0 reading is a convenience for counting on generic profiles; the τQ-oddness criterion behind the edge, by contrast, holds on the whole locus and is what the edge argument actually rests on.

**But the number of balanced-pair cells is not ⌊N/2⌋ in most blocks, and it factors.** How many of them a block holds depends on how much room there is beside the pair. Fix a pair P and ask for the cells with AΔB = P: the two sites of P are split between A and B, and everything else is the shared set S = A ∩ B avoiding P. So the count is one factor for the pair and one for the room,

  **balanced-pair cells in (p, q) = ⌊N/2⌋ · room(p, q),  room = 2·C(N−2, p−1) on the diagonal and C(N−2, min(p,q)) off it,**

the 2 being the two ways to orient the pair. This counts the cells whose disagreement set is a balanced pair, which is what the identity is about; that those are all the cells on the root is the profile-dependent step above. At N = 7 it reads 3·1, 3·2, 3·10, 3·5 and 3·20 for the blocks (0,2), (1,1), (2,2), (1,3) and (3,3), which is 3, 6, 30, 15 and 60. Every one of them collapses to exactly 3 the moment the coupling turns on: **the pair factor is what survives and the room is what collapses.** The factorisation itself is checked for every band block at N = 4..9; the collapse it feeds is the census, so it is read at N = 4..7.

**Nothing departs where there is no room, and that is four blocks, not one.** The room is 1 exactly at the two ends of each off-diagonal ladder, (0,2) and (N−2,N) and their transposes, since C(N−2, 0) = C(N−2, N−2) = 1: a two-site bra beside an empty ket leaves nowhere to put a spectator, and so does a two-site gap beside a full one. In all four the J = 0 count is already ⌊N/2⌋. The diagonal ladder's ends have room 2 instead, which is F140's doubling at J = 0.

**In (0,2) the count is explained, but only in the limit.** The J = 0 kernel is exactly the span of the ⌊N/2⌋ balanced-pair cells, the frozen dimension is still ⌊N/2⌋ at both couplings the check reads, and as J shrinks the frozen subspace converges onto that span: the principal cosines reach 0.998, 0.988, 0.914 at N = 7, J = 1/256. So the pair cells index the modes **in the J → 0 limit**, which is where the number comes from. They are not what the modes look like at finite coupling: at J = 3/4 the same subspace has rotated almost entirely off them (cosines 0.059, 0.018, 0.002 at N = 7). Anyone re-deriving this should note that reading a null space basis out of a numerically zero matrix returns whatever basis the routine happens to emit; the pair alignment has to be measured against the pair-cell span, not read off a basis vector.

**The collapse is the ladder**, and the section above is where it is answered: the room is not protected, one direction per pair is, and that direction is the seed's mode carried up by Φ. A departure-rate reading, in the genre of the corner's own ladder J^{2d_c}, was the move this note expected to need; it turned out not to be necessary, because the survivors are fixed from below by a symmetry rather than selected from above by a rate. What that argument leaves is a floor, not an equality: see the end of that section.

Do NOT try to test the pair indexing by walking one pair off balance. It cannot be done: the pair deviations sum to zero, so no single pair can be unbalanced alone, and any perturbation moves γ̄ and with it the root, detuning every pair cell at once. The J = 0 reading above is the version of that experiment that is well posed.

The read now requires the whole spectrum to avoid the decade band between 10⁻¹² and 10⁻⁶, escalates to the exact rank when it does not and the block is small enough to afford it, and raises rather than returns a number it cannot support. Nothing else moved when that landed, which is the only evidence that the other float reads were sound.

## Open

- **The ceiling holds one N at a time, and the all-N argument is missing.** The certificate settles every rung of the diagonal ladder and of one off-diagonal orientation at N = 5, 6, 7, the third ladder by the fold, and the diagonal ladder alone at N = 8 and N = 9, for generic coupling and profile; it does not scale, because it samples a point rather than proving a nonvanishing. The two shapes it settles at N = 5, 6, 7, that no irreducible component has its lowest weight above the first rung and that Φ is onto rather than into, are what an all-N argument would have to deliver. The uniform profile is where to ask, both because a degenerate point of the family bounds the generic one and because the model there is integrable; the corner's own version of that step is Section 7 of the proof document, which gets every J ≠ 0 out of a closed form rather than a generic subset out of a sample.
- **The off-diagonal floor is one number.** With the ceiling certified on those ladders and free at their room-1 ends, what is left of them is whether the seed (0,2) carries at least ⌊N/2⌋. The τQ shortage that counts the corner has no fixed cell there, and the antilinear involution that is exactly odd there yields no shortage, so both known counters are unavailable and the number is still measured.
- **The occupation-priced ring has the band without the ladder, and it is the only deformation tried that does.** The climber does not commute there and does not carry the corner's frozen subspace onto the rung above, yet the diagonal band is full at N = 5, 6, 7. Pricing the same wrap bond by order restores both, so the missing ingredient is named: it is the Jordan-Wigner string on that one bond, and Φ moving between the periodic and antiperiodic fermion sectors is what a parity-resolved climber would have to repair. What is still open is whether such a climber exists or the occupation-priced ring fills the band for a different reason. Two things now bound the answer. The band it fills is not the same band: at N = 4 its middle rung carries 4 against ⌊N/2⌋ = 2, so whatever fills it does not enforce a uniform rung count. And the exception is not generic to un-quadratic pricing, since the range-2 bond priced the same way keeps only its two end rungs. Until this is settled the ladder is sufficient and not known to be necessary.
- **Whether the edge argument is sufficient as well as necessary.** The section "Why the edge sits where it does" removes the known mechanism outside the band; it does not prove the null out there, which stays measurement. Nor does it say anything about which blocks inside carry: that is the ladder's job, and the ladder answers it for the diagonal and, on a non-bipartite h, correctly leaves the off-diagonal ones empty.
- **The reach is now a law, and belongs elsewhere.** |AΔB| = p + q − 2|A∩B|, so a cell with |AΔB| = 2 exists in (p, q) exactly when |A∩B| = (p+q)/2 − 1 is a non-negative integer at most min(p, q) whose union fits in N. Those three bounds are p + q even with |p − q| ≤ 2, p + q ≥ 2, and p + q ≤ 2N − 2: the band, less (0,0) and (N,N), and 3(N−1) blocks. Gated by enumeration to N = 7 and by the criterion to N = 40. The edge argument is therefore no longer chain-specific evidence but a lemma, and per this note's own earlier judgement it belongs in [PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR](../docs/proofs/PROOF_R90_FROZEN_DIVISOR.md) beside the census it scopes. Moving it is an editorial step this note has not taken.
- **No F number, still, but the reason has changed.** The mechanism is no longer missing: it is an exact symmetry with a two-line proof. What is missing is the ceiling for all N at once, since a certificate settles one N at a time, and the ring says the mechanism is not the whole story. A number minted now would name the chain's half of it. The natural shape when it is ready is not one F but two: the ladder identity, which is general and has nothing to do with the R₉₀ locus, and the band law, which is what the ladder does to F140's corner.
